# Percolation and infection methods

本次整理与历史阅读/审核状态的区别，以及可选本地附件的访问方式，见 [证据与迁移说明](../math-research/references/evidence.md)。

Use this skill when the problem concerns open paths/clusters, renormalization by good blocks, bootstrap infection, graph-bootstrap running time, random-set zero forcing, or a percolation-style infection argument.

Start by identifying the random object, update rule, geometry, and target observable. Keep these distinctions explicit:

- iid bond/site percolation, oriented percolation and dependent random orientations are different models;
- vertex bootstrap, graph-bootstrap edge infection and zero forcing use different update rules;
- random initial blue sets and random forcing events are different zero-forcing models;
- weak saturation adds edges sequentially, while graph-bootstrap rounds add all eligible edges simultaneously;
- a critical-density threshold, one-arm probability, infection-time tail and maximum running time are different quantities.

For block arguments, define the good-block event and prove the needed domination/dependence and bad-cluster bounds before using a coarse-grained path. For enhancements, verify locality, translation invariance, and the essential local surgery condition before applying strict critical-point monotonicity. For bootstrap infection, distinguish \(q_c\) from the auxiliary exponential-decay threshold \(\widetilde q_c\); the current Hartarsky source does not identify them for all update families. Keep Holroyd’s iid two-neighbour process on \(\mathbb Z^2\), Janson et al.’s threshold process on \(G(n,p)\), and probabilistic zero forcing on \(G(n,p)\) as separate models, each with its own update rule and clock.

For two-dimensional finite update families, Balister et al. prove positive critical probability for the subcritical class using stable directions, multiscale barriers, and closed triangular covers; this is a percolation-threshold statement, not an infection-time-tail theorem. For infinite-tree bootstrap, Balogh–Peres–Pete relate complete-occupation thresholds to branching number and give a sufficient anchored-expansion condition on regular graphs; distinguish complete occupation from existence of an infinite occupied component. For extremal graph-bootstrap time, Fabian et al. give exact parity-dependent bounds for cycle rules and results for trees, connectivity, and simulation constructions; Balogh–Kronenberg–Pokrovskiy–Szabó give quadratic lower bounds for each fixed r≥6 and a near-quadratic lower bound for K5, leaving the K5 o(n²) question open. Their Definition 7 calls a uniform hypergraph “induced H-free” when every (not necessarily induced) H-copy in its 2-skeleton is contained in one hyperedge; this custom condition is not ordinary induced-freeness of the 2-skeleton. Balogh–Bollobás–Morris study eventual percolation thresholds in G(n,p), a different observable from maximum running time. Weak-saturation counts in random hosts use a sequential edge order and do not bound synchronous rounds. Kalinichenko et al. Theorem 1.4 gives exact random-host weak-saturation equality under the structural hypothesis wsat(n,F)≥(δ(F)−1)n+D for every n; the paper verifies it for complete and complete bipartite graphs, minimum-degree-one graphs, and connected or cut-edge-free minimum-degree-two graphs. For other targets, proving the hypothesis and checking the sparse p threshold remain separate obligations. Gravner–Paguyo–Slivken bound maximal spanning time for a Hamming-plane row/column-count rule; this does not itself imply a minimum-seed bound in A200. For the same Hamming-plane rule, Gravner–Sivakoff–Slivken state that a finite seed supported in any rectangle containing the zero-set spans the whole plane exactly when it internally spans that rectangle, and Theorem 1.2 reduces the minimum seed to such a finite rectangle. Thus A200’s finite-to-infinite interface is supported for this model. The general stronger inequality γ(Z)≥|Z|/2 remains beyond their Theorem 1.1, which gives only γ(Z)≥|Z|/4 and leaves improvement open. These conclusions concern their stated dynamics and do not automatically transfer to vertex bootstrap, random seeds, or random forcing.

Aizenman–Barsky prove equality of the expected-cluster and infinite-cluster thresholds and a critical ghost-field bound for independent translation-invariant percolation with summable couplings; the proof uses Russo pivotality, a graph decomposition, and van den Berg–Kesten. The square-root bound M(βτ,h)≥c√h is stated for small positive h, with interaction-dependent constants, not for all h>0. Their contact-process extension is discrete-time, and the paper leaves the continuous-time case unproved. For finite-volume bootstrap metastability, Aizenman–Lebowitz prove subsequential step-threshold behavior for internally spanned boxes in the iid two-neighbour model; the threshold is bounded away from zero and infinity but is not identified as a unique universal constant, and their infinite-volume time-density statement is conjectural. Balogh–Bollobás–Duminil-Copin–Morris (BBDM12) prove a sharp-threshold asymptotic for nearest-neighbour r-bootstrap percolation on [n]^d at fixed d≥r≥2, using variable-threshold auxiliary boxes, rectangle hierarchies, crossing estimates, and a Cerf–Cirillo induction; their local 36-page 2010 author manuscript gives the lower-bound proof and cites earlier work for the matching upper bound. The source’s exact version and limits are recorded in [its proof card](evidence/W05/cards/bbdm12.md); do not transfer the result to arbitrary update families, graph-bootstrap clocks, random zero forcing, or the Hamming-plane rule. Sharp finite-grid threshold papers are model-specific: Balogh–Bollobás–Morris prove `p_c([n]^3,3) = (λ(3,3)+o(1))/log log n` for three-neighbour vertex bootstrap in dimension three, and for two-neighbour bootstrap on `[n]^d` when `d ≫ log₂ n` they obtain the high-dimensional asymptotic in [bbmhd10](evidence/W05/cards/bbmhd10.md). Neither result gives a general infection-time tail. Gravner–Sivakoff–Slivken prove seed-size and product-measure rate results for their long-range Hamming-plane rule that counts all points on a row and column; apply the rectangle-to-plane criterion only when the update rule and initial-set hypotheses match. It settles A200’s finite-to-infinite interface for this model, but not the general γ(Z)≥|Z|/2 target. Aldous–Diaconis’s Hammersley/LIS Poisson-path limit is an auxiliary path-growth tool, not a domination theorem for dependent percolation. Fabian–Morris–Szabó’s 2026 survey proves Theorem 4.2 in its own §4.3 for (2,1)-inseparable rules; other cited theorem summaries remain attributed to their primary sources and preserve the distinction between `M_H(n)`, random-host `p_c(n,H)`, and weak-saturation edge counts. Bollobás–Smith–Uzzell classify finite update families on \(\mathbb Z^2\) and give order-of-magnitude critical/supercritical infection times; they do not prove the subcritical infection-time tail in B179, and their torus orders are stated without a separate proof. Duminil-Copin–Tassion’s transitive-graph sharpness argument uses a finite-set criterion and Russo/BK estimates for independent Bernoulli edges, with exponential spatial decay requiring finite-range couplings. Its external-field sections derive the distinct ghost-connection bound \(M(\beta_c,h)\ge c\sqrt h\) and Ising magnetization bound \(\langle\sigma_0\rangle_{\beta_c,h}\ge ch^{1/3}\). Durrett’s unconditional edge speed and large deviations are for the enlarged left-half-line edge; singleton edge speeds are asserted only on the event of an infinite path. Grimmett–Marstrand’s Theorem A applies to a thickening \(2kF+B(k)\): the paper’s original slice \(S(k)\) comes from \(F=\mathbb Z^2\) and has two unrestricted coordinates, while a codimension-one thickening from \(F=\mathbb Z^{d-1}\) is a separate consequence. The paper does not settle whether the infinite-cluster probability vanishes at criticality. Each transfer to a dependent infection process or another geometry needs its own coupling and hypotheses.
