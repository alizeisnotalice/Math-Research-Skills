## Main result and hypotheses

The paper treats independent, translation-invariant bond and site percolation on \(\mathbb Z^d\); the bond formulation allows oriented/partially oriented interactions and summable long-range couplings, with \(|J|=\sum_x J_{0,x}<\infty\). Write \(\beta_\tau\) for the threshold at which expected finite-cluster size ceases to be finite and \(\beta_H\) for the onset of positive probability of an infinite cluster. Theorem 1.1 proves \(\beta_\tau=\beta_H\) (and the corresponding equality for site parameter \(p\)). Theorem 1.2 proves, at the common threshold, for small positive field \(h\) (as \(h\downarrow0\)),

\[
M(\beta_\tau,h)\ge c h^{1/2},\qquad 0<h\le h_0,
\]

where \(M(\beta,h)=1-\sum_{1\le n<\infty}P_n(\beta)e^{-nh}\) is the ghost-field/order-parameter quantity and positive constants \(c,h_0\) may depend on the interaction. The source states “for small \(h\)”; it does not claim this lower bound for all \(h>0\) (indeed \(M\le1\)). It implies the stated mean-field bound \(\delta\ge2\) under the paper’s exponent convention, but does not assert existence of a power law.

For bond percolation, the two main differential inequalities are (1.14)–(1.15), initially derived in finite volume and interpreted in the weak sense after taking limits:

\[
\partial_\beta M\le |J|M\,\partial_hM,
\qquad
M\le h\,\partial_hM+M^2+\beta M\,\partial_\beta M.
\]

The first follows from Russo’s formula for connection to the ghost set: expose the origin cluster as a self-determined set, condition on it, and bound the probability of an exterior connection. The second uses the graph-theoretic decomposition in Lemma 3.5 (exactly one green connection, two bond-disjoint green connections, or a pivotal separating edge), the van den Berg–Kesten inequality, and a bond-subdivision argument for the ghost edges. Combining them gives an ordinary differential inequality in \(h\); Lemma 4.1 integrates it without assuming a critical power law and yields the square-root field bound. Section 5 integrates the finite-volume inequalities in \(\beta\) to identify the susceptibility and percolation thresholds.

## Extensions and transfer limits

Section 7 gives a separate site-percolation adaptation using Russo pivotality at vacant sites and a degree/neighbour-count factor; the bond constants cannot simply be reused unchanged. Section 8.2 applies the oriented-percolation argument to the **discrete-time** contact process, translating infection histories into an oriented space-time cluster. The authors explicitly leave the continuous-time extension unproved because short-time fluctuations obstruct the needed second inequality. Section 8 also discusses weakly nonhomogeneous systems and Ising-type models, but each has its own hypotheses and argument.

This is a sharpness/cluster-size result for product percolation and a particular discrete contact-process extension. It is not a theorem about synchronous bootstrap infection time, random forcing, or dependent random orientations. For C142/C144 it supplies background on threshold mechanisms only; transferring a conclusion to arbitrary Cayley graphs or to a random infinite cluster still requires the missing graph-specific argument.
