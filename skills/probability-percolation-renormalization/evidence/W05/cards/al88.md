## Model and finite-volume theorem

The principal example is synchronous two-neighbour bootstrap percolation on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\): initially each site is occupied independently with probability \(1-e^{-p}\), occupied sites persist, and each vacant site with at least two occupied nearest neighbours becomes occupied at the next step. The paper also isolates deterministic monotonicity, finite interaction range, and rectangular stationarity assumptions under which parts of its argument extend. Let \(A_L=(-L/2,L/2)^d\), and let \(R(L,p)\) be the probability that the process restricted to \(A_L\) internally spans it. Their scaled variable is

\[
\lambda=p^{1/(d-1)}\log L.
\]

Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 2–4; printed pp. 3802–3803) states that along every sequence \(p\downarrow0\) there is a subsequence for which \(R(L,p)=Q_p(\lambda)\) converges pointwise away from a threshold \(\lambda_c\) to a step function. The subsequential threshold is bounded between positive constants depending on dimension; the proof does **not** rule out different subsequential values or identify a universal sharp constant. More explicitly, for small \(p\), the probability has an exponentially small subthreshold form

\[
R(L,p)=\exp\{-p^{-1/(d-1)}[\lambda_c(p)-\lambda+o(1)]\}
\quad (o(1)<\lambda<\lambda_c(p)),
\]

and above threshold it is at least \(1-\exp(-cL^{d-1})\). The transition window in \(\lambda\) shrinks to zero as \(p\downarrow0\). The theorem is a finite-box metastability statement, not a nonzero infinite-volume critical density.

## Proof chain and limits

The deterministic merging lemma (Lemma 1, PDF pp. 5–6) says that if a region is internally spanned then it contains an internally spanned rectangle at an intermediate scale: the maximal side lies between \(k\) and approximately \(2k+D\), where \(D\) is the separation parameter in the rectangular-stationarity hypothesis (\(D=2\) for the two-neighbour model). It explicitly warns that other spanning regions may have no internally spanned rectangle at every small intermediate scale.

The critical-droplet analysis bounds \(R(n,p)\) over scales and identifies a plateau before the rare local spanning event controls the large box. Lemma 5 (PDF pp. 9–10) compares spanning of \(A_L\) with a localized internally spanned block on a spaced sublattice, up to a small symmetric-difference error over the stated intermediate range of \(L\). The localized events have a product structure; counting many disjoint candidate blocks gives the sharp finite-size step, and a further block renormalization extends the supercritical estimate to larger boxes. The proof mechanism is a rare local droplet plus many independent opportunities, not a general FKG domination theorem for arbitrary infection rules.

Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 12–13) shows that the probability \(M(L,p)\) that the origin is occupied in the final configuration of the finite box has the same asymptotic behavior as \(R(L,p)\), uniformly over \(L\) as \(p\downarrow0\). By contrast, the proposed step behavior for the infinite-lattice time-dependent density \(M_T(t,p)\) is explicitly a conjecture in §5.1; §5.2’s Glauber-dynamics extension is also conjectural. Neither is an established infection-time tail theorem.

## Transfer limits

The result concerns iid initial occupation and a specific monotone nearest-neighbour bootstrap mechanism (with carefully listed extensions), and its main threshold is finite-volume. It does not establish the B179 small-density exponential infection-time tail for arbitrary update families, dimensions, random graphs, dependent occupation, oriented paths, or two-player games. The time-dependent infinite-system statement must remain labeled conjectural.
