## Process and exact cycle result

For a fixed graph `H`, the synchronous graph-bootstrap process on an `n`-vertex starting graph adds, in each round, every absent edge whose addition completes a copy of `H`; `M_H(n)` is the maximum stabilization time over all starting graphs. This is edge infection, not vertex bootstrap or a sequential weak-saturation ordering.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 3) gives, for fixed `k≥3` and sufficiently large `n`, an exact parity-dependent formula:

\[
M_{C_k}(n)=\begin{cases}
\left\lceil\log_{k-1}(n+k^2-4k+2)\right\rceil,&k\text{ odd},\\
\left\lceil\log_{k-1}(2n+k^2-5k)\right\rceil,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]

The paper’s “sufficiently large” cutoff is nontrivial and depends on `k`; Remark 1.3 gives a rough scale and notes that smaller `n` can behave differently. Thus the result implies logarithmic time for each fixed cycle, not a uniform-in-`k` bound for all `n`.

## Proof mechanism and extensions

For any newly added edge, its endpoints were joined by a path of length `k−1` in the previous round. Lemma 4.1 (PDF p. 7) therefore contracts graph distances by a factor about `k−1` each round, with an additive `k−2` remainder. Lower bounds start from paths for odd `k` and from a specially arranged non-bipartite `P_Δ` for even `k`. Sets of attainable endpoint differences are tracked as `(k−1)^i−α(k−2)−βk`; the largest nonrepresentable offset is the Frobenius number of the semigroup generated by `k−2,k`. Upper bounds first show which path distances are attained using sumsets and then handle even, non-bipartite graphs by decomposing a shortest odd walk into two paths and tracking when the odd-cycle parity reaches the target pair. For a disjoint union of cycles with largest cycle length `k_1`, Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 3) gives `log_{k_1−1}(n)−1 ≤ M_H(n) < log_{k_1−1}(n)+6s^6k_1^2` for sufficiently large `n`.

These arguments are specific to cycle-completion rules and their path/walk structure. They do not establish the proposed limit or subquadratic behavior for general fixed `H`, and cannot be transferred to a vertex-bootstrap infection or random initial-edge model without a separate simulation/coupling proof.
