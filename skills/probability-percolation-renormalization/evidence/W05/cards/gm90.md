## Slab-approximation theorem

The process is iid **site** Bernoulli percolation on \(\mathbb Z^d\). For an infinite connected set \(F\subset\mathbb Z^d\), write \(p_c(F)\) for its percolation threshold. Theorem A (PDF p. 10; printed p. 447) says: if \(p_c(F)<1\), then for every \(\eta>0\) there is an integer \(k\) such that

\[
p_c(2kF+B(k))\le p_c(\mathbb Z^d)+\eta.
\]

The paper defines its original slice on PDF p. 2 as

\[
S(k)=\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:0\le x_j\le k\text{ for every }j>2\};
\]

the coordinates \(x_1,x_2\) are unrestricted, so it has two unbounded directions and \(d-2\) bounded coordinates. In Theorem A’s stated application (PDF p. 10), \(F=\mathbb Z^2\) embedded in the first two coordinates, and \(2kF+B(k)\) is a translate of this original slice (S(2k)). The introduction states \(p_c(\mathbb Z^d)=p_c(S):=\lim_k p_c(S(k))\) for \(d>3\), and then obtains half-space equality by the squeeze in (1.3)–(1.4); the argument does not settle whether the infinite-cluster probability vanishes at \(p_c\).

Separately, Theorem A can be applied with \(F=\mathbb Z^{d-1}\times\{0\}\) when \(d\ge3\), since \(p_c(\mathbb Z^{d-1})<1\). This gives a codimension-one thickened hyperplane. That is a valid additional consequence, but for \(d\ge4\) it is a different slab geometry from the paper’s original (S(k)), which has two unbounded directions.

## Proof chain and consequences

Section 3’s Lemmas 1–6 (PDF pp. 5–10) are the engine. First, reveal a cluster one vertex at a time; if every conditional success probability is at least some \(y>p_c(F)\), then the adaptive cluster has positive probability to be infinite. Next, a sprinkling lemma turns one likely connection into many boundary connection sites; symmetry and FKG show that many candidate pads occur on a distant face. Lemma 6 handles the dependence caused by the explored boundary: conditional on previously inspected vertices being \(\beta(x)\)-closed, with high probability a path crosses the annulus using a vertex that becomes open at the sprinkled level \(\beta(x)+\delta\). The renormalized construction then explores overlapping site-boxes, steering between fresh pads while maintaining conditional success above \(p_c(F)\). This proves an infinite path at level \(p_c(\mathbb Z^d)+\eta\) inside a sufficiently thickened copy of \(F\).

Section 5 (PDF pp. 16–19) lists applications: finite supercritical correlation length, bounds on large finite-cluster probabilities, many disjoint crossings, transport estimates, and a subadditive limit for chemical distance. The latter uses the slab theorem to obtain finite expected path length between nearest points in the unique infinite cluster and then applies a subadditive ergodic theorem; the resulting time constant is finite and at least the \(\ell^1\) distance. These consequences retain the paper’s iid site-percolation assumptions and dimension restrictions.

## Transfer limits

The proof relies on independent site labels, sprinkling, and \(p_c(F)<1\); it is not a result for dependent infection processes, random update rules, or oriented percolation. Any use for C108 or C142–C144 needs a separate graph/cluster coupling and the exact geometric hypotheses; slab percolation alone does not settle the mapped group or random-cluster claims.
