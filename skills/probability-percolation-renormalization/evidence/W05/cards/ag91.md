## Percolation statement and assumptions

Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3; printed p. 819) asserts that for site percolation on $\mathbb Z^d$ with $p_c>0$, any essential local enhancement and any activation parameter $s>0$ produce a nonempty interval $(\pi(s),p_c)$ on which the enhanced process has positive probability of an infinite cluster. Here essentiality is the existence of a configuration with no doubly-infinite open path for which the local enhancement creates one. This is a strict-shift theorem statement, not a quantitative lower bound on the shift. The paper’s stated result must be distinguished from the proof gap below.

## Proof chain actually read

For a finite box, use an increasing event $E_L$ that a prescribed central region connects to the boundary and write its probability $r_L(p,s)$. Generalized Russo formulas express the two parameter derivatives by pivotal probabilities. Lemma 2 (PDF pp. 12–15) is intended to compare pivotal events by a bounded local surgery: a pivotal configuration is modified so that an enhancement becomes pivotal, after which Russo derivatives yield a finite-volume differential inequality. However, the key existence step is not formally proved in the site-percolation path geometry: on PDF p. 14 the authors explicitly replace the geometrical proof by Fig. 4. The resulting uniform surgery cannot be treated here as established for every external configuration merely from essentiality. The local companion source, Grimmett’s *Selected Problems in Probability*, §6 (PDF pp. 10–11), identifies the omitted site-percolation graph-theoretic detail: d=2 is described as straightforward; a positive answer for d=3 is attributed to another source; for d≥4 the question is described there as apparently open at that time. It also distinguishes the bond-percolation version, whose corresponding step was treated in detail elsewhere. Subject to a valid surgery comparison, the paper derives an inequality of the form

\[
\partial_s r_L(p,s)\;\ge\;g_1(p,s)\,\partial_p r_L(p,s).
\]

The paper then integrates along a characteristic curve and passes to the infinite-volume limit to obtain its Theorem 1. Since the site-surgery input is the skipped step just noted, this card records the theorem as stated but does not certify that comparison for dimensions/configurations where the missing geometry has not separately been supplied. Monotonicity alone does not fill the gap.

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 5) is a separate ferromagnetic Ising result: for $d\ge2$, translation-invariant finite-range nonnegative couplings satisfying the paper’s regularity condition (positive interactions span the lattice), increasing a coupling strictly raises $T_c$, with derivative bounds. Its proof uses random-current identities on PDF pp. 16–19, not the percolation pivotal comparison. The comparison formula and theorem statement were read; the geometry caveat above concerns the site-percolation surgery argument. The contact-process discussion (PDF pp. 10–11) is an extension/outline with extra oriented-space-time work; do not cite it as a fully proved general contact-process theorem from this paper.
