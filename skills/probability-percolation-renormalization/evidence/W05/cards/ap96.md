# ap96 — Chemical distance by block renormalization

**Reading status:** `fulltext_read`, complete 14-page paper, PDF pp. 1–14. R03 approved the strict-v2 scope; the reading status and source-specific limits above remain unchanged.

**Reference:** Peter Antal and Agoston Pisztora, “On the Chemical Distance for Supercritical Bernoulli Percolation,” *Ann. Probab.* 24(2) (1996), 1036–1048. Author-hosted scan verified against the journal title page.

## Result and assumptions

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3): for nearest-neighbour **iid bond percolation** on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), and \(p>p_c(d)\), there is \(\rho(p,d)\ge1\) such that

\[
\limsup_{|y|\to\infty}\frac1{|y|}
\log\mathbb P_p\bigl(0\leftrightarrow y,\ D(0,y)>\rho|y|\bigr)<0,
\]

where \(D\) is graph distance in the open cluster (defined only on the connection event). Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3 give fixed-endpoint large-deviation and almost-sure linear-distance consequences. Constants depend on \(p,d\); this does not assert uniformity as \(p\downarrow p_c\).

## Proof chain read

- Section 2 (PDF pp. 4–7) defines mesoscopic blocks and regularity events \(R_i(N),S_i(N)\). Proposition 2.1 shows that for sufficiently large \(N\) the dependent block process dominates iid site percolation with parameter \(q(N)\to1\). The comparison is a theorem about the particular block event and iid bond model, not a generic consequence of high marginal probability.
- Section 3 (PDF pp. 8–12) first connects two good blocks through a controlled local path. For a macro-path, it repairs segments entering *-connected clusters of bad blocks. The total microscopic path length is bounded by a constant times the macro-path length plus the volume contribution of encountered bad clusters.
- Exponential cluster-mass moments for the bad-block field follow from the high-parameter domination/sharpness input. Chernoff/Cramér estimates then show that a path whose chemical length exceeds \(\rho|y|\) has exponentially small probability.

## Transfer checklist

For C108/C142/C143/C144, this paper is a geometric source, not a direct theorem. One must build the corresponding good-block event on the actual random cluster, prove a suitable domination or mixing bound, control bad-block cluster tails, and verify that the path repair remains inside the model. A finite box simulation of block quality proves none of these uniform claims.
