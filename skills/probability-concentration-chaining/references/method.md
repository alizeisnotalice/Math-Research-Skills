## Matrix inequalities from P001

For a sum of independent random self-adjoint matrices, Tropp's introductory results give these templates (PDF pp. 3–5):

- PSD bounded summands: Chernoff lower/upper tails, with factor `d`; Theorem 1.1.
- Independent Gaussian/Rademacher matrix series: `d exp(-t^2/(2 sigma^2))`, `sigma^2=||sum A_k^2||`; Theorem 1.2.
- Independent centered summands with `X_k^2<=A_k^2`: Hoeffding exponent `-t^2/(8 sigma^2)`, factor `d`; Theorem 1.3.
- Independent centered summands with `lambda_max(X_k)<=R`: Bernstein exponent `-t^2/[2(sigma^2+Rt/3)]`, `sigma^2=||sum E X_k^2||`, factor `d`; Theorem 1.4.
- Rectangular forms use self-adjoint dilation, replacing `d` by `d_1+d_2` and using the maximum of left/right variance proxies; Theorems 1.5–1.6.

The proof chain is Laplace-transform reduction (Proposition 3.1), Lieb concavity (Theorem 3.2), Jensen/conditional expectation (Corollary 3.3 and Lemma 3.4), then mgf-specific bounds and optimization. Section 7 gives selected matrix martingale/bounded-difference results, not a general matrix Freedman or self-normalized theorem.

## Key proof mechanisms and distinctions

- **Generic chaining (P002):** choose admissible nets/partitions; project indices to each scale; telescope increments; union-bound the scale-wise tails; integrate for moments or optimize for deviation. Mixed tails require separate chains for `d_2` and `d_1` and yield `gamma_2+gamma_1` plus square-root/linear diameter terms.
- **Gaussian generic chaining (P015):** Under the paper's centering convention and essential-supremum definition, Theorem 2.5 bounds `E sup X_t` from above by `Kγ₂(T,d)` under the displayed increment-tail condition. For the two-sided Theorem 5.1, take `d(s,t)=sqrt(E(X_s−X_t)²)` from Eq. (1.2), physical p. 3. The source's §5 page 29 refers to distance (1.1), but (1.1) is the general tail-control condition; the lower bound is unsafe for an arbitrary enlarged metric. The lower-bound argument constructs partitions and uses a separated-point Sudakov estimate plus Gaussian concentration; the article cites, rather than proves, those two auxiliary results.
- **C168 finite holdout validation:** Write the target integral using the product standard-Gaussian density. Conditional on training data, a finite candidate family is fixed and each validation score `Z_i=f(G_i)g(H_i)sin⟨G_i,H_i⟩` is independent across `i` and lies in `[-1,1]`. Hoeffding's lemma gives `E exp(λ(Z_i−EZ_i))≤exp(λ²/2)`; Chernoff optimization yields a one-sided error `exp(−Nε²/2)`, doubling gives two-sided error, and a union bound over `M` candidates gives `ε=sqrt(2log(2M/δ)/N)`. Multiply by `(2π)^n` to return to the unnormalized integral. This proof certifies a finite list only; no all-partition or approximation guarantee follows.
- **Entropy method (P003):** tensorize coordinate entropy, control centered conditional replacement fluctuations in Orlicz/moment norms, then optimize the exponential transform. Independence is structural.
- **Dependent-coordinate martingale (P010):** reveal coordinates in order, express the function as conditional-expectation martingale differences, and bound each difference through a Wasserstein matrix for the corresponding kernel. The bounded-metric route then applies Azuma–Hoeffding; the transport-information route tensors conditional log-MGF bounds. The matrix is a proved model input, not a generic dependence coefficient.
- **Convex Gaussian lower deviations (P011):** Ehrhard rearrangement reduces the convex function to a one-dimensional convex quantile profile; Talagrand/Bobkov–Houdré estimates give lower tails and moments, which are combined with Gaussian upper moments by Paley–Zygmund. This proves a lower probability of deviations under a variance condition; it does not sharpen the Gaussian upper tail.
- **Ville/Chernoff (P004):** exponential-supermartingale domination plus Ville's maximal inequality controls a time-uniform crossing. Convex duality chooses the boundary slope; it does not build the supermartingale for a new model.
- **Empirical Gaussian coupling (P018):** reduce to a finite net; smooth the maximum by log-sum-exp; smooth indicators; use Stein's exchangeable-pairs comparison; control net approximation with maximal inequalities and Gaussian concentration. Coupling suprema is weaker than coupling the whole empirical process in sup norm.
- **Anti-concentration (P012):** a finite Gaussian maximum bound passes to separable Gaussian suprema by finite approximation. It controls interval probabilities; it is separate from upper-tail concentration and from the coupling theorem itself.
- **Copula anti-concentration (P019):** the interval probability of a maximum is a diagonal increment `Δ(F(x+ε))−Δ(F(x))`. Copula diagonal constraints give sharp extrema under arbitrary dependence; convexity of `Δ` gives the sharper upper bound. This requires identical marginal CDFs.
- **Variance-sensitive Gaussian anti-concentration (P020/P021):** distinguish the random-variable concentration function, equal versus unequal coordinate variances, and process/order-statistic assumptions. P020 states a bound using `Var(sup |X|)`, but its upper-bound proof chain is not validated: Lemma 7's sum closure for negative `s` fails (see P020 card and `examples/negative.md`). The lower bound in Remark 1 is independent of that gap. P021's unequal-variance result has an additional logarithmic term.
- **Log-concave anti-concentration (P024):** continuous density estimates and lattice moment/variance estimates use different reference measures; preserve that distinction and the exact log-concavity and moment assumptions. In Theorem 3.8, the convex-order comparator is centered uniform on `[-1/(2M),1/(2M)]`, `M=||f_X||∞`.
- **Gaussian-polynomial small balls (P023):** the degree-two estimate is obtained by a Gaussian Laplace-transform/determinant argument and a one-dimensional shifted-normal bound; it does not cover the zero polynomial with positive probability one at its zero threshold.
- **Hilbert-ball anti-concentration (P022):** the controlled variable is a squared Hilbert norm. The density bound comes from the first two covariance eigenvalues/Frobenius spectral tails; rank-one degeneracy can make an `ε`-band probability order `√ε`.
- **Martingale moderate deviations (P025/P027/P028):** P025 assumes a deterministic near-one terminal predictable bracket; P027 replaces that with a sub-Gaussian bracket-deviation bound and keeps the predictable bracket in the denominator; P028 normalizes by the realized square bracket and instead assumes conditional `(2+ρ)` moments. These are distinct interfaces. P028's main proof is external; P027's tilted smoothing details also cite an external supplement.
- **Branching-process moderate deviations (P026):** split the log population increment into an iid environmental sum plus a normalized martingale-ratio term. Cramér estimates control the iid part; harmonic moments and bounds on `log W_n` control the ratio. The resulting MDP/BE statements are annealed and uniform over starting generations under BPRE-specific assumptions.
- **Pickands double-sum (P029):** first obtain a local block probability at scale `u^(−2/α)`, then cover `[0,p]` by such blocks. A union bound gives the upper asymptotic; Bonferroni subtracts pairwise overlaps, split into neighboring, intermediate and far-separated blocks. Local tangent-field limits, a joint-block estimate and Borell's inequality yield the limit constant `H_α`.
- **Manifold Gaussian excursions (P030):** condition at a maximum-variance point and scale by anisotropic `q_t(u)` to obtain a local Pickands field with drift `−h−h_{1t}`. A finite smooth atlas and double-sum partition then separate homogeneous-like, transition and Talagrand regions. The short article outlines rather than reproduces some technical double-sum estimates, referring to earlier work.
- **Scalar Hoeffding refinement (P005):** the proof starts from the chord inequality for `e^{sx}` and an auxiliary two-point MGF; the endpoint of the interval for `τ(1−τ)` changes at left skew. Equation (23), `exp(−t²/(2M_n²))`, is the valid displayed sum tail. With `\bar M_n²=M_n²/n`, its correct equivalent is `exp(−t²/(2n\bar M_n²))`; the later displays with `−n(t/\bar M_n)²/2` are inconsistent for the printed `S_n` events (two independent signs at `n=2,t=2` give `1/4>e^(−4)`). The mean-tail version instead applies the sum bound at threshold `nt`.
- **Markov empirical concentration (P006):** geometric ergodicity yields η-mixing decay for the hidden observations by data processing; the histogram bound then adds an expected `L∞` bias, while the uniform occupation bound uses a rare/frequent state split. Theorem 3's rare-state alternative is total rare mass, with coefficient one.
- **Exchangeable weighted sums (P007):** permutation-average the martingale quadratic form to obtain the finite-population correction; nonnegative weights permit a sharper averaged quadratic-form comparison. Bernstein uses conditional exchangeability and the random empirical variance `N⁻¹Σ_i(X_i−\bar X)²`; the tail event's threshold is random as printed.
- **Without-replacement concentration (P008):** modified log-Sobolev concentration on the interchange process combines with convex-order comparison to replacement sampling. The theorem statement and its printed proof-line mismatch are separately recorded in the card.
- **Talagrand inequalities (P009):** entropy tensorization and Herbst arguments are shared proof tools, but the assumptions remain distinct for product convex distance, biased cube gradients, Gaussian transport, and iid empirical suprema.
- **Large deviations (P016):** the LDP closed/open set bounds and the Laplace variational principle are asymptotic statements. Cramér, Sanov, Markov, small-noise, and hydrodynamic rates each need their own assumptions and proof source.

## Essential distinctions

- Fixed-time versus time-uniform probability.
- Independent versus exchangeable/Markov/martingale dependence.
- Maximum eigenvalue upper tail versus two-sided operator norm.
- Process-supremum upper-tail bound versus Gaussian anti-concentration or small-ball bound.
- Gaussian coupling versus validity of a multiplier bootstrap (the latter needs conditional approximation conditions).
- Non-asymptotic tail inequality versus an asymptotic large-deviation principle.
- Nonasymptotic concentration/anti-concentration versus a high-threshold extreme-value asymptotic: P029/P030 require Gaussian local-covariance and parameter-set geometry and take `u→∞`; their asymptotic equivalents do not provide an error bound at a finite threshold.
