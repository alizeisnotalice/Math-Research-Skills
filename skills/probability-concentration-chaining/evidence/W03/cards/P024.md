## Continuous log-concave density bounds

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 1; proved as Theorem 3.4, p. 6): if `X` has mean `μ` and a log-concave Lebesgue density `f`, then for every `t∈R`,

`2 Var(X) ≤ 1/f(t)^2 + (μ−t)^2`.

At a mode `m`, this gives `2 Var(X)≤||f||∞^(−2)+(μ−m)^2`; at `t=μ`, `Var(X) f(μ)^2≤1/2`. If `t` lies outside the density support, the reciprocal-density term is interpreted as `+∞`. Equality is attained by the asymmetric Laplace comparison family constructed in Proposition 3.3; the proof matches density at `t`, establishes convex order, and compares variances.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2) compares centered Orlicz norms at fixed maximum density. For log-concave `X`, a uniform `U` on an interval and an exponential `Z` are selected so `M(U)=M(X)=M(Z)`. Then for any Young function `ψ`,

`||U−EU||ψ ≤ ||X−EX||ψ ≤ ||Z−EZ||ψ`.

The more detailed Theorem 3.8 (PDF p. 8) gives `U−EU ≺cx X−EX` for any real `X` with finite `M(X)=||f_X||∞`, where `U` is specifically uniform on the centered interval `[-1/(2M),1/(2M)]` with density `f_U(x)=M 1_{[-1/(2M),1/(2M)]}(x)` (so `EU=0`). It also gives `|X−EX| ≺icx |Z−EZ|` when `X` is log-concave, where `Z` has the stated exponential density of rate `M`. Do not replace this exact centered comparator by an arbitrary uniform interval variable. Corollary 3.9 (PDF pp. 8–9), for `p≥1`, records

`1/[2^p(p+1)] ≤ M(X)^p E|X−EX|^p ≤ Γ(1+p)/e + ∫_0^1 (1−x)^p e^(−x) dx`.

The lower bound is universal for real `X` with finite density maximum; equality occurs for a uniform law. The upper bound assumes log-concavity and is attained by an exponential law. For `p=2` this is `1/12≤M(X)^2 Var(X)≤1`.

## Discrete log-concave laws

For integer-valued log-concave `Y`, write `μ=E Y`. Theorem 1.3 (PDF p. 2; restated as Theorem 4.4, p. 10) gives, for every `n∈Z`,

`2 Var(Y) ≤ (P{Y=n}^{−2}−1)+(μ−n)^2`,

with equality iff `Y` is a discrete asymmetric-Laplace law with mode `n`. At a mode `m`, this becomes `2 Var(Y)≤Δ∞(Y)+(μ−m)^2`, where `Δ∞(Y)=M(Y)^(−2)−1`. If `μ` itself is a mode, the source notes the sharper specialization `Var(Y)≤Δ∞(Y)/2`.

Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 2; restated as Theorems 4.5–4.6, p. 11) states, for `M(X)=max_n P{X=n}`,

`M(X)^2 Var(X)+M(X)≤1`,

`M(X)^4 E|X−EX|^4 + M(X)(M(X)^2−10M(X)+18)≤9`.

In both inequalities equality holds exactly for geometric laws, up to translation and reflection. The proof reduces fixed-maximum extremizers to discrete asymmetric-Laplace laws; the variance maximizer is geometric. The fourth-moment case differentiates an explicit one-parameter formula. The authors state why the continuous fixed-maximum equimeasurability method does not transfer verbatim to the lattice.

## Proof mechanism and transfer limits

Section 2 (PDF pp. 3–4) defines `≺cx`, `≺icx`, and `≺n` orders and proves one-/two-crossing criteria. Proposition 3.3 (PDF pp. 5–6) constructs a continuous asymmetric-Laplace comparison matching the density at a chosen point and then matching the mean; density crossing plus the majorization lemma yields convex order. The discrete analogue is Lemma 4.3 (PDF pp. 9–10). Section 5's Theorem 5.3 (PDF pp. 13–14) integrates an `n`-crossing sign condition against a test function with convex `(n−2)`nd derivative, assuming matching moments through degree `n−1` and the stated integrability.

These are one-dimensional log-concavity results. The continuous Orlicz and moment comparisons require a common density maximum and the indicated log-concavity/order assumptions. The lattice conclusions are separate discrete inequalities and are not obtained by treating counting measure as Lebesgue measure. None is a Gaussian-process supremum or generic-chaining theorem; applying the scalar inequalities to a statistic requires proving that the statistic itself has the required log-concave density or mass function.
