## Theorems 4–6 (PDF p. 5)

All data lie in `[-1,1]`, and `w∈R^n` is fixed. Let `\bar X=N^(−1)Σ_{i=1}^N X_i` for a finite exchangeable vector of length `N`. The source's notation (PDF p. 2) defines for a deterministic vector `x∈R^N` its empirical variance `σ_x²=N^(−1)Σ_{i=1}^N(x_i−\bar x)²`; at `x=X` this is the **random** finite-vector empirical variance `σ_X²=N^(−1)Σ_{i=1}^N(X_i−\bar X)²`, not the deterministic marginal variance `Var(X_1)`. For `N≥n≥2`, define `ε_N=(H_N−1)/(N−H_N)`, where `H_N=Σ_{j=1}^N1/j` and hence `ε_N=O(log N/N)`.

- **Theorem 4 (all real weights):** if `X_1,…,X_N` are exchangeable, then for every `λ∈R`,
  `E exp{λΣ_{i≤n}w_i(X_i−\bar X)}≤exp{λ²||w||₂²(1+ε_N)/2}`.
  Thus `P{Σw_i(X_i−\bar X)≥||w||₂√(2(1+ε_N)log(1/δ))}≤δ`.
- **Theorem 5 (nonnegative weights):** if only `X_1,…,X_n` are exchangeable and each `w_i≥0`, the same display holds without the inflation factor `1+ε_N`; the centering is their mean `\bar X=n^(−1)Σ_{i≤n}X_i`.
- **Theorem 6 (Bernstein):** for `N≥n≥2`, put the random proxy `\tildeσ²_{X,N}=σ_X²+4ε_N`. For `|λ|<3/[2||w||_∞(1+ε_N)]`,
  `E exp{λΣw_i(X_i−\bar X)− λ²(1+ε_N)\tildeσ²_{X,N}||w||₂²/[2(1−2|λ|||w||_∞(1+ε_N)/3)]}≤1`.
  The stated tail consequence is
  `P{Σw_i(X_i−\bar X)≥\tildeσ_{X,N}||w||₂√(2(1+ε_N)log(1/δ)) +(2/3)||w||_∞(1+ε_N)log(1/δ)}≤δ`.

Theorem 6's exponential inequality and its stated tail consequence both retain this random empirical variance. In particular the displayed threshold in the tail event is random. Do not replace it with `Var(X_1)` or silently treat it as deterministic. A deterministic, weaker specialization can be obtained separately from the range bound `σ_X²≤1` for coordinates in `[-1,1]`, but that is not the printed theorem.

## Proof mechanism and coverage

Section 1 (PDF pp. 1–2) motivates finite exchangeability, weighted centering and the fixed-time scope. Section 2 (pp. 3–4) reviews iid Hoeffding/Bernstein bounds, sampling without replacement and counterexamples to arbitrary-weight exchangeable bounds. Section 3.1–3.2 (p. 5) states Theorems 4–6; §3.3 (p. 6) recovers iid bounds as `N→∞`. Section 4 (pp. 6–10) compares related finite-population, streaming and weighted results. Appendix A (pp. 10–12) proves Theorem 4: pad weights to reduce to `N=n`, apply a martingale MGF bound to the exchangeable sequence, average the pseudoinverse quadratic form over permutations, and use the identity for the permutation average of `A^T A`. Appendix B (pp. 12–13) proves Theorem 5 by ordering nonnegative weights and bounding the quadratic form by `||w||₂²`. Appendix C (pp. 13–15) establishes the conditional-exchangeability Bernstein MGF and random variance proxy for Theorem 6. References are on pp. 15–16.

The finite-population correction is explicit and vanishes only as the population length grows. These are fixed-`n` inequalities centered at a finite-population mean; they do not imply time-uniform coverage. The bounds need the exchangeability and boundedness assumptions and should not be transferred to arbitrary dependent sequences or data-dependent weights without another argument.
