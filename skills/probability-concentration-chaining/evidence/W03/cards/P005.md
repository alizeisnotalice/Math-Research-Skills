## Statements and proof

For a mean-zero real random variable `X∈[a,b]`, `a<0<b`, put `A=(|a|+b)/2` and `G=√(|a|b)`. The paper's improved MGF statement (equation (5), PDF p. 1) is `E exp(sX)≤exp(s²A²/2)` when `b≥|a|` and `≤exp(s²G²/2)` when `b≤|a|`; at equality `A=G`. The proof is for `s>0`. Only a left-skewed interval (`|a|>b`) receives the geometric-mean improvement.

The proof bounds the convex exponential by its chord on `[a,b]`, sets `λ=−a/(b−a)` and `u=s(b−a)`, and obtains `E e^{sX}≤e^{ψ(u)}`, with `ψ(u)=−λu+log(1−λ+λe^u)`. Taylor's theorem expresses `ψ(u)=½τ(1−τ)u²` for an intermediate `τ∈[λ,1]`. If `λ≤1/2`, the maximum is `1/4` at `τ=1/2`; if `λ>1/2`, it is attained at `τ=λ`, giving `ψ(u)≤s²G²/2` (PDF pp. 1–2, (6)–(17)).

For independent, not necessarily identically distributed, mean-zero `X_i∈[a_i,b_i]` with `a_i<0<b_i`, define `A_i=(|a_i|+b_i)/2`, `G_i=√(|a_i|b_i)`, `I={i:b_i≥|a_i|}`, and `J={i:b_i<|a_i|}`. Set `M_n²=Σ_{i∈I}A_i²+Σ_{j∈J}G_j²`. Chernoff optimization gives (23), `P(S_n≥t)≤exp(−t²/(2M_n²))`. Since (24) defines `\bar M_n²=M_n²/n`, its correct equivalent sum-scale expression is `exp(−t²/(2n\bar M_n²))` (PDF p. 2).

**Printed normalization issue (PDF p. 2, equations (28)–(33), (36)).** Equation (28) instead prints `P(S_n≥t)≤exp(−n(t/\bar M_n)²/2)` and repeats the same `n`-multiplied exponent for the original proxy. Equations (29)–(30) inherit that scale, and the common-interval displays (31)–(32), two-sided display (33), and specialization (36) use it for events written in terms of `S_n` rather than `S_n/n`. This does not follow from (23) and (24): the displayed exponent is stronger by a factor `n²` for the stated sum event. A direct check with two independent symmetric signs, `n=2`, `t=2`, `M_n²=2`, gives `P(S_n≥2)=1/4`, while the printed (28) RHS is `e^(−4)<1/4`; (23) gives the valid RHS `e^(−1)`. Do not cite the later printed displays as sum-tail bounds. **Derived correction, not a silent transcription change:** from (23), the common-interval sum bound is `exp(−t²/(2nA²))` when `−a≤b` and `exp(−t²/(2nG²))` when `−a>b`; apply the same derivation to `−S_n` for the two-sided analogue. The printed `−nt²/(2A²)`/`−nt²/(2G²)` exponents are the appropriate scale for a sample-mean event `S_n/n≥t`, not the sum event actually displayed.

For the opposite tail, swap the roles through `−X_i`: `N_n²=Σ_{i∈J}A_i²+Σ_{j∈I}G_j²` (equation (35)) and

`P(|S_n|≥t)≤exp(−t²/(2M_n²))+exp(−t²/(2N_n²))`.

For a common interval `[a,b]`, the valid sum-scale consequence of (23) is `exp(−t²/(2nG²))+exp(−t²/(2nA²))`; equation (36) prints the sample-mean exponent while displaying a sum event. These are scalar independent-sum bounds; the lemma itself is one-sided in the MGF parameter and supplies no dependent-data result.
