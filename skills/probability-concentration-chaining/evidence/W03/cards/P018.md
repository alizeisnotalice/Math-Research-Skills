## Abstract Gaussian coupling theorem

Let `X_1,...,X_n` be iid with law `P`; let `F=F_n` be a pointwise-measurable, `P`-centered class with measurable envelope `F`. The assumptions are: (A1) pointwise measurability; (A2) `F in L^q(P)` for `q>=3`; (A3) `F` is `P`-pre-Gaussian, with a tight centered Gaussian process `G_P` in `ell_infinity(F)` of covariance `P(fg)`. The class/envelope may change with `n`; pre-Gaussianity gives total boundedness in `e_P(f,g)=||f-g||_(P,2)` and a uniformly continuous version.

Set `G_n f=n^-1/2 sum_i(f(X_i)-P f)`; for `epsilon in (0,1]`, define

`F_epsilon={f-g:f,g in F, e_P(f,g)<epsilon ||F||_(P,2)}`,

`phi_n(epsilon)=E||G_n||_(F_epsilon) vee E||G_P||_(F_epsilon)`,

`H_n(epsilon)=log(N(F,e_P,epsilon||F||_(P,2)) vee n)`, and `M=max_i F(X_i)`. Let `kappa>0` satisfy `kappa^3>=E||E_n[|f(X_i)|^3]||_F`.

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 7).** For every `epsilon in (0,1]` and `gamma in (0,1)`, there is a random variable `Ztilde` distributed as `sup_(f in F) G_P f` such that

`P{|Z-Ztilde|>K(q) Delta_n(epsilon,gamma)} <= gamma[1+delta_n(epsilon,gamma)] + C log(n)/n`,

where `Z=sup_(f in F) G_n f`, `K(q)` depends only on `q`, and

`Delta_n = phi_n(epsilon) + gamma^(-1/q) epsilon ||F||_(P,2) + n^(-1/2) gamma^(-1/q)||M||_q + n^(-1/2) gamma^(-2/q)||M||_2`

`+ n^(-1/4) gamma^(-1/2)(E||G_n||_(F dot F))^(1/2) H_n(epsilon)^(1/2) + n^(-1/6) gamma^(-1/3) kappa H_n(epsilon)^(2/3)`,

`delta_n = (1/4) P{(F/kappa)^3 1(F/kappa > c gamma^(-1/3)n^(1/3)H_n(epsilon)^(-1/3))}`.

Here `F dot F={fg:f,g in F}` and `c,C` are universal. The formula and exponents were verified against the rendered theorem page; the probability statement is a coupling of the *suprema*, not a sup-norm coupling of the entire empirical and Gaussian processes.

**Corollary 2.2 (PDF pp. 10–11).** For a pointwise-measurable VC-type class with envelope `F`, constants `A>=e,v>=1`, `||F||_(P,q)<=b`, `q in [4,infinity]`, and `sup_f P|f|^k <= sigma^2 b^(k-2)` for `k=2,3,4`, let `K_n=c v(log n vee log(Ab/sigma))`. Then a Gaussian supremum coupling exists with error threshold

`b K_n/[gamma^(1/2)n^(1/2-1/q)] + (b sigma)^(1/2)K_n^(3/4)/[gamma^(1/2)n^(1/4)] + (b sigma^2 K_n^2)^(1/3)/[gamma^(1/3)n^(1/6)]`

and failure probability at most `C(gamma+log(n)/n)`. For `q=infinity`, interpret `1/q=0`.

## Main proof mechanism and applications

Theorem 2.1’s proof (PDF pp. 31–33) selects a minimal `epsilon||F||_(P,2)` net, applies a finite-dimensional coupling to the net maxima, and controls the difference between the net and full supremum using empirical-process maximal inequalities and Borell–Sudakov–Tsirelson. Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 21–28) supplies the finite-vector coupling: smooth the maximum by the log-sum-exp/free-energy function, smooth the indicator of a Borel event, and use Stein’s exchangeable-pairs identity. The covariance fluctuation, third moments, and large-coordinate truncation produce the respective remainder terms. The net approximation is where `phi_n`, the envelope moments, and entropy `H_n` enter.

- Propositions 3.1–3.2 (PDF pp. 14–15): for local kernel empirical-process suprema, the Gaussian coupling error is `O_P((n h_n^d)^(-1/6)log n +(n h_n^d)^(-1/4)log^(5/4)n +(n h_n^d)^(-1/2)log^(3/2)n)` for bounded classes. In the unbounded-envelope case with the stated `q>=4` moment and conditional fourth-moment assumptions, the last term becomes `(n^(1-2/q)h_n^d)^(-1/2)log^(3/2)n`.
- Proposition 3.3 (PDF p. 19): for series empirical-process suprema under conditions C1–C4 and either bounded envelope or the stated `q>=4` envelope/conditional-moment assumptions, the error is `O_P(n^(-1/6)xi_n^(1/3)log n+n^(-1/4)xi_n^(1/2)log^(5/4)n+n^(-1/2)xi_n log^(3/2)n)`; in the unbounded case the final term is `n^(-1/2+1/q)xi_n log^(3/2)n`. Here `xi_n=sup_x |psi^(K_n)(x)| vee 1`; the normalization and Lipschitz conditions on `alpha_n(x,g)` are part of C2–C4.
- A coupling error `o_P(log^(-1/2)n)` can imply convergence of the Kolmogorov distance when the Gaussian supremum expectation is `O(sqrt(log n))` and the coordinate variances are uniformly bounded above and away from zero; this conversion uses the Gaussian anti-concentration lemma in Remark 2.5, not coupling alone.

## Transfer boundary

Check centering, measurability, envelope moments, pre-Gaussianity, the local complexity term, and the exact `epsilon,gamma` choices. A non-Donsker, growing class is allowed, but its entropy and envelope growth must still satisfy the theorem’s bound. The theorem couples only supremum statistics and does not by itself prove bootstrap validity; a bootstrap requires an additional conditional coupling/comparison theorem. Coupling of suprema also does not give anti-concentration unless the Gaussian target satisfies the separate variance and anti-concentration hypotheses.
