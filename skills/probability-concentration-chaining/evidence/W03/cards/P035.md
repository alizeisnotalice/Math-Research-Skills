## Statistic and theorem scope

For independent samples of sizes `m,n` from a common continuous distribution, with empirical CDFs `F_m,G_n`, the paper studies

`KS_{m,n}=√(mn/(m+n)) sup_x |F_m(x)−G_n(x)|`,

and the inequality `P(KS_{m,n}≥M)≤C exp(−2M²)` for all `M>0`.

**Theorem 1, equal sample sizes `m=n` (physical PDF p. 2).** The source states: (a) the DKW bound holds with `C=e`; (b) for the equal-sample case at the 5% rejection criterion its stated DKW constant is `2.16863` (the printed sentence’s grammar around “the smallest n” is ambiguous, so this card does not infer a broader quantifier than the text supports); (c) the DKWM constant `C=2` holds for every `m=n≥458`; (d) it fails for every equal sample size `n<458` at a lattice value specified by Eq. (3); and (e) for each `n<458` the DKW bound holds with `C=2(1+δ_n)` for some `δ_n>0`, with an explicit upper bound when `12≤n≤457`.

The paper explicitly separates proof from computation: Theorem 1(a)–(c) are proved in §2, with the proof for `n<6395` completed by a finite search. Parts (d) and (e), and parts (a)–(c) for `n<6395`, are obtained by computation. The equal-size probability is expressed by an exact combinatorial/reflection formula, and the proof reduces the tail inequality to bounds for integer lattice values `k`; analytic estimates handle broad ranges and the remaining finite ranges are searched.

For `m≠n`, the article says it has **no general or theoretical proof** and reports computed values only. Fact 2 covers `1≤m<n≤200`; another computed regime covers `n=2m≤600`. These tables are numerical evidence for those finite ranges, not a theorem for arbitrary unequal sample sizes. Three failing pairs cited in the text are `(1,2)`, `(1,3)`, and `(2,3)`.

Retain the common continuous sampling distribution, sample independence, statistic normalization and threshold quantifiers.
