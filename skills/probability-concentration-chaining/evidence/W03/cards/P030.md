## Model and structural assumptions

The paper studies `P(S;u)=P(max_{t∈S}X(t)>u)` for a zero-mean a.s.-continuous Gaussian field on a bounded `d`-dimensional smooth manifold `S⊂R^n`, as `u→∞`. Its maximum-standard-deviation set is `M={t:σ(t)=max_S σ}`, a finitely connected smooth `r`-dimensional submanifold; the paper also discusses the locally homogeneous boundary case `r=d`. It normalizes `σ=1` on `M`.

The principal regularity inputs are: (i) finite Dudley entropy integral for the standardized field `X(t)/σ(t)` near `M` (Condition 2); (ii) local homogeneity of the correlation, represented by `r_t`, with `r_t(s)<1` away from zero (Condition 3); (iii) anisotropic scales `q_i(u)>0`, bounded positive coordinate factors `C_{it}`, and a uniform local limit `u²[1−r_t(C_tq(u)s)]→h(s)` (Condition 4); and (iv) existence of the variance-loss limit `h_{1t}(s)=lim_{u→∞}u²[1−σ(t+C_tq(u)s)]∈[0,∞]` (Condition 5). The cases `h_{1t}=0`, `0<h_{1t}<∞`, and `h_{1t}=∞` are termed homogeneous-like, transition, and Talagrand, respectively. For the general-case component formula, Condition 6 further requires smooth, finitely connected strata and finite volume of the positive-dimensional `K_0` components.

## Local Pickands lemma and result families

Define a tangent field `χ` with `Eχ(s)=−h(s)` and `Var(χ(s₁)−χ(s₂))=2h(s₁−s₂)`. Lemma 2 (PDF pp. 7–8), for bounded closed `T⊂R^d` and an interior maximum-variance point `t`, gives the local block limit

`P(max_{s∈t+q_t(u)T}X(s)>u)=(1+o(1))P_{q,t}(T)Ψ(u)`,

where `q_t(u)=C_tq(u)`, the paper defines `Ψ(u):=(2π)^(-1/2)u^(−1)e^(−u²/2)` (the asymptotic equivalent of `1−Φ(u)`), and `P_{q,t}(T)=E exp(max_{s∈T∩U}{χ(s)−h_{1t}(s)})`. The proof conditions on `X(t)=u−w/u`; equations (20)–(22) identify the conditional mean and increment variance limits, after which weak and majorized convergence is invoked. At chart boundaries the set is truncated unless `h_{1t}≡0`.

- **Locally homogeneous case, §3.1 (PDF pp. 9–11):** Theorem 1 prints

  `P(max_{t∈U_1}X_0(t)>u)=H_q [∫_{U_1}∏_{i=1}^r C_{ti}^{−1}(u)dt][∏_{i=1}^r q_i(u)^(−1)]Ψ(u)(1+o(1))`,

  with `H_q=lim_{T→∞}T^(−d)H_q([0,T]^d)∈(0,∞)`; it also allows expanding `U_1(u)` when its inner/outer boxes satisfy the stated `U_{1i}^{−}(u)q_i(u)→∞` condition. Here `X_0` is unit variance and locally homogeneous, so its maximum-variance set is full-dimensional (`r=d` in the paper's notation); do not carry this scale product to a nonconstant-variance stratum with `r<d`. Proposition 2 gives the corresponding formula over a maximum-variance submanifold `M_1`, with `H_{q,r}=lim_{T→∞}T^(−r)H_q([0,T]^r×[0,0]^(d−r))∈(0,∞)` and scale products over the `r` tangent coordinates.
- **Homogeneous-like case, §3.2 (PDF pp. 11–12):** If `h_{1t}(s)=0` throughout the neighborhood, Proposition 3 gives

  `P(U;u)=(1+o(1))H_q [∫_{M∩U}∏_{i=1}^d C_{ti}^{−1}(u)dt] L_f(u²)[∏_{i=1}^d q_i(u)^(−1)]Ψ(u)`,

  where `L_f(λ)=∫_Ue^(−λf(s))ds`, `f(s)=½(1−σ²(s))`.
- **Talagrand case, §3.3 (PDF p. 12):** If the `h_{1t}=∞` region covers the neighborhood, Proposition 4 has the same leading scale as Proposition 2, with the maximum-variance manifold integral and `H_{q,r}`.
- **Transition case, §3.4 (PDF pp. 12–13):** If `0<h_{1t}(s)<∞` over the neighborhood, Proposition 5 gives

  `P(U;u)=(1+o(1))[∫_M P^ν_{q,t}∏_{i=1}^r C_{ti}^{−1}(u)dt][∏_{i=1}^r q_i(u)^(−1)]Ψ(u)`,

  with `P^ν_{q,t}=lim_{T,S→∞}T^(−r)P^ν_{q,t}(Π_t)∈(0,∞)` as stated in (33), (15).
- **General case, §3.5–§4 (PDF pp. 13–15):** The variance-loss regimes are assembled into `K_0`, `K_c`, and `K_∞`. Under Condition 6, Lemma 3 gives the contribution from each lower-dimensional `K_{0i}` stratum; Proposition 6 sums these contributions, while Remark 5 directs the full-dimensional component to Proposition 3. Section 4 combines the three regime probabilities by Bonferroni over a finite atlas, then transfers from the informative neighborhood back to `S`.

## Proof coverage and limitations

Section 2 first reduces the full excursion probability to an informative neighborhood of `M`, using Borell–TIS; under Condition 2 it narrows this further to `M_u` via the cited Dmitrovsky inequality. The local lemma is derived by conditioning and Gaussian convergence. The proof then partitions `M` into anisotropic blocks and treats the locally homogeneous case with Slepian comparison and a double-sum argument. The other cases state proof outlines and refer to Piterbarg's monograph and earlier papers for several technical double-sum and limit details; this short article is not a self-contained proof of every proposition it states. Condition 6 is a genuine structural assumption for the general-case decomposition, not a consequence of the local covariance conditions.

The paper supplies asymptotic high-excursion formulas, not nonasymptotic concentration or anti-concentration bounds. Application requires deriving the local covariance scales, variance-loss regime, manifold geometry and chart/boundary conditions for the target field. The Bessel/chi-square examples in §1.2 are contextual illustrations; their quoted constants rely on cited work. All source statements remain pending independent review.

## Transfer boundary

Use only after matching the Gaussian-field continuity, compactness, local covariance regular variation, anisotropic scaling, variance-profile regime, and manifold geometry hypotheses. A conclusion about a high-excursion asymptotic does not yield a tail bound at finite thresholds or a continuity-modulus estimate for an empirical-process coupling. The source explicitly delegates several proof details; do not imply those cited arguments were reproduced here.
