## Main martingale theorem

Let `(ξ_i,F_i)_{i=0}^n` be martingale differences, `X_k=Σ_{i≤k}ξ_i`, and predictable quadratic characteristic `<X>_k=Σ_{i≤k}E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})` (PDF p. 4). The conditions are:

- (A1) for `ε_n∈(0,1/2]`, every integer `k≥2`, and every `i≤n`,
  `|E(ξ_i^k|F_{i−1})| ≤ (1/2) k! ε_n^(k−2) E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})`;
- (A2) for `δ_n∈(0,1/2]` and a positive constant `C`, for every `y>0`,
  `P(|<X>_n−1|≥y) ≤ C exp(−y² δ_n^(−2))`.

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 4, (2.3)) is a two-sided log-relative-error estimate for the *random-bracket normalized* statistic `X_n/√<X>_n`: for `0≤x=o(min{ε_n^(−1),δ_n^(−1)})`,

`| ln{ P(X_n/√<X>_n > x)/(1−Φ(x)) } | ≤ c [ x³(ε_n+δ_n) + (1+x)(δ_n|ln δ_n|+ε_n|ln ε_n|) ]`,

and the same estimate holds for `−X_n/√<X>_n` in the opposite tail. The square root in the normalization is part of the printed theorem. Corollary 2.1 (PDF pp. 4–5) gives a relative `1+O(·)` approximation for `x=O(min{ε_n^(−1/3),δ_n^(−1/3)})`, and `1+o(1)` for little-oh of this scale. Corollary 2.2 (p. 5) *prints* an MDP with speed `a_n²` and rate `x²/2` when `a_n→∞` and `a_n min{ε_n,δ_n}→0`; this condition is contradicted by an explicit sequence satisfying (A1)–(A2), detailed below, and must not be used as stated. Corollary 2.3 (p. 5) gives

`sup_x |P(X_n/√<X>_n≤x)−Φ(x)| ≤ c(δ_n|ln δ_n|+ε_n|ln ε_n|)`.

Theorem 2.2 (p. 5) transfers the logarithmic tail-ratio bound to the unnormalized `X_n` under the same (A1)–(A2) and range, with a constant allowed to depend on the fixed theorem parameter. Do not confuse (A2), a sub-Gaussian *probability bound* for terminal bracket deviation, with the deterministic terminal bracket condition in P025.

## R02 audit: printed Corollary 2.2 scale is invalid

The original PDF p. 5 was visually checked at Corollary 2.2. Its displayed assumption is literally `a_n min{ε_n,δ_n}→0`; (A1) and (A2) on p. 4 impose no relation forcing the two sequences to be comparable. Let `R_i` be independent symmetric signs, `ξ_i=R_i/√n`, `ε_n=n^(−1/2)`, and `δ_n=e^(−n)`. Then `<X>_n=1` exactly, so the probability in (A2) is zero for every threshold. In (A1), odd conditional moments vanish and for every even `k≥2`, the left side is `n^(−k/2)`, while the right side equals `(k!/2)n^(−k/2)`, so (A1) holds. For `a_n=n`, the printed condition holds because `n e^(−n)→0`. But `|X_n/√<X>_n|/a_n≤n^(−1/2)` deterministically, so for the open set `(1,2)` the probabilities are eventually zero. The Gaussian MDP lower bound would require a liminf at least `−inf_{x∈(1,2)}x²/2=−1/2`, whereas the actual scaled log probability is `−∞`. Thus this counterexample refutes the printed Corollary 2.2 condition/conclusion, not Theorem 2.1's tail estimate.

**Safe condition derived from Theorem 2.1 (not the source's printed corollary):** the main tail range at `x=a_n y`, for fixed `y>0`, requires `a_n max{ε_n,δ_n}→0`. Under this condition, dividing the printed error bound by `a_n²` gives terms of orders `a_n(ε_n+δ_n)`, `(δ_n|lnδ_n|+ε_n|lnε_n|)/a_n²`, and `(δ_n|lnδ_n|+ε_n|lnε_n|)/a_n`; all tend to zero. Subject to the main theorem and the usual two-sided tail-to-LDP argument, this is a sufficient MDP scale. It is our derived consequence, distinct from the source's printed `min` condition. The exact-condition check is in `validation/P027_P028_MDP_min_condition.py` and its generated JSON.

## Proof mechanism and scope

Section 4 (PDF pp. 12–15) introduces the exponential change of measure `P_λ`, the centered tilted martingale `Y_n(λ)`, its drift `B_n(λ)`, and predictable cumulant `Ψ_n(λ)`. Lemmas 4.2–4.3 control drift and cumulant from conditional Bernstein moments; Lemma 4.4 bounds tilted moments and the bracket deviation under `P_λ`; Lemma 4.5 supplies a tilted Berry–Esseen estimate. Theorem 2.1 is proved in §5 (pp. 16–22): choose upper- and lower-tail tilts through equations (5.3)/(5.18), compare the tilted expectation with the standard normal integral, and add the discarded bracket-deviation probability using (A2). The lower bound handles small and large tilts separately. Corollary 2.3 is proved in §6 (pp. 23–24). The paper notes that the detailed smoothing proof of Lemma 4.5 is in its separate supplement [22] (PDF p. 15); the main paper itself cites that supplement for those details.

## Applications read in the paper

- **Elephant random walk with random bounded step sizes** (Theorem 3.1, PDF pp. 8–9): with `p∈(0,3/4]`, `0≤Z_1≤C`, step-size mean normalized to one and variance `σ²`, the Cramér tail error changes at `p=1/2` and `p=3/4`. For `0<p≤1/2` it is `O(x³/√n+(1+x)ln(n)/√n)` for `x=o(√n)`; for `1/2<p<3/4`, the scale is `n^((3−4p)/2)` in both the cubic and logarithmic terms; at `p=3/4` it is `O(x³/√ln n+(1+x)lnln(n)/√ln n)` for `x=o(√ln n)`. Both tails are covered. Theorem 3.2 (pp. 10–11) replaces the deterministic variance factor by the observed step-size sum `Σ_i(Z_i−1)²`; its proof controls the random denominator with Hoeffding’s inequality and transfers both tails.
- **Stable autoregression** (Theorem 3.3, PDF p. 11): for `X_{n+1}=θX_n+ε_{n+1}`, `|θ|<1`, iid centered noise with variance `σ²>0` and `|ε_0|≤H`, the least-squares estimator satisfies the two-sided Cramér approximation for `(θ̂_n−θ)√(Σ_{k=1}^n X_{k−1}²)/σ` with error `O(x³/√n+(1+x)ln(n)/√n)` for `x=o(√n)`; relative equivalence holds for `x=o(n^(1/6))`. Corollaries 3.2–3.3 give confidence intervals under their displayed restrictions on the risk sequence. The proof (§8, pp. 31–34) reduces the numerator to martingale differences and controls the random denominator by Azuma–Hoeffding.

## Transfer boundary

Use this source for scalar martingale Cramér/moderate deviations only after verifying the *conditional signed-moment Bernstein condition at every order* and the *sub-Gaussian terminal predictable-bracket concentration* in (A1)–(A2), as well as the exact normalization and `x` range. It is not a generic self-normalized theorem and does not follow from bounded increments alone without deriving both assumptions. The cited tilted Berry–Esseen smoothing details are in an external supplement, not in this 36-page PDF.
