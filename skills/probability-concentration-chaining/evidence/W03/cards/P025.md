## Martingale setup and conditional Bernstein assumptions

Let `(ξ_i,F_i)_{i=1}^n` be square-integrable martingale differences, with `X_k=Σ_{i≤k}ξ_i` and predictable quadratic characteristic `<X>_k=Σ_{i≤k} E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})` (PDF p. 3). Conditions (A1)–(A2) are:

`|E(ξ_i^k|F_{i−1})| ≤ (1/2) k! ε^(k−2) E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})` for every integer `k≥3`, each `i`, with `ε∈(0,1/2]`;

`|<X>_n−1|≤δ²`, with `δ∈[0,1/2]`.

The normalization is variance one up to `δ²`; `ε` controls all conditional moments, not merely an almost-sure jump bound. The differences may depend on `n`.

## Two-sided Cramér-type tail comparison

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 4, equations (8)–(9)): for any `α∈(0,1)`, all `0≤x≤α ε^(−1)`, and a constant `c_α` depending only on `α`,

`P(X_n>x)/(1−Φ(x)) ≤ exp{c_α(x³ε+x²δ²)} [1+c_α(1+x)(ε|log ε|+δ)]`,

and

`P(X_n<−x)/Φ(−x) ≤ exp{c_α(x³ε+x²δ²)} [1+c_α(1+x)(ε|log ε|+δ)]`.

Theorem 2.2 (same page, equations (10)–(11)): there are absolute `α_0,c_{α_0}>0` such that for `0≤x≤α_0 ε^(−1)` and `δ≤α_0`, both ratios are bounded below by

`exp{−c_{α_0}[x³ε+x²δ²+(1+x)(ε|log ε|+δ)]}`.

The two theorems together give a log-relative-error estimate (equation (14), PDF p. 5) in the same range. Corollary 2.1 gives a relative expansion when `x≤α_0 min{(ε|log ε|)^(−1),δ^(−1)}`; use the theorem hypotheses and ranges as displayed, with `δ^(−1)=∞` when `δ=0`.

For normalized martingale sums `n^(−1/2)Ση_i`, Corollary 2.4 (PDF pp. 6–7) assumes the corresponding conditional Bernstein condition with parameter `H` and `|Σ_i E(η_i²|F_{i−1})−n|≤N²`. For `α√log n≤x=o(n^(1/6))`, the two Gaussian tail ratios equal `1+O((H+N)x³/√n)`; the paper states this remainder is optimal in the stated range. Corollaries 2.2–2.3 also give a broader moderate-deviation range through order `√n` with a logarithmic tail-ratio error and a central `O(√log n)` relative approximation, respectively.

## Proof chain and asymptotic consequence

The proof changes measure with the conjugate multiplicative martingale `Z_n(λ)=∏_i e^{λξ_i}/E(e^{λξ_i}|F_{i−1})` (PDF pp. 7, 11). Under the tilted law `P_λ`, it decomposes `X_n=Y_n(λ)+B_n(λ)` into a centered conjugate martingale and predictable drift. Lemma 3.1 proves

`sup_t |P_λ(Y_n(λ)≤t)−Φ(t)| ≤ c(λε+ε|log ε|+δ)` for `0≤λ<ε^(−1)`.

Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 7) is the `λ=0` consequence: `sup_t|P(X_n≤t)−Φ(t)|≤c(ε|log ε|+δ)`. Sections 4–7 prove the estimates used in the tilt: conditional moment bounds (Lemma 4.1), drift approximation `|B_n(λ)−λ|≤λδ²+c_α λ²ε` (Lemma 4.2), and predictable cumulant approximation `|Ψ_n(λ)−λ²/2|≤c_α λ³ε+λ²δ²/2` (Lemma 4.3). The upper proof chooses λ below x to dominate the tilted event, uses the tilted CLT estimate and Gaussian tail comparison; the lower proof chooses λ above x and separately handles small and large `λ` relative to `min{ε^(−1/2),δ^(−1)}`. The smoothing argument controls the conjugate martingale's distribution by adding Gaussian noise, telescoping conditional expectations, and absorbing the final CDF error.

For triangular arrays with `ε_n→0`, `δ_n→0`, any `a_n→∞` with `a_n ε_n→0` yields an MDP for `X_n` with speed `a_n²` and rate `I(x)=x²/2` (PDF p. 5); this follows from the two-sided normal-tail comparisons and their log-error control. In the iid case, Proposition 8.2 (PDF p. 24) identifies Bernstein, Cramér exponential-moment, and Sakhanenko conditions as equivalent; this equivalence is not asserted for general dependent martingale differences.

## Transfer boundary

This is a near-unit-quadratic-variation, conditional-Bernstein theorem for scalar martingale sums. Verify the filtration, centering, all conditional moments, terminal quadratic variation, and the range of `x` before use. It does not give a generic self-normalized inequality or apply solely from a variance bound. The MDP conclusion is asymptotic and requires the displayed triangular-array scaling; it is not a finite-sample LDP for an arbitrary model.
