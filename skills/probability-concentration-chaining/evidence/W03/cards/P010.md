## Setup and main results

Let `X=(X_i)_{i∈T}` take values in a finite product of Polish spaces with law `μ`; each coordinate has a measurable metric `ρ_i`, and the product metric is `ρ(x,y)=Σ_iρ_i(x_i,y_i)`. The coordinate oscillation is

`δ_i(f)=sup{|f(x)−f(y)|/ρ_i(x_i,y_i): x_{T\{i}}=y_{T\{i}}}`.

For a Markov kernel `K`, a nonnegative matrix `V` is a Wasserstein matrix if `δ(Kf)≤Vδ(f)` componentwise for all admissible Lipschitz `f` (PDF pp. 5–7). The matrix records how conditional averaging changes each coordinate's sensitivity.

The authors use the coordinate-reveal filtration and kernels

`K^(i)(x,dy)=δ_{x_[i−1]}(dy_[i−1])⊗μ_[i,n](dy_[i,n]|x_[i−1])`,

with `K^(1)f=E_μf`, `K^(n+1)f=f`, and martingale differences `M^(i)=K^(i+1)f−K^(i)f` (PDF pp. 12–13).

- **Theorem 4.2 (PDF p. 13):** Suppose every coordinate metric has finite diameter `||ρ_i||=sup_{x_i,z_i}ρ_i(x_i,z_i)<∞`. For each `i=1,…,n+1`, let `V^(i)` be a Wasserstein matrix for `K^(i)`. Define `Γ_ij=||ρ_i|| V^(i+1)_ij`. Then for every `f∈Lip(X,ρ)` and `t≥0`,

  `P_μ(|f(X)−E_μf(X)|≥t)≤2 exp(−2t²/||Γδ(f)||_2²)`.

- **Theorem 4.3 (PDF p. 14):** If for every `i` and every prefix `x_[i−1]`, the conditional coordinate law satisfies `μ_i(·|x_[i−1])∈T_{ρ_i}(c_i)`, and `V^(i)` are Wasserstein matrices for the same kernels, then `μ∈T_ρ(c)` with

  `c=Σ_i c_i(Σ_j V^(i+1)_ij)²`.

  The source defines `μ∈T_ρ(c)` by the transportation-information inequality

  `W(μ,ν) ≤ √(2c D(ν||μ))`

  for every Borel probability measure `ν` on `X` (Eq. (14), physical PDF p. 9). Through Bobkov–Götze this is equivalent to the stated sub-Gaussian concentration property for all 1-Lipschitz functions. The square root is part of the source formula.

Theorem 4.2 follows by bounding the conditional range of each `M^(i)` by `||ρ_i||δ_i(K^(i+1)f)≤(Γδ(f))_i`, summing the squared range bounds, and applying Azuma–Hoeffding. Theorem 4.3 instead bounds the conditional log-MGF of each martingale difference by its one-coordinate transport-information inequality and telescopes the conditional MGF bounds (PDF pp. 13–16). The paper checks that product measures with weighted Hamming metrics recover McDiarmid's inequality, including its sharp exponent constant (PDF pp. 15–16).

## Applications and proof map

Under the Dobrushin uniqueness condition, the interdependence matrix `C^μ` has spectral radius `<1`; with `D^μ=Σ_{k≥0}(C^μ)^k`, the source obtains `Γ_ij=||ρ_i||(D^μ)^T_ij` and inserts it into Theorem 4.2 (Corollary 5.3, PDF pp. 17–20). A second route constructs Wasserstein matrices from couplings of conditional future laws. Goldstein's maximal coupling yields an upper-triangular dependence matrix in terms of conditional total-variation distances (PDF pp. 20–23); for directed Markov chains, these distances can be bounded by products of one-step contraction coefficients. These are alternative ways to supply the matrix input, not assumptions automatically implied by dependence alone.

## Transfer boundary

For Theorem 4.2, establish the bounded coordinate diameters, the exact reveal filtration, and a valid nonnegative Wasserstein matrix for every conditional-expectation kernel; preserve matrix orientation and evaluate `Γδ(f)` rather than substituting a generic dependence coefficient. Theorem 4.3 uses conditional transport-information bounds at every prefix and permits unbounded metrics only within the stated hypotheses. The result is a framework: it does not produce useful constants until the matrices or conditional transport bounds are proved for the model. All claims and section coverage remain pending independent review.
