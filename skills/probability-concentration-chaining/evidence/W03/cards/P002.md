## Setup and exact statements

For a finite semimetric space `(T,d)`, write `Delta_d(T)=sup_{s,t} d(s,t)`. A process is `psi_alpha` with respect to `d` if for every `s,t` and every `u>=0`,

`P(||X_t-X_s|| >= u d(t,s)) <= 2 exp(-u^alpha)`.

An admissible sequence has `|T_0|=1` and `|T_n|<=2^(2^n)`; `gamma_alpha(T,d)=inf_(T_n) sup_t sum_(n>=0) 2^(n/alpha)d(t,T_n)`. For `1<=p<infinity`, the truncated functional is `gamma_(alpha,p)(T,d)=inf_(T_n) sup_t sum_(n>=floor(log_2 p)) 2^(n/alpha)d(t,T_n)` (Section 2, PDF pp. 3–4).

**Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 4; proof pp. 5–6).** If `(X_t)_(t in T)` is `psi_alpha` with respect to `d`, then for any `t_0 in T` and `1<=p<infinity`,

`(E sup_(t in T) ||X_t-X_(t0)||^p)^(1/p) <= C_alpha gamma_(alpha,p)(T,d) + 2 sup_t (E||X_t-X_(t0)||^p)^(1/p)`.

For every `u>=1`,

`P{sup_t ||X_t-X_(t0)|| >= e^(1/alpha)[C_alpha gamma_alpha(T,d)+u D_alpha Delta_d(T)]} <= exp(-u^alpha/alpha)`.

`C_alpha,D_alpha` depend only on `alpha`; the paper reports `C_2<=86, D_2<=9` in Remark 3.3(iv). The displayed algebraic expression used to motivate these rough numerical bounds on p. 5 is typographically suspect; use the stated inequalities only as the paper’s reported bounds, not as independently certified simplifications.

**Theorem 3.5 (PDF pp. 7–8; proof pp. 7–8).** Let `d_1,d_2` be semimetrics and suppose that for all `s,t` and `u>=0`,

`P(||X_t-X_s|| >= sqrt(u)d_2(t,s)+u d_1(t,s)) <= 2e^(-u)`.

Then for any `t_0` and `1<=p<infinity`,

`(E sup_t ||X_t-X_(t0)||^p)^(1/p) <= C[gamma_2(T,d_2)+gamma_1(T,d_1)] + 2 sup_t (E||X_t-X_(t0)||^p)^(1/p)`.

There are universal `c,C>0` such that for `u>=1`,

`P{sup_t ||X_t-X_(t0)|| >= C[gamma_2(T,d_2)+gamma_1(T,d_1)] + c[sqrt(u)Delta_(d2)(T)+u Delta_(d1)(T)]} <= e^(-u)`.

The proof chooses separate admissible partition chains for the two metrics, intersects partitions to preserve the cardinality budget, telescopes across scales, applies a union bound to increment tails, and then converts the resulting tail estimate to moments. No independence among process increments is assumed. For infinite `T`, Remark 3.1 defines the `L_p` supremum by a supremum over finite subsets; a concrete application still needs measurability/separability.

## Further results actually read

- Corollary 3.4 (PDF p. 6) gives a uniform Azuma–Hoeffding bound for a family of martingales under the common filtration, with metric `d(s,t)^2=sum_k ||Delta_k(X_t-X_s)||_infinity^2`.
- Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 9–11) considers a unitary `N x N` matrix with `sup_(k,l) sqrt(N)|U_kl|<=K`, independent Bernoulli row selectors of mean `m/N`, and the rescaled sampled matrix `U_I=sqrt(N/m)R_IU`. Its restricted-isometry conclusion is `P(delta_s>=delta)<=eta` when `m >= s K^2 delta^-2 max{d_1 log^2(s)log(m)log(N), d_2 log(eta^-1)}` for universal `d_1,d_2>0`. The proof combines the chaining estimate with a nontrivial entropy estimate inherited from Rudelson–Vershynin; it is not a standalone proof of that entropy bound.
- Corollary 5.2 (PDF p. 12) applies mixed-tail chaining to centered empirical processes with independent coordinates satisfying Bernstein moment conditions; the complexity terms are `m^(-1/2) gamma_2` and `m^(-1) gamma_1`, with separate variance and scale diameters in the tail term.
- Theorem 5.5 (PDF pp. 15–18) bounds suprema of empirical averages of squares using a truncated chaining argument and a `psi_2` metric; proof splits scales at `2^(n/2)<=sqrt(m)` and invokes the appendix inequalities.
- Theorem 6.5 (PDF pp. 21–22) gives a generic-chaining moment/tail bound for suprema of centered quadratic forms of independent mean-zero `psi_2` coordinates, in terms of `gamma_(2,p)(A,d_infinity)`, `Delta_2(A)`, `Delta_4(A)`, and `Delta_infinity(A)`. Its proof uses a net/partition argument, Lemma 6.4 decoupling, and mixed-tail chaining. Theorem 6.2 is cited from Krahmer–Mendelson–Rauhut rather than reproved here.

## Proof details and transfer checks

For Theorem 3.2, choose an optimal admissible sequence, project each index to its nearest net point, split the supremum into a coarse term and a telescoping chain, and union-bound the `psi_alpha` increment tails over pairs between adjacent nets. Integrating the resulting tail gives the truncated `L_p` bound; a moment-to-tail lemma gives the displayed deviation term. For Theorem 3.5, separate partition chains encode `d_2` and `d_1`; the intersection partition makes one admissible chain while preserving both complexity contributions. This explains why the conclusion contains two gamma functionals and both diameter scales.

The compressed-sensing application requires the exact random-row model, coherence bound `K`, logarithmic entropy estimate, and the stated sparsity/sample-size regime. Chaining alone does not imply a restricted-isometry result. Generic chaining controls upper deviations/suprema; it supplies neither a lower bound nor anti-concentration. A model-specific target must establish the correct increment-tail premise and measurable/separable supremum before transfer.
