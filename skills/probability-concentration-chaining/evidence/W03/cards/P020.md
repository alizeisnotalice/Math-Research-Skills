## Order statistics of finite Gaussian fields

For a real random variable `W`, the paper defines `Q(W,ε)=sup_t P(t≤W≤t+ε)` (PDF p. 1). Let `X_1,...,X_n` be a Gaussian field with `Var(X_i)>0` and `Cor(X_i,X_j)<1` for distinct indices. For each order statistic `X_(k)` and each `ε>0`, Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3) states

`ε/[√12 √(Var(X_(k))+ε²/12)] ≤ Q(X_(k),ε) ≤ √12 ε/[√(Var(X_(k))+ε²/12)]`.

The same inequalities hold for the order statistics of `|X_i|`. The lower bound is not specifically Gaussian: Remark 1 states it holds for arbitrary real random variables. For centered Gaussian fields, Corollary 1 applies this theorem to the augmented field `(X_1,...,X_n,−X_1,...,−X_n)` to obtain, for `Z_n=max_i |X_i|`, the sharper upper factor `ε` in place of `√12 ε`, with the same denominator. Degenerate coordinates and perfect correlations are removed from the finite maximum; if all coordinates vanish, the concentration function is one.

## Separable Gaussian-process supremum

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 4): if `X=(X_u)_{u∈U}` is a separable centered Gaussian process, `Z=sup_u |X_u|<∞` almost surely, then the same two-sided bound as Theorem 1 is stated with `Z` in place of `X_(k)`. The proof takes increasing finite subsets from a countable separability set, obtains almost-sure convergence of finite maxima, uses continuity of the nondegenerate limit law and convergence of variances, and passes the finite-dimensional inequalities to the limit (PDF p. 4). **Proof-status limitation:** the upper bound in Theorem 1 is not certified by the paper's displayed proof because its Proposition 4 proof relies on the invalid general sum-closure claim in Lemma 7; Theorem 2's upper-bound proof invokes Theorem 1. This is a proof gap in the cited argument, not a finding that either theorem statement is false. The lower bound in Theorem 1 is stated in Remark 1 for arbitrary real random variables, and Theorem 2 says its lower bound follows from that remark; these lower bounds do not rely on Lemma 7. Remark 2 extends the inequalities to `Z=sup_u X_u` for noncentered Gaussian processes when all one-dimensional marginals are nondegenerate, `inf_u Var(X_u)>0`; that nondegeneracy condition belongs to this extension. Corollary 2 (PDF p. 5) rewrites the bounds using the essential supremum of the density mode, but its upper-bound use inherits the same proof-status limitation.

## Variance estimates supporting the anti-concentration bound

For the stated upper bound, the theorem would be useful when `Var(Z)` is known or can be controlled if its cited proof chain is validated independently. Its lower bound remains supported by Remark 1. Proposition 1 (PDF p. 9) states for separable centered `X`, `Var(X_u)≥σ²>0`, and finite `Z=sup_u X_u`,

`Var(Z) ≥ (1/15²) [σ/(1+E[Z/σ])]²`;

it also holds for `sup_u |X_u|`. The proof splits according to whether `E[Z/σ]≤1/2`, using Chebyshev plus a Gaussian Mills lower bound in the first case, and an independent-copy argument, Lemma 1, and Cantelli's inequality in the second (PDF pp. 9–10). Proposition 2 (PDF p. 10) gives an upper variance bound assuming `Var(X_u)≤σ̄²`, `|Cor(X_u,X_v)|≤ρ`, and finite `Z`:

`Var(Z) ≤ 2²σ̄² ∧ [11²σ̄²ρ + 60²σ̄²/(E[Z/σ̄]−3√log 2)_+²]`.

The same bound holds for `Z=sup_u |X_u|`; `(a)_+=max(a,0)`. The coefficients are squares (`2²`, `11²`, `60²`) and the minimum applies between the first term and the entire bracket. Its proof appears on PDF pp. 10–12 and uses a covariance interpolation identity, correlation-level partitioning, and Gaussian concentration. Preserve the grouping and assumptions if reused.

Theorem 1's proof chain (PDF pp. 16–21) is not validated as written. Proposition 3 (PDF pp. 16–17) represents the order-statistic density as a sum over index/subset terms; each term contains a Gaussian density factor and a conditional probability factor. The Gaussian-density-times-increasing-factor description applies to the maximum case in the cited maximum-density results, not to arbitrary `k`th order statistics. In Proposition 4's proof (PDF pp. 18–19), each conditional summand is claimed to be `s`-concave, then Lemma 7 is used to conclude that their sum, and the smoothed density sum, remain `s`-concave. Lemma 7 (PDF p. 18) asserts that a sum of arbitrary nonnegative `s`-concave functions on `R` remains `s`-concave for every `s<0`; this assertion is false. For `s=−1`, take `f_1(x)=exp(−(x−3)^2)` and `f_2(x)=exp(−(x+3)^2)`. Each is `−1`-concave because its reciprocal has positive second derivative. But for `f=f_1+f_2`, the endpoints `x=−3,3` have common value `1+exp(−36)`, whereas the midpoint has value `2exp(−9)`. The `s=−1` midpoint inequality would require the midpoint to be at least the equal endpoint value, and it fails: `exp(9)>10` gives `2exp(−9)<1/5<1<1+exp(−36)`. The exact symbolic/numeric check is recorded in `catalogue/integration_math_checks/negative_concavity_sum.json` (root-owned independent certificate; not modified here). Therefore the stated argument does not establish Proposition 4's order-statistic and smoothed-law claims, and Proposition 5 cannot be applied to them through this chain. Theorem 1's stated upper bound and the upper-bound chain in Theorem 2 are consequently not treated here as proven usable results; this flags the proof dependency and does not assert that either theorem is false. The lower-bound conclusions noted above remain independent. Proposition 1 and Proposition 2 in §4 have their own proofs (PDF pp. 9–12) and are not part of this Lemma 7 dependency chain. Proposition 5's one-dimensional variance–mode estimate (PDF pp. 19–21) is separately stated for variables already known to have an `s`-concave density; this card does not validate its application to the order statistics.

## Transfer boundary

This result is intended to control the Levy concentration function by the *actual variance of the supremum/order statistic*. It is not an upper-tail theorem and does not by itself estimate that variance. Check separability, a.s. finiteness, centering, and (for the uncentered signed-supremum extension) a positive lower variance bound. For non-Gaussian order statistics only the universal lower bound is asserted here. Do not use the Gaussian upper bounds from this card as validated proof results until the Lemma 7 dependency is independently repaired or bypassed.
