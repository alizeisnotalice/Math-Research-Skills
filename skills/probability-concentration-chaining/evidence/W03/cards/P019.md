## Sharp bounds with no dependence restriction

Let `X_1,...,X_d` share CDF `F`, and let `P_d(F)` be the set of all joint laws with these marginals (via a copula). Theorems 1 (PDF p. 5; displayed equations (3)–(4)) state, for each `x∈R`, `ε≥0`,

`max_(P∈P_d(F)) P{x < max_i X_i ≤ x+ε} = [d(F(x+ε)−F(x))] ∧ F(x+ε)`,

`min_(P∈P_d(F)) P{x < max_i X_i ≤ x+ε} = 0 ∨ [1−F(x)−d(1−F(x+ε))]`.

These are exact extrema over arbitrary copulas, not merely upper bounds under independence. The proof first maps to uniforms with `u=F(x)`, `δ=F(x+ε)−F(x)`, where the event probability is the copula diagonal increment `Δ(u+δ)−Δ(u)`. The universal diagonal constraints `Δ(1)=1`, `Δ(u)≤u`, and `0≤Δ(v)−Δ(u)≤d(v−u)` yield the bounds; explicit piecewise-linear extremal diagonals attain them (PDF pp. 15–16). The quantile transform returns to arbitrary `F`.

## Improved upper bound for diagonally convex dependence

Definition 3 (PDF p. 9) restricts to laws whose copula diagonal `Δ(u)=C(u,...,u)` is convex. Theorem 2 (same page) gives the exact sharp maximum

`max_(P∈P_d^c(F)) P{x < max_i X_i ≤ x+ε} = (F(x+ε)−F(x)) [ (1/(1−F(x))) ∧ d ]`.

For `F(x)=1`, interpret the probability directly (zero for finite `ε` when the tail at `x` vanishes); the displayed hazard-style factor is used when `F(x)<1`. The upper estimate follows from convexity and `Δ(1)=1`, which bound the diagonal increment by `δ(1−Δ(u))/(1−u)`, together with `Δ(u)≥0` and the copula Lipschitz bound `dδ`. The maximizing diagonal `Δ_u` is constructed in the proof (PDF pp. 16–17). This is a pointwise interval bound; a uniform Levy concentration bound additionally requires optimizing over `x` and controlling the marginal increment/density.

Lemma 2 (PDF p. 10, proof p. 17) verifies every multivariate Gaussian law with mean vector and positive-semidefinite covariance has a diagonally convex copula, including degenerate coordinates by deletion/extension. After standardization, the density of the coordinate maximum has form `φ(x)g(x)` with `g` increasing, so the copula diagonal has increasing derivative. Lemma 3 (PDF p. 10, printed p. 9; proof p. 17) gives the following Archimedean-generator criterion. For a `d`-dimensional generator `ψ`, define

`Ψ(t) := d · ψ'(ψ^(-1)(t)) / ψ'(ψ^(-1)(d t))`, for `t>0`.

If this `Ψ` is non-increasing, then the copula has a convex diagonal. The formula was checked against the rendered source page; the factor `d` and the order of the two derivative terms are material. Independence, Fréchet–Hoeffding extremes, and Clayton/Frank/Gumbel–Hougaard families are examples (PDF pp. 10–12).

## Applications and limits

Section 4 (PDF pp. 12–15) applies the theorem to Gaussian-mixture maximum approximations and high-dimensional chi-squared approximations. In particular, the text notes dimension-free bounds for chi-squared marginals with degrees of freedom `p≥2` under a diagonally convex copula, using bounded gamma hazard. The examples do not eliminate the copula assumption. No bound here transfers to arbitrary non-identically distributed marginals or a non-maximum statistic without a separate argument.

## Transfer boundary

First establish a common marginal CDF and identify the joint copula. The arbitrary-copula formula is sharp but can scale linearly in `d`; improved dimension behavior requires a verified convex copula diagonal. Verify the marginal's local increment or density/hazard before taking a supremum over `x`. This anti-concentration statement controls short intervals for a maximum, not upper-tail probabilities, small-ball probabilities, or an empirical-process coupling.
