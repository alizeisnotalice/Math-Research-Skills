## Four inequalities and their hypotheses

The introduction (PDF pp. 1–2) places four results under entropy/log-Sobolev methods. The paper is an exposition of these results and their proofs, not a new claim of all four.

1. **Convex distance (equation (1), pp. 1, 14–19).** For a product law `P=⊗_{i=1}^n μ_i` on `Ω=∏Ω_i` and measurable `A` with `P(A)>0`, define `U_A(x)` as the mismatch indicator vectors for witnesses `y∈A` (a zero coordinate forces `x_i=y_i`), `V_A(x)=conv U_A(x)`, and `d_A(x)=dist(0,V_A(x))`. The displayed inequality is `P(A)∫exp(d_A²/4)dP≤1`.
2. **Talagrand `L¹–L²` variance inequality (equation (2), p. 2; proof §5, pp. 19–22).** On the biased discrete cube with product law `μ_p`, `0<p<1`, define the weighted coordinate differences exactly as printed: `Δ_i f(x)=(1−p)(f(x)−f(U_i(x)))` if `x_i=1`, and `p(f(x)−f(U_i(x)))` if `x_i=0`. For `E_{μ_p}f=0`, the source gives
   `||f||₂²≤K log(2/[p(1−p)]) Σ_i ||Δ_i f||₂² / log(e ||Δ_i f||₂/||Δ_i f||₁)`.
   Ledoux states `K=30` is valid, and `K=14` when `p=1/2` (p. 21).
3. **Gaussian quadratic transport (equation (3), pp. 2, 22–24).** For the standard Gaussian `γ` on `R^n` and `μ=fγ`, `W₂²(μ,γ)≤2 Ent(μ|γ)=2∫f log f dγ`; the constant 2 is sharp. The cost is Euclidean squared distance.
4. **Empirical-process supremum (equation (4), pp. 2, 24–29).** For independent `X_1,…,X_n` in a measurable space and a countable family `F` of measurable real functions, let `Z=sup_{f∈F}Σ_i f(X_i)`, `U=sup_f||f||_∞`, and `V=E sup_f Σ_i f(X_i)²`. For finite `U` and a numerical constant `K`, the source states `P(|Z−EZ|≥t)≤K exp{−[t/(KU)]log(1+tU/V)}` for `t>0` (with degenerate zero scales interpreted by the limiting statement).

## Proof mechanisms and dependencies

Section 3 (PDF pp. 5–14) develops the variational/dual entropy inequalities, product tensorization, two-point logarithmic Sobolev inequality, cube hypercontractivity and Gaussian log-Sobolev inequality. For convex distance (§4), the key identification is `d_A(x)=sup_{a≥0,||a||₂=1}d_a(x,A)`. Tensorized entropy controls the Laplace transform of `F_A²`; the positive-parameter Herbst estimate first gives a weaker constant, while the negative-parameter branch uses the coordinate estimate `F_A²(x)−F_A²(y)≤1`; integrating both branches yields the displayed `1/4` exponent coefficient. For the `L¹–L²` inequality (§5), semigroup variance representation, Poincaré decay and hypercontractivity yield equation (31), then the generator differences are matched to the weighted `Δ_i` convention. For transport (§6), Kantorovich duality and the Hopf–Lax semigroup `Q_s` are combined with Gaussian LSI and `∂_sQ_sφ=−|∇Q_sφ|²/2`; the Herbst differential inequality yields the sharp factor 2. For empirical suprema (§7), first use the entropy inequality for nonnegative `[0,1]` classes to obtain a Poisson MGF/tail. For bounded signed functions, entropy tensorization gives a Bernstein intermediate tail; truncate at a level depending on `V/t`, combine the bounded and large-coordinate parts, and obtain the Bennett-style logarithmic profile.

## Transfer boundary

Keep the four models separate: a product law and the correct convex distance; a biased cube with the paper's weighted coordinate-gradient convention; a density relative to standard Gaussian for `T₂`; independent observations and a countable measurable class for the empirical supremum. The constants and proof route in this review do not justify transferring the results to dependent observations or replacing variance terms by a deterministic envelope without a separate argument.
