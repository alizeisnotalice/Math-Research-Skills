## Setup and principal inequalities

For a supermartingale-difference sequence `(ξ_i,F_i)`, write `S_k=Σ_{i≤k}ξ_i` and the square variation `[S]_k=Σ_{i≤k}ξ_i²`. The paper also uses predictable random controls `V_{i−1}` measurable with respect to `F_{i−1}`.

**Theorem 2.1 (physical PDF p. 4).** Suppose the nonnegative `F_{i−1}`-measurable `V_{i−1}` satisfy, for some fixed `λ>0` and nonnegative functions `f(λ),g(λ)`,

`E[ exp{λξ_i−g(λ)ξ_i²} | F_{i−1} ] ≤ 1+f(λ)V_{i−1}`

for each `i=1,…,n`. For all `x,v,w>0`,

`P{∃ k∈[1,n]: S_k≥x, [S]_k≤v², Σ_{i=1}^k V_{i−1}≤w}`

`≤ exp{−λx+g(λ)v²+n log(1+f(λ)w/n)}`

`≤ exp{−λx+g(λ)v²+f(λ)w}`.

The source’s proof constructs the product exponential supermartingale by dividing each factor `exp{λξ_i−g(λ)ξ_i²}` by `1+f(λ)V_{i−1}`. At the first index where the joint crossing event occurs, the numerator contributes at least `exp(λx−g(λ)v²)` while the product of denominators is bounded by the total `V` budget. The proof refines the denominator bound by Jensen’s inequality to `n log(1+f(λ)w/n)`, then uses `log(1+y)≤y` for the simpler bound.

**Theorem 2.6 (complete statement: physical PDF p. 7, Eq. (2.15)).** Assume `V_{i−1}`, `i∈[1,n]`, are positive and `F_{i−1}`-measurable random variables. If `E(e^{λξ_i}|F_{i−1})≤exp{f(λ)V_{i−1}}` for every `i∈[1,n]`, for a positive function `f(λ)` and some `λ∈(0,∞)`, then for all `x,v>0`,

`P{S_k≥x and Σ_{i=1}^k V_{i−1}≤v² for some k∈[1,n]} ≤ exp{−λx+f(λ)v²}`.

**Corollary 2.7 (complete statement: physical PDF p. 7, Eqs. (2.16)–(2.19)).** Assume `U_{i−1}`, `i∈[1,n]`, are nonnegative and `F_{i−1}`-measurable random variables, and `ξ_i≤U_{i−1}` for all `i`. Define

`C_{i−1}² = E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})` if `E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})≥U_{i−1}²`, and otherwise
`C_{i−1}² = ¼ (U_{i−1}+E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})/U_{i−1})²`.

Then, for all `λ>0`, `E(e^{λξ_i}|F_{i−1})≤exp{λ² C_{i−1}²/2}`; and for all `x,v>0`, `P{S_k≥x and Σ_{i=1}^k C_{i−1}²≤v² for some k∈[1,n]}≤exp(−x²/(2v²))`. If additionally `E(ξ_i²|F_{i−1})≥U_{i−1}²` for every `i`, the paper gives the corresponding maximal-bracket form (2.19). The random predictable `C²` proxy is part of the statement; it is not the unconditional variance of a terminal sum.

## Applications and section coverage

The paper applies the inequalities to linear regression, autoregressive processes and Galton–Watson branching processes. Its proof sections use change of measure, stopping times and conditional exponential moments. These are model-specific examples, not automatic applications absent the corresponding filtration and increment bounds.

## Transfer boundary

Before applying a result, specify the filtration, supermartingale-difference property, exact conditional MGF inequality, predictable control and stopping/crossing event. Theorem 2.6 requires strictly positive predictable `V_{i−1}`; Corollary 2.7 permits nonnegative predictable `U_{i−1}` and uses its own piecewise predictable `C²` proxy. These random controls are not interchangeable with a deterministic bracket or marginal variance. All claims await independent review.
