## Spectral scale

The setting is a real separable Hilbert space `H` and centered Gaussian elements with nuclear (trace-class) covariance operators. Write the nonnegative eigenvalues in decreasing order as `λ_(j,ξ)` and set `Λ_(k,ξ)^2=Σ_(j≥k) λ_(j,ξ)^2` for `k=1,2`. The paper defines (PDF p. 10)

`κ(Σ_ξ)=Λ_(1,ξ)^(-1)` if `3λ_(1,ξ)^2≤Λ_(1,ξ)^2`;

`κ(Σ_ξ)=(λ_(1,ξ)Λ_(2,ξ))^(-1/2)` if `3λ_(1,ξ)^2>Λ_(1,ξ)^2` and `3λ_(2,ξ)^2≤Λ_(2,ξ)^2`;

`κ(Σ_ξ)=(λ_(1,ξ)λ_(2,ξ))^(-1/2)` if both corresponding spike inequalities are strict.

It satisfies `0.9(Λ_(1,ξ)Λ_(2,ξ))^(-1/2)≤κ(Σ_ξ)≤1.8(Λ_(1,ξ)Λ_(2,ξ))^(-1/2)` (equation (2.1), PDF p. 10). If the effective rank is below two, the resulting scale can be infinite and the bound is vacuous; do not silently treat it as finite.

## Large-ball comparison

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 11, equation (2.2)). If `ξ,η` are centered Gaussian elements in `H` with covariance operators `Σ_ξ,Σ_η`, then for every `a∈H`,

`sup_(x>0) |P(||ξ−a||≤x)−P(||η||≤x)| \lesssim [κ(Σ_ξ)+κ(Σ_η)] (||λ_ξ−λ_η||_1+||a||²)`.

Here `λ_ξ,λ_η` are the decreasing eigenvalue sequences, `||·||_1` on sequences is their `ℓ¹` difference, and `\lesssim` hides an absolute multiplicative constant as defined on PDF p. 10. Corollary 2.2 rewrites the scale with `(Λ_1Λ_2)^(-1/2)` and, under `3||Σ||²≤||Σ||_Fr²` for both covariance operators, uses `||Σ||_Fr^(-1)` (PDF pp. 11–12). Corollary 2.3 replaces eigenvalue `ℓ¹` difference by nuclear norm `||Σ_ξ−Σ_η||_1` via Weyl–Hoffman; Corollary 2.4 compares balls with the same shift (p. 12).

The proof in Section 3 (PDF pp. 18–20) interpolates the covariance spectra linearly, differentiates the characteristic function of the squared norm, applies Fourier inversion, and controls the resulting densities using Theorem 2.6. It handles the shift separately by interpolating `a(s)=√s a`, then uses the same density bound and the triangle inequality. This comparison is for Hilbert-ball probabilities; it is not a coordinatewise Gaussian-max comparison.

## Density and short-band anti-concentration

Theorem 2.6 (PDF p. 13) states that for a centered Gaussian element `ξ` in `H` with trace-class covariance `Σ_ξ`, the density `p_ξ(x,a)` of `||ξ−a||²` obeys `sup_(x≥0) p_ξ(x,a) \lesssim κ(Σ_ξ)` for every shift `a∈H`; in particular the scale may be replaced by `(Λ_(1,ξ)Λ_(2,ξ))^(-1/2)`. The proof splits into three covariance-spectrum cases (PDF pp. 16–18): Fourier inversion and characteristic-function products when many eigenvalues contribute; convolution of the two largest-coordinate squared-Gaussian densities for two dominant eigenvalues; and a one-spike-plus-tail decomposition with a normalized weighted chi-square density bounded by Fourier inversion.

Theorem 2.7 (PDF p. 13, equation (2.4)) then gives, for every `ε>0` and arbitrary `a∈H`,

`sup_(x>0) P{x<||ξ−a||²<x+ε} \lesssim κ(Σ_ξ) ε`.

The displayed statement includes the shift in the event and follows uniformly in `a` from Theorem 2.6. It is an interval bound for the *squared norm*, not for `||ξ−a||` without changing variables and accounting for the interval width. A rank-one/degenerate example on PDF p. 15 shows that an `O(ε)` bound cannot depend only on the largest eigenvalue: the small-band probability can be order `√ε`.

## Transfer boundary

Check the Hilbert-space setting, zero mean, trace-class covariance and effective rank scale. For comparison, control the decreasing eigenvalue-sequence difference or the covariance nuclear norm and the shift's squared Hilbert norm. This source is useful for Gaussian norm/ball statistics and dimension-free squared-norm bands; it does not directly bound maxima of coordinates, empirical-process suprema, or large-deviation tails.
