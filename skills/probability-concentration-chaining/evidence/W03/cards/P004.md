## Definition 1: sub-psi process

Let `T=N` for discrete time or `T=(0,infinity)` for continuous time. In continuous time the filtration has the usual hypotheses and the processes are cadlag. Let `(S_t,V_t)` be adapted real processes with `S_0=V_0=0`, `V_t>=0`, and `V_t` nondecreasing. Given `psi:[0,lambda_max)->R` and `l_0>=1`, `(S_t)` is `l_0`-sub-`psi` with variance process `(V_t)` if for each `lambda in [0,lambda_max)` there exists a nonnegative supermartingale `L_t(lambda)` with `L_0(lambda)<=l_0` and

`exp{lambda S_t - psi(lambda)V_t} <= L_t(lambda)` almost surely for all `t` (PDF p. 9; printed p. 8).

The condition applies to the pair `(S,V)`, not to `S` in isolation. `V` is adapted/nondecreasing and need not be predictable. Probabilities in the theorem are conditional on `F_0`, written `P_0`.

For a CGF-like `psi`, the paper assumes strict convexity and differentiability on its domain, `psi(0)=psi'(0+)=0`, and `sup psi=lambda`-domain `psi(lambda)=infinity`; define `bar b=sup_(lambda in [0,lambda_max)) psi'(lambda)` (PDF pp. 10–11; printed pp. 9–10). It defines

`psi*(u)=sup_(lambda in [0,lambda_max)}[lambda u-psi(lambda)]`,

`D(u)=sup{lambda in (0,lambda_max): psi(lambda)/lambda<=u}` (the supremum of the empty set is defined as zero), and

`s(u)=psi(psi*'(u))/psi*'(u)`, for `u in (0,bar b)` (PDF p. 12; printed p. 11).

## Theorem 1: four equivalent line-crossing forms

**Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 12–13; printed pp. 11–12).** For any `psi` and `l_0`, every `(S,V)` in the class of `l_0`-sub-`psi` pairs satisfies, for `a,b>0`,

`P_0{exists t in T: S_t >= a+bV_t} <= l_0 exp{-a D(b)}`.  (a)

If `psi` is CGF-like, the following additional forms are equivalent to (a):

- For `m>0`, `x in (0,m bar b)`,
  `P_0{exists t: S_t >= x+s(x/m)(V_t-m)} <= l_0 exp{-m psi*(x/m)}`.  (b)
- For `m>0`, `x in (0,bar b)`,
  `P_0{exists t: S_t/V_t >= x - ((x-s(x))/V_t)(V_t-m)} <= l_0 exp{-m psi*(x)}`.  (c)
- For `m>=0`, `x,b>0`,
  `P_0{exists t: V_t>=m, S_t>=x+b(V_t-m)}` is at most
  `l_0 exp{-(x-(b wedge bar b)m)D(b)}` if `x>m bar b` or `s(x/m)>b`, and at most
  `l_0 exp{-m psi*(x/m)}` if `x<=m bar b` and `s(x/m)<=b`.  (d)

The paper takes `m bar b=infinity` when `bar b=infinity`. The parameter domains above are part of the theorem; in particular (b) uses `x<m bar b`, while (c) uses `x<bar b`.

## Proof and consequences

The proof first applies Ville's maximal inequality `P_0{exists t:L_t>=a}<=L_0/a` to the exponential supermartingale. For (a), fix any admissible `lambda` with `psi(lambda)/lambda<=b`; crossing `S_t>=a+bV_t` forces the exponential process above `exp(lambda a)`. Optimizing over those `lambda` yields `D(b)`. For CGF-like `psi`, Lemma 2 establishes monotonicity/invertibility of `psi(lambda)/lambda`, `D`, and `s`; the authors then optimize the slope through the Legendre transform to derive (b)–(d) and prove equivalence. The exact proof is on PDF pp. 14–17 (printed pp. 13–16), and the key boundary and convex-transform formulas were checked against rendered pages.

The paper’s examples include sub-Gaussian, sub-Poisson, sub-gamma, sub-exponential and Bernoulli `psi` choices (PDF pp. 22–24). The later corollaries treat fixed-time Chernoff bounds; Hoeffding/Bennett/Freedman/Bernstein martingale inequalities; matrix extensions; self-normalized martingales; smooth Banach-valued martingales; continuous-time scalar processes; and sequential probability ratio testing (PDF pp. 31–43). In every case, the application must construct the required nonnegative supermartingale with the correct `l_0`, filtration, `psi`, and variance process.

## Transfer boundary

The result is an upper bound for a line-crossing event uniformly over time. It does not construct the exponential supermartingale for a target model. A stopping-time interpretation alone is insufficient: verify adaptedness, the variance process, the conditional mgf/supermartingale domination, and initial normalizer. The paper’s scalar, matrix, and Banach-space applications have separate sufficient conditions; the scalar theorem cannot be silently applied to a matrix or norm process. No lower-tail or anti-concentration conclusion follows without a separate argument (for a left tail, the paper recommends applying the framework to `-S` when the required condition holds).
