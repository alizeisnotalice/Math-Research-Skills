## Theorems 1.1–1.6 (PDF/printed pp. 3–5)

Write d for the matrix dimension and lambda_max for the largest eigenvalue.

1. **Matrix Chernoff (Theorem 1.1).** For independent random self-adjoint X_k >= 0 with lambda_max(X_k) <= R almost surely, set
   mu_min = lambda_min(sum_k E X_k), mu_max = lambda_max(sum_k E X_k). Then
   P{lambda_min(sum_k X_k) <= (1-delta)mu_min}
   <= d [e^(-delta)/(1-delta)^(1-delta)]^(mu_min/R), for 0 <= delta <= 1,
   and
   P{lambda_max(sum_k X_k) >= (1+delta)mu_max}
   <= d [e^delta/(1+delta)^(1+delta)]^(mu_max/R), for delta >= 0.
   The dimension prefactor is d; the lower and upper tails use the corresponding extreme eigenvalue of the sum of expectations.
2. **Matrix Gaussian/Rademacher series (Theorem 1.2).** For fixed self-adjoint A_k and independent standard Gaussian or Rademacher xi_k, let sigma^2 = ||sum_k A_k^2||. For t >= 0,
   P{lambda_max(sum_k xi_k A_k) >= t} <= d exp(-t^2/(2 sigma^2)).
3. **Matrix Hoeffding (Theorem 1.3).** For independent mean-zero self-adjoint X_k, fixed self-adjoint A_k, and X_k^2 <= A_k^2 almost surely, put sigma^2 = ||sum_k A_k^2||. Then
   P{lambda_max(sum_k X_k) >= t} <= d exp(-t^2/(8 sigma^2)).
   This is one-sided; the 1/8 is the stated general constant.
4. **Matrix Bernstein (Theorem 1.4).** For independent mean-zero self-adjoint X_k, with lambda_max(X_k) <= R almost surely, set sigma^2 = ||sum_k E X_k^2||. Then
   P{lambda_max(sum_k X_k) >= t} <= d exp(-[t^2/2]/[sigma^2 + Rt/3]).
   The upper bound on lambda_max(X_k) is one-sided; the theorem does not assume X_k >= 0.
5. **Rectangular Gaussian/Rademacher series (Theorem 1.5).** For fixed d1-by-d2 matrices B_k, independent standard Gaussian or Rademacher xi_k, and
   sigma^2 = max{||sum_k B_k B_k*||, ||sum_k B_k* B_k||},
   P{||sum_k xi_k B_k|| >= t} <= (d1+d2) exp(-t^2/(2 sigma^2)).
6. **Rectangular Bernstein (Theorem 1.6).** For independent mean-zero d1-by-d2 matrices Z_k with ||Z_k|| <= R almost surely, let
   sigma^2 = max{||sum_k E Z_k Z_k*||, ||sum_k E Z_k* Z_k||}.
   Then P{||sum_k Z_k|| >= t} <= (d1+d2) exp(-[t^2/2]/[sigma^2 + Rt/3]).
   The rectangular prefactor is d1+d2, coming from self-adjoint dilation.

## Proof dependency chain

- Proposition 3.1 (p. 9): matrix Laplace-transform bound reduces an upper-eigenvalue tail to inf_{theta>0} exp(-theta t) E tr exp(theta Y).
- Theorem 3.2 (p. 10), Lieb's concavity theorem, gives concavity of A -> tr exp(H + log A) for fixed self-adjoint H. Corollary 3.3 uses Jensen to control E tr exp(H+X).
- Lemma 3.4 (p. 11) iterates that conditional expectation bound to obtain subadditivity of the matrix cumulant generating function for independent summands. The scalar product rule for mgfs cannot simply be copied for matrices.
- Theorem 3.6 (p. 12) is the master tail inequality. Corollaries 3.7 and 3.9 combine it with suitable matrix-mgf bounds and trace/eigenvalue inequalities.
- Sections 4–6 supply Gaussian/Rademacher, Chernoff, Bennett and Bernstein mgf controls. Rectangular results use the self-adjoint dilation in §2.6, equations (2.10)–(2.12).
- Section 7 treats matrix Azuma (Theorem 7.1), a martingale corollary (7.2), and a bounded-differences corollary (7.5). The symmetrization argument uses Golden–Thompson and Cauchy–Schwarz; this part does not provide the full later matrix Freedman/self-normalized theory.

## Limits for transfer

- The independence assumptions in Theorems 1.1, 1.3, 1.4 and 1.6 are structural; exchangeability, finite-wise independence, or arbitrary dependence is not a substitute.
- Theorems 1.1–1.6 are finite-dimensional and use a dimension prefactor d or d1+d2. Dimension-free conclusions require a separate argument or a sharper theorem.
- Upper-eigenvalue bounds are one-sided. For two-sided operator-norm tails, also control the negative sum (possibly with a different R).
- The variance proxy is deterministic in the stated independent-sum inequalities. Do not silently replace it with a random/self-normalized quantity or claim time-uniformity.
- Section 7 has a limited martingale treatment. The general stopping, variance-adaptive and matrix Freedman bounds needed by a target problem must be established separately.
