## Anti-concentration of Gaussian suprema

For a separable process `Y=(Y_t)_(t in T)`, define `p_(x,epsilon)(Y)=P{|sup_t Y_t-x|<=epsilon}` and the Levy concentration function `p_epsilon(Y)=sup_(x in R) p_(x,epsilon)(Y)` (Definition 2.1, PDF p. 6).

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 7).** Let `X=(X_t)_(t in T)` be a separable Gaussian process on a semimetric space such that `E X_t=0` and `E X_t^2=1` for every `t`, and assume `sup_t X_t<infinity` almost surely. Then `a(X)=E[sup_t X_t]` is finite and nonnegative, and, for every `epsilon>=0`,

`p_epsilon(X) <= 4 epsilon (a(X)+1)`.

**Corollary 2.1 (same page).** Under the same assumptions,

`p_epsilon(|X|) <= 4 epsilon (a(|X|)+1)`,

where `a(|X|)=E[sup_t |X_t|]` is finite and at least `sqrt(2/pi)`.

The proof in Appendix C (PDF pp. 26–27) takes increasing finite subsets from separability, applies the finite Gaussian-vector anti-concentration inequality, passes to the limit at continuity points, and extends to all `epsilon` by right continuity. The corollary reduces to the finite-index case and the cited finite-vector result. This controls short-interval probabilities around any center; it is the needed reverse-direction input when translating approximation error into distributional/coverage error. It is not an upper-tail bound for a non-Gaussian process.

## Generic honest confidence-band theorem

The paper’s statistical setup is iid density observations, a studentized empirical process over `V_n=X x L_n`, and a centered, unit-variance Gaussian approximation. Its generalized SBR condition H2 requires either uniformly vanishing Levy concentration for shrinking widths (in particular, `epsilon_n log n -> 0`) or the quantitative bound `sup_(f in F) p_(epsilon_n)(|G_(n,f)|) <= C epsilon_n log n`. The Gaussian anti-concentration theorem yields this bound when `E||G_(n,f)|| <= C sqrt(log n)` (PDF pp. 8–9).

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 11).** Define the band radius with estimated critical value and explicit bias allowance by

`C_n(x)=[fhat_n(x,lhat_n)-s_n(x,lhat_n), fhat_n(x,lhat_n)+s_n(x,lhat_n)]`,

`s_n=(chat_n(alpha)+c'_n)sigmahat_n/sqrt(n)`.

Under H1 (coupling of the studentized supremum to a standardized Gaussian supremum), H3 (critical-value accuracy), H4 (uniform relative variance estimation), H5 (bias control), and H6 (control of `c'_n`),

`inf_(f in F) P_f{f in C_n} >= 1-alpha-delta_n-tau_n-pbar_(epsilonbar_(n,f))(|G_(n,f)|)`.

Here `delta_n=delta_1n+...+delta_5n` and `epsilonbar_(n,f)=epsilon_1n+epsilon_2n+epsilon_3n(c_(n,f)(alpha)+u_n sqrt(log n))`, with the symbols as in H1–H6. If H2(a) holds and `epsilon_3n u_n sqrt(log n)<=C n^-c`, coverage is asymptotically at least `1-alpha`; if H2(b) holds, the uniform finite-sample lower bound is `1-alpha-C n^-c` (constants depend on the stated regularity parameters). Corollary 3.1 (PDF p. 13) gives the analogous result for undersmoothing, replacing the radius by `chat_n(alpha)sigmahat_n/sqrt(n)` and requiring a separate bias-negligibility condition.

The proof (PDF pp. 27–28) first bounds the studentized estimation error by the Gaussian critical value plus bias and variance errors, then uses H3 to compare estimated and target Gaussian quantiles, H1 to couple the empirical supremum to the Gaussian one, and finally H2 to pay the Levy concentration cost. This is precisely where anti-concentration enters; the confidence statement does not follow from a Gaussian upper-tail bound alone.

## Gaussian multiplier bootstrap and applications

- Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 17): if the studentized kernel class is pointwise measurable and VC type with envelope `b_n`, entropy constants `(a_n,v_n)`, `K_n=A v_n(log n vee log(a_n b_n/sigma_n))`, and `b_n^2 sigma_n^4 K_n^4/n <= C_2 n^-c2`, then H1–H4 hold for the sample variance estimator and Gaussian multiplier critical value, including both H2(a) and H2(b). For common convolution and wavelet-projection kernels, the paper reduces this to a resolution growth condition of order `2^(l_max,n d)(log^4 n)/n <= C n^-c` under its kernel regularity assumptions.
- Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 21–23): multiplier-bootstrap Lepski selection combined with the bias allowance gives honest adaptive bands over the stated self-similar/Hölder setup, with polynomial coverage error and length of order `(1+u'_n)(log n/n)^(t/(2t+d))` under L1–L4 and the listed tuning restrictions.
- Appendices A–C (PDF pp. 23–31) give Slepian–Stein coupling bounds, the proof of Theorem 2.1, and the proof of the generic coverage theorem. Theorem A.1 is for centered iid empirical processes indexed by pointwise-measurable VC-type classes with finite envelope moments; Theorem A.2 treats conditional Gaussian multiplier processes.

## Transfer boundary

Check the process is separable, centered, and has unit marginal variances before using Theorem 2.1; for non-unit variances or a supremum with a different normalization, a separate comparison/scaling argument is needed. To use Theorem 3.1, verify every H1–H6 condition, including the approximation coupling, critical-value error, variance estimator, and bias. The theorem is an inference/coverage result for the paper’s stated setup, not a generic confidence-band guarantee. Anti-concentration is distinct from upper-tail concentration and does not imply a Gaussian approximation by itself.
