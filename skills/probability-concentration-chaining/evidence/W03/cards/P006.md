## Main results

Write `τ_s=sup_x ||L(X_s|X_1=x)−π||_TV`. The paper assumes geometric ergodicity `τ_s≤G θ^(s−1)` for `G≥1`, `0≤θ<1`; its η-mixing coefficients satisfy `\bar η_{ij}≤2G θ^(j−i)` (Lemma 7, PDF p. 8). For a 1-Hamming-Lipschitz `f:N^n→R` of an `N`-valued HMM whose underlying Markov chain has this bound, Theorem 1 (p. 3) gives each one-sided tail

`P(f(Y_1^n)−Ef(Y_1^n)>nε) ≤ exp(−n(1−θ)²ε²/(2G²))`,

with the identical bound for the lower tail. The proof controls the row sums of the η-mixing matrix, then applies the cited η-mixing bounded-differences inequality; passing through a common emission channel contracts total variation, so it suffices to control the latent chain.

For a stationary, countable-state Markov or hidden Markov chain with stationary law `ρ`, let `\hatρ_y=n^(−1)Σ_i 1{Y_i=y}`. Theorem 2 (PDF p. 4) states

`P(||ρ−\hatρ||_∞ > √((1+2Gθ)/(n(1−θ)))+ε) ≤ exp(−n(1−θ)²ε²/(2G²))`.

For occupation frequencies `\hatρ(E)=n^(−1)Σ_i1{Y_i∈E}`, Theorem 3 (PDF pp. 4–5) defines

`γ_n=½√((1+2Gθ)/(n(1−θ)))`,

`Λ_n(ρ)=γ_n Σ_{ρ_y≥1/n}√ρ_y + min{γ_n Σ_{ρ_y<1/n}√ρ_y, Σ_{ρ_y<1/n}ρ_y}`,

and proves `Λ_n(ρ)→0` for every distribution on `N`, plus (PDF pp. 4–5, Theorem 3). The rare-cell alternative is the total rare mass with coefficient `1`; the rendered p. 4 statement contains no `1/4` factor. The earlier card version's `¼` was a transcription error and must not be used.

`P(sup_{E⊆N}|ρ(E)−\hatρ(E)|>Λ_n(ρ)+ε)≤exp(−n(1−θ)²ε²/(2G²))`.

If `Σ_y√ρ_y<∞`, the paper gives the simpler bias bound `Λ_n≤γ_nΣ_y√ρ_y`. The theorem is a uniform occupation-measure/Chernoff bound, not a DKW bound over arbitrary real-valued thresholds without specifying the countable state order.

## Proof map and scope

Section 2.1 (PDF pp. 5–7) defines Markov and hidden Markov chains and their stationary laws. Section 2.2 (p. 7) relates Markov contraction and the inverse mixing time. In §2.3 (pp. 8–9), Lemma 7 turns geometric ergodicity into η-mixing decay; Theorem 1 then follows by bounding the mixing-matrix norm and applying the general Lipschitz concentration estimate. In §2.4 (pp. 9–12), the empirical histogram's expected `L∞` error is controlled coordinatewise using covariance sums, then Theorem 1 supplies concentration. In §2.5 (pp. 12–13), the expected total-variation error is split between cells with `ρ_y≥1/n` and rare cells; the latter are controlled both by a square-root sum and by total rare mass. Section 2.6 (pp. 13–14) relaxes stationarity by a path-law total-variation comparison and discusses burn-in. Section 2.7 (p. 14) verifies the Hamming-Lipschitz maps used in the applications. The references occupy pp. 15–16.

Theorem 1 does not require stationarity but does require the displayed geometric mixing bound for the underlying chain. The DKW and uniform-occupation statements do require stationarity (or the separate initial-law correction). The state space is countable; the constants depend on `G,θ`. The paper notes that a non-geometric inverse-mixing bound can be substituted by redoing the mixing-matrix summation, but that extension is not stated with a universal closed-form rate here.

## Transfer boundary

Identify the latent chain, its stationary law, and a valid `(G,θ)` bound before using the result. HMM observations inherit the relevant contraction only through the stated common emission mechanism. Do not discard the expectation bias `√((1+2Gθ)/(n(1−θ)))` in Theorem 2 or `Λ_n(ρ)` in Theorem 3.
