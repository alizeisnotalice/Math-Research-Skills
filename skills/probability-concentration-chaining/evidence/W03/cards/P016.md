## Large-deviation principle and rate functions

The survey sets a sequence of probability measures `P_n` on a Polish space `X` and a speed tending to infinity (normalized to `n` in the displayed definition). Its LDP definition (PDF p. 3) uses a nonnegative lower-semicontinuous rate `H:X→[0,∞]`; in the *good* case each sublevel `{x:H(x)≤ℓ}` is compact. For every closed `C⊂X` and open `U⊂X`, respectively,

`limsup_(n→∞) n^(-1) log P_n(C) ≤ -inf_(x∈C) H(x)`,

`liminf_(n→∞) n^(-1) log P_n(U) ≥ -inf_(x∈U) H(x)`.

The paper explicitly notes that compact sublevels are useful but not necessary for the two LDP bounds. Do not infer an LDP from an exponential tail estimate for one event; verify the speed, topology, both set bounds, and the proposed rate function (and goodness if needed).

## Laplace principle and entropy duality

For iid sample means `Y_n=n^(-1)Σ_i X_i`, the survey recalls Cramér's rate `h(a)=sup_θ{θa−log E exp(θX)}` (PDF p. 4); the underlying Cramér hypotheses are not enumerated there and must be checked in the cited theorem before transfer. More generally, if an LDP holds and the test functional `F` satisfies the stated suitable conditions (the survey says bounded continuity suffice),

`n^(-1) log ∫ exp(n F(x)) dP_n(x) → sup_x {F(x)−H(x)}`.

The paper motivates this through `n^(-1)log Σ_i exp(n a_i)→sup_i a_i` and states the entropy duality `log ∫e^V dα = sup_β {∫V dβ−H(β,α)}`; when the rate is convex, it gives the reverse variational representation of relative entropy over bounded measurable or bounded continuous `V` (PDF p. 4). This is a variational/Laplace bridge, not a direct tail bound. It requires the LDP and admissibility of the functional.

For empirical measures of iid observations in a Polish space, the text identifies relative entropy `H(β,α)=∫ log(dβ/dα)dβ` as Sanov's rate, assigning `+∞` unless `β≪α` with the required integrability (PDF pp. 3–4). The survey does not prove Sanov's theorem in full; its derivation from finite-alphabet multinomial probabilities and Stirling's formula is motivation, not a substitute for checking Sanov's hypotheses.

## Other regimes covered and proof scope

The remainder surveys Markov-chain empirical measures through principal eigenvalues and entropy minimization (PDF pp. 5–7), small-noise Brownian/ODE path rates and exit problems (pp. 7–9), Gibbs variational principles (pp. 9–10), interacting-particle superexponential estimates and hydrodynamic limits (pp. 10–15), and quenched/annealed random environments plus stochastic homogenization (pp. 16–22). These are different models and speeds; the paper does not justify carrying the iid Cramér rate or Sanov entropy formula into them without model-specific arguments. The proof-level content is illustrative and selective: Gaussian path rates are derived via the log-mgf dual and change-of-measure entropy costs; several process-level LDP statements are presented as known results or proof sketches.

## Transfer boundary

Use this source for the LDP definition, the Laplace-principle/Legendre-transform viewpoint, and a map of model-dependent rate functions. Before applying a result, identify the topology, speed, exponential tightness or compactness needs, regularity of test functions, and dependence structure. The survey provides neither a one-size-fits-all LDP theorem with hypotheses for every regime nor a concentration inequality for finite-sample deviations. For specific Cramér, Sanov, Markov, small-noise, hydrodynamic, or random-environment claims, consult and verify the cited primary theorem.
