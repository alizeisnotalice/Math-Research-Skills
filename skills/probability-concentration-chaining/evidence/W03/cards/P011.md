## Main result: a lower bound on deviation probability

Throughout, `Z∼N(0,I_n)` and `f:R^n→R` is convex. The paper asks when the usual Gaussian concentration upper bound is tight up to constants. Its headline theorem is a **lower bound on the probability of deviations**, not a new upper-tail concentration inequality.

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3):** If `f` is `L`-Lipschitz and `Var(f(Z))≥α²L²` for `0<α≤1`, then for every `t>0`

  `P(|f(Z)−med(f(Z))|≥tL)≥c(α)exp(−C(α)t²)`,

  where `c(α),C(α)>0` depend only on `α`; the source states `c(α)≥cα^8` and `C(α)≤Cα^(−4)log(e/α)` for universal `c,C`.

- **Theorem 2.8 (PDF p. 7), refinement:** Let
  `ov(f)=√Var(f(Z))/Lip(f)` and
  `s(f)=√Var(f(Z))/(E||∇f(Z)||₂²)^(1/2)`, with `τ=ov(f)s(f)`. For convex Lipschitz `f`,

  `P(|f(Z)−med(f(Z))|≥tL)≥cτ⁴ exp(−C(t/τ)² log(e/τ))`, for every `t>0`.

  Poincaré gives `ov(f)≤s(f)≤1` (PDF p. 6); Theorem 1.1 follows from this refinement and the variance lower bound. Do not reverse the inequality or present these estimates as upper-tail bounds.

## Proof mechanism and supporting statements

The Gaussian isoperimetric inequality gives the classical upper bound around a median. To establish matching lower probabilities for convex functions, §2 uses Ehrhard's inequality to form the Gaussian rearrangement `f*`, which is convex, nondecreasing and equimeasurable with `f`. Talagrand's variance inequality and the Bobkov–Houdré one-dimensional estimate control the derivative of this rearrangement. Lemma 2.5 then yields a one-sided lower-tail probability for a nondecreasing convex real function; Proposition 2.6 transfers it to `f` at the variance scale. Corollary 2.7 integrates these tail lower bounds to obtain centered-moment lower estimates. For Theorem 2.8, the proof combines those lower moments with Gaussian concentration upper moment bounds through Paley–Zygmund, choosing `p` proportional to `max{1,(t/τ)²}`; the factor `τ⁴` and the `log(e/τ)` in the exponent arise from this moment ratio (PDF pp. 4–8).

Corollary 2.9 (p. 7) makes equivalent, up to constants depending on one another, three assertions for convex Lipschitz `f`: a Gaussian-shaped lower probability bound at scale `L`, lower centered moments of order `p≥2` of size `√p L`, and `Var(f(Z))` bounded below by a positive multiple of `L²`. Example 2.11 (p. 8) shows why the restriction on deviation range in the variance-scale proposition cannot simply be dropped when `s(f)` is small. Proposition 2.12 (p. 9) uses Ehrhard concavity to show the lower deviation below the median is no larger than the upper deviation at the same distance. Example 2.14 (p. 9) shows convexity is essential for the small-deviation statement; Proposition 2.15 and Corollary 2.16 (pp. 9–10) extend the rearrangement concavity observation to suitable coordinatewise-monotone convex functions of independent chi-square variables and 1-unconditional convex functions.

Section 3 tilts any norm by `f_t(x)=||x||+t|⟨x,x*_0⟩|`, where `x*_0` is a norming functional. Lemma 3.2 shows, in particular, `Lip(f_t)=(1+t)b(X)` and for `t≥4`, `√Var(f_t(Z))≥Lip(f_t)/8` (PDF p. 11). Proposition 3.3 applies the lower-deviation theorem to this family; Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.5 use it to exhibit a 5-equivalent norm with Gaussian-scale tightness and an instability statement for random almost-spherical sections. This is a geometric application of the lower-probability result.

## Transfer boundary

The Gaussian setting, convexity, Lipschitz constant, variance, median, and distinction between lower and upper tail are essential. The refined parameter `τ` includes both over-concentration and super-concentration; neither can be dropped from Theorem 2.8 without a new argument. The cited Gaussian isoperimetric upper bound is separate from the lower-deviation results proved here. All claims and section coverage remain pending independent review.
