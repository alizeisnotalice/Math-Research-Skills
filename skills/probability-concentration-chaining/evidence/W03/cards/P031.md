## Relevant results in the selected chapter

**Theorem 2.2.6 (Hoeffding inequality for bounded independent variables, printed p. 30 / physical PDF p. 38).** If `X₁,…,X_N` are independent and `X_i∈[a_i,b_i]` almost surely, then for every `t>0`,

`P{Σ_i (X_i−E X_i) ≥ t} ≤ exp(−2t² / Σ_i (b_i−a_i)²)`.

The book presents this as an extension of the preceding Rademacher Hoeffding inequality. It explicitly points to Exercise 2.10 for the proof of the general bounded-variable extension, so the displayed statement is covered here but its proof is not supplied in the main text. The separate C168 finite-candidate card supplies a self-contained Hoeffding-lemma proof for its `[−1,1]` scores rather than relying on an unproved exercise.

The rest of Chapter 2 develops the exponential-moment method and Chernoff bounds, median-of-means, sub-Gaussian characterizations, sub-exponential variables and Bernstein inequalities. The bounded Bernstein theorem uses the variance of the sum `Σ_i E X_i²` and bounded centered independent summands; it is distinct from Hoeffding’s range-only proxy.

## Transfer boundary

Theorem 2.2.6 needs independent summands and almost-sure deterministic ranges. It is a one-sided bound; a two-sided version follows by applying it to `−X_i` and taking a union bound. The chapter’s proof of the general bounded case is left as an exercise, and the book PDF’s personal-use restriction applies to this reading artifact. All claims await independent review.
