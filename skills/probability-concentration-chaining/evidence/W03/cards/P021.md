## Gaussian comparison for maxima

Let `X,Y` be centered Gaussian vectors in `R^p`, with covariance entries `σ^X_jk, σ^Y_jk`, and `Δ=max_(j,k)|σ^X_jk−σ^Y_jk|`. The smooth maximum `F_β(z)=β^(-1)log Σ_j exp(βz_j)` obeys `0≤F_β(z)−max_j z_j≤β^(-1)log p` (PDF p. 4, equation (1)). Theorem 1 (PDF p. 4) gives for bounded-derivative `g∈C²`:

`|E g(F_β(X))−E g(F_β(Y))| ≤ (||g''||∞/2 + β||g'||∞) Δ`;

for maxima, add `2β^(-1)||g'||∞ log p` to the right. Proof: Gaussian covariance interpolation and Stein's identity bound the Hessian contraction of the smooth max.

For `p≥2` and `min_j Var(Y_j)>0`, define `a_p=E max_j(Y_j/σ_j^Y)` and `Δ` as above. Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 4–5) states

`sup_x |P(max_j X_j≤x)−P(max_j Y_j≤x)| ≤ C Δ^(1/3) [1∨a_p²∨log(1/Δ)]^(1/3) (log p)^(1/3)`,

where `C` depends only on the minimum and maximum standard deviations of `Y` and the right side is zero when `Δ=0`. Since `a_p≤√(2 log p)`, the worst-case form is `C' Δ^(1/3){1∨log(p/Δ)}^(2/3)`. The proof smooths the maximum and indicator, compares expectations, pays a Levy concentration cost for the Gaussian maximum, and optimizes the smoothing parameters (PDF pp. 13–15). The anti-concentration input is essential to the Kolmogorov bound; Theorem 1 alone is for smooth test functions.

## Anti-concentration of Gaussian maxima

For a centered Gaussian vector with coordinate standard deviations `σ_j>0`, put `σ=min_j σ_j`, `σ̄=max_j σ_j`, `a_p=E max_j(X_j/σ_j)`. Theorem 3 (PDF p. 8) bounds `L(max_j X_j,ε)=sup_x P(|max_j X_j−x|≤ε)` for `ε>0`:

- If all variances agree at `σ²`, `L≤4ε(a_p+1)/σ`.
- If `σ<σ̄`, `L≤C ε {a_p+√(1∨log(σ̄/ε))}`, where `C` depends only on `σ,σ̄`.

Corollary 1 (PDF p. 9) uses `a_p≤√(2 log p)` to give `Cε√(1∨log(p/ε))`; for equal variances the `log(p/ε)` factor can be replaced by `log p`. The comments and proof distinguish this interval concentration from the small-ball event `P(max_j |X_j|≤x)` and from upper-tail concentration. The equal-variance proof writes the maximum density as `φ(z)G(z)` with monotone `G` and combines Mills and Gaussian concentration bounds; the unequal-variance proof uses a normalized shifted vector and a separate far-tail argument (PDF pp. 15–17). The same modulus applies to maxima of absolute values by augmenting the vector with its negatives.

## Multiplier comparison and transfer boundary

Section 2.2 includes a conditional multiplier central limit theorem for maxima (Proposition 1, PDF pp. 6–8): the empirical covariance discrepancy must satisfy the stated `Δ(E max T)^2 log p=o_P(1)` and `Δ log p(1∨log log p)=o_P(1)` conditions. Read these conditions from the theorem before use; they are not implied by the covariance comparison result alone.

The comparison theorems apply to centered Gaussian vectors and entrywise covariance control, with coordinate variances of `Y` bounded away from zero for the Kolmogorov comparison. Anti-concentration requires positive coordinate variances (and the equal/unequal variance branch dictates the appropriate expression). Neither result proves Gaussian approximation for an arbitrary empirical process; a separate coupling theorem and its hypotheses are needed.
