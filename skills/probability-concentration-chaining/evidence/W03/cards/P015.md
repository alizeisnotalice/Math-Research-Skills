## Main statements and proof scope

The article first assumes a sub-Gaussian increment condition, written as (1.1): for every `s,t∈T` and `u>0`,

`P(|X_s−X_t|>u) ≤ 2 exp(−u²/d²(s,t))`.

It defines the majorizing-measure functional

`γ₂(T,d) = inf_μ sup_{t∈T} ∫₀^∞ √(log(1/μ(B(t,ε)))) dε`,

where the infimum is over probability measures on `T` and the balls use `d`.

- **Theorem 2.5 (majorizing-measure upper bound, physical PDF p. 15).** With the §2 convention on physical p. 5 that `E X_t=0` for every `t` and the essential supremum defined there, assumption (1.1) yields `E sup_{t∈T} X_t ≤ K γ₂(T,d)` for a universal constant `K`. The displayed theorem is an upper bound for centered processes with the stated increment tails; no Gaussian assumption is needed for this direction.
- **Theorem 5.1 (majorizing-measure theorem, physical PDF p. 29).** For a centered jointly Gaussian family indexed by `T`, use the canonical Gaussian metric

  `d(s,t) = (E(X_s−X_t)²)^(1/2)`

  defined in Eq. (1.2), physical PDF p. 3. With this metric the paper states

  `K⁻¹ γ₂(T,d) ≤ E sup_{t∈T} X_t ≤ K γ₂(T,d)`

  for a universal `K`. The two-sided characterization is specific to the Gaussian section; it should not be transferred as a lower bound for a general sub-Gaussian-increment process.

The text defines `E sup_{t∈T}X_t` as the supremum of expectations over finite subsets (the essential supremum) to avoid measurability problems for uncountable families; for centered processes this equals `E sup_{t∈T}(X_t−X_{t₀})` for any fixed `t₀`. Theorem 2.5 is obtained by chaining over partitions/nets, bounding the scale-wise increments with (1.1), and converting the resulting sum into the measure integral. For Theorem 5.1 the upper inequality is cited as a special case of Proposition 2.4. For the lower inequality the proof builds partitions with the partitioning scheme of §4. Its key Gaussian inputs are Sudakov minoration (Lemma 5.2, whose proof is referred to Ledoux–Talagrand, p. 83) and Gaussian concentration (Lemma 5.3, whose proof is referred to Gaussian isoperimetry or Ibragimov–Sudakov–Tsirelson). The paper proves the cluster comparison Lemma 5.4 by applying that concentration bound to local increments, integrating a union bound, and combining with Sudakov minoration. Thus the two-sided proof uses cited auxiliary results whose proofs are not reproduced in this paper. **Source cross-reference caveat:** §5 p. 29 says to equip `T` with the distance (1.1), although (1.1) is a general increment-tail condition and the canonical Gaussian metric is defined in Eq. (1.2) on p. 3. For a safe Gaussian lower-bound application, instantiate `d` by Eq. (1.2) (or keep any constant rescaling explicit and adjust the corresponding `γ₂` scale); the two-sided theorem cannot be transferred to an arbitrary larger tail-control semimetric merely because it satisfies (1.1).

## Transfer boundary

For an upper bound, establish (1.1) with the exact metric normalization and prove that `γ₂(T,d)` is finite or otherwise well-defined in the target setting. The Gaussian lower bound needs a centered jointly Gaussian family and the Gaussian comparison inputs used in the proof. This article does not compute a useful majorizing measure for an arbitrary new index set, and its cited proofs of Sudakov minoration and Gaussian concentration remain external dependencies. All claims await independent review.
