## Definitions and main concentration results

Let `X=(X_1,...,X_n)` have independent coordinates in a measurable space, and let `X'` be an iid copy. For fixed `x=(x_1,...,x_n)`, the centered conditional replacement difference is

`f_k(X)(x)=f(x_1,...,X_k,...,x_n)-E[f(x_1,...,X'_k,...,x_n)]`.

Thus `f_k(X)(x)` is a random variable as a function of `X_k`; the essential supremum in the bounds is over the random input vector `X`. The authors use the equivalent norms `||Z||_(psi_1)=sup_(r>=1)||Z||_r/r` and `||Z||_(psi_2)=sup_(r>=1)||Z||_r/sqrt(r)` (PDF p. 2).

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 3).** For `t>0`,

`P{f(X)-E f(X')>t} <= exp[-t^2/(32e ||sum_k ||f_k(X)||_(psi_2)^2||_infinity)]`.

**Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 3).** Under the same independent-input setup,

`P{f(X)-E f(X')>t} <= exp[-t^2/(4e^2 ||sum_k ||f_k(X)||_(psi_1)^2||_infinity + 2e t max_k || ||f_k(X)||_(psi_1)||_infinity)]`.

**Theorem 3.3 (PDF p. 3).** For conjugate `p,q in (1,infinity)`,

`P{f(X)-E f(X')>t} <= exp[-t^2/(2 ||sum_k ||f_k(X)||_(2p)^2||_infinity + 2e q t max_k || ||f_k(X)||_(psi_1)||_infinity)]`.

All are one-sided bounds. The first proxy uses a sum of essential-supremum-sensitive coordinate fluctuation norms; it is not generally equal to the sum of coordinatewise essential suprema. Lemma 3.4 (PDF p. 4) states that conditional expectation over an iid copy contracts `psi_alpha` for `alpha=1,2`, while centering an arbitrary variable costs at most a factor 2.

## Applications read

- Proposition 4.2 (PDF pp. 5, 14–15): norm concentration for sums of independent random elements; in a Hilbert space, iid averaging yields a simplified bound when `n>=log(1/delta)`, and a conjugate-moment alternative is also given.
- Theorem 4.3 (PDF p. 6): PCA/principal-subspace reconstruction-risk concentration in a Hilbert space under its stated iid, sample-size, and norm-moment conditions.
- Theorem 4.4 (PDF p. 7): an iid Banach-valued generalization bound for a uniformly `L`-Lipschitz class, in terms of expected Rademacher complexity plus a `psi_1` deviation term.
- Corollary 4.5 (PDF p. 8): Hilbert-space linear regression with a 1-Lipschitz loss, under the assumptions stated there.
- Theorem 4.6 (PDF p. 8): for independent `X_i~mu_i` and an `L`-Lipschitz `f` under the sum product metric, the *printed* conclusion is

  `P{f(X)-E f(X')>t} ≤ exp[-t²/(4e L² Σ_i Δ_1(X,d,mu_i)² + 2e t max_i Δ_1(X,d,mu_i))]`,

  with `X'` iid to `X`. The paper defines `Δ_α(X,d,mu)=||d(X,X')||_(psi_α)` for independent `X,X'~mu` (same page); it is not a conditional expected replacement distance. The displayed Theorem 4.6 formula is retained as a transcription of the source, not accepted here as a verified usable inequality: directly substituting the Lipschitz bound into Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 3) appears to give denominator `4e² L² Σ_i Δ_1² + 2e L t max_i Δ_1`, which disagrees in both the quadratic constant and the linear `L` factor. The paper says Theorem 4.6 follows from Theorem 3.2, but this card has not resolved whether a sharper derivation or a source typo explains the discrepancy. Do not use Theorem 4.6's exact constants/scale without checking a corrected version or proof. Independence remains required.

## Proof chain and scope

The paper uses the tensorized entropy method. Theorem 3.1 follows from the entropy decomposition and a sub-Gaussian entropy bound applied to the centered coordinate replacements. For Theorems 3.2–3.3, the authors use a fluctuation representation, bound coordinate entropies in `psi_1`/`L_(2p)`, tensorize, then optimize an exponential-moment inequality; the proof of Theorem 3.3 is in the supplement (PDF pp. 12–13). Appendix proofs for vector concentration, regression, and metric-space concentration occupy pp. 14–15. Theorem 3.1–3.3 formula displays were checked against rendered p. 3; Theorem 4.6 was re-rendered on p. 8 and its source display/derivation discrepancy is recorded above.

Transfer requires a genuine product input law and the paper’s centered coordinate-replacement quantities. Finite-wise independence, exchangeability, and a generic bounded-difference assumption do not imply these hypotheses. The conclusions are one-sided tails and do not imply interval-probability or anti-concentration bounds.
