## Self-normalized martingale theorem

Let `(X_i,F_i)` be martingale differences, `S_k=Σ_{i≤k}X_i`, square bracket `[S]_k=Σ_{i≤k}X_i²`, and predictable bracket `<S>_k=Σ_{i≤k}E(X_i²|F_{i−1})` (PDF p. 4). Assume `max{B_n^(−1),δ_n,γ_n}→0` and:

- (A1) for every `x>0`,
  `P(|<S>_n−B_n²|≥x B_n²) ≤ c exp(−x δ_n^(−2))`, with `δ_n∈(0,1/4]`;
- (A2) for some `ρ∈(0,1]`, every `i≤n`,
  `E(|X_i|^(2+ρ)|F_{i−1}) ≤ (γ_n B_n)^ρ E(X_i²|F_{i−1})`, with `γ_n∈(0,1/4]`.

Set `W_n=S_n/√[S]_n` and `γ̂_n(x,ρ)=γ_n^(ρ(2−ρ)/4)/(1+x^(ρ(2+ρ)/4))` (PDF p. 5, (2.2)). Theorem 2.1 (p. 5, (2.3)) states, uniformly for `0≤x=o(min{γ_n^(−1),δ_n^(−1)})`,

`| ln{ P(W_n≥x)/(1−Φ(x)) } | ≤ c_ρ [ x^(2+ρ)γ_n^ρ + x²δ_n + (1+x)(γ̂_n(x,ρ)+δ_n|ln δ_n|) ]`,

with the analogous negative-tail ratio `P(W_n≤−x)/Φ(−x)`. In particular the relative tail ratio is `1+o(1)` uniformly for `x=o(min{γ_n^(−ρ/(2+ρ)},δ_n^(−1/2)})` (p. 5, (2.4)). The bracket condition (A1) is concentration on a *linear relative-deviation scale* `x` with exponent `−x/δ_n²`; the alternative (A1′) in Remark 2.1 has exponent `−x²/δ_n²` and a different theorem error/range. There is no global strength ordering for the same `δ_n` and constant: on `0<x≤1`, the source notes that (A1) implies (A1′), while for `x≥1` the squared-exponent tail bound is the smaller one. Keep the conditions and their respective theorem consequences separate.

Corollary 2.1 (p. 6) gives a log-tail equivalence on the larger theorem range. Corollary 2.2 (PDF p. 6) *prints* an MDP with speed `a_n²`, rate `x²/2`, when `a_n→∞` and `a_n min{γ_n,δ_n}→0`. This condition is contradicted by an explicit sequence satisfying the displayed assumptions, detailed below, and must not be used as printed. The same page gives the uniform self-normalized Berry–Esseen bound `sup_x|P(W_n≤x)−Φ(x)|≤c_ρ(γ_n^(ρ(2−ρ)/4)+δ_n|lnδ_n|)`.

**Proof-status limit:** the 24-page paper states that the detailed proof of Theorem 2.1 is in the separate supplement [18] (PDF p. 5); that supplement is not part of this local source and was not read here. The main paper proves the applications in §§4–6, not the central theorem itself. Treat Theorem 2.1 as a statement established by a cited external proof, not as a proof reproduced in this paper card.

## R02 audit: printed Corollary 2.2 scale is invalid

The source's PDF p. 6 Corollary 2.2 was rendered and visually checked: the printed condition is `a_n min{γ_n,δ_n}→0`. The standing assumption `max{B_n^(−1),δ_n,γ_n}→0`, (A1), and (A2) do not impose a hidden relation between `γ_n` and `δ_n`. Let `X_i=R_i` be independent symmetric signs, `B_n=√n`, `γ_n=n^(−1/2)`, `δ_n=e^(−n)`, and `ρ=1` for sufficiently large `n` (finite initial terms may be set within the displayed parameter ranges). Then `<S>_n=n=B_n²` exactly, so (A1) holds with zero deviation probability, and (A2) holds with equality because `E|X_i|³=1=(γ_nB_n)E X_i²`. Also `max{B_n^(−1),δ_n,γ_n}→0`. With `a_n=n`, the printed condition is satisfied (`n e^(−n)→0`), but `W_n/a_n` is deterministically bounded in absolute value by `n^(−1/2)`. Hence `P(W_n/a_n∈(1,2))=0` eventually, contradicting the MDP lower bound `−inf_{x∈(1,2)}x²/2=−1/2`. This falsifies the printed Corollary 2.2 condition/conclusion, not the main Theorem 2.1 tail estimate.

**Safe condition derived from Theorem 2.1 (not the source's printed corollary):** the stated range at `x=a_n y` requires `a_n max{γ_n,δ_n}→0`. Under this condition, the main error divided by `a_n²` has terms of orders `(a_nγ_n)^ρ`, `δ_n`, and `a_n^(−1)(γ̂_n(a_n y,ρ)+δ_n|lnδ_n|)` (up to fixed `y` factors), all tending to zero. Subject to the main theorem and the usual two-sided tail-to-LDP argument, this gives a sufficient MDP scale. The theorem's detailed proof is in an unread supplement, so this derived implication is conditional on the main theorem; it is not presented as the printed Corollary 2.2. The exact-condition check is in `validation/P027_P028_MDP_min_condition.py` and its generated JSON.

## Applications and proof mechanisms

- **Student’s t-statistic:** the exact transform `P(T_n≥x)=P(W_n≥x√(n/(n+x²)))` (PDF p. 7) transfers the martingale self-normalized result; the source identifies the classical iid case as context, not as a proof of its martingale extension.
- **Stationary martingale differences in blocks:** partition a stationary sequence into `k=⌊n/m⌋` blocks of length `m` and let `W_n°=Σ_{j≤k}S_j°/√Σ_{j≤k}(S_j°)²` (pp. 7–9). Conditions (C1)–(C2) require exponential concentration of the conditional block variance and a conditional `(2+ρ)`-moment bound for block sums. Theorem 3.1 (p. 8) transfers the main theorem with effective bracket scale `δ_m√ln k`; Theorem 3.2 (pp. 8–9) assumes the stronger uniform approximation `||E(S_m²|F_0)/(mσ²)−1||_∞≤δ_m` for all `m`, and uses `ε_n=k^(−1/2)Σ_{j≤k}j^(−1/2)δ_{mj}`. The proof (§4, pp. 13–14) obtains a maximal inequality for block conditional variances and then applies Theorem 2.1 or Remark 2.1.
- **Branching process in iid random environment (BPRE):** under the paper’s standing supercritical assumptions `p_0(ξ_0)=0` a.s., `E[(Z_1/m_0)ln^+Z_1]<∞`, positive finite variances, and the harmonic-moment hypothesis (3.12), Theorem 3.3 (pp. 11–12) assumes `E|Z_1−m_0|^(2+ρ)+E|m_0−m|^(2+ρ)<∞`. It gives two-sided self-normalized moderate deviations for the Student statistic based on the Lotka–Nagaev ratios `Z_{k+1}/Z_k`, uniformly for `x=o(√(n/ln n))`; the relative approximation holds for `x=o(n^(ρ/(4+2ρ))`. In §5 (pp. 15–20), the proof centers those ratios into martingale differences, lower-bounds their conditional variance using environment variance, controls the sum of reciprocal population sizes through concentration for `ln Z_k` and the harmonic moment of the martingale limit, then applies Theorem 2.1 and the exact t-statistic transform.
- **Standardized BPRE estimator:** Theorem 3.4 (p. 12) assumes Bernstein moment bounds at every order `l≥2` for both `Z_1−m_0` and `m_0−m`, and gives two-sided log-relative error `O(x³/√n+(1+x)ln(n)/√n)` for `x=o(√(n/ln n))`, hence relative equivalence for `x=o(n^(1/6))`. Corollary 3.1 gives its MDP. Section 6 (pp. 20–22) derives a Bernstein condition for the same martingale differences and applies a Cramér martingale bound.

## Transfer boundary

Distinguish the *square bracket* denominator `√[S]_n` from the predictable bracket `<S>_n` in assumption (A1). Verify conditional `(2+ρ)` moments relative to conditional variances and exponential concentration of the terminal predictable bracket. This theorem is self-normalized but not assumption-free, and its main proof is in a separate supplement. The BPRE conclusions additionally require the branching and random-environment assumptions above; they do not transfer to arbitrary population processes.
