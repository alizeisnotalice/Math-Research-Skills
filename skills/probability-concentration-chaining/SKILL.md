## Use this skill when

- choosing or auditing a tail bound for sums, functions of independent inputs, martingales, or random matrices;
- bounding a supremum over a finite or separable stochastic process using metric entropy or generic chaining;
- approximating an empirical-process supremum by a Gaussian supremum;
- controlling short-interval probabilities for a Gaussian supremum or translating approximation error into coverage error;
- deciding whether a large-deviation principle or moderate-deviation approximation is actually available from the read sources.
- bounding the chance that every one of finitely many pre-specified Poisson-process witness certificates fails, where each certificate requires distinct points in labelled, possibly overlapping measurable slots.
- budgeting successful adaptive, disjoint-coordinate positive certificates for one increasing event under a finite Bernoulli product law.

## Workflow

1. **Write the event and target scale.** Specify upper/lower/two-sided tail, supremum versus one variable, fixed-time versus time-uniform control, and whether the task needs an interval probability or a small-ball estimate.
2. **State the law and dependence.** Identify independent coordinates, iid empirical sampling, exchangeability, Markov dependence, or a filtration. Never replace one with another implicitly.
3. **Choose the theorem by its hypotheses.**
   - Independent self-adjoint matrix sums: P001 has matrix Chernoff/Hoeffding/Bernstein theorems with dimension factors.
   - Independent bounded scalar sums: P005 sharpens the one-sided Hoeffding proxy only for left-skewed intervals; use its valid Eq. (23) sum bound. Its later Eq. (28)–(33), (36) displays mis-scale `S_n` by `n` after defining `\bar M_n=M_n/√n`; preserve the source issue and do not cite those as sum-tail bounds.
   - Geometrically ergodic Markov/HMM paths: P006 requires the underlying chain's inverse-mixing bound and retains the expected-bias term in its empirical-law inequalities.
   - Weighted finite exchangeable samples: P007 assumes bounded coordinates and fixed weights; nonnegative weights have a sharper Hoeffding form, and the general Bernstein form retains the finite-population correction and a random finite-vector empirical variance (not `Var(X_1)`).
   - Sampling without replacement and permutation statistics: P008 contributes only Theorem 3.3’s Bennett bound for one nonnegative statistic `f(σ)=Σ_i a_{i,σ(i)}` under a uniform permutation, with a deterministic array `a_ij∈[0,1]`, `n≥2`, and the stated modified log-Sobolev constant `ρ₀≥1/(n−1)`. Use the source’s mean and finite-array setup; the full `1/4` MGF, Proposition 4.1, and downstream claims are not admitted. This is a scoped interface limit, not a verdict that the source theorem is false.
   - Product-measure/Talagrand bounds: for P009 the admitted coefficient from the checked §4 entropy proof is `P(A) E exp(d_A²/14)≤1` for finite product spaces and nonempty `A` with positive mass. The source states coefficient `1/4` in its opening proposition; this continuation does not refute it, because the original induction proof for `1/4` was not audited. Other P009 inequalities require their own scope-register entry.
   - Adaptive positive certificates: [the disjoint-query certificate budget](references/adaptive-positive-certificates.md) gives `E N_success≤log(1/δ)` only for one fixed increasing event of failure probability `δ>0` on a finite independent Bernoulli product law. At every successful transaction, the positive answers newly queried in that transaction **alone** must force the event; every coordinate is queried at most once; all choices use only the transcript and an independent seed; and the number of transactions has a deterministic finite bound. When entering after a prior transcript, first prove that the remaining coordinates still have the relevant product conditional law and compute `δ` in that conditional experiment. This is not a prophet-inequality, stopping-rule, or correlated-input guarantee.
   - Functions of independent inputs: P003 contributes Theorems 3.2–3.3 with a product law, measurable integrable `f`, the source’s centered coordinate replacements, finite `V,M` (or `V_p,M`), and the exact denominators `4e²V+2eMt` and `2V_p+2eqMt`. The displayed entropy proof directly supports a derived `64e` constant; the printed `32e` line is not certified by that proof, and neither this discrepancy nor the separate Theorem 4.6 metric issue is a verdict that the source theorem is false.
   - Dependent product-space functions: P010 requires bounded coordinate metrics and valid Wasserstein matrices for the actual conditional-expectation kernels; alternatively its transport-information route requires conditional one-coordinate inequalities. Dependence alone does not supply these matrices.
   - Convex Gaussian lower deviations: P011 proves lower bounds on deviation probabilities when a convex Lipschitz function has variance at least a specified fraction of its squared Lipschitz constant. It is not an upper-tail inequality; preserve the median and variance-scale hypotheses.
   - Process suprema: P002 requires the stated `psi_alpha` or mixed-tail increment condition and the right semimetric(s); keep both gamma complexity and diameter terms. The cited increment controls are anchored at `t₀`: the theorem bounds `X_t−X_{t₀}` (including its individual increment term), not the raw `sup_t X_t`. A raw supremum needs a separate tail/moment bound for `X_{t₀}`, or one may adjoin an actual zero process and recompute the relevant `γ` functional and diameter.
   - Gaussian generic chaining: P015 contributes only Theorem 2.5’s finite-index sub-Gaussian generic majorizing-measure upper bound, under the source’s increment-tail and `γ₂` measure-ball hypotheses. The Gaussian two-sided lower characterization is outside this candidate’s admitted scope.
   - Time-uniform crossings: P004 requires a valid nonnegative supermartingale domination for the pair `(S,V)`, its filtration, `psi`, and initial normalizer `l_0`.
   - Finite-vector Gaussian approximation: P018 contributes only Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.1 for `n≥3` independent centered `R^p` vectors with finite absolute third moments, matching covariance Gaussian vectors, and the exact `β,δ,B₁,B₂,B₃` (or `B₄`) terms. The auxiliary independent uniform or probability-space extension used for coupling is part of the construction. This does not certify a full empirical-process coupling, VC rate, anti-concentration result, or bootstrap theorem.
   - Finite-candidate scoring for C168: for `G,H` independent standard Gaussian vectors, rewrite the formal integral as `(2π)^n E[f(G)g(H)sin⟨G,H⟩]`. If at most `M` measurable sign-pairs are fixed before an independent validation sample, the finite list has simultaneous two-sided error `(2π)^n sqrt(2 log(2M/δ)/N)` with probability at least `1−δ`. The proof and numerical check are in `evidence/W03/validation/C168_finite_candidate_holdout.md` and its JSON certificate. This scores only that finite list: it does not control all measurable partitions, global optimality, or discretization/approximation error.
   - Gaussian anti-concentration: P012 Theorem 2.1 requires a separable centered Gaussian process with unit marginal variance and finite supremum; it bounds a Levy concentration function, not an upper tail.
   - Gaussian maxima: P021 contributes the smooth covariance comparison under its entrywise `Δ` and bounded-derivative hypotheses, plus the finite-vector anti-concentration bound only on the equal-variance branch `σ²>0` (Levy width `2ε`, constant `4ε(a_p+1)/σ`). Unequal-variance, optimized Kolmogorov, and multiplier-bootstrap extensions are outside this scope.
   - Maxima with common non-Gaussian marginals: P019 contributes the arbitrary-copula interval extrema for common CDF `F`: with `u=F(x)`, `v=F(x+ε)`, and interval `(x,x+ε]`, the upper bound is `min(d(v−u),v)` and the lower bound is `max(0,1−u−d(1−v))`; pointwise extremizers are available under the source’s hypotheses. No convex-diagonal refinement or nonidentical-marginal result is admitted.
   - Continuous log-concave laws: P024 contributes Theorem 3.4’s pointwise density/mean/variance inequality for the chosen log-concave Lebesgue density, `2 Var(X)≤f(t)⁻²+(μ−t)²` (vacuous when `f(t)=0`), with the source’s asymmetric-Laplace comparison. Discrete, lattice, full Orlicz, fourth-moment, and Theorem 3.8 uniform-comparison claims are outside this scope.
   - Gaussian-polynomial small balls: P023 Theorem 1.2 is for a nonnegative degree-two polynomial of a standard Gaussian vector; require positive expectation (or exact degree two) to exclude the zero polynomial exception.
   - Gaussian order statistics/suprema: P020 contributes only the universal finite-variance concentration-function lower bound `Q(W,ε)≥ε/√(12 Var(W)+ε²)` from Remark 1, with its independent uniform-smoothing/density proof. A suspected negative-`s` sum-closure step in the source’s upper-bound proof remains an unclosed proof gap; this is not a verdict that the stated upper theorem is false.
   - Shifted squared Hilbert-Gaussian norms: P022 contributes the explicit density bound `sup_x f_{||G+h||²}(x)≤1/(2√(λ₁λ₂))` for a real separable Hilbert Gaussian with trace-class covariance and `λ₁≥λ₂>0`, for arbitrary shift `h`. This yields short-band bounds for the squared norm. Rank one and full spectral comparison are outside this scope.
   - Large deviations: P016 is a survey reference for LDP set bounds and Laplace/entropy duality. Check the model-specific theorem and topology before using any Cramér, Sanov, Markov, small-noise, or hydrodynamic rate.
   - Model-specific Berry–Esseen bound: P026 contributes the uniform-in-starting-generation annealed log-ratio rate `log(n)/√n` for the supercritical branching process in an iid random environment under its stated no-zero-offspring and integrability conditions, including the explicit imported `E W^{-a}<∞` gate. The independent smoothing proof is recorded in the card. No full Cramér expansion, generic martingale MDP, or confidence-interval theorem is admitted.
   - Stationary Gaussian high excursions: P029 contributes only a fixed-`T` local Gaussian block asymptotic (Lemma 4), including the `Var(B_H(1))=2` constant and an explicit `w`-dependent domination completion. The complete Pickands double-sum theorem is outside this admitted scope; the local statement is asymptotic as `u→∞`, not a finite-threshold inequality.
   - Gaussian fields with a maximum-variance manifold: P030 contributes only the fixed-neighborhood localization in §2, Eq. (3): a compact index set, centered a.s.-continuous field, maximum variance one, fixed open neighborhood of the maximizer set, and a uniform strict variance gap on its compact complement. Do not transfer this to shrinking neighborhoods, Pickands/manifold-stratum formulas, or finite-threshold universal relative errors.
   - Martingale Cramér deviations: P025 contributes its conditional Bernstein tilt/cumulant approximation under its deterministic bracket-tolerance conditions. P027 contributes only Lemma 4.4 tilted-moment/bracket concentration, with `0≤λ`, the stated `λε, λδ→0` regime, and the tail threshold `y≥4λδ`; the supplemental smoothing step is not included. P028 remains held in this candidate’s arXiv-v1 audit. For the journal publication and its separately identified supplement, see [P028 source versions](references/sources.md#p028-source-versions). The printed `min`-scale claims remain separate source statements and are not admitted by these scoped rows.
   - Martingale exponential crossing bounds: P034 Theorem 2.1 needs its per-step conditional exponential inequality and predictable `V` budget; Theorem 2.6 specifically assumes positive predictable `V_{i−1}`, while Corollary 2.7 assumes nonnegative predictable `U_{i−1}`. Separately, Freedman (1975), Theorem 4.1, gives the one-sided anytime bound `P(∃n:S_n≥a,V_n≤b)≤exp[−(b/c²)h(ca/b)]≤exp[−a²/(2(b+ca))]` for centered square-integrable differences with deterministic `X_i≤c`, `c>0`, and predictable conditional variance `V`; do not replace it by realized squares or unconditional variance. The two works are distinct.
   - Labelled Poisson witnesses: when a finite family of certificates is fixed before a Poisson process is sampled, use the partial-matching diagram lower-tail inequality in [Poisson witness lower tail](references/poisson-witness-lower-tail.md). It requires a finite nonatomic standard Borel intensity space, nonempty finite labelled slot lists of positive intensity, and injective point assignments. Retain repeated slot labels and witness types separately.
   - Two-sample DKW: P035 contributes the exact equal-sample reflection tail for two independent samples of size `n` from a common continuous law, plus the separately derived finite-`n` bound `2 exp(−k²/(n+1/2))` on lattice thresholds. The source’s sharp `C=2` iff `n≥458` certification and unequal-size tables are not admitted here.
   - For exact source scope, including the reviewed-only interpretation of P009/P020/P027/P028/P029/P030/P031/P034/P035, see `references/scope_register.md`. R03 accepted 29 works from the original concentration cohort and one distinct canonical Freedman (1975) use; P028 remains held. This is not a blanket approval of every theorem in any source.
4. **Keep all error and complexity terms visible.** Record variance proxy, envelope moments, gamma functional or entropy, diameter, dimension/prefactor, confidence parameter, and constants. Do not hide terms behind an unexplained `O(1)` when they control transfer.
5. **Check the transfer interface.** Show how the target model produces independent summands, coordinate replacements, process increments, a Gaussian coupling, or a nonnegative supermartingale. The cited theorem does not supply that model-specific reduction.
6. **Separate coupling and anti-concentration.** A coupling controls distance between statistics on a joint space. A continuity/Kolmogorov conclusion also needs anti-concentration or another modulus-of-continuity bound for the Gaussian target. An upper-tail inequality does not control `P(|X-t|<=epsilon)`.
7. **Separate finite-sample inequalities from asymptotics.** P016 provides LDP definitions and a survey of rate functions, but it does not supply one general theorem for all models; the specific hypotheses, speed, topology, and proof source remain necessary. Do not infer an LDP from concentration inequalities or unread entries.
8. **Record source state.** Cite the exact theorem, PDF page, version, and assumptions from the bound reader card. The source-use review is high-scoped; whole-entry scientific review, case review, and portable review are separate gates.
