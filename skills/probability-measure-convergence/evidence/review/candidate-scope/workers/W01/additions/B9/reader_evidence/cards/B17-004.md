## Main reusable result: general weak/strong representation

The model is a Polish input space `S2`, Polish output space `S1`, a fixed input law `ν`, and an arbitrary allowed set `S_{Γ,ν}` of joint probability laws on `S1 × S2` with input marginal `ν` (pp. 2–3). A solution is any pair `(X,Y)` whose joint law belongs to this set. “Strong” means there is a Borel `F:S2→S1` with `X=F(Y)` a.s. (Definition 1.2, p. 3). Pointwise uniqueness (Definition 1.4, p. 3) means any two admissible outputs on one probability space, with the same input `Y`, agree a.s.; joint uniqueness in law means `S_{Γ,ν}` contains at most one measure.

**Theorem 1.5 (p. 3; proof continues p. 4).** The following are equivalent:

1. `S_{Γ,ν}` is nonempty and pointwise uniqueness holds.
2. A strong solution exists and joint uniqueness in law holds.

The theorem does **not** assume convexity of `S_{Γ,ν}` or compatibility. In the forward proof, disintegrate each solution law given `Y` and represent its conditional output using a measurable randomizer `G(Y,ξ)` with independent uniform `ξ` (Lemma 1.3, p. 3). Couple two such realizations with independent randomizers. Pointwise uniqueness forces their outputs to agree; independence of the randomizers then makes the common output a measurable function of `Y` (the proof cites the measurable-factor argument in Kurtz 2007, Lemma A.2). Applying this to any two solution laws gives joint uniqueness. Conversely, the unique solution law is concentrated on the graph of its strong map, so any two admissible outputs with the same input coincide.

The conditional-law decomposition is explicit in Lemma 1.3 (p. 3): every `μ∈P_ν(S1×S2)` has a transition kernel `η(y,dx)`; it is strong exactly when `η(y,dx)=δ_{F(y)}(dx)` almost surely. The randomization statement uses a Borel map `G:S2×[0,1]→S1`, with independent uniform randomizer. It is a standard-Borel/Polish-space interface, not a measurable selection claim for arbitrary nonstandard spaces.

## Compatibility interface for stochastic equations

Theorem 1.5 alone does not encode adaptedness. Section 2 defines temporal compatibility for processes `X,Y` with their complete natural filtrations by
`E[h(Y) | F_t^{X,Y}] = E[h(Y) | F_t^Y]` for every bounded Borel `h` (Definition 2.1, p. 4). Lemma 2.4 (p. 5) is a sufficient condition: if each input variable decomposes into a past-measurable component and a future component independent of the joint past, then compatibility follows. It is not stated as an iff. In the independent-increment temporal setting, any reverse implication between future-increment independence and the conditional compatibility identity must be derived separately from that identity, not attributed to Lemma 2.4. Definition 2.2 (p. 5) extends this to arbitrary compatibility structures and conditional sigma-fields. Pointwise uniqueness for compatible solutions must use **joint** compatibility of both candidate solutions with `Y` (Definition 2.9, pp. 6–7); Lemmas 2.10–2.11 (pp. 7–8) justify the randomization coupling needed to transfer uniqueness.

**Important correction, Lemma 2.6 and Remark 2.7 (p. 6).** Under the filtration assumptions, full compatibility implies the martingale condition that every `F^Y`-martingale remains a martingale after joining `F^X`. The converse is **false**. The paper explicitly corrects an “equivalence” asserted casually in Kurtz (2007) and in an earlier version of this 2014 article. The martingale condition is only a partial compatibility condition (it tests a restricted family of functions); do not use it as a substitute for full conditional compatibility.

**Theorem 3.4 (p. 10).** Assume (i) pointwise uniqueness for every pair of solutions that are jointly `(C,H)`-partially compatible with `Y`, and (ii) at least one fully `C`-compatible solution exists (`S_{Γ,C,ν}≠∅`). Then there is a unique partially compatible solution, and it is strong and fully compatible. Both assumptions matter: partial uniqueness alone is not the theorem. Proof: combine the fully compatible solution with an arbitrary partial one using independent randomizers (Lemma 3.3, p. 9), obtain a jointly partially compatible pair, apply uniqueness, and identify the arbitrary law with the unique strong compatible graph law.

**Weak-limit transfer.** Lemma 3.5 (pp. 10–11) says compatibility is preserved under weak convergence when the relevant sigma-fields are generated by bounded continuous functions and the input law stays fixed. Remark 3.6 relaxes continuity to almost-everywhere continuity under the limiting marginal, which matters for evaluation maps at fixed jump times. The RC-compatibility variant in Definitions 3.7–Lemma 3.8 (pp. 11–12) handles right-continuous paths and fixed discontinuity times through `F^Y_{t+}` and `F^X_{t−}`. These details prevent an unjustified passage from weak convergence to adaptedness.

Examples 2.8 and 3.9–3.11 (pp. 6, 12–15) show stochastic integral equations, Euler approximations, BSDEs, and time changes as applications. Example 3.10 explicitly uses Meyer–Zheng/Jakubowski compactness to get a subsequential weak limit before invoking compatibility closure; those compactness assumptions and the mapping continuity hypotheses are essential. This is a method interface, not a ready-made existence proof for a queueing model.

## Proof/source boundaries and W01 use

- A conditional output kernel plus an independent uniform variable produces a weak solution from a joint law; pointwise uniqueness can then eliminate that extra randomizer. This is the source’s actual weak-to-strong mechanism.
- If applying to stochastic processes, first encode the process law and the input/output constraints on Polish path spaces, then establish the correct full or partial compatibility condition and joint uniqueness. The abstract theorem does not supply these model-specific conditions.
- W01 mapping proposals only use this as an auxiliary interface for A007/A008: for weak solution-limit arguments one must still formulate the queue/state process law, input filtration and uniqueness class. Do not infer an SDE representation or strong uniqueness for polling/queue models from this paper.
- The 2007 version’s Lemma 3.2 “iff every input martingale remains a martingale” is not a valid general compatibility interface; the 2014 correction is controlling. The older result is retained only as historical/version-family context and for the randomization lemma cited in the 2014 proof.
