## Setting and exact conditional-measure result

The paper works primarily with **completely regular Souslin spaces** (Polish spaces are a special case). This is more precise than saying simply “standard Borel”: topology is used for weak convergence and measure-space structure, while Borel/Souslin sigma-algebras control measurability. For an arbitrary parameter measurable space, the fixed projection result is stated separately in Corollary 3.6.

For a single Borel map (f:X\to Y) and a probability μ on a Souslin space, a *proper* conditional family is supported on each fiber (f^{-1}(y)), while its disintegration identity holds after integration against the pushforward. Conditional families are unique only almost everywhere in the conditioning law. Requiring fiber support at every (y) together with Borel dependence is a genuine extra constraint: the discussion on PDF p. 6 gives a necessary-and-sufficient right-inverse criterion for the single-map setting and explains why everywhere-proper Borel versions need not exist in general.

**Theorem 3.4 (PDF pp. 8–10, with the source's attainable-fiber scope made explicit).** Let (X,Y,Z) be completely regular Souslin spaces; let (f:X\times Z\to Y), (f_z(x)=f(x,z)), be Borel; and let each μ_z be a Borel probability on (X). Assume (z\mapstoμ_z\in\mathcal P(X)) is Borel, or more generally measurable from ((Z,\sigma(\mathcal S(Z)))) to ((\mathcal P(X),\mathcal B(\mathcal P(X)))). Put (\Omega_1=\{(y,z):y\in f_z(X)\}). The construction gives proper conditional probabilities μ_z^y supported on (f_z^{-1}(y)) for attainable pairs ((y,z)\in\Omega_1), with (y,z)\mapsto\mu_z^y(A) measurable with respect to (\sigma(\mathcal S(Y\times Z))) for each Borel (A\subset X). For ((y,z)\notin\Omega_1), the proof extends the family by a fixed Dirac mass (\delta_{x_0}), which is measurable but cannot be fiber-supported because the fiber is empty. The source's final p. 10 sentence claiming fiber concentration “for all y and z” conflicts with its p. 5 definition of properness and this explicit extension; we retain the attainable-fiber statement and flag that local source inconsistency rather than add surjectivity.

\[
(y,z)\longmapsto \mu_z^y(A)
\]

is measurable with respect to (\sigma(\mathcal S(Y\times Z))). In particular the measure-valued map into Π(X) is measurable for that sigma-algebra. This conclusion is **Souslin-generated measurability**, not Borel measurability in general.

**Proof mechanism actually read:** for each fixed (z), approximate the sigma-field (f_z^{-1}(\mathcal B(Y))) by increasing finite algebras and write the corresponding conditional measures explicitly as finite-partition ratios, setting zero-denominator terms to zero (PDF pp. 8–9). Their integrals are parameter-measurable by Lemma 3.1. The martingale convergence theorem gives conditional-expectation convergence for each fixed (z), but does not by itself select jointly measurable measure-valued limits. The authors embed (X) into a compact cube, use a countable determining family of polynomials to define the measurable set on which the approximate measures converge, and retain only limits concentrated on the requested fiber (p. 9). The fiber-concentration set is measurable by Lemma 3.3. On the remaining attainable fibers ((y,z)\in\Omega_1) they use a measurable selection from the Souslin graph (f_z(u)=y) and put a Dirac mass at the selected point; outside (\Omega_1) they put the fixed Dirac mass (\delta_{x_0}) (PDF pp. 9–10). Finally a countable family of bounded continuous functions determines equality almost everywhere with any ordinary conditional version, which proves the disintegration identity for each fixed (z) (p. 10). This is not a continuity theorem for (z\mapsto\mu_z^y); the all-y fiber-support line at the end of p. 10 is not compatible with the outside-image extension.

**Theorem 3.5 (PDF p. 10).** Under Theorem 3.4, if each (f_z) is a Borel surjection with a jointly Borel right inverse (g_z(y)) (or the attainable-domain graph and right inverse satisfy the stated Borel conditions), and (z\mapsto\mu_z) is Borel, the conditional family can be chosen jointly Borel in ((y,z)). A fixed Borel-surjective map with Borel right inverse and the standard projection (X_1\times X_2\to X_2) are explicit cases. **Proposition 3.10 (PDF pp. 12–13)** supplies the converse obstruction: a jointly Borel, everywhere fiber-supported family for Polish (X,Y,Z) yields a Borel right-inverse selection (g:Z\times Y\to X). Thus do not claim the joint-Borel upgrade from standard-Borel spaces alone without the selection condition.

**Corollary 3.6 (PDF p. 11).** For a fixed projection (X_1\times X_2\to X_2), if (T) is any measurable space and (t\mapsto\mu_t\in\mathcal P(X_1\times X_2)) is (T)-measurable, there is a conditional family ((t,x_2)\mapsto\mu_t^{x_2}\) measurable for (T\otimes\mathcal B(X_2)). This is a directly reusable parameterized disintegration template. Corollary 3.7 (pp. 11–12) gives a measurable gluing construction from two compatible pairwise marginals using these parameter-measurable disintegrations.

## Other main methods in the same work

- **Parameterized Kantorovich problem, Theorems 4.1–4.2 and Corollaries 4.3–4.4 (PDF pp. 13–15).** The setup preceding the theorems assumes jointly measurable (h(t,x,y)\ge0), lower semicontinuous in ((x,y)) for each (t), measurable parameterized marginals, and finite transportation values. Theorem 4.1 handles a general measurable parameter space with continuous costs: the value is measurable and an optimal-plan selection exists at the weaker σ(S(T)) measurability level. Theorem 4.2 assumes (T) Souslin and Borel parameterized marginals; it gives Borel measurability of the value and a Borel optimal-plan selection for the stated lower-semicontinuous costs. The proof chain on pp. 15–26 uses Kantorovich duality, measurable compact-support truncations (Lemma 5.2), approximation of lower-semicontinuous costs by bounded Lipschitz costs (Lemmas 5.5–5.8), measurable dense selectors for coupling sets, and a measurable optimal-plan selector (Lemma 5.9). Corollary 4.4 then disintegrates those selected optimal plans Borel-measurably in parameter and second coordinate. Do not drop finiteness, joint measurability, l.s.c./continuity, or the Souslin parameter condition when claiming Borel selection.
- **Parameterized Skorohod representation, Theorem 6.1 (PDF pp. 26–27).** For Polish (X), there is a jointly Borel map ((\mu,t)\mapsto\xi_\mu(t)) on (Π(X)\times[0,1]) with law μ under Lebesgue measure, and weak convergence of μ_n to μ is equivalent for this construction to almost-everywhere convergence of the representing maps. This is about a measurable representation of laws, not conditional convergence.

## Transfer boundary and W01 use

Concrete W01 use: selecting measurable versions of conditional laws in parameterized disintegration problems, with the exact measurable structure (Borel versus σ(S)) and fiber-support requirement checked first; secondarily, measurable optimal couplings when its parameterized OT hypotheses hold.
