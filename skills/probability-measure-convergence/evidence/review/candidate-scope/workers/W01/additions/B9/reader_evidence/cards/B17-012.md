## Main result and hypotheses

Let `X` be Polish, `Ω=X^N` for a **finite discrete horizon**, and `P(Ω)` the process laws. The path metric is built coordinatewise from a compatible metric `ρ_X`; for the weak-topology version, `ρ_X` is bounded.

For a coupling `π` of two process laws, causality means the conditional law of the target history through time `t`, given the entire source path, depends only on the source history through `t` (equation (3), p. 3). A coupling is bicausal when the same holds after swapping source and target. The paper defines `CW_p` by minimizing path `p`-cost over causal couplings, `SCW_p(μ,ν)=max(CW_p(μ,ν),CW_p(ν,μ))`, and the adapted Wasserstein `AW_p` by minimizing over bicausal couplings (equations (4)–(6), p. 3).

**Theorem 1.2 (p. 6).** If `ρ_X` is bounded and `Ω=X^N`, the topologies induced by `AW_p`, `SCW_p`, Hellwig’s information maps, Aldous’ extended weak topology, and all bounded continuous adapted optimal-stopping values are equal. “Optimal-stopping topology” means the coarsest topology making `μ↦v_L(μ)` continuous for every bounded, continuous, adapted reward process (pp. 5–6). The bounded-metric hypothesis only makes the ordinary Wasserstein and weak topologies coincide.

**Theorem 1.3 (p. 7).** For arbitrary Polish `X` and `P_p(Ω)`, the analogous `p`-Wasserstein information, extended, and stopping topologies coincide with `AW_p` and `SCW_p`; admissible stopping rewards have growth bounded by `c(1+ρ(x_0,x)^p)`. Lemma 1.4 (pp. 7, 41–42) relates bounded and unbounded versions by adding convergence of `p`th moments.

The theorem is finite-horizon and discrete-time. It is not a continuous-time, infinite-horizon, stationary-limit, or queueing theorem; a problem-specific approximation still has to prove convergence in this adapted topology and handle any horizon/tail limit.

## Proof structure and reusable interfaces

1. **Adapted Wasserstein as a nested conditional-law distance.** Section 1.8 (pp. 9–11) recursively encodes a law by the history and the conditional law of the next/future coordinates. Theorem 1.5 (p. 10) embeds `(P_p(Ω),AW_p)` isometrically into a complete nested-distribution space. The recursion explains why AW sees information available at each time, not only full-path weak convergence.
2. **Information topology versus AW.** Section 3 (pp. 16–26) factors Hellwig’s disintegration maps and the nested-law embedding through continuous integration/disintegration diagrams. Lemma 3.8 is the key continuity step: disintegration is not generally continuous under plain weak convergence, so the proof refines the domain with a compatible law. Its proof (pp. 24–26) uses the modulus-of-continuity lemma from companion reference [24]; this imported lemma is a genuine dependency, not a consequence of elementary weak convergence alone.
3. **Symmetrized causal distance.** Section 4 (pp. 26–39) rewrites the two-time causal cost using conditional laws; Jensen gives `SCW_p≤AW_p` (Lemma 4.9, p. 32), gluing gives the triangle inequality, and Theorem 4.18 (p. 36) proves `SCW_p` is a metric on the completed/nested law space. Theorem 4.23 (pp. 38–39) lifts the two-time comparison to a finite horizon.
4. **Aldous prediction laws.** Section 5, Theorem 5.3 (pp. 39–41), constructs continuous maps both ways between the joint law of the process with its measure-valued prediction process and Hellwig’s history/future conditional-law maps.
5. **Stopping-value topology.** Lemma 7.1 (pp. 42–43) couples stopping rules under a bicausal coupling to bound the value difference by expected sup reward discrepancy. In the reverse direction, Lemmas 7.3–7.4 (pp. 43–44) show suitable functionals of disintegrated conditional laws determine weak convergence. Lemma 7.5 and its proof (pp. 44–45) realize a determining functional with a two-date stopping reward, using dynamic programming. This establishes that continuity of every allowed stopping value forces convergence of the information laws.

The exact conditional laws and p-norms matter throughout. In particular, continuity of the disintegration map fails in the ordinary weak topology; the proof uses adapted/nested structure to regain it.

## Exact local verification and a positive/negative example

The saved exact-arithmetic calculation `../math/adapted_topology_finite_example.py` and its output file use `μ_n=½δ_(0,0)+½δ_(1/n,2)` and `μ=½δ_(0,0)+½δ_(0,2)` on two time points with `d(x,y)=min(1,|x-y|)`, `p=1`. Ordinary weak convergence holds because the moving atom converges. In any bicausal coupling, reverse causality forces `X_1` independent of `Y_2` since `Y_1` is constant, so with the two Bernoulli marginals the four coupling atoms all have mass `1/4`. The exact adapted cost is therefore `1/2+1/(2n)` (the script checked `n=2,…,20`, including `11/20` at `n=10`), not a quantity tending to zero.

The same laws and bounded continuous adapted rewards `L_1=1`, `L_2(x_1,x_2)=min(2,max(0,x_2))` give source-convention values `v_L=inf_{τ≤2}E[L_τ]` of `1/2` under `μ_n` and `1` under `μ`: under `μ_n`, the first observation reveals whether continuing yields 0 or 2; under `μ`, the first observation is constant and the conditional continuation mean is 1. If the same process were instead optimized with `sup_{τ≤2}E[L_τ]`, the values would be `3/2` under `μ_n` and `1` under `μ`; do not mix the two conventions. Thus weak process-law convergence alone does not ensure continuity of the stopping value. The script is finite-instance verification; the general topology equivalence remains the article’s theorem.

## W01 tool use and model boundary

- For A007/A008 the paper is, at most, an **auxiliary convergence interface**: after a model is reduced to fixed finite-horizon discrete-time process laws on a Polish state space, an adapted-Wasserstein convergence proof would imply convergence of the specified stopping values. Neither source proves such a reduction, the adapted-law convergence, continuous-time approximation, stationary/steady-state exchange, or uniform horizon-tail control for polling/queueing models.
- The paper therefore supports a precise warning and a usable criterion, not a direct solution of the queueing question. If only ordinary weak convergence is known, the finite example above shows why stopping continuity cannot be presumed.
- Do not cite Theorem 1.2 for unbounded rewards, unbounded-metric weak convergence without p-moment control, infinite horizons, or continuous-time filtration convergence. Use Theorem 1.3 only with the stated `P_p` and growth conditions.
