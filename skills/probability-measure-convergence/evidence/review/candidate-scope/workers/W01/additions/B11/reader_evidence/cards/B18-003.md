## Convergence and integrability conventions (PDF pp.2–4; printed pp.271–273)

Weak convergence is bounded-continuous-test convergence on a metric space; setwise convergence is convergence on every measurable set; total-variation convergence is the supremum over measurable h:S→[−1,1] of the absolute integral difference. A finite-measure weak limit is assumed; eventual μ_n are finite because their masses converge.

The paper distinguishes:

- uniform integrability w.r.t. μ_n: lim_{K→∞} sup_n ∫|f_n|1_{|f_n|≥K}dμ_n=0;
- asymptotic uniform integrability (a.u.i.): lim_{K→∞} limsup_{n→∞}∫|f_n|1_{|f_n|≥K}dμ_n=0.

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p.4) says a.u.i. for the whole sequence is equivalent to uniform integrability after deleting finitely many initial indices. Do not silently replace the paper’s a.u.i. assumption by uniform integrability of all indices.

## Core results and exact assumptions

**Known weak Fatou (Theorem 2.2, PDF p.4).** If μ_n⇒μ on a metric S and f_n are measurable real-valued, then the two-parameter-envelope inequality
∫ liminf_{n→∞,s′→s} f_n(s′)dμ ≤ liminf_n∫f_n dμ_n
holds if either (i) f_n^- is a.u.i. w.r.t. μ_n, or (ii) there are measurable g_n≤f_n with
-∞<∫ limsup_{n→∞,s′→s}g_n(s′)dμ ≤ liminf_n∫g_n dμ_n.
The lower envelope cannot be replaced by pointwise liminf without the paper’s further hypotheses.

**Semiconvergence (Definitions 3.1–3.4, PDF pp.5–6; printed pp.274–275).** Lower semiconvergence in μ means μ({f_n≤f−ε})→0 for each ε>0. Lower semiequicontinuity means that for every point s and ε>0 there is δ>0 such that f_n(s′)>f_n(s)−ε for every n and every s′ in B_δ(s); importantly δ is uniform over the sequence index. Equicontinuity is lower and upper semiequicontinuity together.

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp.7–8; printed pp.276–277).** At a fixed s, lower semiequicontinuity implies each f_n is l.s.c. at s and liminf_{n,s′→s}f_n(s′)=liminf_n f_n(s). Conversely, if each f_n is l.s.c. at s, this equality holds, and f_n(s) converges, then the sequence is lower semiequicontinuous at s. The convergence-at-s condition is essential; Example 3.1 shows why.

**Classical weak Fatou (Theorem 4.1, PDF pp.9–10; printed pp.278–279).** Let S be metric, μ_n⇒μ∈M(S), f_n be real-valued measurable and lower semiequicontinuous, and f measurable real-valued. If (i) f_n lower semiconverges to f in μ-measure, and (ii) either {f_n^-} is a.u.i. w.r.t. μ_n or the envelope hypothesis of Theorem 2.2(ii) holds, then
∫f dμ ≤ liminf_n∫f_n dμ_n.
The theorem is sufficient, not an iff criterion.

Proof chain (PDF p.10): choose a subsequence realizing liminf of the integrals; lower semiconvergence in measure gives a further subsequence with f≤liminf f_n μ-a.e.; Theorem 3.1(i) plus lower semiequicontinuity converts this to f≤liminf_{n,s′→s}f_n(s′); integrate and invoke Theorem 2.2 for that subsequence. Corollary 4.1 (PDF p.10, printed p.279) states the signed pointwise-liminf inequality (2.6), ∫liminf_{n→∞}f_n(s)μ(ds)≤liminf_n∫f_n(s)μ_n(ds), when μ_n⇒μ on the metric space, the real-valued sequence f_n is lower semiequicontinuous, and Theorem 4.1 condition (ii) holds: either {f_n^-} is a.u.i. w.r.t. {μ_n}, or the exact lower-envelope condition in Theorem 2.2(ii) holds. There is no nonnegative-integrand premise in this corollary; all integrals in theorem assumptions must be defined.

**Setwise counterpart (Theorem 4.2, PDF pp.11–12; printed pp.280–281).** If μ_n→μ setwise on a measurable space, f_n lower semiconverges in μ-measure to measurable real f, and f_n^- is a.u.i. w.r.t. μ_n, then the same classical inequality holds. Corollary 4.2 replaces a.u.i. by f_n≥g_n and the plain-index envelope condition
-∞<∫ limsup_n g_n dμ ≤ liminf_n∫g_n dμ_n.

**Lebesgue convergence (Corollaries 5.1–5.3, PDF pp.13–14; printed pp.282–283).** For weak convergence, if f_n is a.u.i. w.r.t. μ_n and lim_{n→∞,s′→s}f_n(s′) exists μ-a.e., then integrals converge to the integral of that two-parameter limit and to ∫lim_n f_n dμ when the pointwise limit exists. Corollary 5.2: equicontinuity, convergence in μ-measure to f, and a.u.i. suffice for ∫f_n dμ_n→∫f dμ. For setwise convergence (Corollary 5.3), convergence in μ-measure and a.u.i. suffice without a spatial equicontinuity assumption.

**Monotone convergence (Theorem 6.1, PDF pp.14–15; printed pp.283–284).** For μ_n⇒μ on metric S and pointwise nondecreasing l.s.c. real-valued f_n with f=lim_n f_n, assume f is upper semicontinuous and f_1^- and f^+ are a.u.i. w.r.t. μ_n. Then ∫f_n dμ_n→∫f dμ. Since f is also l.s.c., its assumed upper semicontinuity makes f continuous. Corollary 6.1 (PDF p.15, printed p.284) still assumes a pointwise nondecreasing sequence of measurable extended-real-valued f_n and defines f(s)=lim_n f_n(s); it also defines the lower regularizations f̲_n(s)=liminf_{s′→s}f_n(s′). Its exact sufficient conditions are: (i) f is real-valued and upper semicontinuous; (ii) f̲_n lower semiconverges to f in μ-measure; and (iii) f̲_1^- and f^+ are a.u.i. w.r.t. μ_n. It does not drop pointwise monotonicity or the definition f=lim_n f_n; it replaces the individual l.s.c. assumption by lower-regularization and measure/UI conditions. Corollary 6.2 gives the setwise pointwise-monotone version assuming f_1^- and f^+ a.u.i.

## MDP application and proof dependency (PDF pp.16–21; printed pp.285–290)

The model is a discrete-time Borel MDP: X and A are Borel subsets of Polish spaces, the one-step cost c:X×A→extended ℝ is measurable and globally bounded below (the model permits c(x,a)=+∞ to encode infeasible actions), and q is a transition kernel (PDF pp.16–17, printed pp.285–286). These are standing model assumptions, not replacements for the additional conditions. W* requires K-inf-compact cost and weakly continuous transition law; S* requires cost inf-compact in actions and setwise continuity in actions. Assumption B imposes finite infimum of average costs and pointwise bounded discounted relative values. Theorems 7.1–7.2 are cited prior ACOI results rather than independently read additional sources in this batch: W* uses u(x)=liminf_{n,y→x}u_{α_n}(y), while S* uses pointwise liminf. Corollary 7.1 makes them agree when the sequence is lower semiequicontinuous.

LEC requires α_n↑1, lower semiequicontinuity (for the W* result), existence of pointwise limits, and for every state-action pair a.u.i. of u_{α_n} under that transition law. Theorem 7.3 then gives a stationary policy satisfying the ACOE under W*+B+LEC; proof (PDF p.20, printed p.289) starts with the cited ACOI, writes the discounted optimality equation, obtains the one-sided inequality (7.9), sends n→∞ using the cost-limit relation, Fatou, and a.u.i., then combines this with ACOI to force equality. Theorem 7.4 gives the corresponding S* result with only LEC(ii),(iii), because pointwise liminf already supplies the ACOI.

## Reusable method / limitations

This source provides an exact decision tree: weak measure convergence gives the two-parameter Fatou bound by default; upgrade to pointwise/classical liminf only after verifying lower semiequicontinuity plus convergence in measure and a negative-tail control (or the exact lower-envelope alternative). For equality use both signs, which is why a.u.i. is required. In MDPs, the local a.u.i. claim is per fixed (x,a), not automatically uniform over actions or states. These convergence theorems do not prove the target model’s tightness, weak/setwise convergence, policy existence assumptions, or the a.u.i. condition.
