## Definitions and exact main theorem

For finite measures μ,ν on a measurable space (S,Σ), the paper defines

dist(μ,ν)=sup{|∫h dμ−∫h dν| : h measurable, h:S→[−1,1]}.

Thus convergence in total variation here is convergence in this bounded-test norm (PDF p.2); keep this convention explicit if comparing with the alternative convention using sup_A |μ(A)−ν(A)|.

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p.2). Assume μ_n∈M(S) and dist(μ_n,μ)→0; f∈L¹(μ); and f_n∈L¹(μ_n) for every n. Then

liminf_{n→∞} inf_{A∈Σ} (∫_A f_n dμ_n − ∫_A f dμ) ≥ 0

holds if and only if both:

(i) for every ε>0, μ({s:f_n(s)≤f(s)−ε})→0. Consequently one may choose a subsequence with liminf_k f_{n_k}≥f μ-a.e.; the full-sequence pointwise liminf is not asserted.

(ii) liminf_{K→∞} inf_{n≥1} ∫_S f_n(s)1_{ {f_n(s)≤−K} } μ_n(ds) ≥0.

Since each tail integral is nonpositive, (ii) rules out a uniformly persistent loss from far negative tails. Nonnegativity of every f_n makes (ii) automatic (Corollary 1.3). Corollary 1.6 (PDF p.4) is only for nonnegative f_n: with fixed μ, f∈L¹(μ), and each f_n∈L¹(μ) nonnegative, the uniform inequality is equivalent to condition (i). For general signed f_n,f∈L¹(μ), Corollary 1.4 (PDF p.3) requires both (i) and the uniform negative-tail condition (1.8); condition (i) alone is insufficient. Remark 1.5 (PDF p.3) gives the exact identity inf_{A∈Σ}(∫_A f_n dμ−∫_A f dμ)=−∫(f_n−f)^−dμ, so the uniform inequality in the fixed-measure setting is equivalent to ∫(f_n−f)^−dμ→0, which encodes both required clauses for signed functions.

Corollary 1.8 (PDF pp.4–5) gives the companion uniform-over-sets convergence criterion: under the same finite-measure/TV/L¹ assumptions,

sup_{A∈Σ}|∫_A f_n dμ_n−∫_A f dμ|→0

iff f_n→f in μ-measure and lim_{K→∞}sup_n∫|f_n|1_{|f_n|≥K}dμ_n→0. When μ_n=μ, Corollary 1.9 reduces this to L¹ convergence. Do not replace the TV premise by setwise convergence.

## Proof chain actually read

- Necessity of (i), Lemma 2.2 (PDF pp.6–8): if a fixed ε-drop set has μ-mass bounded below along a subsequence, total variation transfers a positive amount of that mass to μ_n. Truncate the integrable reference f outside a large-|f| set; the uniform-over-A inequality then forces an incompatible positive integral discrepancy on the remaining bounded set.
- Necessity of (ii), Lemma 2.3 (PDF pp.8–9): combine the assumed inequality on the sublevel set {f_n≤−K} with f∈L¹(μ), split according to whether f_n is within ε of f, and control the error via (i) plus absolute continuity of the integral of |f|.
- Sufficiency, Lemma 2.5 (PDF pp.9–11): for each A split into f_n≤−K tail, −K<f_n with |f_n|<K, and f_n≥K. The negative tail is controlled by (ii). On the bounded middle region, split at f−f_n=ε and use (i), then let ε↓0. On the positive tail, TV controls the bounded mass discrepancy while ∫_{f>K}f dμ→0. Send n→∞, then K→∞. Theorem 1.1 follows from Lemmas 2.1–2.5.

## Counterexample and source anomaly

Example 3.3 (PDF pp.13–14, formula (3.4)) uses Lebesgue μ on [0,1], g_n=1/2 on the first half of every dyadic interval of level n and 3/2 on the other half, μ_n=g_nμ, f_n=1/g_n, and f≡1. The measures are probability measures and converge setwise to μ, but not in TV. On every measurable A, ∫_A f_n dμ_n=μ(A) exactly. Nevertheless μ({f_n≤2/3})=1/2 for every n, so the necessity direction of the uniform criterion fails under setwise convergence. This also shows why one cannot infer convergence in measure from exact equality of all these integrals when TV is absent.

Example 3.2 (PDF pp.12–13) additionally gives signed functions with a persistent negative tail. Preserve the source display exactly: the final tail expression on PDF p.13 prints +1/2. Separately, direct substitution into the source definitions gives −1/2: for n≥2, the set where g_n=1/n has Lebesgue measure 1/2, f_n=−n there, and μ_n has density 1/n; for K>1 this entire half is in {f_n≤−K} when n≥K, while the other branch is outside the tail. Thus the displayed all-n infimum is −1/2, not +1/2. This is a localized printed sign discrepancy in Example 3.2 only; do not alter the original PDF or general theorem statements.

## Reusable method / task interface

Use the theorem when the desired conclusion is uniform over every measurable subset and total-variation convergence is genuinely available. The two clauses diagnose separate failure modes: convergence in μ-measure from below and escaping negative mass. This is sharper than ordinary Fatou and provides necessary-and-sufficient hypotheses, but is not a replacement for proving TV convergence. For merely setwise convergence retain only the paper’s stated counterexamples and do not transfer the TV equivalence.
