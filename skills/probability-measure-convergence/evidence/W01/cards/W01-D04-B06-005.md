## Main construction and proof chain

The paper develops finite, discrete “skeletons” that approximate both sample paths and the information they carry. It does not formulate a general weak-convergence or Prokhorov compactness criterion.

**Theorem 2.2.1** (§2.2, physical pp.7–9; printed pp.547–549) starts with an integrable R^d-valued X and an increasing sequence of finite partitions refining to σ(X). Set X_n=E[X|F_n], with the partition cell carrying the conditional average. Conditional expectation gives a finite-valued martingale; martingale convergence gives X_n→X a.s. and in L1. Because X is integrable, the martingale is UI. The law of X_n is the finite pushforward approximation; the paper’s remark identifies weak convergence, which also follows directly by bounded convergence under this a.s. coupling. The same section gives a grid/rectangle construction and checks finite support and nested cells.

§2.3 (physical pp.13–19; printed pp.553–559) studies equivalent changes of measure and how to keep the skeleton’s martingale-centering properties. Its conclusions require the source’s absolute-continuity and finite-integrability hypotheses.

§3 (physical pp.20–27; printed pp.560–567) defines discrete-time process skeletons and shows how selected structural properties survive the approximation. Theorem 3.2.1 (physical pp.22–23; printed pp.562–563) constructs joint process/filtration approximations from the finite-vector scheme. The skeleton paths converge a.s. at the relevant times and the generated information converges; Markov and martingale properties are preserved only under the theorem’s stated construction/hypotheses. The paper does not infer this from marginal weak convergence alone.

§4 (physical pp.28–41; printed pp.568–581) extends the construction to continuous-time adapted processes. Theorem 4.3.1 (physical p.33, printed p.573) specifically states a skeleton approximation of the pair (F^X,X), where F^X is the filtration generated by X; under the construction and source hypotheses, the skeletons approximate the path and its natural-filtration information. The scope must not be widened to an arbitrary ambient filtration F. The explicit note on physical p.41 (printed p.581) says that for F⊃F^X the constructions still approximate X pathwise, but convergence of information F^(n)↑F generally fails because the skeletons may not capture information beyond X. The special binary skeleton construction in Theorem 4.4.1 requires the stated continuity of the natural filtration together with the usual right-continuity conditions; this extra hypothesis cannot be dropped just because sample paths are continuous. §5 (physical pp.42–57; printed pp.582–597) gives Brownian-motion and continuous-martingale constructions, including an example where a surprise jump shows why filtration continuity matters.

## Erratum and limits

The official 1989 Numdam erratum was checked at https://www.numdam.org/item/SPS_1989__23__583_1.pdf. It directs readers to delete one assertion on printed p.568 that right-continuity of the natural filtration follows from sample-path continuity. The erratum says the results are unaffected because the “usual conditions” cited in the paper already assume right-continuity. The corrected distinction is retained above. The erratum was web-checked but not separately acquired/read as a local PDF.

Use Theorem 4.3.1 for the source's natural-filtration pair (F^X,X), with its continuous-time construction hypotheses. For an arbitrary larger filtration F, retain only the pathwise approximation claim; the source expressly warns that information convergence need not hold. The weak convergence of discrete laws in §2 is a consequence of an a.s. approximation, not an independent general theorem. The paper does not replace weak-convergence tightness criteria on C or D, and it does not imply convergence of arbitrary stochastic processes from finite-dimensional convergence. Formula-sensitive original pages 33 and 41 were rendered and visually checked. Review status remains pending.
