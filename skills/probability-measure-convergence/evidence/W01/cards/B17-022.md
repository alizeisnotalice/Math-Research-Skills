## Core setup and filtered martingale problem

Let (E,E_0) be complete separable metric spaces, (A:D(A)\subset B(E)\to B(E)), initial law \(\nu_0\in\mathcal P(E)\), and Borel observation map \(\gamma:E\to E_0\). A solution (Z) of the martingale problem has (Z(0)\sim\nu_0) and, for each (f\in D(A)),
\[
 f(Z_t)-\int_0^t Af(Z_s)\,ds
\]
is a martingale for a filtration with respect to which (Z) is progressively measurable (PDF p. 2). Its one-time laws \(\nu_t=\mathcal L(Z_t)\) solve the forward equation \(\nu_t f=\nu_0f+\int_0^t\nu_sAf\,ds\) (p. 2).

Set (Y_t=\gamma(Z_t)). The paper's observation filtration \(\widehat{\mathcal F}^{Y}_t\) is the completion of the sigma field generated by \(\int_0^s h(Y_u)\,du\), for (s\le t) and bounded Borel (h\) (PDF pp. 2–3). This is deliberate: no path regularity is assumed in the general setup. The conditional law \(\pi_t=\mathcal L(Z_t\mid\widehat{\mathcal F}^{Y}_t)\) admits an optional, progressive \(\mathcal P(E)\)-valued version by Appendix A.3. It satisfies
\[
 \pi_t f-\int_0^t\pi_sAf\,ds\quad\text{is an }\widehat{\mathcal F}^{Y}\text{-martingale},
 \qquad
 \int_0^t\pi_s(h\circ\gamma)\,ds=\int_0^t h(Y_s)\,ds\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
for (f\in D(A)), bounded Borel (h\), and (t\ge0) (Eqs. (1.2)–(1.3), pp. 2–3). If (Y) is càdlàg with no fixed discontinuities, \(\widehat{\mathcal F}^{Y}_t\) is the completed natural observation sigma field and the second identity can instead be written pointwise as \(\pi_t(h\circ\gamma)=h(Y_t)\) (p. 3). Do not assume this pointwise version for arbitrary measurable paths.

Definition (p. 3): a pair \((\widetilde\pi,\widetilde Y)\) solves the filtered martingale problem for \((A,\nu_0,\gamma)\) if \(\mathbb E\widetilde\pi_0=\nu_0\), the observation integral identity holds, and each \(\widetilde\pi_t f-\int_0^t\widetilde\pi_sAf\,ds\) is a martingale for \(\widehat{\mathcal F}^{\widetilde Y}\). The central uniqueness statement is Theorem 3.2, not an assumption-free filtering theorem.

## Forward-equation hypotheses and their boundaries

Section 2 supplies alternative sufficient conditions used by Theorem 3.2; they are not all required simultaneously.

- **Pre-generator definition and positive maximum principle (PDF p. 4):** write \(A_s\) for the linear span of the graph \(A\subset B(E)\times B(E)\). The paper calls \(A\) dissipative when \(\|\lambda f-g\|\ge\lambda\|f\|\) for every \((f,g)\in A_s\) and \(\lambda>0\). It calls \(A\) a pre-generator when it is dissipative and admits the following pointwise jump-kernel approximation: there are maps \(\mu_n:E\to\mathcal P(E)\), \(\lambda_n:E\to[0,\infty)\) such that for every \((f,g)\in A\) and every \(x\in E\),
  \[
  g(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_n(x)\int_E(f(y)-f(x))\,\mu_n(x,dy). \tag{2.1}
  \]
  The article explicitly does not require \(\mu_n\) or \(\lambda_n\) to be measurable. Its positive maximum principle is: if \((f,g)\in A_s\) and \(f(x_0)=\|f\|\), then \(g(x_0)\le0\) (p. 4). Under the paper's stated settings, this principle implies pre-generator status when \(E\) is compact and \(A\subset C(E)\times C(E)\); for locally compact \(E\) and \(A\subset C_0(E)\times C_0(E)\), it gives an extension to a pre-generator on the one-point compactification \(E^\Delta\). The same page gives another route: when \(A\subset\bar C(E)\times\bar C(E)\), right-continuous-at-zero forward solutions from each point imply pre-generator status. These are not an unrestricted assertion that the positive maximum principle alone gives a martingale-problem solution or uniqueness.
- **Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 5; proof p. 5):** for linear dissipative \(A\subset B(E)\times B(E)\), suppose some \(A'\subset A\) satisfies \(R(\lambda-\overline{A'})\supset D(A')\) for some \(\lambda>0\), where \(\overline{A'}\) is the operator-graph closure in the sup norm, and suppose \(D(A')\) separates points. Then the semigroup generated by \(\overline{A'}\) acts on \(L=\overline{D(A')}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}\), the sup-norm closure of the domain; for forward solutions, \(\nu_t f=\nu_0T(t)f\) on the stated domain, yielding forward-equation and martingale-problem uniqueness. This is a semigroup on \(L\), not a claim that it acts on all of \(B(E)\). The proof uses Hille–Yosida, the resolvent identity, iteration, and a law of large numbers for sums of independent exponentials.
- **Theorem 2.3 (PDF p. 5; proof pp. 5–6):** if (A) is linear and dissipative, there is a càdlàg MP solution for every \(\nu_0\), and \(R(\lambda-A)\) is bounded-pointwise dense in (B(E)) for one \(\lambda>0\), then both the MP and forward equation are unique for every initial law. The proof first represents \((\lambda-A)^{-1}h\) by the discounted occupation integral of a càdlàg solution, extends the range condition to the bp closure, and then invokes Theorem 2.1.
- **Hypothesis 2.4 and Theorem 2.6 (PDF pp. 6–7; proof is deferred to Appendix A.1, pp. 21–23):** (A\subset\bar C(E)\times\bar C(E)) is a pre-generator, its graph is bp-generated by countably many continuous-domain test pairs, and (D(A)) is closed under multiplication and separates points. Then (a) each forward solution corresponds to an MP solution; (b) a stationary forward law yields a stationary MP solution; (c) MP uniqueness at fixed \(\nu_0\) implies forward uniqueness. The proof's nontrivial construction is fully in A.1: compact-coordinate encoding, a stationary exponential-time augmentation, regeneration at zeros, and a change of measure.
- **Theorem 2.7 (PDF pp. 7–8; technical proof referred to Appendix A.2, p. 24):** for complete separable (E,F), a transition kernel \(\eta:E\to\mathcal P(F)\), and \(A_\eta f(x)=\int A^0f(x,y)\eta(x,dy)\), assume (D(A^0)) is a multiplicative algebra separating points, each fixed-(y) operator (A_y) is a pre-generator, and (A_\eta) satisfies Hypothesis 2.4. The same three conclusions as Thm. 2.6 hold. Remark 2.8 stresses (A_\eta f) need not be continuous; bounded-coefficient diffusion operators fit this representation. Do not drop the fixed-(y) or separability assumptions.
- **Theorems 2.9 and Lemma 3.1 (PDF pp. 8–11):** adjoining bounded coordinates satisfying (\dot U=-aU+b\circ\gamma(Z)), with (a\ge1), (0\le b\le1), (U_0\in[0,1]), gives a product-state generator and preserves the relevant existence/uniqueness and forward-to-martingale correspondences under the respective hypotheses. The variation in 2.9(d) handles kernel-averaged (A_\eta) without assuming \(\gamma\) continuous.

One explicit warning (PDF p. 6): uniqueness of the MP need not imply uniqueness of the forward equation without extra hypotheses. The example is reflecting Brownian motion on ([0,1]) with a restricted (C^2) test domain: it has a stationary forward solution with density (3\mathbf1_{[1/3,2/3]}) that is not the one-time law of a solution to that MP. Do not use MP uniqueness alone to claim forward uniqueness outside the stated conditions.

## Main filtered-law theorem and proof mechanism

**Theorem 3.2 (PDF pp. 12–13).** Let (A\subset B(E)\times B(E)), \(\nu_0\in\mathcal P(E)\), and Borel \(\gamma:E\to E_0\). Assume every solution of the forward equation for the augmented generator \((\widehat A,\nu_0\times\delta_0)\) corresponds to a solution of its martingale problem. The paper notes this follows under the hypotheses of Theorem 2.3, 2.6, or 2.7. Then every filtered-MP solution \((\widetilde\pi,\widetilde Y)\):

1. lifts to an MP solution (Z) for \((A,\nu_0)\) with \(\widetilde Y\) having the same law on measurable-path space (M_{E_0}[0,\infty)) as (Y=\gamma\circ Z\);
2. has a measurable filter version (H_t:M_{E_0}[0,\infty)\to\mathcal P(E)) such that \(\pi_t=H_t(Y)\) is the conditional law of (Z_t) given the observation filtration and \(\widetilde\pi_t=H_t(\widetilde Y)\) a.s.;
3. if the observation paths are càdlàg, the equality upgrades to path-law equality on (D_{E_0}[0,\infty)), and (H_t) restricts to a Borel map there;
4. if the original MP is unique for this fixed \(\nu_0\), then the filtered MP is unique in the joint finite-dimensional distributions \((\pi_{t_1},\ldots,\pi_{t_m},Y)\).

Proof chain (pp. 11–13): choose bounded continuous (b_k\) whose span is bounded-pointwise dense and rational (a_i\ge1\); augment with the countable convolution coordinates
\[
 U_{ki}(t)=\int_0^t e^{-a_i(t-s)}b_k(Y_s)\,ds.
\]
Laplace-transform uniqueness identifies the completed observation sigma field with the sigma field generated by (U(t)). Applying the filtered identities to a candidate filtered-MP solution shows the mixed law \(\widetilde\nu_t h=\mathbb E\int h(z,\widetilde U(t))\widetilde\pi_t(dz)\) solves the augmented forward equation. The stated forward-to-MP assumption supplies a genuine augmented process ((Z,U)). Equality of one-time augmented laws, disintegration through measurable maps of (U(t)), and the squared-difference calculation (Eq. (3.5), p. 13) identify the filters; the convolution map from observed paths to (U) is Borel, yielding (H_t). Finally, uniqueness of (Z)'s MP fixes the joint law. This theorem does not manufacture the forward-to-MP hypothesis or the original MP uniqueness.

**Corollaries.** Cor. 3.3 (p. 13) gives well-posedness when Theorem 2.3 applies. Cor. 3.4 (pp. 13–14) gives the same lifting under Theorem 2.6 or 2.7. Cor. 3.5 (pp. 14–15) is the Rogers–Pitman Markov-function criterion: a Borel transition kernel \(\alpha(y,dz)\) supported on the fiber \(\gamma^{-1}(y)\), with \(\nu_0=\int\alpha(y,\cdot)\mu_0(dy)\), induces a quotient generator (C) by averaging (f) and (Af) against \(\alpha\). Under the augmented forward-to-MP assumption, a quotient MP lifts and
\[
 \mathbb P\{Z_t\in\Gamma\mid\widehat{\mathcal F}^{Y}_t\}=\alpha(Y_t,\Gamma).
\]
If the original MP is unique, the quotient MP is unique (measurable-path version, or càdlàg-path version when applicable) and (Y) is Markov. Remark 3.6 (p. 14) gives the equivalent transition-kernel intertwining condition. Cor. 3.7 (pp. 15–16) extends this to restricted martingale problems with side conditions \(\mathbb E h(Z_t)=0\); its Markov conclusion additionally requires either the stated càdlàg solution condition or uniqueness for every admissible initial law \(\mu\in\mathcal P_H(E)\).

## Applications and appendix interfaces

- **§4.1 / Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 16–17):** quotient by the uniform permutation kernel turns uniqueness for an (n)-particle generator into uniqueness of the empirical-measure/quotient MP and conditional exchangeability. The concrete independent-motion/resampling example requires the base particle MP to be well posed.
- **§4.2 (PDF p. 18):** exchangeable infinite particle systems, de Finetti empirical measures, and the Fleming–Viot measure-valued generator; relies on well-posedness of the particle MP and exchangeable initial data.
- **§4.3 / Theorem 4.2 (PDF pp. 19–20):** in stationary (M/M/1), λ<μ and geometric stationary queue length imply a Poisson departure process of rate λ and independence of (Q(t)) from past departures. The extension to pure-jump functionals needs the explicit intensity identity (4.6), not just stationarity.
- **§4.4 (PDF pp. 20–21):** a single reflected Brownian service station with
  \(X(t)=X(0)+\sum_{i=1}^m a_iW_i(t)-bt+\Lambda(t)\),
  \(Y(t)=Y(0)+CW(t)+c\Lambda(t)\), and \(D=CC^T\) (p. 20). Let \(S=\sum_i a_i^2\); the stationary exponential law requires \(b>0\) and \(S>0\), so \(\beta=2b/S>0\) and \(\pi_0(dx)=\beta e^{-\beta x}dx\) (p. 21). In the boundary operator \(Bf=f_x+\sum_j c_j f_{y_j}\), the stated cancellation condition is, componentwise,
  \[
  c_j=\frac{2\sum_i c_{ji}a_i}{\sum_i a_i^2}\quad(j=1,\ldots,d),
  \]
  where \(C=(c_{ji})\) (p. 21). This makes the boundary term in (4.7) vanish for the paper's domain \(D(A)=\{f\in C_c^2(\mathbb R^{d+1}):Bf=0\}\). The observed generator is
  \[
  A_0f(y)=\tfrac12\sum_{j,k}d_{jk}f_{y_jy_k}(y)+\sum_j\Bigl(\alpha\sum_i c_{ji}a_i\Bigr)f_{y_j}(y),
  \]
  The source prints \(\alpha\) in this displayed definition of \(A_0\), although the preceding paragraph uses \(\alpha(y,\cdot)=\pi_0\times\delta_y\) for the averaging kernel; retain the printed symbol and flag the notation as unresolved. Separately, the preceding calculation (4.7), after its boundary term vanishes, gives the cross-drift coefficient \(\beta\sum_i c_{ji}a_i\); this is a coefficient derived from (4.7), not a silent correction of the printed \(A_0\). The stated Brownian/output-independence conclusion is for the fixed initial law \((X(0),Y(0))\sim\pi_0\times\delta_0\) (equivalently \(X(0)\sim\pi_0\) and \(Y(0)=0\)), not arbitrary initial data (p. 21). This is a model-specific calculation, not a general queueing theorem.
- **Appendix A.1 (PDF pp. 21–23):** proof of Theorem 2.6: countable algebra coordinates embed (E) into compact \hat E; Lemma A.1 uniquely transfers a law through those coordinates; Lemma A.2 is the convex chain-rule inequality for a pre-generator; exponentially weighted occupation measures and regeneration realize forward solutions as MP marginals.
- **Appendix A.2 (PDF p. 24):** proof interface for Theorem 2.7 by adding a kernel coordinate and applying controlled-MP existence results; technical details are referred to earlier papers rather than expanded further here.
- **Appendix A.3 / Theorem A.3, Cor. A.4 (PDF pp. 24–25):** optional projection of measurable probability-valued processes onto a complete filtration, valid at every a.s.-finite stopping time without right-continuity of the filtration; càdlàg projection follows if the filtration is right-continuous and the input process càdlàg. Proof uses finite sigma fields from a countable base, tightness, and a monotone-class argument.
- **Appendix A.4 / Theorem A.5 (PDF pp. 25–27):** uniqueness of an MP at fixed initial law implies the Markov property for the enlarged filtration generated by process values and bounded path integrals. The proof uses a bimeasure extension to paste two copies at time (r), verifies the pasted process still solves the MP, applies uniqueness to its finite-dimensional distributions, and identifies the enlarged-filtration conditional expectation. Remark A.6 reduces this filtration to the natural one for càdlàg paths (or progressive processes).
- **References (PDF pp. 28–29):** both pages read; not counted as separate full-text sources.

## W01/A007–A008 use and limits

Use as an **auxiliary conditional-law template only** if the queueing model can first be encoded as a Polish-state Markov martingale problem with a Borel observation map and the augmented forward-to-MP and relevant uniqueness hypotheses are independently proved. For A007, this does not give the renewal-age augmentation, stationary-law existence, a large-switch-over fluid limit, uniform tail control, or the exchange of steady-state and switch-over limits. For A008, it does not give the embedded-chain representation, a necessary-and-sufficient stability region, or renewal-input stability criteria. Finite-state calculations do not supply those interfaces. The paper is not Kurtz 2007/2014 compatibility theory and must not be used to infer their compatibility lemmas automatically.
