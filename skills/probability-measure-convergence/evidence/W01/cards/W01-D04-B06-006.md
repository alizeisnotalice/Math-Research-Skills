## Theorem 1 and proof mechanism

Let X^n be special semimartingales in H1 with canonical decompositions X^n=X^n_0+M^n+A^n. For one constant K, the hypotheses are
E[∫_0^∞ |dA^n_s|]≤K,   E[(M^n)*]≤K,
and E[(X^n−X)*]→0.
Here star denotes path supremum, M^n is the local martingale component and A^n the predictable finite-variation component. Theorem 1 (physical p.3, printed p.189) concludes X∈H1 is a special semimartingale with canonical decomposition X=X_0+M+A, E[M*]≤K and E[∫|dA_s|]≤K, and
||M^n−M||_{H1}→0,   E[(A^n−A)*]→0.
This is strong expected-uniform convergence; it is not merely weak convergence of process laws.

The proof (physical pp.5–6, printed pp.191–192) first takes an a.s.-uniformly convergent subsequence, yielding càdlàg X. For elementary predictable H, convergence of the H-integrals and the uniform variation/martingale bounds give the quasimartingale criterion; Bichteler–Dellacherie then gives a special semimartingale decomposition. Localization of the local martingale and the same bounds give expected total variation control for A and an H1 bound for M. Writing Y^n=X^n−X, N^n=M^n−M, B^n=A^n−A, integration by parts controls [Y^n] by terms involving Y^n against N^n and A^n; Proposition 3 supplies the martingale-transform maximal inequality for p>1/2, and Cauchy–Schwarz makes these terms vanish. The cited Dellacherie–Meyer quasimartingale result then gives H1 convergence of martingale parts and expected uniform convergence of finite-variation parts. The argument retains the predictable canonical-decomposition requirement.

Corollary 2 (physical pp.3–4) gives the special-semimartingale closure conclusion when the variation bound holds and the whole sequence converges in expected path-supremum norm. Proposition 3 (physical pp.4–5) states, for p>1/2, an Lp bound on the maximal stochastic integral in terms of the L2p norms of K* and M*; the paper explicitly warns it is not generally true for 0<p≤1/2.

## Use and limits

This is an adjacent, specialized stability result for semimartingale decompositions under expected uniform convergence and uniform H1 component bounds. It cannot establish general weak convergence, tightness, or convergence of arbitrary laws. Before using it, verify the common filtered probability space, canonical decompositions, expected variation and martingale-maximal bounds, and the strong E sup-norm convergence. Theorem 1’s consequences concern stability of stochastic integrals/decompositions only under those conditions. Review status is pending.
