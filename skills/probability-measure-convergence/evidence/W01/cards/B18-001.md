## Exact result

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p.2; proof pp.2–3). Let S be an arbitrary metric space, let probability measures μ_n converge weakly to a probability μ, and let f_n:S→[0,+∞] be measurable. Then

∫_S liminf_{n→∞, s′→s} f_n(s′) μ(ds) ≤ liminf_{n→∞} ∫_S f_n(s) μ_n(ds).

The left side uses the joint lower envelope in the sequence index and nearby spatial argument. It is not the pointwise expression ∫ liminf_n f_n(s) μ(ds). The counterexample on PDF p.2 takes S=[0,1], μ_n=δ_{1/n}, μ=δ_0, and f_n=f=1_{ {0} }; then the pointwise left integral is 1 and every right integral is 0. Conversely the two-parameter lower envelope is 0 at s=0, so Theorem 1.1 remains consistent.

For a fixed nonnegative lower-semicontinuous f, Theorem 1.1 gives the usual Portmanteau inequality ∫f dμ≤liminf_n∫f dμ_n (PDF p.2). No Polishness, tightness, or compactness is assumed beyond S being metric and the measures being probabilities.

## Proof chain actually read

1. First assume f_n≤K uniformly. Set the tail infimum F_n(s)=inf_{m≥n}f_m(s), then take its lower-semicontinuous regularization \underline{F}_n(s)=liminf_{s′→s}F_n(s′). It is \underline{F}_n, not the unregularized tail infimum F_n, that is l.s.c.; the source states \underline{F}_n↑liminf_{n→∞,s′→s}f_n(s′) (PDF p.3, (2.1)).
2. Monotone convergence moves the joint lower-envelope integral to lim_n∫\underline{F}_n dμ.
3. Portmanteau applied to each nonnegative l.s.c. \underline{F}_n gives ∫\underline{F}_n dμ≤liminf_m∫\underline{F}_n dμ_m. The pointwise inequalities \underline{F}_n≤F_n≤f_n and monotonicity \underline{F}_n↑ close the estimate by liminf_m∫f_m dμ_m (PDF p.3, (2.2)–(2.4)).
4. For general nonnegative extended-real f_n, truncate f_n^λ=min(f_n,λ). Apply the bounded-above case and then Fatou/monotone limiting in λ↑∞ (PDF p.3). This proves Theorem 1.1.

## Signed-function interface and boundary

Theorem 4.3 (PDF pp.5–6) extends the inequality to measurable real-valued f_n when all displayed integrals are defined and there are measurable g_n≤f_n such that

-∞ < ∫_S limsup_{n→∞,s′→s} g_n(s′) μ(ds) ≤ liminf_n ∫_S g_n(s) μ_n(ds).

The proof sets h_n=f_n−g_n≥0, applies Theorem 1.1 to h_n, and combines the two envelope bounds. Corollary 4.7 (PDF p.7, (4.18)) is a convenient special case: a common bounded-above measurable g≤f_n and -∞<∫\overline{g} dμ=lim_n∫g dμ_n suffice, where \overline{g}(s)=limsup_{s′→s}g(s′) is the upper-semicontinuous regularization defined on PDF p.2. Only when g is upper semicontinuous (Remark 4.8, PDF p.7) may \overline{g} be replaced by g. A common finite lower bound works by taking g constant. These hypotheses are essential: Example 3.1 (PDF p.4) has setwise-convergent probabilities on (0,1], continuous f(s)=−1/s with each integral defined, but ∫f dμ=−2ln2 while ∫f dμ_n→−∞.

Do not infer that weak convergence alone yields the classical pointwise-liminf Fatou inequality for varying integrands. Do not apply the signed result without a verified lower-envelope g_n and finite lower integral. The note also does not provide a converse to Theorem 1.1.

## Reusable method / task interface

When probability laws μ_n converge weakly but integrands vary, use the joint lower envelope first. To replace it by a pointwise lower limit, prove an additional condition on the integrands; this paper itself points to lower semicontinuity for the fixed-integrand Portmanteau case. For signed costs, explicitly construct g_n≤f_n and check both sides of the envelope inequality. This is a convergence-integral lemma only: it does not establish tightness, identify a weak limit, or prove uniform integrability for a target stochastic model.
