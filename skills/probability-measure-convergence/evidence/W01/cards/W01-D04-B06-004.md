## Main finite-rank conditioning theorem

Theorem 6 (§3, physical pp.10–11; full proof in Appendix B, pp.18–24) assumes real separable Banach spaces X,Y, a Gaussian Borel law μ on X with mean m_μ and covariance C_μ:X*→X, and a bounded linear G:X→Y. Its pushed covariance C_ν=G C_μ G*:Y*→Y must have finite rank p. For any C_ν-representing sequence y_i* (orthonormalizing the covariance directions), the conditional mean and covariance are
m̃_μ(y)=m_μ+Σ_{i=1}^p ⟨y−Gm_μ,y_i*⟩ C_μG*y_i*,
C̃_μ=C_μ−Σ_{i=1}^p ⟨C_μG*y_i*,·⟩ C_μG*y_i*.
The construction gives a Gaussian disintegration; the mean satisfies Gm̃_μ(y)=y on the covariance-support affine range Y0=Gm_μ+C_ν(Y*). The finite-rank formulas depend on the chosen representative sequence only through its covariance projection, as the proof verifies.

Theorem 7 (§3, physical p.12; proof Appendix B) assumes real separable Banach X,Y1,Y2, a Gaussian μ, bounded linear G1,G2, and each pushed covariance C_ν1,C_ν2 finite rank. Disintegration first under G1 and then under G2 agrees with disintegration under the bundled map (G1,G2) for almost every pair with respect to the joint pushforward μ∘(G1,G2)^{-1}. This is an almost-everywhere equality, not a statement for every data pair.

Theorem 8 (physical p.13; proof pp.23–24) claims an infinite-rank disintegration on a full-ν-measure data subspace. Its covariance-series and conditional-mean convergence argument on physical p.23 is distinct from the p.24 step used to show the data reconstruction set Y3 has full measure. For that latter step, the paper proves the defining series reconstructs y−Gm_μ when y−Gm_μ lies in the Cameron–Martin range C_ν(Y*), then invokes density of that range's closure in full measure and concludes that some full-measure subset satisfies the same series property. Density/closure approximation alone does not imply convergence of the particular prescribed partial sums defining Y3; the proof does not establish that implication. Isolate this proof step as unresolved, and do not use the infinite-rank Theorem 8 conclusion as verified W01 evidence unless the step is independently supplied. This is not a counterexample to Theorem 8 and is not a claim that the theorem is false. Theorem 9 inherits the infinite-rank scope and should not be invoked on that unverified basis.

## Source-internal formula conflict

Theorem 6’s mean in equation (3.1) has the standard plus sign. Corollary 5 (physical p.11, equations (3.3)–(3.6)) prints
m̃_s(y)=m_s−K_sG K_GG^{-1}(y−Gm·),
while defining K_sG and K_GG as the ordinary covariance cross-vector and covariance matrix. Under the scalar check Z~N(0,1), GZ=Z, y=1, the conditional mean must be 1; Theorem 6 gives +1 while equation (3.3) gives −1. The source therefore has an internal sign inconsistency in the printed Corollary 5 mean. W01 does not use that minus-sign mean formula. Its covariance update (3.4) has the usual subtraction form and matches the finite-rank covariance expression in Theorem 6. The exact rational diagnostic is in workers/W01/math_checks/travelletti_corollary5_sign.py and its output; it is a one-dimensional formula-consistency check, not a proof of the general theorem.

## Use and limits

This is a specialized Gaussian conditioning source. Always check separable Banach support, Gaussian law, bounded linear observation operators, and finite-rank pushed covariance for Theorems 6–7; their finite-rank formulas remain the verified usable part of this card, subject to the separate Corollary 5 sign erratum above. The infinite-rank proof has an unresolved density-to-series-convergence step on physical p.24, so do not invoke Theorems 8–9 as verified results from this card. The paper is not an arbitrary regular-conditional-distribution theorem, weak compactness result, or fixed-point theorem. In particular, it supplies no continuity, contraction, or convergence theorem for a nonlinear map on probability laws. Formula-sensitive pp.10–13 and 24 were rendered and visually checked.
