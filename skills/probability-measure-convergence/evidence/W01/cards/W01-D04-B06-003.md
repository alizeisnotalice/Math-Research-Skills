# LaGatta–Hahn (2014), A Structural Approach to Coordinate-Free Statistics

**Source identity.** Tom LaGatta and P. Richard Hahn, arXiv:1405.0110v2 (5 May 2014), 31-page preprint. W01 read every physical page, SHA-256 01bea4dd10b4de38ea9aed603bef3ea7b2bd810406d089a938d6cb2719317d2a. Metadata and abstract checked against the official arXiv record.

## Actual assumptions

The framework in §2.1 uses complete Hausdorff topological vector spaces V whose continuous dual separates points, Radon probability laws with separable support, and finite variance for every continuous linear functional. A continuously parameterized family must have continuous mean and covariance maps. The finite-variance assumption is explicit; the authors warn that heavy-tailed infinite-variance settings are not covered by their covariance-operator argument.

## Main disintegration results and proof route

In §2 (physical pp.4–9), covariance factors through a Hilbert space H_θ and an embedded Cameron–Martin space U_θ. Lemma 2.2 (three-space diagram, p.6) writes k_θ=ι_θι*_θ. Theorem 2.5 (pp.8–9) proves that the restricted observation map Υ between the lifted Cameron–Martin subspaces is a Hilbert-space isomorphism with continuous inverse in the corresponding Hilbert topologies. The Hilbert-topology inverse is also continuous into V when V is given its ambient topology through the continuous inclusion; what does not follow is continuity when the observation-side Cameron–Martin range is instead given the weaker ambient subspace topology inherited from Y. The next paragraph on physical p.9 asserts that the Banach-space ambient-continuity condition from LaGatta is “always” satisfied and derives Corollary 2.6 (all Gaussian measures admit continuous disintegrations through continuous linear maps). That inference changes the topology on the inverse's domain and is invalid in general.

An exact audit example is X=ℓ² with independent coordinates X_n=2^{-n}Z_n, Z_n standard normal, and η=diag(1/n). Both X and ηX have full-support Radon Gaussian laws and finite variance under every continuous linear functional; η is continuous and injective. In the observation-side Cameron–Martin range equipped with its ambient ℓ² subspace topology, y_n=e_n/n→0 while η^{-1}y_n=e_n does not converge to 0. Thus the inverse is not continuous for that ambient subspace topology; this does not contradict its continuity in the Cameron–Martin Hilbert topology. More strongly, since the marginal law of ηX has full support, a closed full-measure continuity set must be all ℓ²; injectivity forces the conditional kernel to be δ_{η^{-1}y} on a full-measure set, which cannot have a weakly continuous version (use the bounded continuous test x↦min(1,||x||)). The rigorous argument and exact finite-coordinate checks are in the read-only shared audit `catalogue/integration_math_checks/compact_observation_disintegration.md` and `.json`; the finite checks support, but do not replace, its infinite-dimensional proof. Corollary 2.6 is therefore excluded as a general theorem. This does not invalidate Theorem 2.5's Hilbert-space isomorphism.

Part 2 treats the statistics applications:
- §§3–4 (pp.9–11): ordinary least squares and the generalized Gauss–Markov property, derived from the geometry of the lifted covariance spaces.
- §5 (pp.11–15): a stochastic OLS estimator formed by adding independent residual noise to an OLS estimator. Theorem 5.6 gives a disintegration for the resulting convolution law when the OLS estimator used in the construction is continuous as required there. Theorem 5.7 characterizes equality with the original model law under the paper’s Υ-relative “uncorrelated implies independent” (UII) property; Appendix B.7 uses independence of orthogonal covariance components and Fubini. Gaussianity supplies UII, but does not by itself establish ambient-topology continuity of an OLS inverse/estimator. Any continuity-dependent construction or corollary must therefore separately assume or prove an ambient-continuous estimator (for example via a bounded ambient inverse); Theorem 2.5’s Hilbert-topology inverse is not sufficient for this inference. The equivalence is not generalized here to non-UII laws.
- §6 (pp.15–21): covariance tensors, random arrays, and kriging; the arguments retain their C(I,V) path and covariance-kernel assumptions.
- §7 (pp.21–24): a support-vector-machine construction in the same abstract statistical setting.

The statistical applications show where continuous disintegration enters an estimator construction; they do not relax the model’s finite-variance or topology assumptions.

## Use and limits

Use Theorem 2.5 only as a Hilbert-topology isomorphism on the Cameron–Martin subspaces. Continuity into V follows when the domain carries the Cameron–Martin topology and the inclusion is continuous; the unsupported step is continuity of the inverse from the observation-side range with the weaker ambient subspace topology. Do not cite Corollary 2.6 as establishing generic ambient-topology continuous disintegration: the ℓ² compact-injection example above shows the blanket conclusion fails. In §§3–5 and conclusions depending on OLS continuity, require a separately supplied/proved ambient-continuous estimator or bounded ambient inverse; Gaussianity and UII do not automatically supply it. The paper’s estimator/statistical sections are not direct weak-convergence criteria or generic conditional-kernel theorems. Review status is pending.
