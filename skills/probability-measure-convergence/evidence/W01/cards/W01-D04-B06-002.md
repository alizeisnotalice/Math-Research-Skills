## Results and assumptions

Definition 1.1 (physical pp.1–2) defines a disintegration of a Radon probability P on a complete metric space X through a measurable η:X→Y, with each P_y a probability, y↦P_y(B) measurable, fiber support for P_Y-a.e. y, and the averaging identity. “Continuous” means weak continuity y_n→y on a closed full-P_Y-measure subset Y0.

**Theorem 1 (physical p.1):** For a mean-zero, almost-surely continuous Gaussian process ξ on [0,T] with covariance c, and S≤T, if
M = sup_{s≤S} [sup_{t≤T}|c(s,t)| / sup_{s'≤S}|c(s,s')|] < ∞,
then there is a closed full-measure set Y0⊂C([0,S]) on which the regular conditional Gaussian laws given ξ|[0,S]=y are weakly continuous. A stationary process has M=1. A nonzero mean is handled by centering. The source states this as a process-continuity result, but this card has not independently verified the step from its scalar covariance ratio to a Banach-space operator bound; do not use Theorem 1 as that bridge without a separate argument.

**Theorem 2 (physical pp.5–6):** For Banach spaces X,Y, a continuous linear η:X→Y, and a mean-zero Radon Gaussian P on X with covariance K, assume
M = sup_{e∈Y*, e(ηKη*e)≠0} ||Kη*e||_X / ||ηKη*e||_Y < ∞.
Then η^{-1} on the observed covariance range extends continuously to its norm closure, which is the appropriate domain Y0=overline(ηKη*Y*) for m:Y0→X. The source explicitly defines the extension domain on physical p.5 as the closure of the observed covariance range; the earlier W01 transcription dropped the overline. Set K̂=K−Kη*m*. For y∈Y0 the Gaussian P_y with mean m(y) and covariance K̂ is a continuous disintegration. Its conditional mean is a right inverse on Y0; residual covariance is supported in ker η. The operative hypothesis is the Banach-norm/operator bound (2.19), not a coordinatewise covariance-ratio surrogate.

## Proof chain of the sufficient theorem

Lemmas 2.2–2.3 (physical pp.4–5) identify the covariance Cameron–Martin Hilbert space H, embed it into X, and show the residual covariance K̂=ιπ⊥ι* is positive and no larger than K; here π projects onto the closed subspace generated by η*Y*. The finite M bound is exactly continuity of the inverse map on the observed Cameron–Martin range, so the extension m is continuous and ηm(y)=y.

The proof of Theorem 2 (physical pp.6–7) splits the canonical centered Gaussian cylindrical measure on H along orthogonal subspaces H_Y and H_Y⊥. Orthogonal Gaussian coordinates are independent, giving the product/convolution identity. Pushing the two factors to X and Y yields the Gaussian conditional kernel and the disintegration equation. The identity ηm=id on Y0 and K̂X*⊂kerη give fiber support. For y_n→y, continuity of m gives m(y_n)→m(y); for each bounded continuous f, apply bounded convergence to ∫f(m(y_n)+x)P_0(dx), obtaining weak convergence. This proof establishes the sufficient result without a finite-dimensional approximation claim.

**Theorem 3 (physical pp.7–8):** The paper claims necessity of M<∞ for continuous disintegration on any closed full-measure Y0. It invokes Tarieladze–Vakhania Theorem 3.11 for a Gaussian disintegration and full-measure linear subspace Y1, uses uniqueness for a full-measure set Y2, and defines Y′=overline(Y0∩Y1∩Y2) on p.7. The p.8 contradiction uses the open-set Portmanteau lower bound on the 1/2-neighborhood of K̂X*. However, the p.8 displayed chain changes an X-norm of ιh to the H-norm of h, including the step ||m(y)||_X²=||ι*η*g||_H². The Cameron–Martin embedding ι:H→X is not generally an isometry in the ambient Banach norm, so this Pythagorean step is not justified. In addition, Tarieladze–Vakhania Theorem 3.11 and the cited uniqueness result were not independently checked here. Mark the necessity proof as unverified with this specific invalid general norm identity; do not use Theorem 3 as W01 evidence. This finding does not assert that the theorem is false.

**Theorem 4 (physical pp.8–9):** The source claims a compact-index Gaussian-field extension and, in (2.32), bounds its conditional mean extension by M times the observed-path sup norm, where M is the coordinatewise covariance ratio (2.29). That scalar ratio is not the operator-norm hypothesis (2.19), and the identification made in the p.9 proof fails. An exact finite Gaussian field on T={1,2,3}, S={1,2}, X=(U,U+εV,V), with independent standard U,V and 0<ε<1, has the source's scalar M=1, but at observed path y=(0,ε) its conditional mean is (0,ε,1); hence ||m(y)||∞=1>M||y||∞=ε. The exact SymPy regression calculation is in `workers/W01/math_checks/lagatta_gaussian_bound_audit.py` and `.txt`; it verifies this finite-dimensional formula, not a general theorem. Finite-dimensional Gaussian conditioning still exists. Do not use (2.32) or the claimed scalar-to-operator-bound step; the generic continuity conclusion of Theorem 4 is not established by this proof as written.

## Use and limits

For a conditional law given an observed path segment, Theorem 1 is a source-stated Gaussian-specific process-continuity result, not a generic regular conditional probability theorem. Its scalar covariance ratio has not here been bridged to the operator-norm condition, so it is not operational evidence for that Banach-space step. For abstract Banach Gaussian conditioning, Theorem 2 supplies a usable sufficient criterion when the actual Banach-norm ratio (2.19) is finite, with the extension taken on the closed covariance-range closure. Do not replace that bound by Theorem 1 or Theorem 4 scalar ratios. Theorem 3 is not used because its necessity proof contains an unjustified norm identification; Theorem 4 is not used as a general result because the scalar-to-operator bound fails in the displayed finite Gaussian example. Formula-sensitive physical pp.5, 8 and 9 were rendered and checked. Review status is pending.
