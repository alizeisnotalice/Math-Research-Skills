## Conditional-kernel conventions

Section 3 distinguishes (i) a regular conditional kernel for a measure ν and measurable map τ, and (ii) a product regular conditional kernel for a law μ on (X\times Y) and projection π. They are related by constructing the product law μ-tilde with rectangle masses \(\tilde\mu(A\times B)=\nu(A\cap\tau^{-1}(B))\) (Theorem 3.6, PDF p. 9). Section 2 defines \(\mu(A\mid B)\) only when the conditioning event has positive probability. This convention matters in the neighborhood-ratio criteria below.

The paper’s conditioning variables are **kernel versions**. A regular conditional probability is only determined almost everywhere in the conditioning marginal; Theorem 4.3 (PDF pp. 10–11) shows weak continuity on the support makes it unique on that support. Outside that support its values remain arbitrary (Remark 4.4, pp. 10–11). For metric (X) and first-countable (Y), the Portmanteau-style open-set lower/closed-set upper conditions are equivalent to weak continuity (Theorem 4.10, p. 12); Lemmas 4.11–4.12 (pp. 13–14) bound conditional probabilities on a neighborhood by the extrema of the kernel on the conditioning support and pass to local limits. These facts are not consequences of joint weak convergence alone.

## Main exact result: product regular conditional probabilities

**Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 4–5; printed pp. 1061–1062).** Let (X,Y) be metric spaces and π(x,y)=y. Suppose probability measures μ_n on (\mathcal B(X)\otimes\mathcal B(Y)) satisfy an LDP with good rate (J:X\times Y\to[0,\infty]). For each (n), assume a product regular conditional probability η_n under μ_n exists and is weakly continuous on the nonempty support of the second marginal. Fix (y) with \(\inf_x J(x,y)<\infty\), and put

\[
I(x)=J(x,y)-\inf_{u\in X}J(u,y).
\]

Then (I) has compact sublevel sets and each η_n is unique on the support. The conditional kernels η_n(y_n,\cdot)\) satisfy the LDP lower bound with rate (I) for **every** sequence (y_n\to y) that lies in the second-marginal support for all sufficiently large (n) iff the local lower condition (A2) holds. They satisfy the LDP upper bound for every such sequence iff (B2) holds. The source conditions are:

- for every (x\in X,r>0), (U=B(x,r)), the supremum over ε>0 of the liminf over (n) and infimum over small conditioning balls (B(z,\delta)\subset B(y,\varepsilon)) of
  \(n^{-1}\log\mu_n(\overline U\times Y\mid X\times B(z,\delta))\)
  is at least \(-\inf I(U)\) (Eq. (1.5), PDF p. 4);
- for every finite family of balls, with (W=X\setminus\bigcup_i B(x_i,r_i)), the infimum over ε>0 of the limsup over (n) and supremum over those small conditioning balls of
  \(n^{-1}\log\mu_n(W^\circ\times Y\mid X\times B(z,\delta))\)
  is at most \(-\inf I(W)\) (Eq. (1.6), PDF pp. 4–5).

In these displays, the ratio is taken only where its conditioning denominator is positive, as stipulated in the paper’s §2 convention. The closure in the lower test set and the interior (W^\circ) in the upper test set are part of the displayed conditions; preserve them. The theorem is a necessary-and-sufficient local criterion in this metric setting, not an unconditional consequence of an LDP for the joint laws.

**Theorem 1.3 (PDF p. 5; printed p. 1062).** For a continuous τ:X→Y and laws ν_n with an LDP of good rate (J:X\to[0,\infty]), assume regular conditional probabilities η_n under ν_n given τ are weakly continuous on the nonempty support of ν_n◦τ⁻¹. If \(\inf_{\tau^{-1}(y)}J<\infty\), the rate on (X) is exactly

\[
I(x)=\begin{cases}J(x)-\inf_{\tau^{-1}(y)}J,&\tau(x)=y,\\+\infty,&\tau(x)\ne y.\end{cases}
\]

The all-support-sequence LDP lower/upper claims are equivalent respectively to Eqs. (1.8)/(1.9), the same local conditional-ratio criteria with \(\nu_n(\overline U\mid\tau^{-1}(B(z,\delta)))\) and \(\nu_n(W^\circ\mid\tau^{-1}(B(z,\delta)))\). Do not omit the support, weak-continuity, continuous-τ, good-rate, or finite-fiber-infimum premises.

## General criterion and proof chain

Section 6 (PDF pp. 15–22) treats a more general setting: (X) normal, (Y) topological, bases (\mathcal G,\mathcal H), a rectangular-set LDP for μ_n with good rate (J), nonempty second-marginal supports, product kernels obeying the open-set liminf continuity condition on support, and at least one support sequence (y_n\to y); the fiber infimum is finite. Theorem 6.3 (pp. 16–18) characterizes a **fixed** support sequence through conditional probabilities on shrinking conditioning neighborhoods. Lemma 6.7 (pp. 18–19) derives local ratio bounds from the joint LDP and compact sublevels. Theorem 6.9 (pp. 19–22) gives the general implications \((A5)\Rightarrow(A4)\Leftrightarrow(A3)\Rightarrow(A2)\Rightarrow(A1)\) and the analogous upper-bound chain; when (Y) is first countable, the principal all-sequence criteria satisfy (A1\Leftrightarrow A2) and (B1\Leftrightarrow B2). The proof uses: local conditional/kernel brackets (Lemma 4.11); compact-sublevel stability of infima on shrinking fibers (Lemma 5.3); basis approximations to open and closed sets (Lemma 5.5); and a subsequence/diagonal contradiction for necessity. Theorem 1.2 is the metric specialization (p. 22).

Section 7 (PDF pp. 22–25) transfers this theory to regular conditional kernels under a continuous map. Theorem 7.1 (pp. 23–24) constructs \(\tilde\mu_n(A\times B)=\nu_n(A\cap\tau^{-1}(B))\) and proves a rectangle-set LDP with rate (J_\times(x,y)=K(x)) on (\tau(x)=y), (+\infty) otherwise, where (K) is the original rate. It also transfers compact sublevels. Equation (7.8) identifies the neighborhood conditional ratios. Theorem 7.5 (pp. 24–25) then gives the regular-kernel neighborhood criteria and Theorem 1.3 follows. Remark 7.4 cautions that the lifted LDP is stated on rectangles; extension to the full product sigma-algebra is not asserted as automatic.

## Applications, examples, appendices

- **Section 8 / Theorem 8.2 (PDF pp. 25–31).** For finite state sets (R,S) and a pair law λ positive on every second-coordinate state, condition on an empirical measure ψ_n of the (S)-coordinates. Sanov and contraction give the joint empirical-law rate (J(\rho,\sigma)=\inf_{\xi:m(\xi)=(\rho,\sigma)}H(\xi\mid\lambda)). The conditional empirical-measure rate is \(I(\phi)=\inf_{m(\xi)=(\phi,\psi)}H(\xi\mid\lambda)-\inf_{m(\xi)\in\mathcal P(R)\times\{\psi\}}H(\xi\mid\lambda)\) (Eq. (8.6), p. 27). The proof verifies the neighborhood criteria using finite-type Sanov bounds with polynomial factor ​\((n+1)^{\pm M}\) (Lemma 8.3), entropy continuity on finite-entropy sets, approximation of target empirical marginals by types (Lemmas 8.6–8.8), then Theorem 6.9 (pp. 27–31). Finite (R,S) and full support of λ on (S) are essential in this proof.
- **Section 9 (PDF pp. 31–35).** Example 9.1 gives Gaussian kernels and its scaled log-mgf on physical p. 31 (printed p. 1088); the following prose rate sentence is on physical p. 32 (printed p. 1089). Example 9.4 gives a failure of the lower conditional LDP for a particular support-point sequence, Remark 9.5 distinguishes quenched a.e. behavior, Example 9.6 gives no LDP at a chosen point, and Remarks 9.7–9.8 discuss exponential tightness and a different neighborhood-conditioning interpretation. The counterexamples show why support/version and local-ratio hypotheses cannot be skipped.
- **Appendix 1 (PDF pp. 35–36):** finite-sum log liminf/limsup identity (Lemma 9.9).
- **Appendix 2 (PDF pp. 35–36):** compact-sublevel set bounds that extend LDP lower bounds over unions and upper bounds over intersections (Lemmas 9.10, 9.12; Theorem 9.13).
- **Appendix 3 (PDF pp. 36–38):** full proof of Theorem 9.2, the mixture construction behind the counterexample; separates the two mixture components by shrinking neighborhoods and uses the finite-sum exponential-rate identity.
- **References (PDF pp. 38–39):** both pages read; the reference list includes standard LDP and conditional-probability sources but is not itself evidence for unproved assumptions here.

## Source formula discrepancy: Example 9.1

The displayed Gaussian density and conditional kernel in Example 9.1 (physical PDF p. 31, printed p. 1088) are proportional to \(e^{-\frac n2(x-ry)^2}\), and Eq. (9.3) displays the limiting scaled log-mgf \(\lambda r y+\tfrac12\lambda^2\). Its Legendre transform is \(\tfrac12(x-ry)^2\). This agrees with the general conditional rate \(J(x,y)-\inf_uJ(u,y)\) from Theorem 1.2 and with the quadratic joint rate displayed in Example 9.1. The prose sentence following Eq. (9.3) is on physical PDF p. 32 (printed p. 1089) and literally prints the kernel rate as \((x-ry)^2\), omitting the factor (1/2); this source discrepancy is isolated and not silently corrected.

The example states only \(r\ne0\), but its normalizing integral \(Z_n\) over \(\mathbb R^2\) is finite only when the quadratic-form matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}1&-r\\-r&1\end{pmatrix}\) is positive definite, i.e. \(1-r^2>0\). Thus the probability model as written requires \(0<|r|<1\); for \(|r|\ge1\) the displayed integral is not a finite normalization. This is an explicit integrability condition, not a change to the source's stated nonzero-parameter condition. The local SymPy check verifies the completion of the square, this determinant condition, and the log-mgf Legendre transform; it does not prove the LDP.

## Transfer boundary and W01 use

Concrete W01 use: conditional LDP transfer **after** (1) a good-rate joint LDP, (2) existence of the chosen regular/product conditional kernels, (3) weak continuity on the relevant marginal supports, and (4) the theorem’s local numerator/denominator criteria have been verified. A continuous observation map gives the regular-kernel version with an infinite rate off the target fiber. The source does not say that joint weak convergence alone entails conditional weak convergence, and its conditional LDP conclusion cannot be inferred from the joint LDP without the continuity and local-ratio assumptions. It also does not claim continuity of conditional kernels under arbitrary parameter changes.
