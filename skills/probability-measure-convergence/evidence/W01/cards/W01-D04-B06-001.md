## Scope and theorem statements

The paper deliberately treats disintegration of σ-finite measures, not only conditional probability kernels. Definition 1 (physical p.6, printed p.292) calls {λ_t} a (T,μ)-disintegration of λ when: each λ_t is σ-finite and supported on the fiber T^{-1}{t} for μ-a.e. t; t↦λ_t f is measurable for every nonnegative measurable f; and
λ f = ∫ λ_t f μ(dt).
The base spaces are measurable; the theorem adds topology and countability assumptions.

**Theorem 1, existence and common-null-set uniqueness** (physical p.7, printed p.293). Assume X is metric, λ is a σ-finite Radon measure on X, T:X→(T,B) is measurable, and μ is a σ-finite measure on B dominating Tλ. If B is countably generated and contains every singleton {t}, then λ has a (T,μ)-disintegration. Any two such families agree as measures λ_t=λ'_t outside one μ-null set, simultaneously for all measurable subsets of X. The Radon condition includes inner regularity by compact sets and finite mass on compact sets.

**Theorem 2, normalization** (physical pp.8–9, printed pp.294–295). If λ has a (T,μ)-disintegration and λ,μ are σ-finite, then Tλ≪μ with density t↦λ_t X. The fibers λ_t are finite μ-a.e. exactly when Tλ is σ-finite; they are probabilities μ-a.e. exactly when μ=Tλ. If Tλ is σ-finite, then Tλ gives zero mass to fibers where λ_t X is zero or infinite, and λ_t/(λ_t X) on 0<λ_t X<∞ is a probability-kernel T-disintegration of λ. This is the condition needed before interpreting general disintegrating measures as conditional probabilities.

**Theorem 3, change of measure** (physical pp.10–11, printed pp.296–297). If ρ=rλ with finite density r and λ, μ, ρ are σ-finite, then ρ_t has density r(x) with respect to λ_t, and the image Tρ has density t↦λ_t r with respect to μ. Where Tρ is σ-finite, standardization gives conditional probability measures with fiber density proportional to r. This is the abstract density-ratio formula; it does not assume Euclidean coordinates or a Lebesgue density.

**Composition/tower example** (Example 4, physical pp.11–12, printed pp.297–298). If λ disintegrates over T with mixing μ, and μ disintegrates over S with mixing ν, then averaging λ_t over the S-fiber of t gives an S∘T disintegration. The proof checks the mixing identity by iterated integration and fiber support by the two support properties.

## Proof chain actually read

Appendix §6 (physical pp.29–30, printed pp.315–316) reduces σ-finite measures to finite restrictions on compact pieces. On compact metric X it uses a countable additive cone C0 of nonnegative continuous functions determining finite Borel measures. For each f∈C0, (x,t)↦f(x)g(t) under the graph pushforward measure defines a measure dominated by μ; Radon–Nikodym derivatives supply t↦λ_t f. One common null set enforces positivity/additivity on C0; the Riesz representation theorem then gives λ_t. A generating-class argument gives measurability and the averaging identity for bounded measurable functions. Countable generation of B and inclusion of all singleton sets make the graph measure concentrate on {(x,t):Tx=t}; the same identity yields fiber support. Uniqueness tests the two candidate kernels against the countable determining cone, then removes only a countable union of null sets.

Theorem 2’s key identity is
(Tλ)g = ∫ g(Tx) λ(dx) = ∫ g(t) λ_t X μ(dt).
This proves the claimed density and drives the finite/probability/standardization cases. Theorem 3 follows by substituting rf into the disintegration identity, then applying Theorem 2 to the new total mass.

## Use and limitations

Use Theorem 1 only with a σ-finite Radon source measure, a σ-finite dominating mixing measure, and the countably generated target σ-field containing all singleton sets. Use probability-valued conditional laws only after verifying the image-measure σ-finiteness and normalization conditions in Theorem 2. The paper does not assert existence on arbitrary measurable spaces. Its examples do not replace a general regular-conditional-probability reference on standard Borel spaces. Theorem 1 is useful as a foundational disintegration argument; no W01 problem-specific result is claimed from it by itself.
