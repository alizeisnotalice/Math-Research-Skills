# Variational-limit source applications

Use this skill when a proof passes an optimization problem, random objective, expected value, or optimizer through a probabilistic or analytic limit. First decide what is supposed to converge: the value, the laws of optimizers, a saddle point, or a family of values uniformly over another parameter. Those conclusions require different arguments.


## Source-use boundary

Use the documented theorem only within the model and interface in its source-use card. For formally published work, verify the citation and the cited statement against the publication. Verify the active claim and relevant proof steps for a preprint-only result. Historical review notes retain the scope of their original versions. Keep source-card gaps, unavailable evidence, and open transfers as research leads.

The optional attachments named here are available in the original local installation or the central literature archive. See [evidence and local attachments](../../math-research/references/evidence.md).

## 1. Write the limit problem before choosing a theorem

Record the following in a one-page model ledger:

- the probability law at each index, including whether it changes with the index;
- the feasible set and its topology, including whether it is random and whether it is compact;
- whether the objective is minimized or maximized, its sign, and whether it is bounded;
- every index and external parameter, with the order in which limits and suprema are taken;
- the target claim: value convergence, a one-sided value bound, optimizer subsequences, optimizer-law convergence, minimax equality, or an LDP/Laplace formula;
- atoms, ties, randomization, measurability, and any conditioning convention that changes the model.

Do not use “the optimization converges” as a single claim. A value can converge while minimizers oscillate; a minimizer can exist while its law is not identified; a finite minimax identity says nothing by itself about a horizon limit.

## 2. Route the claim to the matching transfer

### Weak convergence and unbounded objectives

Weak convergence transfers bounded continuous test functions. For changing laws and changing integrands, name the exact integral theorem and check its envelope and tail hypotheses. In particular, for a lower bound on expected costs, control the negative parts; a one-sided Fatou-type result is not equality of expectations. For equality, supply a second bound or suitable uniform integrability of the full signed objectives.

### Optimization values and optimizers

For minimization, prove the lower-bound direction with a liminf inequality for every convergent feasible sequence. Prove the reverse value bound by constructing a recovery sequence for a limit competitor. Add compactness or equicoercivity to obtain convergent subsequences of near-minimizers. For maximization, reverse the inequalities. Check that the feasible domains and topology actually match the theorem; a liminf inequality alone is not value convergence.

## 4. Separate finite certificates from asymptotic evidence

An exact LP, dual, solver, or finite-grid certificate proves a statement about that encoded finite problem. Before using it asymptotically, verify that the finite model matches the target and add a convergence theorem or a quantitative error bound uniform over the changing parameters. Keep the deterministic model, sample law, truncation, and discretization assumptions visible.

## 5. Worked checks and failure examples

- **Pointwise convergence on a compact set need not pass minima.** On `[0,1]`, `f_n(x)=−max(1−2n|x−1/n|,0)` is continuous and converges pointwise to zero, but `min f_n=−1` while `min 0=0`. Use uniform convergence or prove the matching liminf and recovery conditions; add level compactness when transferring optimizers.
- **Convergence in probability need not pass expectations.** With `U∼Unif[0,1]`, `X_n=n·1{U≤1/n}` tends to zero in probability while `E X_n=1`. Name the missing UI/tail premise; a one-sided Fatou bound is not equality of expectations.
- **The IID law class may not be convex.** The half-half mixture of `δ_(0,0)` and `δ_(1,1)` is supported on the diagonal. The product of its mixed Bernoulli marginals assigns probability `1/4` to each off-diagonal point. Verify convexity in the actual joint-law class before applying Sion.
- **Γ-convergence does not alone give minimum-value convergence.** For the fixed-real-Dirichlet Sierpinski-gasket example, `φ=(0,1,0)` and integer vertex assignments followed by harmonic-spline extension give zero discrete energy at every level, while the continuum minimum is `1`. This is a derived source-specific diagnostic, not a counterexample to the paper's Γ theorem; the missing step is convergence/compact capture of the minimizers. - **Correctors are model-specific.** In planar directed corner growth, stationary cocycles provide a fixed-law optimizer/corrector route only under the source's own moment, lower-bound, variance, and differentiability or one-sided-cocycle hypotheses. This is not a general Γ theorem, finite-size estimate, or proof of an unrelated two-limit exchange. R03 records the source-local A.6(b) sign slip and the separate derived completion.
