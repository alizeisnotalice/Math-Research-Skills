# Method guide — staged G18 expansion

> Current seven-interface routing, accepted-source status, and open proof boundaries are summarized in the G18 method entry（本机附件：`references/method_entry.md`）. This file retains expanded theorem notes and calibration details.

## Determinant conventions

- Finite weighted UST: use the conductance-weighted transfer-current determinant and the square-root-conductance coordinate transform.
- Infinite FSF/WSF: state the resistance-energy flow space and distinguish the free projection onto the cycle-space orthogonal complement from the wired projection onto the star-flow space.
- A symmetric transfer-resistance matrix is not automatically the actual transfer-current matrix. For the calibration triangle, the exact check records both matrices and the weighted determinant.

## Negative dependence and BK

- Theorem 6.5 of Lyons (PDF pp. 19–20) concerns increasing events on disjoint fixed coordinate supports for a finite projection-DPP law.
- BK asks for disjoint witnesses that may depend on the configuration. A union over such witnesses needs a separate proof.
- Lyons's unnumbered open-question paragraph in §9 on PDF p. 35, immediately after Conjecture 9.1 and before Conjecture 9.2, explicitly leaves the DPP BK question, including UST, open. Conjecture 9.1 itself concerns conditional DPP measures.
- Borcea–Brändén–Liggett define strong Rayleigh through real-stable generating polynomials and prove finite DPP/weighted-tree examples and closure implications. The multiaffine Rayleigh-difference criterion requires every real vector, not only positive vectors. These results give CNA+/fixed-coordinate dependence, not a BK inequality.
- Markström's three-coordinate examples prove that NA alone does not imply BK; the source does not show those measures are DPP or strong Rayleigh.
- Merkl–Rolles proves nonnegativity only for the second-order Taylor polynomial of the finite Hermitian DPP BK gap along C² paths through a diagonal kernel in the strict domain 0<G<I. Reimer's butterfly theorem gives the zeroth-order term and its constrained coordinate-pair variant gives the quadratic term. This does not prove global BK or control higher orders.
- Positive dependent-law BK examples are model-specific: van den Berg–Jonasson proves BK on the uniform k-out-of-n slice and specified weighted/product extensions; Jonasson proves it for pivotal sampling with a fixed linear order or fixed binary-tree order by mapping disjoint witnesses to disjoint sets of independent match coins. Neither result transfers to weighted USTs without a separate coupling or witness map.
- For Bernoulli product measure with 0<p<1, Cerf–Tesio characterizes equality by every A×B configuration pair admitting disjoint witnesses and proves the strengthened comparison with an independent-copy witness event. These are product-law statements.
- For finite bipartite matching laws, Mészáros proves BK for the specific subset `B(M)=V(M) symmetric-difference S` by partitioning matching pairs into alternating-cycle/path fibers and applying Reimer to endpoint switches. Positive edge weights, fixed-coordinate conditioning, and maximum-cardinality limits have the paper's precise scope; this map is not available for a general DPP/UST.
- van den Berg–Gandolfi's folding theorem gives a separate proof template: for each locked-set folding, construct an RCR whose allowed states are invariant under the fold flip and prove selected witnesses lie in distinct random clusters. The fiber argument then maps to Reimer's inequality. Their Ising theorem uses cluster-disjoint occurrence; it is not ordinary BK. The theorem gives no RCR for general DPPs or USTs.
- Brändén–Jonasson gives strong-Rayleigh closure and sampling examples with NA/CNA conclusions. Brown Kramer–Cutler–Radcliffe shows an arbitrary SSP match order can fail even NA (their seven-item example has covariance excess 2/245), while their tournament-order result is NA/CNA. Neither source gives BK from NA.
- Goldman 1905.03579v5 proves DPP BK only for the paper's increasing events generated by simple points. It is not an arbitrary-events or general UST BK theorem.

## Strong Rayleigh, SCP, and concentration

- Strong Rayleigh means the nonnegative multiaffine generating polynomial has no zero when all coordinates lie in the strict upper half-plane. For a real multiaffine polynomial, the equivalent Rayleigh-difference inequalities must hold on all of R^n.
- Strong Rayleigh implies the stochastic covering property through a homogeneous extension and conditional negative association. Adjacent rank laws P_{k+1} and P_k are covered only when both rank masses are positive.
- A k-homogeneous SCP law and a Hamming-Lipschitz-1 function give exp(-a²/(8k)) upper tails. The nonhomogeneous finite DPP bound needs finite mean μ and has the 3/5 bounds with denominator 16(a+2μ). SCP/concentration does not supply a union bound over random BK witnesses.
- For finite weighted spanning trees, the stated balanced-matroid route gives an SCP concentration exponent proportional to the tree rank |V|-1. Keep this application distinct from a global BK claim.

## Additional B05 interfaces

- **Sofic monotone couplings.** Lyons–Thom Theorem 2.1 gives finite DPP stochastic domination under Hermitian positive-contraction operator order. The final paragraph on physical p. 6 extends ordinary WSF≼FSF stochastic domination to general graphs by finite approximations and weak limits. Their invariant infinite coupling (Theorem 5.1) requires a countable sofic group, finite generating set and equivariant operators; Corollary 5.2 is the sofic-Cayley invariant-coupling consequence, not the only ordinary-domination case. Their finite-dependent approximation and rooted-network extension have separate theorem hypotheses. None implies BK.
- **Continuous-space tail.** Osada–Osada require a bounded Hermitian locally trace-class positive contraction and a refining binary partition sequence exhausting the Borel sigma field on the stated LCSC space. The tree-indexed DPP lift plus conditional-expectation martingale convergence proves configuration-tail triviality; it is not a blanket group-action ergodicity theorem.
- **Rayleigh versus strong Rayleigh.** Wagner’s all-positive-fugacity pairwise Rayleigh inequalities are weaker than real stability. Homogeneity is required for the cited Rayleigh-to-NA theorem. His weak-Rayleigh matroid support conclusions do not show arbitrary matroid basis laws are strong Rayleigh; broad graph/matroid statements marked as conjectures remain conjectures. No BK follows.
- **SCP functional inequalities.** Hermon–Salez assume the positive-probability conditional covering-coupling property and a specified reversible generator. Preserve the directed and symmetrized adjacent-rate definitions when quoting constants; a DPP application first needs SCP for that exact law.
- **Conditional DPP correlations.** Goldman’s explicit CS-decomposition proof treats real orthogonal projection DPPs and positive-probability avoidance conditions, proving the two- and three-constraint hierarchy stated in the paper. Proposition 1 uses reduced Palm DPPs for its disjoint-set reduction. The preprint’s later positive-contraction extension is a citation to earlier work. Neither result is arbitrary-event BK or a random-witness union estimate.

## Positive-contraction kernels

- Lyons Theorem 8.1 (PDF pp. 32–33) extends the projection-DPP framework to a positive contraction $Q$ on a countable coordinate space by dilating $Q$ to a projection on a larger space and restricting the sample. It gives mixed finite inclusion/exclusion determinants, conditional negative association with external fields, tail triviality, and a concentration bound.
- For commuting positive contractions $Q_1,Q_2$ with $Q_1\le Q_2$, the source proves $P_{Q_1}\preceq P_{Q_2}$ by a joint spectral representation and nested-subspace dilation. Do not drop commutation or call this a general stochastic-covering coupling.
- Tail triviality of this DPP is not group-action ergodicity; the NA conclusion still does not prove BK over random witnesses. The additional L10 use card is `expansions/20261115/readers/L10/G18_determinantal_dependence/usage_cards/LYO2003DPP_positive_contractions.md` and has passed scoped R10 review with no additional source-count credit.
- Bufetov–Qiu–Shamov prove that conditioning a self-adjoint locally trace-class positive-contraction DPP on its configuration in any Borel set leaves a DPP on the complement, with kernel obtained as a local-trace-class limit of bounded conditional kernels. Projection kernels additionally give sampled-kernel completeness. Their tail-triviality result applies to all such self-adjoint positive contractions; it does not assert general group-action ergodicity.
- Georgii–Yoo's determinant-ratio Papangelou formulas require a Hermitian locally trace-class K with `0<=K<=I` and `||K||<1`. They yield local conditional-intensity monotonicity, disjoint-vacuum-event negative correlation, and Poisson domination by intensity `J(x,x)` when the diagonal kernel representative satisfies the trace formula. This is not full negative association or the strong-Rayleigh stochastic covering property. Their global explicit conditional determinant further requires almost-everywhere continuity.

## Stability certificates and rank-specific boundaries

- Choe–Oxley–Sokal–Wagner use right-half-plane nonvanishing. For homogeneous degree-r polynomials, rotate each variable by `-i` to match the upper-half-plane stability convention without changing zeros. Their determinant formula `det(A diag(x) A*)=Σ|det A_S|²x^S` needs full row rank for a nonzero polynomial and follows by Cauchy–Binet; modulus-one maximal minors are additionally required for the unweighted matroid basis polynomial. Their permanent construction requires a nonnegative matrix and derives stability from the Heilmann–Lieb matching theorem.
- The fixed-size BK theorem and pivotal-sampling theorem each provide a positive dependent-law example using a combinatorial witness reduction. Keep those constructive reductions separate from polynomial negative-dependence implications.

## Singularity

- State whether the claim is equality, absolute continuity, singularity, tail triviality, or ergodicity for a specified action.
- BLPS Corollary 8.2 is an action-based sufficient singularity result under an infinite vertex orbit. Its use requires an actual automorphism action and the paper's network assumptions.
- Do not infer group ergodicity from tail triviality alone.
- For NA Bernoulli sequences, Alishahi–Barzegar–Zamani's tail-zero-one theorem needs rowwise summable absolute covariances. Strong-Rayleigh sequences satisfy that condition through stochastic covering, and NA weakly stationary Bernoulli sequences satisfy it through a separate covariance-sum bound. For a Gaussian sequence, their theorem instead assumes every off-diagonal covariance is nonpositive and uses Gaussian maximal correlation; Gaussian thresholding then inherits tail triviality.
- Their Gaussian threshold examples show NA need not be strong Rayleigh and covariance summability is not necessary for this Gaussian tail result. These distinctions give useful G18.03/.06 boundaries without supplying a general network group action or a BK inequality.
- Any finite or symbolic calculation is an example check, not proof of an infinite-network criterion.


## Stationary lattice DPP and matroid interfaces

- Lyons–Steif consider the stationary `Z^d` law `P_f` with measurable symbol `f:T^d→[0,1]`. Theorem 2.1 gives conditional NA after a finite coordinate pattern of positive probability. Theorem 5.11 uses `GM(f)` for product stochastic domination, while Theorem 5.16 uses `HM(f)` for full domination; these thresholds must remain distinct. Theorem 3.1 proves Bernoullicity by Fejér finite-dependent approximation and `d`-metric closure. The strong-K iff criterion is one-dimensional. Do not promote any of these to arbitrary network/group claims or BK.
- For a generalized Ginibre/Fock projection DPP on `C`, Bufetov–Qiu require `φ∈C²` and `0<m≤Δφ≤M`; exterior-conditioned bounded-region laws are finite OPEs, with the simpler `Γ` weight only for radial `φ` and `0∈B`. Bufetov’s real-line version requires a rigid projection DPP, division-invariance, and either its trace bound or HS bound plus the specified regulator. These are model-specific conditional laws, not general DPP closure under conditioning.
- Choe–Wagner define a matroid as Rayleigh by `Δ_M{e,f}(y)≥0` for every pair and every `y>0`; this gives disjoint-fixed-support negative correlation for the positive-fugacity basis law. Their HPP→Rayleigh implication is specific to matroid basis polynomials. Rayleigh does not mean real-stable/strong-Rayleigh, and neither gives BK for random witnesses.
- Goldman’s Theorem 3 proves discrete-DPP BK for increasing events generated by simple points. Its finite proof uses CSD conditional inequalities and a wedge-product estimate; the source’s discrete extension is separate. Conjecture 1 is an unproved stronger conditional-correlation claim. Do not use fixed-support NA to remove the simple-point hypothesis.
