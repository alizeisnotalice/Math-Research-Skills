# Determinantal dependence and measure singularity

**Evidence scope:** R10 accepted the G18 method-entry science, documented cases, and 31 scoped source uses. Apply each result only under the source-specific hypotheses and transfer limits recorded in the references. The general non-iid regression proof in Dubhashi–Ranjan Theorems 31/37 is excluded because its printed conditional-averaging identity omits per-bin likelihood weights; this proof gap does not establish that Theorem 31 is false.

This draft covers G18 only.

Start by naming the law, ground set, kernel, graph/network assumptions, boundary condition, and exhaustion. Keep finite weighted UST, infinite FSF/WSF, and minimal spanning forests separate. For a determinant, state the edge orientation, conductance weights, coordinate convention, and whether the matrix is a current or resistance matrix.

For A141, keep negative association for increasing events on fixed disjoint coordinate sets separate from BK disjoint occurrence with configuration-dependent witnesses. Lyons's Theorem 6.5 gives the first statement; the paper explicitly leaves the DPP/UST BK case open. Strong-Rayleigh properties also require their own hypotheses and do not by themselves sum over random witness choices.

For G18.03, define a strong-Rayleigh law through a real-stable multiaffine generating polynomial. The Borcea–Brändén–Liggett criterion tests the Rayleigh difference at every real vector; checking only positive coordinates is weaker. Finite Hermitian DPPs with positive-contraction kernels and finite weighted spanning-tree laws provide strong-Rayleigh examples, hence fixed-coordinate negative dependence under the cited finite hypotheses. This gives no BK theorem for random witnesses.

For stationary lattice DPPs `P_f` with measurable symbol `f:T^d→[0,1]`, Lyons–Steif give positive-probability finite-coordinate conditional NA, geometric-mean product-domination thresholds, a distinct harmonic-mean full-domination threshold, and a Bernoulli-shift proof by Fejér finite-dependent approximation. Their strong-K threshold is stated for the one-dimensional shift. These conclusions do not extend automatically to arbitrary DPPs or group actions, and none gives a random-witness BK bound.

For the generalized Ginibre/Fock projection DPP on `C` with `φ∈C²` and `0<m≤Δφ≤M`, Bufetov–Qiu express the exterior-conditioned finite-particle law in a bounded region as an OPE with a configuration-dependent weight; radial `φ` and a region containing zero give a `Γ`-product formula. Bufetov gives a related one-dimensional projection-DPP OPE under a division-invariance condition, rigidity, and either a trace bound or an HS bound plus regulator. Neither card licenses a general conditioned-DPP theorem.

For matroid basis laws, Choe–Wagner test Rayleigh differences at every positive fugacity; the Rayleigh property gives fixed-support negative correlation, while HPP implies Rayleigh under the matroid basis-polynomial hypotheses. Keep Rayleigh distinct from real stability/strong-Rayleigh and balancedness. Goldman’s discrete-DPP BK theorem is directly usable only when both increasing events are generated by simple points; it does not cover arbitrary witnesses in weighted USTs.

For a self-adjoint locally trace-class DPP with (0\le K\le I), Bufetov–Qiu–Shamov give an explicit conditional DPP kernel for conditioning on points in any Borel set and prove tail triviality along bounded exhaustions. Their almost-sure completeness result additionally requires that (K) is an orthogonal projection. Their tail theorem does not provide ergodicity for an arbitrary group action or a disjoint-witness BK inequality. Georgii–Yoo give determinant-ratio Papangelou intensities when the Hermitian kernel also satisfies (\|K\|<1); local intensity monotonicity and negative correlation of disjoint vacuum events are limited to that interface. Their Poisson stochastic domination uses a trace-compatible diagonal kernel and is not the strong-Rayleigh stochastic covering property.

For a homogeneous fixed-rank generating polynomial, distinguish upper-half-plane real stability from the right-half-plane convention used by Choe–Oxley–Sokal–Wagner; the substitution `x=-iz` preserves zeros up to a constant phase in one fixed degree. Their determinant and nonnegative-permanent constructions certify specific HPP polynomials, and their matroid-support theorem is only a necessary condition. Do not infer that every matroid basis polynomial is stable: the paper gives non-HPP examples. A normalized determinant/permanent polynomial supplies a fixed-size law only after checking its coefficient and normalization hypotheses.

Use stochastic covering only under its conditional-coupling hypotheses. Strong Rayleigh implies SCP, and SCP yields concentration for homogeneous laws with Hamming-Lipschitz functions; the nonhomogeneous DPP bound additionally requires finite mean. It remains a coupling/concentration interface and does not control the BK witness union. For an exact boundary, Markström's three-coordinate examples are NA but violate BK, but are not shown to be DPP or strong Rayleigh. Merkl–Rolles proves only nonnegativity of the second-order Taylor polynomial of the finite DPP BK gap along admissible paths through a diagonal kernel; higher-order/global BK remains unresolved.

BK has positive dependent-law examples, but each uses its own construction: van den Berg–Jonasson for the uniform fixed-size slice (and the stated weighted/product extensions), Jonasson for fixed-order or fixed-tree pivotal sampling via a witness-preserving map to independent match coins, and Mészáros for the covered-vertex symmetric-difference subset of bipartite matchings via alternating-path fibers. van den Berg–Gandolfi give an abstract dependent-law route: every folding needs a random-cluster representation with flip symmetry and separation of selected witnesses, after which a fiberwise Reimer count applies. Their ferromagnetic Ising result is cluster-disjoint occurrence, not ordinary BK. Brändén–Jonasson and Brown Kramer–Cutler–Radcliffe show why model assumptions matter: strong-Rayleigh/CNA sampling results are not BK, and an arbitrary SSP match order can fail even NA. Classical Bernoulli-product BK equality and strengthening remain product-specific. Goldman’s later DPP theorem is limited to increasing events generated by simple points. None of these results settles arbitrary increasing-event BK for finite weighted USTs.

For continuous-space DPP tail triviality, Osada–Osada require a bounded Hermitian locally trace-class positive-contraction kernel and a refining binary partition exhaustion generating the Borel sigma field; their tree lift and martingale argument do not give arbitrary action ergodicity. The DPP kernel route and the NA sequence route have distinct hypotheses. Bufetov–Qiu–Shamov prove tail triviality for self-adjoint locally trace-class positive contractions. Alishahi–Barzegar–Zamani prove it for NA Bernoulli sequences with rowwise summable covariances, for strongly-Rayleigh sequences through stochastic covering, and for weakly stationary NA Bernoulli sequences; their Gaussian result instead needs nonpositive off-diagonal covariances. NA Gaussian threshold laws can be tail trivial without summable covariance and can fail strong Rayleigh. Tail triviality in any of these forms does not imply an unspecified group-action ergodicity claim.

For general graphs, Lyons–Thom derive ordinary WSF ≼ FSF stochastic domination by finite DPP order and weak limits. Their invariant coupling of the corresponding infinite DPPs requires sofic hypotheses; the FSF/WSF invariant-coupling application is limited to sofic Cayley graphs, while finite-dependent approximation has its own stated construction. This is an invariant-coupling tool, not BK or an unrestricted ergodicity result. Wagner’s Rayleigh criterion tests every pair at every positive fugacity; homogeneous Rayleigh implies NA, but Rayleigh is weaker than strong-Rayleigh and neither supplies random-witness BK. Hermon–Salez derive functional inequalities from the stochastic covering property for a specified reversible generator, not from NA alone. Goldman’s conditional avoidance inequalities cover two or three positive-probability constraints in their projection-DPP proof; they are not arbitrary-event BK.

For A025, keep equality of FSF and WSF separate from mutual singularity. The BLPS projection and transfer-current results give an equality criterion. Its Corollary 8.2 provides a singularity route under an automorphism group with an infinite vertex orbit. Do not use that route for a general network lacking the required action. Tail triviality is not the same as group ergodicity.

For A027 and A147, G18 is a boundary tool only. Tree environment-chain ergodicity belongs to G19; binary/ternary tree harmonic-measure singularity belongs to G25. A shared term such as “singularity” is not a source-use justification.

The current accepted source set includes Kahn–Neiman's iid-urn CNA theorem, Dubhashi–Ranjan's occupancy NA and restricted iid/one-hot interfaces, and Wagner's rank-three Rayleigh theorem. R16 confirmed an allocation-weight error in the printed general non-iid Theorem 37 proof; that route is excluded without judging Theorem 31 false. The accepted BK examples remain restricted to their stated event classes and witness reductions.
