## Independent Bernoulli sums

For independent `B_i~Bernoulli(p_i)`, compute the exact law from `G_S(z)=Π_i(1−p_i+p_i z)` or recurrence `c_{i,j}=(1−p_i)c_{i−1,j}+p_i c_{i−1,j−1}`. Use rational arithmetic where possible. Remove `p_i=0` terms and shift by deterministic `p_i=1` terms before odds-based recurrences or conditional samplers. An instance calculation is not a theorem proof.

### Log-concavity and generating functions

Johnson–Goldschmidt’s convolution result is safe when each summand pmf has contiguous support as well as the local log-concavity inequalities. Bernoulli pmfs have interval support `{0,1}`, so their finite independent convolution is ordinarily log-concave. Their dependent extension additionally requires contiguous support of `V` and both displayed Condition 1 kernel partial-sum families; arbitrary dependence is insufficient. The literal adjacent inequalities alone allow internal zeros and do not suffice. See the source-scope counterexample（本机附件：`examples/tang_and_johnson_scope.md`）. The proof card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision7a/cards/PR-c1d76a09dd00.md`） also gives a sufficient geometric-smoothing ratio test and support-gap obstruction.

Tang–Tang is a survey map for cited Hoeffding, majorization, Newton, and Stein–Chen results; it does not reproduce those primary proofs, so do not use it as proof or count it toward those atomic methods. Its exact PGF factorization and recurrence (26) are an auxiliary recipe for computing Poisson-binomial coefficients, checked in the scope example（本机附件：`examples/tang_and_johnson_scope.md`）; that computation has no atomic G05 method ID.

## Dependent common-margin sums

For `X_i~Bernoulli(p)` with a common margin and unrestricted dependence, Fontana–Semeraro Proposition 5.2 identifies the minimal sum law in convex order: support on `{floor(dp),ceil(dp)}` with mean-fixing weights, or `δ_dp` if `dp` is integer. Attainment follows from the explicit exchangeable construction: sample this sum `S`, then choose a uniform subset of `[d]` of size `S`. This is a direct derivation, not a claim that the cited 2020 paper was read. The source theorem is used in its rational-`p` setting. R02 approved this scoped use as G05.02; its broader Theorem 5.1 nonexchangeable construction is excluded pending case-by-case conditions. ## Stochastic order and discrete tails

For positive integers `n_i` and `p_i∈(0,1)`, Klenke–Mattner give the exact ordinary stochastic-order criterion for two binomial laws: `n_1≤n_2` and `(1−p_1)^{n_1}≥(1−p_2)^{n_2}`. Their half-monotone-likelihood-ratio test is sufficient only when the one-turn likelihood-ratio and both endpoint conditions hold. In the proof, do not infer `p_2≥p_1` from the zero-atom condition when `n_1<n_2`; the direct implication applies in the equal-`n` branch, while the unequal-support branch needs its endpoint argument. R02 approved this as an auxiliary interface, with no atomic G05 method ID. A sorted Poisson-binomial product test is cited from earlier work, not proved there. Do not infer convex/majorization order; see the source card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision6/cards/EXSRC-9bd08b5845027a60.md`） and R02 decision（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/reviews/R02/l02_revision3a/review.json`）.

For independent nonnegative integer `S,X`, apply Fokkink–Papavassiliou–Pelekis only after checking `s≥m≥1`, `S` unimodal with mode `s`, every required right-skew inequality, and integer `m=E[X]` plus L1 or L2 left-loadedness. The conclusion is `P(S+X≥s+m)≤P(S≥s)`; it does not require `E[S]=s`. This is a conditional integer-tail lemma, not a general stochastic order. See the source card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision6/cards/EXSRC-f2b1ad9a54a1b27c.md`）.

R02 accepted Xu–Balakrishnan as a scoped G05.02 use. For two independent finite Bernoulli sums with every parameter in `(0,1)`, Xu–Balakrishnan give two exact parameter-comparison tests. Sort each parameter vector in descending order. If every prefix sum of `1/p_i` is no larger than the matching prefix sum of `1/p_i*`, then `Σ Bernoulli(p_i) ≥lr Σ Bernoulli(p_i*)`. If every prefix sum of `−log(p_i)` is no larger than its starred counterpart, then the first sum is `≥rh` the second. Since `−log` is decreasing, the latter test can be checked by comparing products of the largest `j` probabilities. These are weak-supermajorization conditions in the paper’s ascending-vector convention. Check every prefix; equality of means or the first prefix alone does not suffice. The result concerns independent sums and likelihood/reversed-hazard order; it does not establish convex or stop-loss order. See the Xu–Balakrishnan proof card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision10/cards/EXSRC-c482a03ddeebb053.md`）, R02 report（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/reviews/R02/l02_revision10/xu_review.json`）, R02 admission（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/reviews/R02/admissions/l02_revision10_xu_two_order_interfaces.json`）, and exact cases（本机附件：`examples/l02_revision10_batch10_cases.md`）.

R02 accepted Shuldiner–Oldford as an auxiliary use with no atomic method ID. For a finite Bernoulli sum `X=ΣY_i`, including dependent indicators, Shuldiner–Oldford express `E[X^k]` through joint success probabilities: `Σ_{m=1}^k S(k,m) Σ_{|A|=m} E[∏_{i∈A}Y_i]`, where `S(k,m)=m!{k\brace m}` counts surjections. Under independence only, replace each joint expectation by `∏_{i∈A}p_i`. Their factorial-moment formula is `E([X]_k)=k!Σ_{|A|=k}E[∏_{i∈A}Y_i]`, and the factorial-moment generating function satisfies `H_X(s)=G_X(1+s)` when the series converge (in particular, for finite support). They also express count moments through upper tails. These identities help evaluate fixed-moment constraints and exact examples; they provide no extremal-order theorem. Use only the finite indicator-sum, finite-support PGF, and convergent count-tail identities reviewed by R02; exclude the unaccepted application closed forms, `E[X!]` rewrite, and general moment-limit claims. See the source card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision10/cards/EXSRC-f798e86bb13956c4.md`）, R02 report（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/reviews/R02/l02_revision10/shuldiner_review.json`）, R02 admission（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/reviews/R02/admissions/l02_revision10_shuldiner_moment_auxiliary.json`）, and dependence boundary（本机附件：`examples/l02_revision10_batch10_cases.md`）.

## Arbitrary dependence with a fixed sum law

When only the sum pmf `p_k=P(S=k)` is fixed and the Bernoulli coordinates may be dependent, Fontana–Semeraro describe the whole fiber of joint pmfs: within each Hamming-weight-`k` layer, allocate total mass `p_k` among the `binom(d,k)` configurations. Its vertices put each layer mass on a single configuration. Their sharp cross-moment interface is `p_d≤E[∏_{j∈J}X_j]≤Σ_{k=|J|}^d p_k`; this is a dependent-joint-law feasibility result, not a PBD product identity. A fixed sum law and fixed marginal means need not identify independence. See the fiber card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision8/cards/EXSRC-9f0e4748607cb6cd.md`）.

For common `Bernoulli(1/2)` margins with arbitrary dependence, Mutti–Semeraro identify the convex-order-minimal sum as `δ_{d/2}` for even `d`, and the equal mixture on the two adjacent integers around `d/2` for odd `d`. Attain it by drawing that sum `S` and then selecting a uniform size-`S` subset. For even `d` this is a joint mix. Do not transfer this construction to the independence-only model. Their broader algebraic characterization of every minimizer uses a cited polynomial-generation result not read in this packet, so that algorithm remains excluded. See the scoped source card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision8/cards/EXSRC-981c81c2d08721c5.md`）.

## Revision 9: Poisson bounds and point-mass extremizers

For independent Bernoulli indicators `X_i~Bernoulli(p_i)`, `W=ΣX_i`, and `λ=Σp_i`, Barbour–Hall Theorems 1–2 give the total-variation bracket

`(1/32)(1∧λ^{-1})Σp_i² ≤ d_TV(L(W),Pois(λ)) ≤ ((1−e^{-λ})/λ)Σp_i²`,

with the lower inequality used for `λ>0` and the upper coefficient continuously extended to `1` at `λ=0`. Their proof uses the Stein–Chen identity and a Poisson Stein solution; the finite-difference factor is cited to Barbour–Eagleson rather than reproved in this article. Use the theorem with independence stated and keep the factor-of-two total-variation convention explicit. R02 accepted this scoped G05.04 interface. See the Barbour–Hall card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision9/cards/EXSRC-7be05ebc93db2bd9.md`）.

Wang's independent-trial results give two narrower checks. Under a fixed mean vector, doubly stochastic averaging increases `Var(ΣX_i)=Σp_i(1−p_i)` and maximizes it at equal probabilities; this is only the variance extremum, not a convex-order theorem. The exact Poisson-binomial pmf also satisfies `f(k)^2 > C(k) f(k−1)f(k+1)`, `C(k)=max{(k+1)/k,(n−k+1)/(n−k)}`, for interior probabilities; this proves ordinary log-concavity and unimodality, not a normalized Newton hierarchy. See the Wang card（本机附件：`evidence/project/expansions/20261115/readers/L02/revision9/cards/EXSRC-5a9f56d27672e7a1.md`）.

## Two-dependent-Bernoulli increasing-concave order: corrected derived test

Egozcue–Fuentes García compare `aX+(1−a)Y` with `bX′+(1−b)Y′` for `0<b<a≤1/2`, where dependence is encoded by `k=P(X=0,Y=0)`. Their piecewise CDF difference gives a short exact route: integrate to the piecewise-linear `H(x)=∫(F_b−F_a)`, then check its support breakpoints. R02 accepted the independently derived four-undivided-endpoint criterion as a scoped partial G05.02 use.
