# Method contract: stationary-law proofs

Start at the method-selection guide（本机附件：`references/method_selection.md`） to choose a proof route and the 31-source index（本机附件：`references/source_index.md`） to locate its exact reviewed card. The guide is a router; the obligations below still apply to a new model.

## 1. Freeze the dynamics

Record the state space (E), its sigma-field, transition kernel/semigroup, and the exact law of all randomness. For a jump process give the drift vector field (b), jump intensity measure (lambda(x,dz)), update map (J(x,z)), and boundary rule. For a tree environment give the root move or child-subtree transition explicitly. Do not switch between the AGW rerooting chain, a child-only flow-rule chain, the walk path shift, and an induced last-exit chain under one label.

## 2. Separate necessary and sufficient statements

For a stationary (mu) and a justified Dynkin class (f), stationarity implies
\[
\int_E Lf\,d\mu=0.
\]
Derive this from the path identity, non-explosion/conservativeness, integrability of drift and jump compensators, and valid expectation/Fubini interchange. This implication supplies a falsification test. The converse needs a specified generator domain/core and a theorem that extends the identity to semigroup invariance; otherwise label it unproved.

For piecewise deterministic dynamics, integrate the transport part and jump part separately. Boundary terms from ordered-coordinate collisions, zero/infinity endpoints, and cutoffs must be displayed and settled. A periodic-reset example can satisfy an interior formal generator identity while retaining a boundary flux, so an interior test domain alone is insufficient.

## 3. Validate regenerative and induced constructions

For regeneration times, state whether they are stopping times. A change-of-measure formula at stopping times cannot be applied directly to non-stopping regeneration times; split by fresh epochs or another proved decomposition. Check return-time tails, finite mean, and any uniformity in parameters used by a limit theorem.

For inducing on (A\subset E), define the first return time and induced kernel. If the original invariant law is finite, use the normalized conditional law on (A); verify (0<\mu(A)<\infty), return almost surely, and the induced law. Kac's formula identifies the mean return time. A stationary law on the induced chain may be lifted to the original chain only through the verified tower/occupation construction.

## 4. Validate tree-chain hypotheses

Let (T(x)) be the descendant subtree. A consistent flow rule θ induces \(p_\theta(T,T(x))=\theta_T(x)\). Check the rule on all positive-flow descendants and distinguish:

- LPP Proposition 5.1: if \(\theta_T(x)+\theta'_T(x)>0\) for every first child, for GW-a.e. (T), then the event \(\theta_T=\theta'_T\) has GW probability zero or one. Its proof propagates equality to each child and obtains \(s\leq\sum_k p_k s^k\).
- LPP Proposition 5.2: if \(\theta_T(x)>0\) for every first child, GW-a.s., then the child chain started from GW is ergodic, though GW need not be stationary. If an absolutely continuous θ-stationary measure exists, this condition yields equivalence and ergodicity. It does not answer whether equivalence alone suffices.
- The simple-random-walk environment chain under AGW is a different chain. LPP Theorem 3.1 proves stationarity and reversibility. The full path-shift ergodicity is established via the induced Exit system of Theorem 8.1 and its tower remark.

For a path-weight or cocycle argument, compute the exact finite-path likelihood ratio as a product of the specified child-transition probabilities (and any reference transition factors). Check normalization and null sets on the actual path sigma-field before inferring equivalence or ergodicity; an unnormalized product is not a stationary-law proof. For mass transport, state the sampled rooted-network law and verify the unimodular identity for every nonnegative isomorphism-invariant transport. This root-law identity does not by itself establish invariance of a separate directional environment kernel.

If a proof needs every child subtree transient, establish this under the actual branching law. Under the nondegenerate supercritical (p_0=0), finite-mean assumptions, each descendant subtree has GW law and is transient a.s.; since there are countably many vertices, all such subtrees are transient simultaneously a.s. For (p_0>0), use the paper's conditional nonextinction / infinite-descendant subtree reduction instead of a generic claim about arbitrary transient trees.

## 5. Validate projective limits

For finite configurations \(X^{(k)}\), let \(\pi_k\) delete coordinates beyond prefix \(k\). First prove the semigroup intertwining identity
\[
P_t^{(k+1)}(f\circ\pi_k)=(P_t^{(k)}f)\circ\pi_k
\]
for the claimed bounded measurable test class, or the equivalent compatibility of transition kernels. Agreement of formal generators on a test class alone does not establish semigroup compatibility. Next prove each candidate \(\mu_k\) is invariant for \(P_t^{(k)}\) and \(\mu_k=(\pi_k)_\#\mu_{k+1}\). Kolmogorov extension supplies a consistent one-time probability law. A stationary path process requires a consistent family of path laws plus existence/regularity in a specified path space (for example, tightness or compact containment); record those conditions separately. Uniqueness of an infinite stationary law is distinct from existence and from an explicit formula.

## 6. Usage-card fields

For every source record: exact identity/version/hash; physical and printed page coordinates if both are known; theorem or proposition number; exact quantifiers and hypotheses; key proof route; what is independent derivation; what follows only by invoking an outside result; domain/model boundary; open transfer; and review disposition. Do not count an abstract, title page, metadata page, or theorem statement alone as a complete source read.
