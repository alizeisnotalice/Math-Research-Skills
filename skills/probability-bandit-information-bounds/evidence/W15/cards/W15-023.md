## Model and conclusions

For finite K, arm i has a fixed unknown reward law ν_i on [0,1], mean μ_i, with independent draws on successive pulls. The forecaster chooses allocation I_t, observes reward, and then outputs recommendation J_t; both can use auxiliary randomization. With μ*=max_i μ_i, Δ_i=μ*−μ_i, and Δ=min_{i:Δ_i>0} Δ_i, the simple regret is the random one-shot loss r_n=μ*−μ_{J_n}, while cumulative regret is R_n=Σ_{t=1}^n(μ*−μ_{I_t}). In general R_n≠Σ_{t=1}^n r_t. EDP recommendation, which samples J_n according to empirical allocation proportions T_i(n)/n, gives E[r_n]=E[R_n]/n (physical pp. 4–5, 6).

### Theorem 1: cumulative-regret upper bounds force a simple-regret lower bound

Physical pp. 6–10, theorem statement on p. 6. For any fixed forecaster and any ε:N_{>0}→R for which every Bernoulli environment has a finite environment-dependent constant C≥0 satisfying E[R_n]≤Cε(n), every unordered set of K≥3 Bernoulli reward laws with pairwise distinct means, none equal to 1, admits a reordering and a constant D≥0 such that, for all n,

$$
E[r_n] \ge \frac{\Delta}{2}\exp[-D\epsilon(n)].
$$

Here Δ is the smallest positive gap of that chosen instance. The reordering is essential: the result is existential over orderings, not a lower bound for every labeling, because a label-sensitive forecaster can happen to play a fixed best arm. Corollary 1 follows from R_n≤n, giving E[r_n]≥βe^(−γn) for some β>0, γ≥0 and a suitable ordering. Corollary 2 applies to UCB(α) for α>1: for every recommendation strategy and each such set, there are β>0, γ≥0, independent of α, and an ordering with E[r_n]≥βn^(−γα) (physical p. 7).

**Proof chain recorded from pp. 7–10.** The proof fixes the forecaster and an ordering/permutation family, writes p_n for the random recommendation distribution, and uses Bernoulli instances with selected arms replaced by zero-reward laws. Jensen/likelihood comparisons relate expected counts of the altered best/worst arms to the assumed cumulative-regret envelope. A permutation-symmetry step prevents the recommendation mass from concentrating on the best-position index for all reorderings. Combining the zero-reward events gives the exponential factor and the Δ/2 prefactor. The argument does not yield the same lower bound for every ordered tuple.

### Finite-arm upper bounds and online/pure-exploration distinction

- Proposition 1, Unif + EBA (physical p. 13): the recommendation is the empirical-best arm at round \(K\lfloor n/K\rfloor\), after exactly \(\lfloor n/K\rfloor\) samples per arm; the remainder \(n-K\lfloor n/K\rfloor\) is discarded for this recommendation. For \(n\ge K\), \(E[r_n]\le\sum_{i:\Delta_i>0}\Delta_i\exp(-\Delta_i^2\lfloor n/K\rfloor)\); a second bounded-differences form is stated with tuning \(\eta\). Corollary 3 (physical p. 14) uses this same round-\(K\lfloor n/K\rfloor\) EBA recommendation and gives \(\sup_{\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K} E[r_n]\le2\sqrt{K\ln K/(n+K)}\).
- Theorem 2, UCB(\(\alpha\)) allocation + most-played-arm recommendation (MPA), for \(\alpha>1\): \(E[r_n]\le K/(\alpha-1)\cdot(n/K-1)^{2(1-\alpha)}\), for sufficiently large \(n\), e.g. \(n\ge K+4K\alpha\ln n/\Delta^2\) and \(n\ge K(K+2)\) (p. 15). The threshold is instance dependent through \(\Delta\).
- Theorem 3, same UCB(\(\alpha\))+MPA pair: for \(n\ge K(K+2)\),
  $$
  \sup_{\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K}E[r_n]\le
  \sqrt{\frac{4K\alpha\ln n}{n-K}}+
  \frac K{\alpha-1}(n/K-1)^{2(1-\alpha)}.
  $$
  The paper writes this two-term bound as \(O(\sqrt{K\alpha\ln n/n})\) for \(\alpha>1\) (physical p. 15). With fixed \(K,\alpha\), the displayed sum directly implies that rate when \(\alpha\ge5/4\): its second term is \(O(n^{-2(\alpha-1)})\), which is then \(O(n^{-1/2})\). For \(1<\alpha<5/4\), the second displayed term decays more slowly than \(n^{-1/2}\sqrt{\ln n}\), so the displayed-sum algebra alone does not establish the printed simplification. Preserve the source’s claimed \(O\)-statement as printed; this proof-scope gap is not evidence that the algorithm’s regret rate is false. The proof uses Lemma 1’s allocation-count implication plus the UCB pull-count tail bound (pp. 16–17); the paper relies on cited prior UCB results for that tail bound.
- Appendix A.2, Theorem 5 (physical p. 32), sharpens the UCB(α)+MPA distribution-dependent bound. Let \(\mathcal I^*\) be the set of optimal arms, \(K^*=|\mathcal I^*|\), and \(\beta=(K^*/\Delta^2+\sum_{i\notin\mathcal I^*}1/\Delta_i^2)^{-1}\). For α>1,
  $$
  E[r_n]\le\frac1{\alpha-1}\sum_{i\notin\mathcal I^*}
  \left(\frac{\beta n}{\Delta_i^2}-1\right)^{2(1-\alpha)}
  $$
  for sufficiently large n, e.g. \(n/\ln n\ge(4\alpha+1)/\beta\) and \(n\ge((K+2)/\beta)(\Delta')^2\), where \(\Delta'=\max_i\Delta_i\). Its proof applies Lemma 1 with sampling weights proportional to inverse squared gaps (pp. 32–33).
- Section 5’s 10^4-instance simulations compare regimes and are empirical only (pp. 18–20, Appendix A.2 pp. 32–33). They do not certify an ordering or an asymptotic theorem.

### Continuous action-space result

Section 6 defines an environment E:X→P([0,1]); “continuous” means its mean-payoff function is continuous. A family is explorable if some forecaster has expected simple regret o(1) for every member, and explorable–exploitable if expected cumulative regret is o(n). Lemma 2 (p. 22, proof §6.3 pp. 23–25) shows these notions equivalent by staging exploration and exploitation. Theorem 4 (p. 22; proof §6.4 pp. 25–30): for a metric space X, the family of all continuous-mean environments is explorable iff X is separable. Corollary 4: for an arbitrary set X, all environments are explorable iff X is countable. The proof uses a full-support probability on a dense sequence in the separable direction; in the converse, disjoint open balls in a nonseparable metric space contradict a probability measure assigning positive mass to every open set. This is a qualitative existence characterization, with no B056-style structural finite-time or log-T rate.
