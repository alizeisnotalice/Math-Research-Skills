## Model and finite-arm information bound

For an arm set \(\mathcal A\subset\mathbb R^d\), the observation is \(r_t=\mu^\top a_t+\eta_t\), with unknown \(\mu\in\mathbb R^d\), Gaussian noise written by the source as \(\eta_t\sim N(0,\sigma)\), and \(\mathcal A\) spanning \(\mathbb R^d\). The best arm is assumed unique. The filtration is generated by past arm/reward pairs, the sampling rule is predictable, stopping is a stopping time, and the reported arm is measurable at stopping. For finite \(\mathcal A\), the goal is \(\delta\)-PAC exact identification; for continuous \(\mathcal A\), it is \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-PAC approximate identification. This is a pure-exploration stopping-time objective, not cumulative regret.

For finite \(\mathcal A\) with \(K\) arms, Theorem 1 (physical p.3, Eq. (1)) states
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[\tau]\ge \sigma^2 T_\mu^*\,\mathrm{kl}(\delta,1-\delta),\qquad
(T_\mu^*)^{-1}=\sup_{w\in\Delta_K}\min_{a\ne a_\mu^*}
\frac{(\mu^\top(a_\mu^*-a))^2}
{2(a_\mu^*-a)^\top(\sum_{b\in\mathcal A}w_bbb^\top)^{-1}(a_\mu^*-a)}.
\]
The simplex weight \(w\) is an allocation. Appendix B, Lemmas 1–2 (pp.14–15) rewrites the value as \(\max_w\psi(\mu,w)\), proves continuity/concavity and compactness/upper hemicontinuity of the optimizer correspondence \(C^*(\mu)\), and requires a unique best arm. These optimizer-set properties are what allow lazy tracking of a non-singleton set of optimal allocations.

## Lazy Track-and-Stop and proof chain

The least-squares estimator is \(\hat\mu_t=(\sum_{s\le t}a_sa_s^\top)^\dagger\sum_{s\le t}a_sr_s\). Lemma 3 (p.4; Appendix C.2, pp.15–16) gives almost-sure consistency when the minimum eigenvalue grows as \(t^\alpha\), \(0<\alpha<1\); Lemma 4 gives a nonasymptotic tail bound if a deterministic lower bound \(\lambda_{\min}(\sum_{s\le t}a_sa_s^\top)\ge ct^\alpha\) holds. Lemma 5's forced exploration cycles through a spanning subset \(A_0\) whenever the design matrix is too small, and Lemma 6 tracks a compact convex target set by cumulative proportions.

The allocation rule uses plug-in targets \(C^*(\hat\mu_t)\), with updates only on a deterministic set of times. The “lazy condition” (7), p.5, requires the chosen target to approach \(C^*(\hat\mu_s)\) for some recent \(s\ge\ell(t)\), with \(\ell(t)\to\infty\). Proposition 1 (p.5; proof Appendix E.3, p.24) combines estimator consistency, Lemma 2's upper hemicontinuity, and Lemma 6 to show empirical arm proportions approach \(C^*(\mu)\) almost surely. Expected optimality additionally assumes the quantitative tail condition (10) on tracking error; almost-sure tracking alone is not enough for that conclusion.

For each pair \((a,b)\), the source defines a Gaussian generalized log-likelihood ratio \(Z_{a,b,\epsilon}(t)\); Lemma 7 (p.5; proof Appendix D.1, pp.17–18) gives its closed form from the constrained least-squares projection. The stopping statistic is \(Z(t)=\max_a\min_{b\ne a}Z_{a,b,0}(t)\), with threshold \(\beta(\delta,t)=(1+u)\sigma^2\log(\det((uc)^{-1}\sum_{s\le t}a_sa_s^\top+I_d)^{1/2}/\delta)\) and a positive-definiteness condition on the design matrix (Eqs. (8)–(9), p.5). Proposition 2 (p.5; proof Appendix D.2, pp.18–19) uses a stopped self-normalized inequality to show the probability of stopping with an incorrect best arm is at most \(\delta\); a sampling rule that makes stopping almost surely finite is separately needed for PAC validity.

Theorem 2 (p.6) gives an almost-sure asymptotic sample-complexity upper bound for any rule (5)–(6) satisfying lazy condition (7), with stopping rule (8)–(9):
\[
\mathbb P_\mu\!\left(\limsup_{\delta\to0}\frac{\tau_\delta}{\log(1/\delta)}\lesssim \sigma^2T_\mu^*\right)=1.
\]
Do not replace \(\lesssim\) by \(\le\) with constant one: the source defines \(f\lesssim g\) on p.3 to mean that \(f(x)\le Cg(x)\) for a universal constant \(C\). Theorem 3 (p.7) adds condition (10) and bounds the normalized expected stopping time at the same constant-factor scale. Appendix F (pp.25–29) proves the almost-sure result by continuity of \(\psi\), convergence of allocations, and the GLR identity \(Z(t)=t\psi(\hat\mu_t,N(t)/t)\); its expected-time proof separately controls bad events using the polynomial tracking-error tail plus the forced-exploration least-squares tail. These are asymptotic statements in \(\delta\), not a finite-time regret guarantee.

## Continuous unit-sphere result and source discrepancy

For \(\mathcal A=S^{d-1}\), the authors restrict to \(\mu\in M(\epsilon_0)=\{\eta:\eta^\top a_\eta^*>\epsilon_0\}\). Theorem 4 (p.7) claims, for \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0/5\),
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[\tau]\ge \frac{\sigma^2(d-1)}{20\|\mu\|\epsilon}\,\mathrm{kl}(\delta,1-\delta).
\]
The proof in Appendix G.1 uses the sphere identity \(\mu^\top(a_\mu^*-a)=\|\mu\|\|a_\mu^*-a\|^2/2\), a change-of-measure constraint for every confusing parameter, successive reductions to a \((d-1)\)-dimensional set of alternatives, and a Courant–Fischer/trace bound on the expected exploration matrix. **The source's stated constant and its proof do not agree:** physical p.33, after Eqs. (34)–(36), concludes \(\mathbb E[\tau]\ge \sigma^2(d-1)/(40\|\mu\|\epsilon)\,\mathrm{kl}(\delta,1-\delta)\), whereas the Theorem 4 statement on rendered p.7 has denominator 20. Preserve both printed forms; do not cite the sharper factor 20 as proved by the displayed Appendix G.1 chain.

On the sphere, the algorithm cycles through an orthonormal basis. Its confidence-GLR stop uses \(\epsilon_t\uparrow\epsilon\), a determinant-based \(\beta(\delta,t)\), and an extra minimum-eigenvalue threshold \(\rho(\delta,t)/\|\hat\mu_t\|^2\) (Eqs. (11)–(12), p.7); Proposition 3 proves its error guarantee in Appendix G.2 (pp.34–35). Theorem 5 (p.8; Appendix G.3, pp.35–37) gives asymptotic order \(d\sigma^2/(\epsilon\|\mu\|)\log(1/\delta)\) almost surely and in expectation under the stated schedule.

## B056 boundary

This is a Gaussian linear fixed-confidence BAI source. It is useful for the architecture of information lower bounds, design-matrix allocation, tracking an optimizer correspondence, and a GLR stopping proof. It does **not** give the B056 result: feedback is Gaussian additive noise, the mean is a finite-dimensional linear score, the objective is a PAC stopping time, and its allocation geometry is a covariance design. B056 instead asks about direct \(\mathrm{Ber}(f(x))\) observations with an RKHS-constrained mean and cumulative regret governed by Bernoulli KL. The finite-arm change-of-measure idea is transferable only after rebuilding alternatives and information geometry for the direct Bernoulli/RKHS family; the sphere result and its constant discrepancy do not close that gap.
