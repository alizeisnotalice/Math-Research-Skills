## Identity and model

Emilie Kaufmann and Shivaram Kalyanakrishnan, “Information Complexity in Bandit Subset Selection,” JMLR Workshop and Conference Proceedings 30 (2013), 1–24. The first page identifies authors, title, venue, volume, and pagination.

There are K≥2 independent Bernoulli arms with means ordered p_1≥…≥p_K, and a target subset size 1≤m<K. For tolerance ε, define the acceptable set S*_{m,ε}={a:p_a≥p_m−ε}; the exact top-m set is S*_m={1,…,m}. A PAC Explore-m algorithm returns an m-element set S_δ after a finite (possibly random) number N of samples, with P(S_δ⊂S*_{m,ε})≥1−δ. The fixed-budget Explore-m-FB variant instead uses a fixed n and minimizes P(S_n⊄S*_{m,ε}) (physical pp. 1–3). This is terminal pure exploration, not cumulative-reward maximization.

**Common threshold and concentration conditions (physical p. 7).** The paper assumes \(c\in(0,1)\) and excludes the endpoint-degenerate cases \(p_m=p_{m+1}=0\) and \(p_m=p_{m+1}=1\). Lemmas 1 and 2 additionally require \(p_a\ne c\) for each arm to which the lemma is applied; this keeps \(d^*(p_a,c)>0\) in their displayed denominators. In the \(\epsilon=0\) complexity expression, an arm with \(p_a=c\) makes \(\max\{d^*(p_a,c),\epsilon^2/2\}=0\), so the corresponding reciprocal and resulting bound are infinite/vacuous. Do not extend the stated concentration lemmas across \(p_a=c\) or present that case as a finite bound without a separate argument.

For Bernoulli KL divergence d(x,y), define Chernoff information d*(x,y)=d(z*,x)=d(z*,y), where z* is the unique equalizer d(z*,x)=d(z*,y). The paper’s complexity scale is
$$
H^*_{\epsilon,c}=\sum_{a=1}^K\frac{1}{\max\{d^*(p_a,c),\epsilon^2/2\}},\qquad
H^*_\epsilon=\min_{c\in[p_{m+1},p_m]}H^*_{\epsilon,c}.
$$
The authors call this a first Chernoff-information upper bound and explicitly leave open whether a sharper arm-wise complexity removes the auxiliary c (physical pp. 3–4, 9).

An actual local Python arithmetic check at x=1/5, y=4/5 verifies that z=1/2 equalizes the two Bernoulli KL values and both evaluate numerically to ln(5/4)=0.2231435513142098. The executable and saved output are evidence/W15/math/chernoff_check.py and evidence/W15/math/chernoff_check.out.txt. This is a one-instance arithmetic check, not a proof of the general equalizer lemma or of any sample-complexity bound.

## Algorithms and theorem statements

Section 3 (pp. 4–6) defines uniform Racing (accept a candidate empirical best when its lower bound exceeds all outside upper bounds; discard the empirical worst when its upper bound falls below all inside lower bounds) and adaptive LUCB (sample the two critical arms u_t=argmax outside U_j(t), l_t=argmin inside L_j(t); stop when U_{u_t}(t)−L_{l_t}(t)≤ε). The KL intervals invert Bernoulli KL: endpoints satisfy N_a(t)d( p̂_a(t), endpoint )≤β(t,δ). By Pinsker, these confidence intervals are no wider than the corresponding Hoeffding intervals.

**Theorem 1, δ-PAC correctness (physical p. 7; proof Appendix A, pp. 16–17).**
- KL-Racing with β(t,δ)=ln(k_1Kt^α/δ), α>1 and k_1>1+1/(α−1), is correct with probability at least 1−δ.
- KL-LUCB with β(t,δ)=ln(k_1Kt^α/δ)+ln ln(k_1Kt^α/δ), α>1 and k_1>2e+1+e/(α−1)+(e+1)/(α−1)^2, is correct with probability at least 1−δ.

The proof isolates a simultaneous confidence event W; on W the accept/reject decisions cannot incorrectly remove a truly acceptable arm, and LUCB’s stopping inequality implies the returned set is ε-acceptable. A union/self-normalized bound controls Wᶜ. The source’s full derivation is in Appendix A.

**Theorem 2, KL-Racing high-probability sample complexity (physical p. 8; proof Appendix B, pp. 17–18).** For c∈[p_{m+1},p_m], ε=0, β(t,δ)=ln(k_1Kt^α/δ), α>1 and k_1>1+1/(α−1),
$$
P\left(\mathcal N\le
\max_{a\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}\left[
\frac K{d^*(p_a,c)}\cdot
\ln\frac{k_1K(H^*_{\epsilon,c})^\alpha}{\delta}
\right]+K,\quad S_\delta=S_m^*\right)\ge1-2\delta.
$$
The result gives an instance-dependent high-probability upper bound but the paper notes it is a first bound and not a matching lower bound.

**Theorem 3, KL-LUCB stopping time (physical p. 8; proof §4.3, pp. 10–11).** As printed, the statement says c∈[p_m,p_{m+1}], ε≥0, with β as above plus ln ln(k_1Kt^α/δ), α>1 and k_1>2e+1+e/(α−1)+(e+1)/(α−1)^2. It asserts δ-PAC and
$$
P\left(\tau\le C_0(\alpha)H^*_{\epsilon,c}\cdot
\ln\frac{k_1K(H^*_{\epsilon,c})^\alpha}{\delta}\right)\ge1-2\delta.
$$
For α>2 it also gives
$$
E[\tau]\le 2C_0(\alpha)H^*_{\epsilon,c}\cdot
\ln\frac{k_1K(2H^*_{\epsilon,c})^\alpha}{\delta}+K_\alpha,
$$
with K_α and C_0(α) specified on p. 8. **Printed interval issue:** the theorem’s [p_m,p_{m+1}] endpoint order is reversed relative to the paper’s stated ordering p_m≥p_{m+1}, the definition of H* (which minimizes over [p_{m+1},p_m]), and Theorem 2’s interval. Preserve the statement as printed and flag this for independent review; do not silently use the reversed/empty interval as a theorem hypothesis. The intended interval is strongly suggested by the surrounding definitions, but this card does not replace the printed statement with an asserted erratum.

**Supporting concentration chain.** Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 (physical pp. 7–8; Appendix C, pp. 17–20) bound how often a critical arm’s KL interval can contain c after a specified number of samples. The equalizer yields d*(p_a,c); the proof introduces C_1=d(p_a,c)/d*(p_a,c)>1 and an exponential rate F_{C_1}. Appendix B sums armwise error probabilities for Racing. For LUCB, §4.3/Appendices D–E control the stopping time by partitioning uncertain arms and solving a tail/expectation bound. Appendix F analyzes the distinct fixed-budget KL-LUCB-E procedure; it assumes a known H*_{ε,c}. Its endpoint calculation (physical p. 24) gives, for b≤n/(2H*_{ε,c}),
$$
e_n\le H^*_{\epsilon,c}e^{-b}+\frac{Kn}{2}e^{-b},
\quad\text{hence at }b=\frac{n}{2H^*_{\epsilon,c}},\quad
e_n\le\left(H^*_{\epsilon,c}+\frac{Kn}{2}\right)
\exp\!\left(-\frac{n}{2H^*_{\epsilon,c}}\right).
$$
The parameter-dependent fixed-budget procedure is distinct from the PAC stopping-time result. Numerical comparisons in §5 (pp. 11–13) are empirical evidence favoring adaptive sampling, not an optimality theorem.
