## Identity, model, and task

Shie Mannor and John N. Tsitsiklis, “The Sample Complexity of Exploration in the Multi-Armed Bandit Problem,” Journal of Machine Learning Research 5 (2004), 623–648; first-page metadata says submitted 1/04, published 6/04.

There are n independent Bernoulli coins/arms with unknown biases p∈[0,1]^n. A policy sequentially samples an arm, may stop at an adaptive time T, and selects I; it must terminate almost surely for every p. It is (ε,δ)-correct if for every p, P_p(p_I>max_ℓ p_ℓ−ε)≥1−δ (physical pp. 3–4). The criterion is expected sample count, except §7.3 which explicitly studies a worst-case pathwise cap. The policies may randomize and may choose stopping based on observations.

## Central lower bounds and proof chains

### Theorem 1: worst-case PAC lower bound

Physical p. 4. There are absolute positive c_1,c_2, ε_0,δ_0 such that for every n≥2, ε∈(0,ε_0), δ∈(0,δ_0), and every (ε,δ)-correct policy, some p∈[0,1]^n has
$$
E_p[T]\ge c_1\frac n{\epsilon^2}\ln\frac{c_2}{\delta}.
$$
The paper states ε_0=1/8 and δ_0=e^(−4)/4. This is existential in the hard instance p; it is not a lower bound at every fixed p.

**Proof chain, pp. 4–7.** Use n+1 hypotheses over n+1 coins. Under H_0, coin 0 has mean (1+ε)/2 and the others 1/2; under H_ℓ, coin ℓ has mean 1/2+ε while coin 0 remains (1+ε)/2 and other coins are 1/2. Correctness forces selecting 0 under H_0 and ℓ under H_ℓ. If the null expected sample count of some coin ℓ is too small, Markov’s inequality gives an event of few pulls; a martingale maximal inequality controls centered Bernoulli sums, and a likelihood-ratio lower bound on the intersection forces the alternative to select incorrectly with probability >δ. A counting/averaging argument over ℓ yields the n/ε² factor. Lemmas 1–4 supply stopping-time truncation, a maximal deviation bound, and the change-of-measure step. The constants are absolute; the proof does not require a fixed-horizon policy.

### Theorem 5: instance-dependent refinement below one half

Physical pp. 7–12. Fix a baseline \(\underline p\in(0,1/2)\). There are δ_0=e^(−8)/8 and c_1>0 depending only on \(\underline p\), such that for every ε∈(0,1/2), δ∈(0,δ_0), p∈[0,1/2]^n, and every (ε,δ)-correct policy,
$$
E_p[T]\ge c_1\left\{
\frac{(|M(p,\epsilon)|-1)_+}{\epsilon^2}
+\sum_{\ell\in N(p,\epsilon)}\frac1{(p^*-p_\ell)^2}
\right\}\ln\frac1{8\delta},
$$
where p*=max_i p_i and
$$
M(p,\epsilon)=\{\ell:p_\ell>p^*-\epsilon,\ p_\ell>\underline p,\
p_\ell\ge(\epsilon+p^*)/(1+\sqrt{1/2})\},
$$
$$
N(p,\epsilon)=\{\ell:p_\ell\le p^*-\epsilon,\ p_\ell>\underline p,\
p_\ell\ge(\epsilon+p^*)/(1+\sqrt{1/2})\}.
$$
The source’s Remark (b), p. 8, says the last threshold can be replaced by \((\epsilon+p^*)/(2-\alpha)\) for any positive α after changing proof constants. M consists of near-optimal arms that count as acceptable; N consists of more-than-ε-suboptimal arms near enough to the top to require discrimination. The \((|M|-1)_+\) accounts for the fact that at most one such arm can receive selection probability above one half under the null.

**Proof chain, pp. 8–12.** Fix a reference vector with p*=p_1 and compare H_0 to alternatives H_ℓ that raise only the ℓth coin to p_1+ε. If E_0[T_ℓ] is below \(t^*_\ell\), Markov gives few pulls with probability ≥3/4; a uniform-in-time martingale deviation event has probability >7/8; and correctness ensures the event of not selecting ℓ under H_0 has enough mass for every ℓ∈N and all but at most one ℓ∈M. Their intersection has null probability >1/8. The likelihood ratio on that event transfers it to H_ℓ and contradicts δ-correctness when the exploration threshold is too small. Summing the resulting per-arm lower bounds yields Theorem 5. The threshold condition on M/N is used in the likelihood-ratio algebra; it is not a cosmetic condition.

### Other results read in full

- **Theorem 8 (physical p. 13):** for integer s≥2 and baseline \(\underline p\in(0,1/2)\), the transformed-coin reduction extends Theorem 5 to p∈[0,1−2^(−s)]^n and ε∈(0,2^(−(s+2))), δ∈(0,e^(−8)/8). With \(\tilde p_i=1-(1-p_i)^{1/s}\), η=2^(s+1)/s, and M,N from Theorem 5 evaluated at \((\tilde p,\eta\epsilon)\), the bound is
  $$
  E_p[T]\ge\frac{c_1}{s\eta^2}
  \left\{\frac{(|M(\tilde p,\eta\epsilon)|-1)_+}{\epsilon^2}
  +\sum_{\ell\in N(\tilde p,\eta\epsilon)}\frac1{(p^*-p_\ell)^2}\right\}
  \ln\frac1{8\delta}.
  $$
  The proof repeats each simulated pull s times, feeds the policy a Bernoulli aggregate, and controls the inverse transformation derivative (pp. 13–14).
- **Theorem 9, Bayesian lower bound (physical p. 14):** for every n≥2 and small ε there exists a prior such that any prior-(ε,δ)-correct policy has \(E[T]\ge c_1(n/\epsilon^2)\ln(c_2/\delta)\), with stated ε_0=1/8, δ_0=e^(−4)/12. Proof averages H_0 and H_ℓ and reduces to Theorem 5’s armwise change-of-measure argument (pp. 14–15).
- **Theorem 10, printed statement and proof-scope audit (physical p. 16; proof pp. 16–18):** the source prints that for every \(n\ge2\) and every policy there exists \(p\in[0,1]^n\) such that simultaneously for every \(t\ge1\),
  \(E_p[R_t]\ge\min\{c_1t,\ c_2n+c_3t,\ c_4n(\ln t-\ln n+c_5)\}\),
  for positive \(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4\) and real \(c_5\). Here \(R_t=t\max_i p_i-S_t\), with \(S_t\) total heads, so this is realized-reward regret. But the proof explicitly fixes \(t\) on physical p. 16 before its Bayesian averaging and selection of a hypothesis. That argument yields a hard \(p_t\) for each fixed horizon \(t\); it does not yield one instance \(p\) working simultaneously for all horizons.
  
  Under the usual all-horizon online-policy interpretation of §6, the printed \(\exists p\,\forall t\) statement is strictly false. Set \(T_0=1\), choose an arbitrary first arm, and for \(j\ge1\) use arm \(a_j=1+((j-1)\bmod n)\) throughout the block \(T_{j-1}<s\le T_j\), where \(T_j=2^{(T_{j-1}+1)^2}\). This is one deterministic, parameter-independent online policy, and every arm—including an optimal arm for each fixed \(p\)—appears as \(a_j\) infinitely often. At each endpoint \(T_j\) whose block arm is optimal, the final block has zero regret and \(0\le E_pR_{T_j}\le T_{j-1}\), while \(\ln T_j=(T_{j-1}+1)^2\ln2\). Hence, using \(\ln 2>1/2\), \(E_pR_{T_j}/\ln T_j\le2T_{j-1}/(T_{j-1}+1)^2\to0\) for every \(p\). For fixed \(n\), the displayed minimum divided by \(\ln t\) tends to \(c_4n>0\), contradicting a single-instance all-horizon lower bound for this policy. This proof and the finite exact-arithmetic checks in math/ are scoped to the unrestricted online interpretation.
  
  **Stopping-policy caveat.** §2 defines PAC policies to terminate almost surely for every \(p\), whereas §6 states the regret result over policies without specifying post-stopping continuation. The infinite block policy above is an all-horizon online policy, not an a.s.-terminating PAC procedure. Therefore this counterexample does not refute a theorem restricted to the §2 stopping-PAC class; the paper leaves that interface unspecified. It also does not challenge the other PAC, instance-dependent, known-multiset, or permutation results recorded here. Do not use the printed all-horizon quantifier as a verified uniform-in-time lower-bound tool.
- **Theorem 11, known bias multiset (physical p. 18):** when q∈[0,1]^n and the true bias vector is an unknown permutation of q, with q*=max q known, the repeated median-elimination plus validation policy terminates a.s., is (q,ε,δ)-correct, and for every permutation has \(E[T]\le\epsilon^{-2}(c_1n+c_2\ln(1/\delta))\); constants depend on the internal δ′ (pp. 18–20). The knowledge of q* is material and is not present in the fully unknown model.
- **Theorem 13, expected sample lower bound for permutation uncertainty (physical p. 20):** there are positive c_1,c_2,ε_0,δ_1 such that for every n≥2 and ε∈(0,ε_0), there is q∈[0,1]^n so that for every δ∈(0,δ_1) and every (q,ε,δ)-correct policy, some permutation σ satisfies
  \(E_{q\circ\sigma}[T]\ge\epsilon^{-2}(c_1n+c_2\ln(1/\delta))\).
  The proof sets ε_0=1/4, δ_1=δ_0/5 with δ_0 from Theorem 5, and q=(0.5+ε,0.5−ε,…,0.5−ε) (Eq. 19). It splits the claim into a two-coin testing reduction for the log(1/δ) term and an n/ε² reduction using an auxiliary family of hypotheses and two interleaved processes (pp. 20–24).
- **Theorem 14, pathwise sample complexity (physical p. 24):** positive c_1,c_2,ε_0,δ_1 exist such that for every n≥2 and ε∈(0,ε_0), there is q∈[0,1]^n so that for every δ∈(0,δ_1) and every (q,ε,δ)-correct policy, some permutation has pathwise sample complexity at least \(c_1(n/\epsilon^2)\ln(c_2/\delta)\). The proof takes ε_0=1/8, δ_1=δ_0/2 with δ_0=e^(−8)/8 from Theorem 5 and reduces via a validation procedure (pp. 24–25). This is a max-over-paths statement, distinct from Theorem 11’s expectation bound.
