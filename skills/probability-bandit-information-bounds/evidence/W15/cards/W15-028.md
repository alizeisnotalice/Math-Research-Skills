## Model and regularity assumptions

The domain is \(\mathcal X=[0,1]^d\). At time \(t\), the learner selects \(x_t\), observes \(y_t=f(x_t)+\eta_t\), and incurs cumulative pseudo-regret \(R_T=\sum_{t=1}^T(f(x^*)-f(x_t))\). The paper assumes zero-mean sub-Gaussian noise; the main adaptivity lower bound specializes to \(d=1\) and 1/4-subgaussian noises. The reward lies in an RKHS ball \(\mathcal H_k(\mathcal X,B)\) generated by a symmetric positive-definite translation-invariant kernel \(k(x,x')=\kappa(x-x')\). Fourier decay controls regularity: Lemma 1, p.5, assumes two-sided polynomial bounds \(c_1(1+\|\omega\|^2)^{-s}\le\widehat\kappa(\omega)\le c_2(1+\|\omega\|^2)^{-s}\), with \(s>d/2\), integer. On a Lipschitz domain this gives RKHS/Sobolev norm equivalence; Lemma 2 further relates the RKHS norm to the top-order Sobolev seminorm for functions in the stated zero-boundary/compact-support Sobolev subspace.

“Misspecification” here means the learner does not know which kernel-regularity class contains \(f\); the paper compares performance on a smoother RKHS ball and a less-smooth one. It is not the same as approximation error of a misspecified likelihood or a wrong parametric mean model.

## Main adaptivity lower bound (Theorem 3 / full Theorem 9)

The concise Theorem 3 (physical p.5, Eq. (12)) takes positive integers \(m_1<m_2\), \(\tilde R>0\), 1/4-subgaussian noise, and a finite upper bound on the functions' \(L_2\) norms. It asserts existence of positive radii \(B_1,B_2\) such that any algorithm with
\[
\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{k_{m_2}}(\mathcal X,B_2)}\mathbb E[R_T]\le\tilde R
\]
must have, on the rougher class, the lower bound
\[
\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{k_{m_1}}(\mathcal X,B_1)}\mathbb E[R_T]
\ge \frac18\left(\frac{C(m_1)}{32}\right)^{\frac{m_1-1/2}{m_1+1/2}}
\left(\frac{B_1}{\bar c}\right)^{\frac1{m_1+1/2}}
\tilde R^{-\frac{m_1-1/2}{m_1+1/2}}T.
\]
Read as the displayed product: the radius factor multiplies \(\tilde R^{-\dots}T\). The full statement is Theorem 9, Appendix A.2, physical pp.12–13, with the constraints (24) on \(B_1,B_2\); those conditions are essential and are not optional. In equivalent Sobolev radii, Theorem 11 (Appendix B.1, p.13) requires
\[
\max\!\left\{\frac{3^{m_1+1/2}}{32}C(m_1)^{-m_1+1/2}\tilde R^{-1},\ K(m_1,m_2,\gamma_0,\mathcal X)L_2^{m_1/m_2}\right\}\le L_1
\le C'(m_1,m_2)^{-(m_1+1/2)}L_2^{m_1+1/2}\tilde R^{m_1-1/2},
\]
with bounded \(L_2\)-norm \(\|f\|_2\le\gamma_0<\infty\), integers \(m_2>m_1>0\), and constants defined by Eqs. (58), (64). The lower and upper radius inequalities must be jointly feasible.

### Proof mechanism and dependencies

1. **Regularity conversion.** Appendix A.1 (p.12) imports Wendland's translation-invariant RKHS/Sobolev norm equivalence and uses Adams–Fournier's seminorm equivalence on \(W_0^{m,p}([0,1])\), giving Eq. (23). This is the bridge from the later Sobolev bump construction to RKHS balls.
2. **Localized alternatives.** Appendix B.1 (pp.14–17) partitions the one-dimensional domain into \(M+1\) bins and places compactly supported rescaled smooth bump functions in them. Lemma 12 (pp.15–16) controls derivative seminorms and amplitudes; Lemma 13 (pp.16–17, Theorem 11 proof continuation) imposes constraints so each bump fits its Sobolev ball. The baseline has peak \(\Delta/2\); each alternative has peak \(\Delta\) in its own bin and agrees with the baseline away from that bin. The RKHS memberships then follow from the norm equivalence plus the radius relations.
3. **Information comparison.** For trajectory laws \(P_0^T,P_s^T\), the text uses Pinsker's inequality (Eq. (54)) and a KL calculation (Eq. (55)) to relate expected visits to bump bin \(H_s\) to the baseline. This yields the average alternative-regret inequality (56). The source labels the argument a “Proof Sketch” in main §4.2.1 and points to Hadiji (2019, §F) for a fuller version; Appendix B.1 nevertheless writes the construction and subsequent calculations through Eqs. (52)–(65) in this article.
4. **Optimization and feasibility.** Choosing \(M\) via Eq. (59) and \(\Delta\) via Eq. (60) maximizes the lower bound subject to bump feasibility. Eq. (61) gives the resulting \(T\tilde R^{-(m_1-1/2)/(m_1+1/2)}\) dependence. The last part, Eqs. (62)–(65), checks \(M\ge2\) and the second bump constraint. Omitting the radius-feasibility check would overstate the theorem.

### A sub-Gaussian/KL proof-scope warning for direct Bernoulli

The model statement and Theorem 9 allow 1/4-subgaussian noise, but Appendix B.1 Eq. (55), p.18, writes the exact identity \(D_{\rm KL}(P_0^T,P_s^T)=2\mathbb E_0[N_{H_s}(T)]\Delta^2\) and attributes it to that sub-Gaussian assumption. A sub-Gaussian tail bound alone does not determine an exact shifted-noise KL. In particular, for direct Bernoulli observations with means \(p=0.1\) and \(q=0.2\), the centered noise is 1/4-subgaussian by Hoeffding's lemma, but the one-observation Bernoulli KL is \(\mathrm{kl}(0.1,0.2)\ne2(q-p)^2\); the saved local calculation records the numerical values. Thus this exact KL step cannot be imported for B056 merely from the sub-Gaussian envelope. An additional noise-law restriction or a valid upper bound on the shifted-noise KL is needed. This flags a proof-scope gap in the stated generality; it is not by itself a proof that every lower-bound conclusion is false.

## Matérn specialization and adaptive upper bounds

For \(d=1\), Matérn-\(\nu\) Fourier decay is \(m=\nu+1/2\) (Definition 4, pp.6–7). Corollary 5 (p.6; proof Appendix B.2, p.20) requires \(0<\nu_1<\nu_2\) and \(\nu_i+1/2\in\mathbb N\), and gives the rougher-class exponent
\[
\widetilde\Omega\!\left(T^{\frac{\nu_1\nu_2+2\nu_2+1}{(\nu_1+1)(2\nu_2+1)}}\right)
\]
when the algorithm achieves the smoother-class minimax rate. A local MATLAB Symbolic Math Toolbox run substituted \(m_i=\nu_i+1/2\) into Theorem 3's exponent and simplified the difference from the printed Corollary 5 exponent to zero; see `kernel_regularity_exponent_check.m` and its output. This verifies algebraic exponent consistency only, not hypotheses or proof validity.

Theorem 6 (pp.7, 20) obtains the non-adaptive Matérn minimax rate by embedding the RKHS in a Hӧlder class and using UCB-Meta. CORRAL Theorem 7 (pp.7–8; proof Appendix B.4, pp.20–21) assumes the unknown true pair \((\nu^*,B^*)\) belongs to a supplied candidate set of nested RKHS balls and that each base algorithm has an anytime minimax regret guarantee; the user-selected \(\tilde\nu\) controls the master learning rate. Its bound is the maximum of the stated minimax-like and adaptation terms (Eq. (19), p.8). The article says matching the lower bound occurs only for certain regularity pairs. RBBE Theorem 10 (p.13; proof Appendix B.5, pp.22–23) gives a different high-probability exponent and does not match the stated lower-bound exponent generally.

**Source-internal direction typos are retained:** the explanation immediately before the CORRAL conclusion on p.8 discusses a rougher \(\nu_1^*\le\tilde\nu<\nu_2^*\), but the concluding sentence reverses the ordering/inequalities and states \(\nu_1^*\ge\tilde\nu\) and \(\nu_2^*<\tilde\nu\). Appendix B.2 p.20 also says “let \(m_1\) be an integer larger than \(m_2\)” immediately before applying Theorem 3, although Theorems 3/9 require \(m_1<m_2\) and the later substitutions for \(\nu_1<\nu_2\) use that order. Keep these as printed prose discrepancies; follow the displayed theorem hypotheses, not the conflicting sentences.

## B056 transfer boundary

This source studies continuum-armed cumulative regret under additive sub-Gaussian feedback and unknown regularity; it is useful for kernel/Sobolev norm equivalence, localized alternative constructions, and the statistical cost of adaptation between nested smoothness classes. B056's direct Bernoulli mean can be written as \(Y=f(x)+(Y-f(x))\), but this does not turn the source into a Bernoulli-KL result: the centered noise depends on \(f(x)\), and Eq. (55)'s exact quadratic KL is not justified by sub-Gaussianity alone. In addition, W15-028 is a continuum-domain regularity-adaptation lower bound, not an instance-dependent finite-action logarithmic-regret theorem with a Bernoulli-KL allocation. It does not resolve B056 or justify using a logistic link; the direct-mean model and finite-action structured alternative geometry still require their own proof.
