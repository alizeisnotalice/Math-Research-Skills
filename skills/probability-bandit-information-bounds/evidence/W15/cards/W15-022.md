## Model and objective

There are \(K\) known feature vectors \(x_a\in\mathbb R^d\), a common unknown parameter \(\theta\) with \(\|\theta\|_2\le S\), and reward mean
\[
\mu_a=\mu(\theta^\top x_a).
\]
Each reward follows a distribution from an exponential family, is almost surely bounded above by \(R\), and has the displayed mean. The inverse-link \(\mu\) is known, strictly increasing, continuously differentiable and \(k_\mu\)-Lipschitz; on the true parameter ball its derivative has positive lower bound \(c_\mu=\inf_{\|\theta\|_2\le S,\,x\in\mathcal X}\dot\mu(\theta^\top x)>0\). Features obey \(\|x\|_2\le L\). Logistic inverse link is one example (Bernoulli rewards with \(R=1,k_\mu=1/4\)), but is not the only model considered. For \(\epsilon>0\), the goal is to stop and return an \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-optimal arm: probability of reward gap greater than \(\epsilon\) is at most \(\delta\). The theorem's stated range is \(0<\delta<\min(1,d/e)\).

The initial exploratory phase uses \(E>d\) arms to ensure \(M_{E+1}=\sum_{l\le E}x_lx_l^\top\) is nonsingular; \(\lambda_0\) is its minimum eigenvalue. The source defines \(\kappa=\sqrt{3+2\log(1+2L^2/\lambda_0)}\) on physical p.7. Eq. (4) states the ML estimate through the score equation \(\sum_{l<t}(r_l-\mu(\theta_t^\top x_l))x_l=0\); no projection of this equation's solution onto \(\{\theta:\|\theta\|_2\le S\}\) is stated there. For each pair, define estimated reward gap \(\Delta_t(i,j)=\mu(\theta_t^\top x_i)-\mu(\theta_t^\top x_j)\). The paper bounds the gap error directly rather than converting a parameter confidence ellipsoid into separate arm intervals.

## Confidence sets and GLGapE sampling

For \(t>E\), Eq. (6) uses \(C_t=\alpha\sqrt{2d\log(t)\log(\pi^2dt^2/(6\delta))}\), and the theoretical value in Eq. (7) is \(\alpha=2\kappa R/c_\mu\). Eq. (8) defines
\[
C_t(i,j)=\{\Delta:|\Delta-\Delta_t(i,j)|\le\beta_t(i,j)\},\qquad
\beta_t(i,j)=C_t\max_{c,c'\in[c_\mu,k_\mu]}\|cx_i-c'x_j\|_{M_t^{-1}}.
\]
Algorithm 1 stops when the largest pessimistic alternative gap \(B(t)\) is at most \(\epsilon\). Otherwise Algorithm 2 selects the empirically best arm \(i_t\), then the most ambiguous competitor \(j_t\), and the worst-case derivative pair \((c_1,c_2)\), producing direction \(y_t=c_1x_{i_t}-c_2x_{j_t}\). Algorithm 3 solves the LP \(\min_w\sum_a|w_a|\) subject to \(\sum_aw_ax_a=y_t\); it converts the solution into frequencies \(p_a=|w_a^*|/\sum_b|w_b^*|\) and samples the arm minimizing its count-to-frequency ratio \(T_a(t)/p_a\). Thus observations of one feature inform all arms through shared \(\theta\), while allocation focuses on the selected gap direction.

## Theorem assumptions and proof dependencies

Proposition 1 (physical pp.10–11, Eqs. (13)–(20)) is a fixed-time statement: for each fixed \(t>\max(2,d)\), it gives the gap bound with probability at least \(1-\delta\), simultaneously over feature pairs \(x,x'\). Its proof uses the score equation and the mean-value theorem to write gap error in the direction \(cx-c'x'\), followed by a lower bound on the integrated Hessian and a self-normalized GLM score bound. Theorem 1 (p.11; proof p.12) is the separate all-times result: it applies Proposition 1 at each time with \(\delta_t=6\delta/(\pi^2t^2)\), then uses \(\sum_t\delta_t=\delta\) to obtain simultaneous coverage over all \(t>\max(2,d)\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\). Theorem 2 (p.12) then proves the stopped arm is \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-optimal from that event and the stopping rule.

There is a further proof-scope condition to check before relying on this confidence chain. The source defines \(c_\mu=\inf_{\|\theta\|_2\le S,\,x\in\mathcal X}\dot\mu(\theta^\top x)>0\), over the bounded true-parameter ball. In Proposition 1's mean-value/Hessian step, however, the derivative is evaluated along segments between the true \(\theta\) and the Eq. (4) score-equation estimate \(\theta_t\); the source does not state a projection or otherwise establish that this estimate exists finitely and that the whole segment remains in the region where \(\dot\mu\ge c_\mu\). Require a well-defined estimator and verify the derivative lower bound on the actual segment before invoking \(G_t\succeq c_\mu M_t\). This records a missing check in the displayed algorithm/proof interface, not a claim that the entire theorem is false.

A finite logistic example makes the estimator issue concrete. Let \(d=1,S=L=1,\theta^*=0\), feature pulls \(x_1=1,x_2=1/2\) (so \(E=2>d\) and \(M_3=5/4>0\)), and observe \(r_1=r_2=1\). For the logistic link, the score as a function of a finite candidate \(z\) is
\[
(1-\sigma(z))+\tfrac12(1-\sigma(z/2))
=\frac{e^{z/2}+e^z/2+3/2}{e^{3z/2}+e^{z/2}+e^z+1}>0.
\]
Thus Eq. (4) has no finite root on this history. Conditional on those two feature pulls and the true \(\theta^*=0\), the reward-history probability is \(1/4\). A local Decimal evaluation at \(z=-100,-20,0,20,100\) is saved in `evidence/W15/logistic_mle_well_definedness_check_results.txt`; the exact positive expression, rather than that finite grid, establishes the sign for every finite \(z\).

For the stopping-time upper bound, Eq. (22), p.12, sets
\[
H_\epsilon=\frac{18Kk_\mu}{\max(3\epsilon,\epsilon+\Delta_{\min})^2}.
\]
Theorem 3 (physical pp.13, 15–17, Eqs. (23)–(24)) gives a high-probability upper bound on \(\tau\), depending on \(d,\kappa,R,c_\mu,H_\epsilon,K,\delta\); the leading displayed form is quadratic in a logarithm and includes the additive \(\sqrt{(K+1)/(dH_\epsilon)}\) term. Its proof proceeds through Lemmas 1–4 (pp.14–15): the LP design controls uncertainty in direction \(y\); event \(E\) and the gap confidence bound limit the last pull count of every arm; \(\|w^*(y)\|_\infty\le2k_\mu\) closes the arm-count bound; then solve a scalar inequality in \(\sqrt\tau\) and invoke Theorem 1 for probability at least \(1-\delta\). This is an explicit finite-time bound, not an asymptotic optimality theorem.

## Relevance and exact boundary for B056

This is an adjacent finite-dimensional GLM pure-exploration result, not an RKHS theorem. If \(\mu(z)=z\) and reward noise fits the model, it is linear; with logistic \(\mu\), the Bernoulli mean is \(\sigma(\theta^\top x)\). Neither has the direct B056 parameterization \(Y\mid x\sim\mathrm{Ber}(f(x))\) with an arbitrary RKHS mean \(f\), except after a separate model embedding and a proof that the link and RKHS constraints match. The source's confidence width uses local derivatives and an inverse empirical feature covariance; the action allocation solves a feature-space \(\ell_1\) LP. It has no Bernoulli-KL alternative set or cumulative-regret result. Use it for gap-confidence design patterns only, and keep logistic link results explicitly labeled as logistic-link analogues rather than direct-mean Bernoulli results.
