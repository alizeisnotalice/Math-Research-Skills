## Extension and representation framework

Let `Δ` be a locally subelliptic symmetric diffusion operator on a smooth manifold `M`, essentially self-adjoint on `C₀∞(M)` for a smooth measure `μ`; the intrinsic metric is complete and the associated diffusion `X_t` is nonexplosive. Let `V≤0` be a smooth lower-bounded potential and `L=Δ+V`; the Feynman–Kac semigroup is sub-Markov. Independently, let the vertical process be a one-dimensional diffusion on `(0,∞)`,
\[
 B=a(y)^2\partial_y^2+b(y)\partial_y,
\]
killed at 0, with smooth `a,b`, `a>0`, the scale-integrability conditions (4) on PDF p. 5, an SDE realization independent of `X`, and almost-sure finite hitting time `τ`. Its hitting-time transform `K(y,λ)=E_y(e^{−λτ})` solves `BK=λK`, and the Green kernel in Lemma 2.2 is
\[
G(y,z)=\frac{h(z)}{a(z)^2}\int_0^{z\wedge y}\frac{dw}{h(w)},\qquad
G(+\infty,z)=\frac{h(z)}{a(z)^2}\int_0^z\frac{dw}{h(w)}.
\]
The extension `U_f(x,y)=∫₀∞P_t f(x)q_t(y)dt=K(y,−L)f(x)` solves `(L+B)U_f=0` with boundary value `f` (Theorem 2.4). Its proof is probabilistic: the stopped process `e^{∫₀^{t∧τ}V(X_s)ds}U_f(X_{t∧τ},η_{t∧τ})` is a martingale; Itô’s formula computes its quadratic variation and yields the PDE.

## Multipliers: strong (L^p) only (Theorems 1.1, 3.3–3.6; PDF pp. 3, 11–16)

The paper defines
\[
\Phi(\lambda)=\int_0^\infty G(+\infty,y)\,[\partial_yK(y,\lambda)]^2a(y)^2dy,
\qquad W=\Phi(-L).
\]
For its general multiplier construction it also assumes bounded `a`, at-most-polynomial growth of `G(+∞,y)` and `G(z,y)`, and rapid decrease of `K` and `∂_yK` in the indicated large variables (PDF p. 11).

- **Theorem 3.3:** the Gundy–Varopoulos-type formula represents `Wf(x)` as one half of a `y₀→∞` limit of the conditional expectation, given `X_τ=x`, of the potential-weighted vertical stochastic integral `∫₀^τ e^{−∫₀^sV(X_u)du} ∂_yU_f(X_s,η_s)a(η_s)dβ_s`. The proof pairs both sides against `g∈C₀∞(M)`, applies Itô isometry, the Green occupation identity, self-adjoint spectral calculus `U_f=K(y,−L)f`, and then takes `y₀→∞`.
- **Theorem 3.4:** for `1<p<∞`, `W` is bounded with `||Wf||_p≤½(p*−1)||f||_p` if `V≡0`, and `||Wf||_p≤3/2(p*−1)||f||_p` if `V≢0`. In the potential case the proof uses positivity for `f≥0`, Lenglart–Lépingle–Pratelli, domination by `U_|f|`, and the martingale-transform inequality; it is not an endpoint argument.
- **Theorem 3.6:** if a finite complex Borel measure `α` on `[0,∞)` represents `Φ(x)=∫(1−m/√(m²+x))dα(m)`, then `||Φ(−L)f||_p≤6(p*−1)|α|||f||_p`; for `V≡0`, the factor improves to `2(p*−1)`. All claims have `p>1`. This is a strong-Lp multiplier estimate, not weak-(1,1).

### Generalized Riesz bounds

- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 3–4):** on a compact-type Lie group with bi-invariant metric, if `Φ(x)=∫dα(m)/√(x+m)`, then `||Φ(−Δ)X_i f||_p≤cot(π/(2p*))|α|||f||_p`. The paper also prints a second-order symmetrized-transform bound `(p*−1)|α|||f||_p` for all index pairs. The source's compact-group Proposition 4.8 has the same all-`i,j` scope. W25's exact diagonal check below shows that this all-index reading fails at `i=j`; route only `i≠j` or require a separately certified matrix/operator norm. This finding does not affect the first-order estimate. The domain is the specified compact-type group; `p>1`.
- **Theorem 1.3 (PDF p. 4):** on a complete Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Weitzenböck curvature, the corresponding Hodge-de Rham/vector-bundle transform satisfies `||Φ(L)dη||_p≤6(p*−1)|α|||η||_p` for `p>1`.
- **Section 4 (PDF pp. 17–25):** `T_i` and `S_ij` are represented by stopped stochastic integrals. The `V=0` martingale integrands are differentially subordinate to the extension martingale and give factors `½(p*−1)`; nonzero `V≤0` gives `3/2(p*−1)`. On Euclidean space with Brownian vertical drift, Proposition 4.6 prints shifted first- and second-order estimates with constants `cot(π/(2p*))` and `p*−1`. Its proof uses a norm-one matrix only for `i≠j`; at `i=j` the coefficient matrix has diagonal entry 2 and norm 2. The all-index second-order bound is therefore isolated. Remark 4.7 retains its `σ↓0` heat-extension statement: the text separately gives a `c_p` estimate for diagonal `R_i²`; this does not alter the first-order result. The exact diagonal counterexample below does not refute the distinct-index estimate or Remark 4.7's first-order conclusions. The Bessel vertical-diffusion formulas in §§3.3.2 and 4.3.2 have their own parameter-dependent constants; these are not dimension-free weak-(1,1) conclusions.
- **Theorem 4.11 (PDF p. 25):** for a complete manifold and finite-dimensional bundle with locally subelliptic, nonpositive, essentially self-adjoint `L=F+∇₀+Σᵢ∇ᵢ²`, require `d_aL=Ld_a` and `||d_aη||²≤CΣᵢ||∇_{X_i}η||²`. The source prints `||Φ(−L)d_aη||_p≤6C(p*−1)|α|||η||_p` for `p>1`. A normalized-circle example with `d_a=ε∂_x`, `η=sin x`, `Φ(x)=x^(−1/2)`, `C=ε²`, and `ε=1/12` gives left/right norm ratios `1/12` and `1/24`; it refutes the generic linear-`C` reading. Keep the source-stated formula, but do not operationally use it for generic `C` without an independently corrected estimate or normalization. The `C=1` Hodge/general-multiplier uses remain available under their exact hypotheses.

### Exact diagonal scope check for the printed second-order bounds

For Proposition 4.6(14), take the one-dimensional circle with `−Δ sin x=sin x`, `i=j=1`, `m=σ=1`, and `p=2`. The multiplier applied to `∂_x² sin x` has absolute norm ratio `(√2−1)^(−1)·2^(−1/2)=1+1/√2`, while the printed right side is `(p*−1)||sin x||₂=||sin x||₂`. Thus the all-index statement fails on this diagonal instance. On physical p. 21 the proof matrix has norm one for distinct indices; when `i=j` its diagonal coefficient is 2 and the norm-one argument no longer applies. Proposition 4.8 and Theorem 1.2 carry the same all-index diagonal reading. Keep the source-printed claims visible, but route operational use only for `i≠j` or with an independently verified matrix/operator bound. ### Reusable stopped-extension interface (method-source candidate; review pending)

**Inputs.** (1) A symmetric locally subelliptic base diffusion generator `Δ`, essentially self-adjoint on `C₀∞(M)`, with complete intrinsic metric and nonexplosive diffusion; add a smooth lower-bounded potential `V≤0` when using `L=Δ+V`. (2) An independent vertical diffusion `B=a(y)^2∂_y²+b(y)∂_y`, killed at 0, satisfying the source's scale-integrability conditions, SDE assumptions, and almost-sure finite hitting time `τ`. (3) For the general multiplier theorem, the extra boundedness/growth/decay conditions on `a`, `G`, `K`, and `∂_yK` recorded above. (4) An input `f` in the source's smooth/dense class so the extension and stochastic integrals are defined.

**Construction.** Form `K(y,λ)=E_y(e^(−λτ))`, the Green kernel `G`, and `U_f(x,y)=K(y,−L)f(x)=∫P_t f(x)q_t(y)dt`, which solves `(L+B)U_f=0` with boundary data `f`. The stopped potential-weighted process is a martingale by Itô's formula. For `Φ(λ)=∫G(+∞,y)[∂_yK(y,λ)]²a(y)²dy` and `W=Φ(−L)`, Theorem 3.3 represents `Wf(x)` as one half of the `y₀→∞` limit of the conditional expectation, given the terminal base point `X_τ=x`, of `∫₀^τ exp(−∫₀^sV(X_u)du) ∂_yU_f(X_s,η_s)a(η_s)dβ_s`.

**Output/proof chain.** The source proves the identity by pairing against a smooth test function, applying Itô isometry, the Green occupation identity, and self-adjoint spectral calculus, then taking the starting-height limit. To transfer an inequality, independently verify that the target is this multiplier, that the stochastic integrand has the required subordination/matrix norm, and that conditional expectation is applied in the correct norm. This is a concrete representation method, not itself an endpoint inequality. Its operator bounds in this paper are strong-Lp for `p>1`.

**Exact endpoint boundary.** AEP-017 is a conditional method-source candidate for quota review because this stopped-extension/Green/spectral/conditional-expectation interface is reusable. It is not being counted yet. The strong-Lp conclusions alone do not establish weak-(1,1).


## Endpoint-tool boundary

The paper states strong-Lp operator estimates for `1<p<∞`; it does not state a weak-(1,1) or LlogL-to-L¹ theorem. Strong-Lp bounds imply distribution estimates at the same p by Chebyshev, but do not supply a p=1 endpoint or identify a sharp weak-Lp operator norm. The paper is fully read.
