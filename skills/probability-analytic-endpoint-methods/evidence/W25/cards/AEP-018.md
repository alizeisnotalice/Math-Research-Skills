## Model and claims read from the source

Let ((M,g)) be complete, (d\mu_\phi=e^{-\phi}d\mu_0), (L_\phi=\Delta+d(\cdot)(\nabla\phi)\) the nonnegative weighted Laplacian, and (\operatorname{Ric}_\phi=\operatorname{Ric}+\nabla^2\phi). For (a\ge0), the shifted Riesz transform is
\[
R_a=d(a^2I+L_\phi)^{-1/2}.
\]

**Theorem 3 (physical p. 4):** If (\operatorname{Ric}_\phi\ge-a^2g), (1<p<\infty), (q=p/(p-1)), (p^*=\max(p,q)), and (f,\omega) are smooth compactly supported real-valued function and one-form, then
\[
\int_0^\infty\!\int_M |\bar\nabla P_t^a f|\,|\bar\nabla\vec P_t^a\omega|\,t\,d\mu_\phi\,dt
\le 3(p^*-1)\|f\|_{L^p(M,\mu_\phi)}\|\omega\|_{L^q(T^*M,\mu_\phi)}.
\]
Here (P_t^a=e^{-t(a^2I+L_\phi)^{1/2}}) on functions and (\vec P_t^a) is the corresponding Poisson semigroup on one-forms. The proof is stated for real data; Remark 7 says the complex case follows by treating real and imaginary parts separately, with a larger constant (or by a complex Bellman function to retain the displayed constant).

**Corollary 4 (physical p. 5):** Under the same curvature hypothesis,
\[
\|R_af\|_{L^p(T^*M,\mu_\phi)}\le12(p^*-1)\|f\|_{L^p(M,\mu_\phi)}.
\]
The stated domain is the (L^p)-closure of the range of (a^2I+L_\phi) (with the (L^p) intersection specified in the display). This is a strong (L^p) upper bound, dimension-free and linear in (p^*-1); it is not presented as the optimal constant.

## Proof chain and source sections

1. §2 records the weighted Hodge Laplacian, Bochner–Weitzenböck formula, function/one-form Poisson semigroups, and semigroup domination identities (physical pp. 2–4).
2. §4 defines the Nazarov–Treil type Bellman function (Q(\zeta,\eta)=\frac12\beta(|\zeta|,|\eta|)), with (q=p/(p-1)), (\delta=q(q-1)/8), and proves its Hessian lower bound after mollification (Theorems 8–9, physical pp. 5–11).
3. In §5, regularize by (b_{s,l}(x,t)=\widetilde Q_{\kappa_{s,l}}(P_t^af(x),\vec P_t^a\omega(x))). Proposition 13 and the weighted Bochner identity use (\operatorname{Ric}_\phi\ge-a^2g) to obtain
   \(-L'_{\phi}b_{s,l}\ge2\delta|\bar\nabla P_t^af|\,|\bar\nabla\vec P_t^a\omega|\).
4. Lemma 14 controls (b_{s,l}) from above by the (p)- and (q)-semigroup averages. Proposition 15 integrates on geodesic balls and a time window, using a distance cutoff, Laplacian comparison, integration by parts, and limiting arguments (physical pp. 11–14).
5. Fatou and (\varepsilon\downarrow0) yield the bilinear estimate for (p\ge2); scaling (f\mapsto\lambda f,\omega\mapsto\lambda^{-1}\omega) and optimizing gives the constant (3(p^*-1)). For (1<p<2), the proof interchanges the function and one-form semigroups (physical p. 15). Corollary 4 follows from the Poisson-semigroup representation of (R_a) and Cauchy–Schwarz (physical p. 5).

## C207 use and migration gap

For Euclidean (M=\mathbb R^d), (\phi=0), (a=0), the transform is (\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1/2}), the vector of scalar Riesz transforms with its Euclidean vector norm. The corollary supplies the dimension-free strong-(L^p) upper bound (12(p^*-1)) for this vector operator. It does not identify the best dimension-uniform constant, prove sharpness, or produce a matching lower bound; thus it is auxiliary to C207, not a solution. It is not a weak-((1,1)) claim.

The local arithmetic check at (p=4,q=4/3) gives (p^*=4), Theorem 3 constant (9), and Corollary 4 constant (36); this checks the displayed formulas only, not their analytic proof.
