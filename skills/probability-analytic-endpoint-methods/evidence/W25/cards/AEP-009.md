## Theorem 1: unweighted weak-(1,1) (PDF pp. 1, 4, 6–8)

Let `T` be a Calderón–Zygmund operator on `R^n`. Its complex kernel has size `|K(x,y)| ≲ |x-y|^{-n}` and Hölder smoothness of some order `δ>0` in each variable when displacement is at most half the separation. Assume `T` is bounded on `L²(R^n)` and off-support integration represents `Tf` for smooth compactly supported `f`. Then every `f ∈ L¹(R^n)` satisfies:

\[
\|Tf\|_{L^{1,\infty}}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\,|\{|Tf|>\lambda\}|\lesssim_{n,T}\|f\|_1.
\]

The result is a scalar-output weak-distribution estimate; the paper does not give an explicit or sharp constant. The kernel and L² assumptions are Euclidean. The proof begins with nonnegative continuous compactly supported `f`. For signed or complex input, split into positive and negative real and imaginary parts and use the weak-quasinorm triangle inequality; this changes only the implicit constant.

### Proof chain actually used

1. **Cancellation away from an enlarged cube (Lemma 1, PDF p. 4).** If `b` is supported in `Q(x,r)` and has integral zero, write
   \[
   Tb(y)=\int_{Q(x,r)}(K(y,z)-K(y,x))b(z)\,dz.
   \]
   Integrate outside `Q(x,2√n r)`, use Fubini and the smoothness estimate, and integrate the radial tail `|x-z|^δ |x-y|^{-n-δ}`. This gives an exterior L¹ bound `≲ ||b||₁`.
2. **Dilated finite unions (Lemma 2, PDF pp. 5–6).** Order the cubes by decreasing radius, assign each point to its first cube, and show each newly added portion of the dilated union lies in the dilation of its original portion. Doubling then controls the dilated union by the original union. For Lebesgue measure the dilation factor is `a^n`.
3. **Good part (Theorem 1, PDF pp. 6–7).** With `Ω={f>λ}`, use a Whitney decomposition `Ω=⊔ᵢ Qᵢ`, `g=f 1_{Ωᶜ}`, and `bᵢ=f 1_{Qᵢ}`. The bounds `||g||∞≤λ`, `||g||₁≤||f||₁`, and L² boundedness yield
   \[
   |\{|Tg|>\lambda/2\}|\lesssim \lambda^{-2}\|g\|_2^2\le\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_1.
   \]
4. **Matched-measure cancellation.** For finite `N`, let `aᵢ=∫_{Qᵢ} bᵢ` and choose centered cubes successively so the disjoint sets `Eᵢ=Q(cᵢ,rᵢ) minus the union `⋃_{k<i}E_k`` have `|Eᵢ|=aᵢ/λ`. Then `E=⋃_{i≤N}Eᵢ=⋃_{i≤N}Q(cᵢ,rᵢ)` and each `bᵢ-λ1_{Eᵢ}` has zero integral. With `E*=⋃_{i≤N}Q(cᵢ,2√n rᵢ)`, the exceptional term `|Ω∪E*|` is controlled by the finite-union lemma and `|E|=Σᵢaᵢ/λ≤||f||₁/λ`.
5. **Three bad-part terms (PDF pp. 7–8).** Outside `Ω∪E*`, Lemma 1 and Chebyshev control `T(b_N-λ1_E)` in L¹. The remaining `T1_E` term uses L² boundedness and `|E|≤λ⁻¹||f||₁`. These estimates are uniform in `N`; pass to `b` by the finite-sum limit and the L² convergence/localization used in the paper.

This route inserts cancellation with sets `Eᵢ` of matched Lebesgue measure. It does not invoke a point-mass weak estimate, the kernel size estimate, duality with `T*`, or maximal-truncation control in the unweighted proof; it does use L² boundedness, kernel smoothness, and doubling to control the relevant terms.

## Theorem 2: weighted weak-(1,1) (PDF pp. 2, 8–11)

For `1<p<∞`, `w∈A_p`, and `f∈L¹(w)`,
\[
\|T(fw)w^{-1}\|_{L^{1,\infty}(w)}
\lesssim [w]_{A_p}\max\{p,\log(e+[w]_{A_p})\}\,\|f\|_{L^1(w)}.
\]
The source states this weighted estimate for level sets measured by `w(x)dx`. Its proof route is not accepted for use yet: it relies on Lemma 2 (PDF p. 5), whose proof claims `μ(tilde F_j)≤C_{μ,a}μ(F_j)` for an arbitrary measurable piece `F_j` because `tilde F_j` is its affine dilation. Doubling controls centered cubes, not arbitrary measurable sets under that affine map. A finite check makes the gap concrete: for `μ(dx)=|x|dx` on `R`, `F=[0,1/10]`, and `A(x)=2(x−1)+1`, `μ(AF)/μ(F)=36`, although the centered-cube dilation factor at `a=2` is at most `4`; this weight is in `A_3`. This does not disprove Lemma 2 or Theorem 2. It means the weighted proof chain needs a separate valid union/dilation estimate for the chosen measure before the tool can use it. The unweighted Lebesgue route remains supported by direct dilation scaling.

## Transfer boundary and tool use

- **Direct use:** real or complex scalar `T` on `R^n` meeting the stated standard-kernel, L², and representation hypotheses; Theorem 1 yields an implicit constant depending on `n,T`.
- **Do not infer:** an explicit/sharp constant, a vector-valued operator theorem, a non-Euclidean result, or an endpoint statement for an operator missing the stated kernel/L² assumptions. Theorem 2 remains a source-stated weighted claim; do not use its printed proof until the arbitrary-set dilation step is independently justified.
- The construction is useful when a proof needs cancellation for pieces `bᵢ` without replacing them by atoms. It is a genuine weak-`(1,1)` source for this tool, unlike a strong-`L^p` theorem with `p>1` alone.
