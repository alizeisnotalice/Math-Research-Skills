## Main result: exact hypotheses and output

The paper proves the A2 conjecture for a **general standard Calderón–Zygmund operator**. Fix `N≥1` and `T∈𝓛(L²(R^N))` with off-support kernel representation and standard bounds, for some `α∈(0,1]`:
\[
|K(x,y)|\le C|x-y|^{-N},\qquad
|K(x+h,y)-K(x,y)|+|K(x,y+h)-K(x,y)|
\le C|h|^\alpha |x-y|^{-N-\alpha}
\]
whenever `|x−y|>2|h|>0`. For positive-a.e. `w∈A₂`, Theorem 1.3 (printed p.1475, physical p.3) establishes
\[
\|Tf\|_{L^2(w)}\le C(T)[w]_{A_2}\|f\|_{L^2(w)}.
\]
The bound is linear in `[w]_{A₂}`, optimal in general; `C(T)` depends on the operator's CZ data (including dimension, Hölder exponent/constants). By sharp extrapolation it yields the printed weighted `L^p(w)` dependence `[w]_{A_p}^{max(1,1/(p−1))}` for `1<p<∞` (equation (1.2), physical p.2). The theorem is a weighted strong-type result, not an unweighted scalar weak-`(1,1)` theorem.

## Proof architecture and exact reusable components

1. **Pérez–Treil–Volberg reduction (Theorem 1.3 discussion, physical pp.3–4; printed pp.1475–1476).** The A2 estimate reduces to the testing inequality
   \[
   \|T(w1_Q)\|_{L^2(w^{-1})}\le C(T)[w]_{A_2}w(Q)^{1/2}
   \tag{1.4}
   \]
   for all cubes `Q⊂R^N`. The paper also writes a two-sided weighted weak-(L^2) reduction, but explicitly says the second estimate is **not** used in its proof; it is included only to indicate a connection with weak-type bounds. Do not describe the present paper as proving a weak-L1 reduction.
2. **Random dyadic “good cube” decomposition (§2.D–§3, Theorem 3.1, physical pp.7–13; printed pp.1479–1485).** A random binary sequence selects one dyadic grid. A cube `I` is bad if it is close to the boundary of a much larger cube `J` with `ℓ(J)≥2^rℓ(I)`; with `γ=α/[2(N+α)]`, the bad probability obeys `π_bad≲_{γ,N}2^(−rγ)`. Independence of a cube's position and goodness permits an exact expected Haar martingale-difference representation using good cubes and good pairs. Theorem 3.1 is an identity in the limit, not an estimate that simply drops all bad terms in one fixed grid.
3. **Good dyadic shifts (§4, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2, physical pp.13–20; printed pp.1485–1492).** A shift `X=Σ_K A_K` with parameters `(u,v)` is built from Haar input/output cubes at relative scales `(2^(−u),2^(−v))`; it is good when its participating Haar cubes stay a specified distance from the boundary of `K`. Theorem 4.2 writes the bilinear form of (T) as a weak limit of random averages of good, finite, uniformly `L²`-bounded shifts, with coefficients
   \[
   2^{-\max(u,v)\alpha/2},\qquad u,v\ge r.
   \]
   This representation depends on the standard kernel smoothness and random-grid construction, and the shift operator norms are uniform in `(u,v,n,β)`.
4. **Unweighted shift endpoint (Proposition 5.1, physical p.21; printed p.1493).** A bounded dyadic shift with parameters `(u,v)` maps `L¹(R^N)→L^{1,∞}(R^N)` with norm `O(u)`. The proof is a dyadic Calderón–Zygmund decomposition. Cancellation kills terms from ancestors above `u` generations; the remaining (u) ancestors yield the linear dependence. This is a weak-(1,1) statement for the **individual bounded dyadic shift class**, with a parameter-dependent constant; it is not yet a dimension-free estimate for a given Riesz transform.
5. **Weighted shift testing (Theorem 6.1, physical pp.22–23; printed pp.1494–1495) and main proof (§7, physical pp.23–30; printed pp.1495–1502).** For a good, finite, bounded shift `X` with parameters `(u,v)`, `w∈A₂`, `σ=w^(−1)`, and any cube `Q⊂R^N`,
   \[
   \|X(w1_Q)\|_{L^2(\sigma)}
   \lesssim 2^{\max(u,v)\gamma N/2}uv\,[w]_{A_2}\,w(Q)^{1/2}.
   \]
   If `Q` is a cube of the shift's own dyadic grid, the exponential factor is unnecessary. The proof handles large scales by the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function and Buckley's weighted estimate, then small scales by scale pigeonholing, local A2 freezing, stopping cubes, John–Nirenberg exponential tails for the restricted shift, and the goodness margin for arbitrary (Q). Combining this with Theorem 4.2 is summable because
   \[
   \frac\alpha2-\frac{\gamma N}{2}>\frac\alpha4>0.
   \]
   The paper proves the exact main estimate; this card only records its dependency chain and does not transfer it to a new operator without checking the CZ and shift hypotheses.

## Limits and relation to C208

C208 asks for one absolute constant uniformly over all dimensions (N), all integrable inputs, thresholds, and a **single scalar-coordinate Riesz transform** in unweighted Lebesgue measure. This paper proves weighted `L²(w)` strong type for each standard CZ operator with a constant `C(T)` carrying its CZ data. Its Proposition 5.1 gives weak-L1 only for an individual shift, with `O(u)` dependence; Theorem 4.2 then uses an averaged shift representation and the paper sums it for the weighted A2 testing problem. The paper does not establish that this series transfers the shift endpoint bound to a dimension-free Riesz weak-(1,1) estimate. Nor does weighted strong `L²`/extrapolated `L^p` alone imply the desired endpoint as `p↓1`. Thus this is a concrete reusable **CZ decomposition / random dyadic representation / weighted testing method**, but no direct C208 mapping or resolution claim is made.
