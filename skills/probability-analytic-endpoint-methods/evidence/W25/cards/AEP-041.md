## Exact operator class and theorem (physical pp. 1–3)

Here \(T\) is an \(L^2(\mathbb R^n)\)-bounded Calderón–Zygmund integral operator, represented off \(\operatorname{supp}f\) by a kernel \(K(x,y)\) satisfying
\[
|K(x,y)|\le c|x-y|^{-n},\qquad
|K(x,y)-K(x',y)|+|K(y,x)-K(y,x')|
\le c\frac{|x-x'|^\delta}{|x-y|^{n+\delta}}
\]
when \(|x-x'|<|x-y|/2\), for some \(0<\delta\le1\). The \(A_2\) characteristic is
\[
[w]_{A_2}=\sup_Q\frac{w(Q)w^{-1}(Q)}{|Q|^2}.
\]
Theorem 1.1 states
\[
\|T\|_{L^2(w)\to L^2(w)}\le c(n,T)[w]_{A_2},
\qquad w\in A_2.
\]
This is a weighted strong-\(L^2\) estimate; it is not itself a Lebesgue weak-\((1,1)\) theorem.

## Truncation notation: printed defect and operational scope

On physical p. 3, the article prints the truncation region as \(\varepsilon<|y|<\nu\) in the integral of the two-variable kernel \(K(x,y)\). This printed annulus is centered at the origin and need not exclude \(y=x\), so it does not, literally as printed, remove the kernel singularity on the diagonal. I record this notation defect rather than silently treating the printed formula as centered.

For the method discussion below, \(T^\natural_{\rm cent}\) means the standard centered truncation
\[
T^\natural_{\rm cent}f(x)
=\sup_{0<\varepsilon<\nu<\infty}
\left|\int_{\varepsilon<|x-y|<\nu}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|.
\]
The sparse-method interpretation is restricted to this centered truncation and requires verifying its standard classical weak-\((1,1)\) input together with the stated Calderón–Zygmund assumptions. No theorem is asserted here for the literal origin-centered region \(\varepsilon<|y|<\nu\), and the sparse argument is not presented as a new proof of weak type \((1,1)\).

## Reusable local-oscillation and sparse mechanism (physical pp. 3–11)

Under that operational centered-truncation convention, the concrete intermediate output is inequality (1.2): for a Banach function space \(X(\mathbb R^n)\),
\[
\|T^\natural_{\rm cent} f\|_X
\le c(n,T)\sup_{\mathcal D,\mathcal S}
\|A_{\mathcal D,\mathcal S}|f|\|_X,\qquad
A_{\mathcal D,\mathcal S}g
=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal S}\langle g\rangle_Q\mathbf1_Q.
\]
Here \(\mathcal D\) ranges over general dyadic grids and \(\mathcal S\) is sparse: cubes at a fixed generation are disjoint, generation unions are nested, and the next-generation union occupies at most half of each parent cube. The source then obtains Theorem 1.1 by applying the sparse-average \(L^2(w)\) estimate. Neither this weighted conclusion nor the sparse domination removes the need for the CZ kernel and \(L^2\) hypotheses.

The proof chain is specific. Theorem 2.2 decomposes a function by local median oscillations into a sparse sum. Proposition 2.3 bounds local oscillations of \(Tf\) and the centered maximal truncation on \(Q\) by
\[
c\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m\delta}\langle|f|\rangle_{2^mQ},
\]
using kernel smoothness on the exterior part and the classical weak-\((1,1)\) estimate for the interior part. This first yields the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator plus positive dyadic complexity-\(m\) averaging operators
\[
T_{\mathcal S,m}f(x)
=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal S}\langle f\rangle_{2^mQ}\mathbf1_Q(x).
\]
For \(m=0\), \(T_{\mathcal S,0}f=A_{\mathcal D,\mathcal S}f\) directly, so this term is already a sparse average. The complexity estimate derived from Proposition 3.1 and Lemmas 3.2–3.3 is for \(m\ge1\):
\[
\sup_{\mathcal S}\|T_{\mathcal S,m}f\|_X
\le c(n)m\sup_{\mathcal D,\mathcal S}
\|A_{\mathcal D,\mathcal S}f\|_X,\qquad f\ge0.
\]
In that derivation the formal adjoint \(A^*_{m,\alpha}\) has \(L^2\) norm at most 8 (Proposition 3.1), weak-\(L^1\) norm at most \(c(n)m\) (Lemma 3.2), and local oscillation at most \(c(n)m\langle f\rangle_Q\) (Lemma 3.3), for positive integer \(m\). Applying Theorem 2.2 again, now to the adjoint, gives the sparse bound of size \(c(n)m\). The series is then controlled by the direct \(m=0\) sparse term plus
\[
\sum_{m=1}^\infty 2^{-m\delta}c(n)m<\infty.
\]
A covering by \(2^n\) shifted dyadic grids reduces arbitrary cubes to dyadic cubes with side length at most six times the original. Thus the method's actual inputs are the CZ size and smoothness bounds, \(L^2\) boundedness, the classical weak-\((1,1)\) input for centered truncations, dyadic covering and sparse averaging. Its output is centered maximal-truncation domination by positive sparse operators, from which the stated weighted \(A_2\) bound follows.

The classical weak-\((1,1)\) estimate used inside Proposition 2.3 is an input for this CZ class, not the theorem being established by the sparse argument. A transfer to C207 must verify that its target operator and truncations satisfy this exact Euclidean kernel representation and hypotheses. Neither a weighted strong-\(L^2\) estimate nor sparse domination alone establishes an endpoint for an operator outside that class.
