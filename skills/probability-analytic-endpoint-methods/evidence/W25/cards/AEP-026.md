## Scope and use

The paper surveys how sharp martingale inequalities feed into strong-Lp estimates for the Hilbert, first-/second-order Riesz, Beurling–Ahlfors, and other multipliers; it also connects Burkholder's function to Bellman functions and rank-one/quasiconvexity. Its concrete reusable mechanism is: **quadratic-variation domination / orthogonality for martingales → a supermartingale majorant (Burkholder/Bellman function) → sharp strong-Lp or selected martingale weak-type estimates; for Riesz operators, a stochastic-integral representation plus conditional-expectation projection → strong-Lp operator estimates.**

The source itself draws the endpoint boundary explicitly: §3.4 says weak-type martingale inequalities do not in general pass through the conditional expectation in the Riesz representation (printed p.35, physical p.37). Problem 7, equation (3.4.16), is on that same printed p.35/physical p.37 and asks for the optimal strong-weak constant; its p=1 discussion says a dimension-independent constant is not known and cites Janakiraman's `C log(n)` upper estimate. Physical p.38/printed p.36 continues the discussion and states Problem 8 for Gaussian Riesz transforms. Thus the article is a rigorous martingale-method source and an important boundary check; it does **not** prove C208.

## Exact statements and proof routes

### 1. Sharp martingale-transform and differential-subordination bounds

- **Discrete transform (Theorem 1.1.2, printed p.3; physical p.5).** For a real martingale `f={f_n}` with differences `d_k` and a real predictable sequence `v_k` with `|v_k|≤1`, the martingale transform satisfies
  \[
  \|v*f\|_p\le (p^*-1)\|f\|_p,\qquad 1<p<\infty,
  \quad p^*=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\}.
  \]
  The constant is best possible. This theorem is attributed to Burkholder [37]; the survey records it but does not replace that primary proof.
- **Continuous-time differential subordination (Definition §2.1 and Theorems 2.1.1–2.1.2, printed pp.8–9; physical pp.10–11).** For right-continuous martingales, `Y << X` means `|Y_0|≤|X_0|` and `[X]_t−[Y]_t` is nonnegative and nondecreasing. For continuous-path `ℓ²`-valued martingales (Theorem 2.1.1), and separately general continuous-time parameter `ℓ²` martingales (Theorem 2.1.2),
  \[
  \|Y\|_p\le(p^*-1)\|X\|_p,\quad 1<p<\infty.
  \]
  The inequalities are sharp (strict away from p=2 when the norm is finite and nonzero). Theorem 2.1.2 also states a maximal tail inequality for `sup_t(|X_t|+|Y_t|)`; preserve its printed form if using it. The paper attributes the continuous-path result to [23] and the general-time extension to [159].
- **Brownian stochastic-integral corollary (Theorem 2.1.3, printed p.10; physical p.12).** If the coordinate stochastic integrands obey `Σ_i |K^i_t|² ≤ Σ_i |H^i_t|²` a.e. in time, then the associated `ℓ²` martingales are differentially subordinate and the same sharp constant follows. Matrix martingale transforms with predictable operator norm bound inherit this via (2.1.6) and Corollary 2.1.1 (printed pp.10–11; physical pp.12–13).

### 2. Orthogonality improves the constants; proof outline and its limit

- **Theorem 2.2.1 (printed p.11; physical p.13).** For real-valued continuous-time orthogonal martingales `X,Y` with `Y << X`,
  \[
  \|Y\|_p\le \cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2p^*}\right)\|X\|_p,
  \qquad
  \big\|(|X|^2+|Y|^2)^{1/2}\big\|_p\le
  \csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2p^*}\right)\|X\|_p,
  \]
  for `1<p<∞`; for all `λ≥0`, `λ P(|Y|≥λ)≤D_1||X||_1`, with the source's sharp-constant formula `D_1=π²/(8β(2))`. At equation (2.2.4), printed p.12 (physical p.14), the source also prints `≈1.328434313301` and `β(2)≈0.9159655`; direct evaluation of that displayed formula gives about `1.346885`, so the printed decimal is internally inconsistent. The exact formula and stated sharpness are retained, while the decimal is flagged as a source numerical error pending independent review. The strong constants are sharp. Orthogonality here is the cross-variation condition `⟨X,Y⟩_t=0`; it is stronger than mere subordination.
- **Theorem 2.2.2 (printed pp.11–12; physical pp.13–14).** Janakiraman's sharp weak `(p,p)` extension is stated for `1≤p≤2`; it is a martingale estimate, not a Riesz weak `(1,1)` theorem.
- **Brownian-integrand form (Corollary 2.2.1, printed p.13; physical p.15).** For `X=∫H·dB`, `Y=∫K·dB`, with `K_t·H_t=0` and `|K_t|≤|H_t|` a.e., the sharp orthogonal bounds above hold; a matrix transform with `[A(t)v]·v=0` is covered by Corollary 2.2.2.
- **Proof outline (printed pp.16–21; physical pp.18–23).** The paper uses `V(x,y)=|y|^p−c^p|x|^p` and a majorant `U`, where `c` is the norm-bound constant in `||Y||_p≤c||X||_p`; for the subordinate inequality it applies Itô's formula, uses `V≤U`, nonpositivity of `U` on `|y|≤|x|`, and the sign of the Hessian/quadratic-variation term under `d⟨X⟩−d⟨Y⟩≥0`. In the orthogonal real case an angular harmonic majorant yields cot/csc constants. The text expressly says the rigorous Itô/approximation details needed to complete this outline are Lemma 1.1 and Proposition 1.2 of [23], and sends readers to [23,24] (printed p.20; physical p.22). Do not claim this survey page sequence reproduces all technical proof details of those primary sources.
- **Method algorithm (§2.4, printed pp.20–21; physical pp.22–23).** Find the smallest norm constant `c` in `||Y||_p≤c||X||_p`, and use the moment difference `V(x,y)=|y|^p−c^p|x|^p`. The source seeks a majorant `U≥V` with `U(0,0)=0` and nonpositive Itô drift on the allowed quadratic-variation directions. Its displayed expectation chain is `E V(X_t,Y_t)≤E U(X_t,Y_t)≤E U(X_0,Y_0)=U(0,0)=0`; this last equality requires the zero-start case `X_0=Y_0=0`. For a nonzero start, `U(0,0)=0` alone is insufficient; the argument needs an initial condition such as `E U(X_0,Y_0)≤0`. The supermartingale expectation step also requires the Itô/localization/integrability hypotheses that make `U(X,Y)` and the local-martingale term legitimate. The source expressly sends the approximation details to Lemma 1.1 and Proposition 1.2 of [23] (printed p.20; physical p.22) and full details to [23,24]; do not claim those conditions are established by this survey outline.

### 3. Hilbert/Riesz stochastic representation and endpoint obstruction

- **Hilbert transform (§3.1, printed pp.23–24; physical pp.25–26).** Pichorides' sharp strong-Lp estimate and Davis' sharp weak-L1 bound for the one-dimensional Hilbert transform are related to the orthogonal martingale estimates. Theorem 3.1.1 states Janakiraman's sharp weak `(p,p)` estimate for `1≤p≤2`.
- **Harmonic functions and first-order Riesz transforms (§3.1–3.2, printed pp.24–27; physical pp.26–29).** Orthogonal, differentially subordinate harmonic functions satisfy sharp strong-Lp and weak `(p,p)` bounds with harmonic measure. For `f∈C_0^∞(R^n)`, `R_j` is normalized to Fourier multiplier `iξ_j/|ξ|`; Poisson extension gives `R_j f=∫_0^∞ ∂_jP_y f\,dy` and `R f=∇(-Δ)^{-1/2}f` (equations (3.2.1)–(3.2.6), printed pp.25–27; physical pp.27–29).
- **Background-radiation representation (§3.3, printed pp.27–28; physical pp.29–30).** Extend `f` by the Poisson semigroup to the upper half-space, compose with the background-radiation Brownian process, transform its stochastic integral by a bounded matrix `A(x,y)`, then project by `E[·|B_0=(x,0)]`. Occupation-time identities (3.3.2)–(3.3.5) identify the boundary distribution/Green kernel.
- **General projected transform and Riesz conclusion (§3.4, printed pp.29–36; physical pp.31–38).** Equation (3.4.1) defines `T_A f(x)=E[A^*f|B_0=(x,0)]`. Theorems 3.4.1–3.4.2 give strong-Lp estimates for bounded matrix fields; for real matrices with `[A(x,y)v]·v=0`, the scalar constant improves to `cot(π/(2p*))`. The skew matrix `A_j` in (3.4.8) has norm one and yields `T_{A_j}=R_j`; Corollary 3.4.3 (equations (3.4.11)–(3.4.12), printed p.33; physical p.35) recovers sharp strong-Lp coordinate estimates.
- **Critical endpoint discussion (unnumbered projection paragraph and Problem 7, printed p.35; physical p.37; Remark 3.4.2 and Problem 8 continue on printed p.36/physical p.38).** The paper expressly explains that weak martingale inequalities do not directly yield weak-type bounds for singular integrals because conditional expectation/projection need not preserve weak type. Problem 7 equation (3.4.16) is printed p.35/physical p.37; the following page (printed p.36/physical p.38) continues with Problem 8 for Gaussian Riesz transforms. For the Euclidean Riesz transform it lists the dimension-free p=1 estimate as open; it cites a `C log(n)` estimate as best then available. This is a method boundary, not an endpoint theorem.
- **Space-time Brownian second-order route (§3.8–3.10, printed pp.42–58; physical pp.44–60).** Heat extensions composed with space-time Brownian motion give `S_A`; Theorem 3.9.1 obtains the bilinear Littlewood–Paley representation as `T→∞` (with details cited to [19]); Theorem 3.9.2 and the matrix choices in §3.10 yield strong-Lp second-order Riesz/Beurling estimates. The source itself says weak `(1,1)` constants remain unknown for second-order Riesz/Beurling transforms (§3.10 Remark 3.10.1, printed p.58; physical p.60).

### 4. Lévy multipliers and calculus-of-variations connections

- **Lévy multipliers (§4, printed pp.63–72; physical pp.65–74).** Under a Lévy measure satisfying `ν({0})=0` and `∫ |x|²/(1+|x|²) dν(x)<∞`, bounded jump modulation `||φ||∞≤1`, bounded Gaussian modulation `||ψ||∞≤1`, and finite spherical measure `μ`, equation (4.1.2) defines a normalized multiplier `M`; Theorem 4.1.1 gives `||S_M f||_p≤(p*−1)||f||_p`, `1<p<∞`. The survey sketches the symmetric compound-Poisson proof (martingale jump transform, then conditional expectation and multiplier identification) and says the general theorem is reduced by symmetrization/approximation; full details are in [13,14]. Several examples recover second-order Riesz/Beurling multipliers. This is another strong-Lp method, not weak-L1 evidence.
- **Morrey/Iwaniec (§5, printed pp.73–80; physical pp.75–82).** The Burkholder function's rank-one convexity is related to quasiconvexity and Iwaniec's Beurling–Ahlfors conjecture. The main quasiconvexity statements are posed as open questions/conjectures in this survey; cited results have their own scope.
- **History/acknowledgments/references (§6–7 and bibliography, printed pp.81–97; physical pp.83–99).** Read through the final references; no external appendix or deferred supplemental PDF was supplied with this source.

## C208 and tool boundary

C208 asks for an absolute constant independent of every dimension `d`, for all `f∈L¹(R^d)` and all `t>0`, controlling the scalar-coordinate distribution `t·Leb{|R_j f|>t}` by `||f||_1`. The source's nearest concrete Riesz output is the sharp **strong-Lp** bound for `R_j`, `1<p<∞`, from a Brownian martingale representation plus conditional expectation. Strong-Lp bounds whose constants diverge as `p↓1` do not give the desired weak-(1,1) estimate by a limit argument. The source explicitly identifies this gap. Its orthogonal-martingale weak-L1 theorem applies before the projection; its Hilbert-transform endpoint is one-dimensional. No C208 input is mapped to a theorem here, no formal C208 mapping is proposed, and no resolution claim is made.
