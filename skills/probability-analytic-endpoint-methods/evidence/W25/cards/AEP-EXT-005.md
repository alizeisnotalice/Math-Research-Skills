## Model and theorem (physical pp. 1–2)

Let $μ$ be a positive Radon measure on $ℝ^d$ with polynomial growth
$$μ(B(x,r))≤C_0r^n\quad(x∈ℝ^d,r>0),\qquad 0<n≤d.\tag{1.1}$$
Doubling is explicitly not assumed. The kernel $k(x,y)$ is Calderón–Zygmund: $|k(x,y)|≤C|x-y|^{-n}$, and for some $0<δ≤1$,
$$|k(x,y)-k(x',y)|+|k(y,x)-k(y,x')|≤C\frac{|x-x'|^δ}{|x-y|^{n+δ}},\quad |x-x'|≤|x-y|/2.$$
The truncated operators $T_εf(x)=\int_{|x-y|>ε}k(x,y)f(y)\,dμ(y)$ are uniformly bounded on $L^2(μ)$; this is the paper's meaning of $L^2$-boundedness. **Theorem 1.1** then says $T$ maps complex Radon measures $M(ℂ)$ into $L^{1,∞}(μ)$, hence is weak type $(1,1)$ on $L^1(μ)$. The theorem is uniform in truncation. Constants are implicit and depend on the growth, kernel regularity/size, and uniform $L^2$ bounds; no sharp explicit constant is stated.

## Decomposition and proof chain

- **Doubling-cube setup and Lemma 2.3 (§2, physical pp. 3–4; physical p. 3 rendered and visually checked).** The definition uses $\alpha>1$ and $\beta>\alpha^n$. Keep the two existence thresholds separate: Remark 2.1 proves existence of arbitrarily large centered doubling cubes from growth when $\beta>\alpha^n$; Remark 2.2 states existence of arbitrarily small centered doubling cubes for $\mu$-a.e. point only when $\beta>\alpha^d$. The differentiation sequence used immediately afterward is $(2,2^{d+1})$, which satisfies the small-cube threshold. The later $(6,6^{n+1})$ cubes are selected large, so the large-cube condition is the relevant one there. These are not interchangeable. For example, for $\mu=\mathcal L^2|_{[0,1]^2}$ the growth bound holds with $n=1,d=2$, but for every interior point and all sufficiently small centered cubes, $\mu(2Q)/\mu(Q)=4$: with $\alpha=2,\beta=3$, the weak inequality $\beta>\alpha^n$ holds but $\beta>\alpha^d$ fails and there are no such small doubling cubes. If concentric cubes $Q\subset R$ have no intermediate $(\alpha,\beta)$-doubling dilation of $Q$, Lemma 2.3 bounds $\int_{R\setminus Q}|x-x_Q|^{-n}\,d\mu(x)$ by a constant depending only on $\alpha,\beta,n,d,C_0$. The proof sums annuli using the geometric decay forced by failure of doubling.
- **Adapted decomposition, Lemma 2.4 (physical pp. 4–5).** The statement literally says “finite family”; for unbounded $Ω=\{|f|>λ\}$, do not read this as a global finite cover. For $f∈L^1(μ)$ and $λ>0$, the construction uses almost-disjoint cubes $Q_i$ with $μ(2Q_i)^{-1}∫_{Q_i}|f|dμ>λ/2^{d+1}$, while larger dilates $ηQ_i$ have average at most that threshold for $η>2$. Outside $∪_iQ_i$, $|f|≤λ$ a.e. For each $Q_i$, choose a concentric $(6,6^{n+1})$-doubling cube $R_i$ with $ℓ(R_i)>4ℓ(Q_i)$ and set $w_i=1_{Q_i}/∑_k1_{Q_k}$. There are constant-sign corrections $φ_i$ supported in $R_i$, with $∫ φ_i dμ=∫_{Q_i}fw_i dμ$, $∑_i|φ_i|≤Bλ$, and $||φ_i||_{L∞(μ)}μ(R_i)≲∫_{Q_i}|f|dμ$. Then $g=f1_{R^d\setminus∪_iQ_i}+∑_iφ_i$, $b_i=w_if-φ_i$, and $b=∑_i b_i$ give $f=g+b$, $|g|≤Cλ$, $∫ b_i dμ=0$, $supp(b_i)⊂R_i$, and $||b_i||_1≲∫_{Q_i}|f|dμ$. Physical p. 8 passes from finite initial segments to the infinite family by weak-* diagonal limits; for unbounded $Ω$, use the local truncation-and-limit or countable annular finite-overlap repair on pp. 8–9 described below. If $μ$ is finite, Lemma 2.4 states the decomposition for $λ>2^{d+1}||f||_1/||μ||$; smaller $λ$ are handled by the trivial measure bound in the theorem proof.
- **Bad part off the enlarged bad cubes (physical pp. 5–6).** The union $\cup_i2Q_i$ has measure $\lesssim\|f\|_1/λ$. On $ℝ^d\setminus2R_i$, cancellation of $b_i$ and kernel smoothness give $\int|T_εb_i|dμ\lesssim\|b_i\|_1$. On $2R_i\setminus2Q_i$, the correction $φ_i$ is controlled by the $L^2(μ)$ norm and doubling of $R_i$; the $w_if$ term uses kernel size and Lemma 2.3, since no intermediate $(6,6^{n+1})$-doubling cube lies between the chosen scales. Summing yields a distribution estimate for $T_εb$ outside $\cup_i2Q_i$.
- **Good part and conclusion (physical pp. 5–6).** The $L^\infty$ control of $g$, its $L^1$ bound, and uniform $L^2$-boundedness give $μ\{|T_εg|>λ\}\lesssim\|f\|_1/λ$. Combine with the bad-part estimate, uniformly over $ε$; approximation/extension gives Theorem 1.1. The authors state that an analogous decomposition for complex measures yields the stronger $M(ℂ)\to L^{1,∞}$ assertion.
- **Proof of Lemma 2.4 and the Besicovitch issue, §3 (physical pp. 7–9; pp. 7–9 rendered and visually checked).** The proof initially invokes Besicovitch on $\Omega=\{|f|>\lambda\}$, which may be unbounded. Remark 3.1 explicitly says this direct global use is invalid because the boundedness hypothesis is needed. The source repairs it in either of two ways: localize to $\Omega_r=\Omega\cap B(0,r)$, prove the distribution estimate on each fixed $B(0,R)$, and let $R\to\infty$; or cover annuli $Q_m\setminus Q_{m-1}$ with $Q_m=(5/4)^mQ_0$ and prove that the combined countable cover has finite overlap. On physical p. 8, the correction functions for an infinite cube family are first built on finite initial segments and then obtained by weak-* diagonal limits. Thus the globally applicable construction is a localized limit or a countable finite-overlap cover, not an unjustified finite Besicovitch family for unbounded $\Omega$. Physical p. 10 contains the complete references.

## Tool use and transfer boundary

This is an **auxiliary weak-endpoint decomposition source** for probability-analytic-endpoint-methods. It supplies a rigorous non-doubling Calderón–Zygmund decomposition and proof for the stated growth-measure class. For C208 it is not a sharp constant result: a use with Lebesgue measure and a Riesz kernel is possible only after checking the exact kernel and truncation hypotheses, and the paper's implicit constant has no claimed sharp dimension dependence. The decomposition proof does not by itself establish C208's target statement or sharpness.
