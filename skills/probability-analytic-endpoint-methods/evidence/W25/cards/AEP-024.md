## Main estimates and ranges (physical PDF pp. 2–6)

The model is
\[
H=-\Delta+V\quad\text{on }\mathbb R^n,
\qquad V\ge0,\quad V\in L^1_{\rm loc},\quad V\not\equiv0.
\]
Here \(B_q\) is a reverse-Hölder class: \(V\in B_q\) means
\[
\left(\fint_Q V^q\right)^{1/q}\le C\fint_Q V
\]
for every cube \(Q\), and \(A_\infty=\bigcup_{q>1}B_q\). The constants and \(\varepsilon>0\) depend on the stated \(B_q/A_\infty\) data, dimension and exponents.

**Theorem 1.2(1):** if \(V\in B_q\), \(q>1\), then
\[
\|\nabla u\|_p+\|V^{1/2}u\|_p
\lesssim\|H^{1/2}u\|_p,
\qquad 1<p<2(q+\varepsilon).
\]
**Theorem 1.2(2)** is a genuine weak estimate, with a different operator and input domain. If \(V\in A_\infty\), then for every \(f\in C_0^\infty(\mathbb R^n)\) and \(\alpha>0\),
\[
\big|\{x:|H^{1/2}f(x)|>\alpha\}\big|
\le\frac{C}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\big(|\nabla f|+V^{1/2}|f|\big)\,dx.
\]
Thus the output is \(H^{1/2}f\), and the right side is the \(L^1\) Sobolev-energy seminorm of the test input \(f\), not \(\|f\|_1\). The same item also gives
\[
\|H^{1/2}u\|_p\lesssim
\|\nabla u\|_p+\|V^{1/2}u\|_p,\qquad 1<p<2.
\]
**Theorem 1.2(3):** if \(V\in B_q\), \(q>1\), and \(q\ge n/2\), then
\[
\|\nabla u\|_p\lesssim\|H^{1/2}u\|_p.
\]
The range is \(1<p<q^*+\varepsilon\) if \(q<n\), and all \(1<p<\infty\) if \(q\ge n\), where \(q^*=nq/(n-q)\). This is a strong-\(L^p\) Riesz comparison in a potential-dependent range. Theorem 1.1 separately gives
\[
\|\Delta u\|_p+\|Vu\|_p\lesssim\|Hu\|_p,
\qquad V\in B_q,\quad 1<p<q+\varepsilon;
\]
it is not a weak endpoint theorem.

The exponent relation \(q^*\ge2q\Longleftrightarrow q\ge n/2\), for \(1<q<n\), was checked with exact Python fractions.Fraction arithmetic in 1,711 rational cases; see [the script](../math/check_range_relations.py) and [its result](../math/check_range_relations.json). This checks exponent algebra only, not an analytic estimate.

## Weak-estimate proof mechanism (physical PDF pp. 19–28)

The proof of (7.1) uses the nonnegative-potential semigroup and a Calderón–Zygmund decomposition adapted to the Sobolev-energy density \(|\nabla f|+V^{1/2}|f|\), not to an arbitrary scalar \(L^1\) input. It first truncates \(V\) to \(V_{\varepsilon,N}=\min(V+\varepsilon,N)\), obtains constants uniform in these cutoffs using only the \(A_\infty\) data, and passes to \(N\to\infty\), then \(\varepsilon\to0\) by \(L^2\) convergence and functional calculus.

For \(1\le p<2\), Lemma 7.1 decomposes \(f=g+\sum_i b_i\) using Whitney cubes for the level set
\[
\Omega=\{x:\mathcal M(|\nabla f|^p+|V^{1/2}f|^p)(x)>\alpha^p\}.
\]
The good term satisfies
\[
\|\nabla g\|_2+\|V^{1/2}g\|_2
\le C\alpha^{1-p/2}
\big(\|\nabla f\|_p+\|V^{1/2}f\|_p\big)^{p/2}.
\]
Each \(b_i\) is supported in \(Q_i\), and
\[
\int_{Q_i}\big(|\nabla b_i|^p+R_i^{-p}|b_i|^p\big)
\le C\alpha^p|Q_i|,\qquad
\sum_i|Q_i|\le C\alpha^{-p}\int
\big(|\nabla f|^p+|V^{1/2}f|^p\big),\qquad
\sum_i\mathbf1_{Q_i}\le N(n).
\]
The good contribution at \(p=1\) follows from the form identity
\[
\|H^{1/2}g\|_2^2
=\|\nabla g\|_2^2+\|V^{1/2}g\|_2^2
\]
and Chebyshev. For the bad contribution, use the spectral formula
\[
H^{1/2}f=c\int_0^\infty H e^{-t^2H}f\,dt,
\]
split each bad piece at a time \(r_i\simeq R_i\) into short- and long-time parts, and estimate the short-time tail outside \(4Q_i\) by Gaussian off-diagonal bounds for \(tHe^{-tH}\), duality, maximal functions and finite cube overlap. The long-time term is handled by semigroup square-function inequality (7.9), obtained from Gaussian bounds and \(L^2\) functional calculus in the \(q=2\) case. Both bad terms are controlled by
\[
C\alpha^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\big(|\nabla f|+V^{1/2}|f|\big)\,dx.
\]
This proves (7.1) for \(H^{1/2}f\) and the specified Sobolev-energy input.

## Endpoint and C207 boundary

Estimate (7.1) is **not** a weak-\((1,1)\) inequality for a Riesz transform \(\nabla H^{-1/2}\), or for a scalar coordinate \(R_j=\partial_j(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\) acting on an \(L^1\) input. Its theorem domain is \(f\in C_0^\infty\), its output is \(H^{1/2}f\), and its controlling input quantity is \(\|\nabla f\|_1+\|V^{1/2}f\|_1\). Formally setting \(f=H^{-1/2}g\) reverses the direction: it bounds \(g\mapsto H^{1/2}H^{-1/2}g\) in terms of a Sobolev norm of Riesz-type outputs, and does not upper-bound the distribution of a scalar Riesz coordinate from \(\|g\|_1\). The theorem is for \(V\in A_\infty\), \(V\ge0\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), not automatically the free scalar operator in C207. A transfer needs an additional representation and endpoint estimate; the shared word “Riesz” is insufficient.
