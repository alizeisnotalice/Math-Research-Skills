## Exact results and proof chain

1. **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2; abstract p. 1):** for a coordinate Riesz transform (R_j), the paper states an absolute-constant reduction
   \[
   |\{|R_j f|>\lambda\}|
   \le C\left(\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_1+
   \sup_{\nu}|\{|R_j\nu|>\lambda\}|\right),
   \]
   where \(\nu=\sum_{k=1}^N a_k\delta_{c_k}\) is a finite **positive** atomic measure with \(a_k\ge0\) and total mass at most \(16\|f\|_1\). The proof first treats nonnegative (f), then uses a positive/negative decomposition. The printed theorem does not separately specify the scalar field; the proof as written establishes the real-valued case. No complex-input extension is used here.
2. **Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 10–12):** its printed general-kernel statement for K=Omega/|x|^n has regular part (C4 + C5 ||Omega||_1) lambda^-1 ||f||_1, plus twice the finite-positive-atom supremum. The proof uses a Whitney decomposition of U={f>lambda}, replaces bad cubes by equal-mass center atoms, controls the good part by an L2 estimate, and uses zero-mass cancellation outside radius n r_k for the bad terms. The finite-cube limit uses L2 convergence; signed data are split into positive and negative parts, accounting for the factor 2 and mass budget 16.
   **Printed-proof norm audit (PDF p. 11; rendered and checked):** Lemma 3.4 gives ||Th||_2 <= C2 ||Omega||_1 ||h||_2. Squaring for the first Chebyshev step yields 4 C2^2 ||Omega||_1^2 lambda^-2 ||g||_2^2 <= 4 C2^2 ||Omega||_1^2 lambda^-1 ||f||_1. The displayed good-part estimate omits ||Omega||_1^2. The next Chebyshev estimate for T(b-b^(N)) likewise does not carry the square of the Lemma 3.4 bound: with level lambda/4 its bound from that lemma is 16 C2^2 ||Omega||_1^2 lambda^-2 ||b-b^(N)||_2^2. For each fixed finite ||Omega||_1 this corrected factor still tends to zero by the stated dominated-convergence argument, but the printed proof's norm dependence is not the one obtained from Lemma 3.4. The subsequent collection of terms and Theorem 4.1's displayed C4 + C5 ||Omega||_1 dependence are therefore not established by the printed proof for variable general-kernel norm. Do not infer a corrected universal linear-in-||Omega||_1 bound. For general-kernel use, provide an independently certified L2 operator norm M2(T) (giving good-part contribution 4 M2(T)^2 lambda^-1 ||f||_1 and the analogous vanishing finite-sum error) or independently complete the norm argument. For the Riesz-coordinate application, ||Omega||_1=2/pi is fixed, so these squared factors are absorbed into absolute constants and the scalar Riesz reduction remains supported.
3. **Theorem 1.3 (PDF pp. 3, 13–16):** the finite-atom scalar-coordinate estimate is \(\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda |\{|R_j\nu|>\lambda\}|\le C\log n\,\|\nu\|_{M_b}\) for (n\ge2). Theorem 4.2 proves this by assigning disjoint sets (E_k) of volume (a_k/\lambda), subtracting the transform of (\lambda1_{E_k}) outside (B(c_k,nr_k)), and estimating the intervening annuli by \(\log n\|\Omega\|_1\). The remaining maximal-function term uses the adjoint maximal truncation's (L^2) bound and Cauchy–Schwarz. For the first-order Riesz kernel the rendered PDF p. 5 gives \(\int_{S^{n-1}}|\Omega|\,d\sigma=2/\pi\), while its angular continuity estimate is at most (C\sqrt n\,\delta\int|\Omega|); the extractor's missing fraction bar and square root were checked against the rendered page. The resulting (C\log n) estimate is dimensional and does not solve the finite-atom question uniformly in (n).

## Assumptions, constants, and transfer limits

- Kernel framework in Definition 2.1 (PDF p. 4): `Omega` is homogeneous of degree zero, has spherical mean zero, and satisfies the stated angular L1 continuity estimate for `0<delta<1/n`. Lemma 3.4 (PDF p. 9) additionally states oddness for the L2 and maximal-truncation estimates used by Theorem 4.1; the theorem statement does not restate oddness. Keep the general reduction architecture available with this proof-level condition visible and with an independently certified L2 operator norm (or a separately completed norm argument). The printed proof loses the squared angular-norm factor as described above. For Riesz coordinates, oddness holds and the fixed angular mass `2/pi` absorbs that factor.
- The Riesz-coordinate conclusion is scalar and dimension-dependent through the atomic term; it is neither a bound for the Euclidean vector norm nor a proof of a dimension-free scalar constant.
- The finite atomic measures are positive; the reduction's finite mass cap is (16\|f\|_1). Theorem 1.3 gives the paper's logarithmic upper bound for (n\ge2); it does not close the open atomic-measure estimate.
- Page 5's rendered (2/\pi) is independently consistent with the sphere normalization, but the saved local calculation is only an identity check, not a proof of the reduction.
