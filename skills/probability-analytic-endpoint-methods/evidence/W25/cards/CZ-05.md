## Exact statements and scope

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2):** for every (n\ge1), coordinate (1\le j\le n), \(\Omega=\mathbb R^n\) or \(\mathbb T^n\), and every **real-valued** (f\in L^1(\Omega)), the source claims \(\|R_jf\|_{1,\infty}\le K_D\|f\|_1\) and that (K_D) is best possible for every (n,j). Here \(\|g\|_{1,\infty}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\mu\{|g|>\lambda\}\), \(K_D=\pi^2/(8\beta(2))\), and \(\beta(2)=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k/(2k+1)^2\) is Catalan's constant.
- The Euclidean Fourier convention is \(\widehat f(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx\); (R_j=-\partial_j(-\Delta)^{-1/2}), multiplier \(-i\xi_j/|\xi|\). On the torus the multiplier is \(-im_j/|m|\), set to zero at (m=0), with normalized Haar measure. The result is scalar-coordinate, not an assertion of the same sharp bound for \((R_1f,\dots,R_nf)\) with Euclidean norm.
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 2–3):** if (Y) is a nonzero standard \(\sigma\)-finite measure space, (L\ge0) is self-adjoint on (L^2(Y,\nu)), and (P_t=e^{-tL}) is a symmetric sub-Markov semigroup (positive and (P_t1\le1) in the consistent (L^\infty) realization), then on (X=\mathbb T\times Y) or \(\mathbb R\times Y), with product measure, the **horizontal** transform \(R_L=-\partial_x(-\partial_x^2+L)^{-1/2}\) extends uniquely from real (L^1\cap L^2) to real (L^1\), continuously from (L^1) convergence to convergence in measure, and satisfies \(\lambda\mu\{|R_Lf|>\lambda\}\le K_D\|f\|_1\). The claimed weak constant is exact.
- **Integral refinement (Theorem 2.2, PDF pp. 10–11):** define (a=K_D^{-1}=8\beta(2)/\pi^2), \(q(s)=\tfrac4\pi\operatorname{arctanh}(\tan(\pi s/4))\) for (0\le s<1), and \(\Phi(t)=\int_0^{\min(t,1)}q(s)ds\). Then \(\Phi(1)=a\), \(\Phi(t)=a\) for (t\ge1), and \(\Phi(t)\ge t^2/2\) for (0\le t\le1\). With (B(r,s)=U(r,s)+|r|\), the theorem claims
  \[
  \lambda\int_X B(f/\lambda,R_Lf/\lambda)\,d\mu\le\|f\|_1,
  \qquad \lambda\int_X\Phi(|R_Lf|/\lambda)\,d\mu\le\|f\|_1.
  \]
  In particular, source inequality (2.15) is
  \[
  a\lambda\,\mu\{|R_Lf|\ge\lambda\}
  +\frac1{2\lambda}\int_{\{|R_Lf|<\lambda\}}|R_Lf|^2d\mu\le\|f\|_1.
  \]

## Proof dependency chain as actually read

1. **Davis strip function and real-variable Bellman majorant (PDF §§2.1–2.3, pp. 5–10).** Brownian exit from `|Im z|<1` gives `a=E_0|X_tau|=8 beta(2)/pi^2`. The harmonic extension `u` of boundary data `|x|` yields `U=a-u`, extended by `a-|x|` outside the strip. The pasted function is superharmonic; the Hopf boundary-point argument checks the interface sign. A Poisson-kernel comparison proves obstacle domination by `Phi(|y|)-|x|`. In Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 10), the paper composes the **superharmonic** `U` with the analytic disk map; conformality preserves superharmonicity, and its mean-value upper bound gives the circle integral inequality. Fatou/Poisson approximation extends it to real `L1(T)`. Keep this distinct from PDF p. 13, where the different function `Re(Psi_tilde)` is subharmonic and yields a lower-bound inequality in the finite-transverse-space proof.
2. **Finite transverse Markov space (PDF §3.1, pp. 11–17).** For each state, apply the harmonic-conjugate inequality. Reversibility rewrites \(\langle q,H_t(I-P_t)h\rangle\) as a sum of pairwise terms. Set (T=(A/|D_x|)-I) on zero-horizontal-mean functions and use the scalar subordination identity
   \[
   \sqrt{1+s/r^2}-1=\frac1{2\sqrt\pi r}\int_0^\infty e^{-tr^2}(1-e^{-ts})t^{-3/2}dt,
   \]
   to represent (T) as \((2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\int_0^\infty H_t(I-P_t)t^{-3/2}dt\). Analyticity of (F(S_1)-F(S_2)=B(S_1-S_2)), with \(\Re B\ge0\) because \(\Re F'>0\), makes weighted Fourier partial sums nonnegative; Abel summation for decreasing nonnegative weights proves each pairwise inequality. Killing adds nonnegative missing-mass terms.
3. **Remove the strip restriction (PDF §§3.2–3.3, pp. 17–27).** Vertically convolve (U) to a globally superharmonic (U_\delta), add a quadratic penalty \(-\varepsilon\|Rf\|_2^2\), and maximize on real (L^2(\mathbb T\times E)). Poisson comparison plus the penalty yields (f,Rf\in W^{1/2,2}). The Euler–Lagrange relation is expressed via a convex subgradient (needed because of the cusp in the first variable), giving \(|p|\le1\), (Ap=\partial_x\eta-2\varepsilon\partial_xg), and (Ag=-\partial_x f). A Lipschitz cutoff \(\Theta(g)\) supported where (|g|>1+\delta), truncation (f_M), integration by parts, and the sub-Markov Dirichlet-form inequality show (E(\Theta(g),\Theta(g))=0). Since (A\ge|D_x|) in quadratic-form sense and (g=Rf) has zero horizontal mean, this forces \(\Theta(g)=0\), hence \(|Rf|\le1+\delta\). Letting \(\delta,\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves the finite-space estimate.
4. **General spaces and (L^1) (PDF §3.4, pp. 27–32).** Periodize compactly supported functions to pass from the circle to the line. Approximate (Y) with finite partitions and compress (e^{-rL}), not (L), preserving the sub-Markov property; spectral calculus and dominated convergence identify the limiting transforms in (L^2), while Fatou passes the integral estimate. A weak-type extension lemma gives a unique (L^1\to L^0) (convergence-in-measure) extension and preserves continuous nonnegative integral functionals by Fatou.
5. **Sharpness (PDF pp. 31–32).** Use Davis's circle test pair and high horizontal frequencies so the multiplier \(-i\xi(\xi^2+L)^{-1/2}\) converges to \(-i\operatorname{sgn}\xi\) on each fixed transverse spectral value. This constructs a lower bound (K_D) on any nonzero transverse space; no nonzero vector in \(\ker L\) is assumed.

## Strict limits on transfer

- Real-valued inputs are explicit in both Theorems 1.1 and 1.2. No complex-input theorem is asserted here.
- The product result differentiates only in the added line/circle coordinate. The transverse generator enters through a symmetric sub-Markov semigroup. It does not prove a sharp bound for transforms whose numerator differentiates transversely (intrinsic Gaussian/manifold Riesz vector components, for example).
- The exact weak-((1,1)) claim is scalar-coordinate. The paper expressly leaves the sharp vector constant separate (Introduction, PDF p. 4). It does not solve C207's (1<p<\infty) best vector (L^p) constants.
- This is arXiv v1 submitted 9 November 2026. W25 records the theorem and proof route accurately but does not independently certify the new proof or elevate C208 to a resolved formal problem.
