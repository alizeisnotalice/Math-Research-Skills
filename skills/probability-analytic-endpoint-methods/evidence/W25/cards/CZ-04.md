## Exact result and proof chain

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 1):** for the full vector transform \(Rf=(R_1f,\ldots,R_nf)\), with pointwise Euclidean norm and weak norm \(\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda|\{x:|Rf(x)|>\lambda\}|\), the paper claims \(\|Rf\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\le2\|f\|_1\), uniformly in (n). This is an upper bound; the paper does not assert that 2 is the optimal vector constant.
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 2, 11):** if (f\in L^1\cap L^2(\mathbb R^n)) is nonnegative, \(\lambda>0\), and (0<\alpha<2), there are nonnegative \(\mu,u\) with \(f=\mu+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u\), \(\mu\in L^1\cap L^\infty\), (u\in H^\alpha\cap L^1), \(\|\mu\|_\infty\le\lambda\), \(\|\mu\|_1=\|f\|_1\), \(\mu=\lambda\) a.e. on \(\Omega=\{u>0\}\), and \(|\Omega|\le\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_1\). If \(\alpha\ge1\), then (u\in H^1) and \(\nabla u=0\) a.e. on \(\Omega^c\).
- **Variational construction (PDF pp. 5–8):** use (X_\alpha=\{v\in L^1:E_\alpha(v)<\infty\}), positive cone (K_\alpha), and \(J^\alpha_\lambda(v)=\frac12E_\alpha(v)-\int(f-\lambda)v\). The Gagliardo representation for (0<\alpha<2), Lipschitz Markov contraction, and the interpolation bound \(\|v\|_2^2\le K_{n,\alpha}\|v\|_1^{2\alpha/(n+\alpha)}E_\alpha(v)^{n/(n+\alpha)}\) yield coercivity. Weak compactness and lower semicontinuity yield a minimizer; the parallelogram identity gives uniqueness. First variation gives the variational inequality and energy identity.
- **Lewy–Stampacchia step (PDF pp. 8–11):** the distribution \(\eta=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u-(f-\lambda)\) is positive from the variational inequality. Testing with \(v=(u-\varepsilon\phi)_+\), using \(E_\alpha(u,z_- )\le -E_\alpha(z_-)+\varepsilon E_\alpha(\phi)^{1/2}E_\alpha(z_-)^{1/2}\), and (0\le z_-\le\varepsilon\phi), gives \(\langle\eta,\phi\rangle\le\int(\lambda-f)_+\phi\). Thus (0\le\eta\le(\lambda-f)_+\le\lambda). With \(\mu=\lambda-\eta\), radial cutoffs prove \(\|\mu\|_1=\|f\|_1\); the energy identity gives \(\eta u=0\), hence \(\mu=\lambda\) on \(\Omega\). Theorem 1.2 follows.
- **Weak-vector conclusion (PDF pp. 2–3):** apply the decomposition at \(\alpha=1\) separately to (f^+) and (f^-), then (Rf=R\mu+\nabla u), and \(\nabla u=0\) outside \(\Omega^+\cup\Omega^-\). The bad set is contained in this obstacle set plus \(\{|R\mu|>\lambda\}\). The obstacle set costs at most \(\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_1\); vector (L^2) isometry and \(|\mu|\le\lambda\) give the same bound for the second set. Summing yields 2.

## Field and geometry boundaries

- The displayed Theorem 1.1 writes (f\in L^1\) without stating the scalar field. Its proof uses the real positive/negative decomposition (f=f^+-f^-), and Theorem 1.2 is explicitly for nonnegative scalar functions. Treat the constant-2 proof as established for real-valued inputs; this paper does not supply a complex-valued extension with the same constant.
- The output in Theorem 1.1 is the **vector Euclidean norm**. Pointwise \(|R_jf|\le|Rf|\) implies the same dimension-free upper bound 2 for any scalar coordinate, but not the sharp scalar constant (K_D), and not equality for the vector norm.
- This is a Euclidean-space obstacle-decomposition theorem. It does not establish the product-semigroup theorem or horizontal-generator scope in CZ-05.

## Use in W25

Use as a vector-versus-coordinate comparison: dimension-free vector weak type with upper constant 2 controls each scalar component, but it cannot certify a sharp scalar coordinate norm or a sharp vector norm. The obstacle decomposition is reusable only with its stated (L^1\cap L^2), nonnegativity, fractional-order, and level assumptions.
