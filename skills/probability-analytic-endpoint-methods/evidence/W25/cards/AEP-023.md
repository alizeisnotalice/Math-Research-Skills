## Main theorem and exact hypotheses (physical PDF pp. 3–6)

Let \(M\) be a complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold with measure \(\mu\), gradient \(\nabla\), and nonnegative Laplace–Beltrami operator \(\Delta\). The global doubling condition is
\[
V(x,2r)\le C V(x,r)\qquad(x\in M,\ r>0),
\]
and the scaled \(L^2\) Poincaré condition is
\[
\int_B|f-f_B|^2\,d\mu\le Cr(B)^2\int_B|\nabla f|^2\,d\mu.
\]
Under these hypotheses, \((D)+(P)\) is equivalent to the two-sided Li–Yau Gaussian heat-kernel estimates \((LY)\) used in the paper. For \(p_0\in(2,\infty]\), assume the operator-norm heat-gradient bound
\[
\big\|\,|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}|\,\big\|_{p_0\to p_0}\le C t^{-1/2}\qquad(t>0).
\]
**Theorem 1.2:** the Riesz transform \(\nabla\Delta^{-1/2}\), with output norm the tangent-vector length, is bounded from \(L^p(M,\mu)\) to \(L^p(TM)\) for every \(2<p<p_0\). **Theorem 1.3** says, under the same \((D)+(P)\), that this strong-\(L^p\) boundedness for every \(p\in(2,p_0)\) is equivalent to \((G_p)\) for every such \(p\). Neither theorem includes \(p=p_0\). The paper separately recalls that \((D)+(DUE)\) implies strong \(L^p\) boundedness for \(1<p<2\) (Theorem 1.1, from earlier work); that result does not extend above 2 without a further gradient hypothesis.

Theorem 1.4 gives an all-\(1<p<\infty\) strong bound under the more specific pointwise hypothesis
\[
|\nabla_x p_t(x,y)|\le \frac{C}{\sqrt t\,V(y,\sqrt t)}
\qquad(x,y\in M,\ t>0),
\]
plus \((D)+(DUE)\). Here \((G)\) has no Gaussian off-diagonal factor. It yields the full Li–Yau estimates in this setting and hence the integrated \((G_p)\) conditions. This remains a strong-\(L^p\) theorem for the manifold, vector-output Riesz transform.

For the local variants, Theorems 1.5 and 1.7 replace the global geometry by exponential local volume growth \((E)\), local Poincaré \((P_{\rm loc})\), and local heat estimates. The operator bound
\[
\|\nabla e^{-t\Delta}\|_{p_0\to p_0}\le Ce^{\alpha t}/\sqrt t
\]
then gives strong \(L^p\) boundedness of the local transform \(\nabla(\Delta+a)^{-1/2}\) for \(2<p<p_0\), \(a>\alpha\) (Theorem 1.5). Theorem 1.7 gives the full \(1<p<\infty\) local range from \((E),(DUE_{\rm loc}),(G_{\rm loc})\) for sufficiently large \(a\). These do not assert a Euclidean scalar weak-\((1,1)\) bound.

## Reusable strong-type criterion and proof chain (physical PDF pp. 13–34)

Theorem 2.1 is a good-\(\lambda\) criterion on a doubling metric-measure space. Let \(T\) be sublinear and \(L^2\)-bounded, let \(p_0\in(2,\infty]\), and let \(\{A_r:r>0\}\) be linear operators acting on \(L^2(M,\mu)\). For **every** \(f\in L^2(M,\mu)\), **every** \(x\in M\), and **every** ball \(B\ni x\), with \(r(B)\) its radius, require
\[
\left(\frac1{\mu(B)}\int_B|T(I-A_{r(B)})f|^2\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}
\le C\,\mathcal M(|f|^2)^{1/2}(x),
\]
and
\[
\left(\frac1{\mu(B)}\int_B|T A_{r(B)}f|^{p_0}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p_0}
\le C\,\mathcal M(|Tf|^2)^{1/2}(x).
\]
When \(p_0=\infty\), the left side of (2.2) is the essential supremum over \(B\). The theorem also requires that \(Tf\in L^p(M,\mu)\) whenever \(f\in L^p(M,\mu)\). Under these hypotheses, for \(2<p<p_0\), \(T\) is of strong type \((p,p)\); the bound depends only on its \(L^2\) norm, the doubling constant, \(p,p_0\), and the constants in (2.1)–(2.2). The proof builds a sharp-function variant from \(T(I-A_r)\), proves a good-\(\lambda\) estimate with error \(C(\gamma^2+K^{-p_0})\), integrates the distribution inequality, and absorbs the resulting good term. It is a strong-type mechanism above 2, not an endpoint theorem.

The theorem is stated for an operator already acting on the target \(L^p\) space. The authors explicitly note that applications can instead use suitable approximations of \(T\): a bound uniform in the approximation parameter permits passage to \(T\) by a limiting argument. In their Riesz-transform proof, \(Tf=|\nabla\Delta^{-1/2}f|\) is approximated for \(0<\varepsilon<1\) by
\[
T_\varepsilon f=
\left|c\int_\varepsilon^{1/\varepsilon}
\frac{\nabla e^{-t\Delta}f}{\sqrt t}\,dt\right|.
\]
For each \(\varepsilon\), \(T_\varepsilon\) has an \(L^p\) bound \(C_\varepsilon\), and \(T_\varepsilon f\to Tf\) in \(L^2\). Applying Theorem 2.1 gives an \(L^p\) bound uniform in \(\varepsilon\), because the criterion constants are uniform; the limit gives boundedness on \(L^2\cap L^p\), hence on \(L^p\). In the finite-volume case the authors restrict to mean-zero functions, or define the transform to vanish on constants.

For the Riesz-transform application, they use the Gaffney \(L^2\) off-diagonal estimate (3.1), choose
\[
I-A_r=(I-e^{-r^2\Delta})^n
\]
with \(n\) sufficiently large relative to the polynomial volume-growth exponent from doubling, and prove (2.1) by annular decomposition and finite-difference decay in the semigroup integral for \(\nabla\Delta^{-1/2}\). For (2.2), \((G_{p_0})\) interpolated with Gaffney gives \(L^p\) off-diagonal heat-gradient bounds for \(2<p<p_0\). Gaussian upper bounds from \((D)+(P)\), the Poincaré estimate for \(f-f_{4B}\), annular summation, and heat-semigroup conservation control the regularized term by the maximal function of \(|\nabla f|^2\). Theorem 2.1 yields Theorem 1.2. For the pointwise case, §3.2 proves a maximal estimate using \((G)\), Gaussian bounds and a Sobolev–Poincaré pointwise inequality; §3.3 links the pointwise gradient estimate to integrated heat-gradient estimates. Section 4 localizes this architecture with a covering, local doubling and Gaffney bounds. Section 5 transfers the transform bound to the exact-form Hodge projector by duality and states additional assumptions needed for full Hodge decomposition.

## Endpoint and C207 boundary

This source supplies **strong \(L^p\)** bounds for a geometric, vector-output Riesz transform under \((D),(P)\) and heat-gradient hypotheses. It does not state weak type \((1,1)\), an \(L\log L\to L^1\) estimate, or a sharp scalar-coordinate endpoint constant. Transferring it to a scalar Lebesgue weak-\((1,1)\) assertion would require reducing the target operator/domain to the paper's manifold transform, verifying the exact semigroup/geometry assumptions, scalarizing the vector output, and supplying an endpoint argument that is absent here. Its good-\(\lambda\) criterion is a reusable **strong-type** method under its explicit inputs, not an endpoint conclusion.
