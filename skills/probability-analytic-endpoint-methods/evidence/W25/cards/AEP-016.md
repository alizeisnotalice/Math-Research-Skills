## Sharp matrix martingale transform inequality (Theorem 2.2, PDF p. 12; proof pp. 14–18)

Let `X,Y` be continuous `R^d`-valued martingales with `Y` differentially subordinate to `X`: `|Y₀|≤|X₀|` and `[X,X]-[Y,Y]` is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Let `M₀=I` solve `dM_t=V_tM_tdt`, where `V_t` is adapted, continuous, symmetric, and nonpositive. For `a≥0`, set
\[
Z_t=e^{-at}M_t\int_0^t e^{as}M_s^{-1}\,dY_s.
\]
For every `1<p<∞` and `T≥0`,
\[
\|Z_T\|_p\le(p^*-1)\|X_T\|_p,\qquad p^*=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\},
\]
and the constant is sharp. The bound is independent of `d`. The proof for `1<p<2` stops at a Burkholder special function, applies Itô’s formula, and integrates the family of stopping-radius inequalities. For `p≥2`, it mollifies a C¹ special function whose Hessian inequality makes the quadratic-variation contribution nonpositive under differential subordination; a radial-gradient sign condition controls the nonpositive matrix drift. Localization and removal of the mollifier finish the proof. This matrix condition is a real hypothesis: a scalar covariance ratio is not an operator/matrix bound.

The same section proves endpoint-shaped martingale estimates:

- **Theorem 2.5 (PDF pp. 13, 22–25):** for `K>1`, any event `E∈F`, and differentially subordinate `R^d`-valued martingales,
  \[
  \sup_t\mathbb E\bigl[|Y_t|1_E\bigr]
  \le K\sup_t\mathbb E\Psi(|X_t|)+\frac{\mathbb P(E)}{2(K-1)},
  \quad \Psi(t)=(t+1)\log(t+1)-t.
  \]
  The additive constant is stated best possible. The proof builds a special function from two auxiliary functions by integration, proves an Orlicz majorization, and splits `E` according to whether `|Y_t|` exceeds `1/(2(K-1))`.
- **Theorem 2.6 (PDF pp. 13, 25–30):** for `1<p<∞`,
  \[
  \sup_t\mathbb E[|Y_t|1_E]\le K_p\|X\|_p\,\mathbb P(E)^{1-1/p},
  \]
  The source states `K_p=(½ Γ((2p−1)/(p−1)))^{1−1/p}` for `1<p<2`, and `K_p=(p^{p−1}/2)^{1/p}` for `p≥2`, with sharpness asserted. The printed p<2 proof uses Lemma 2.7 on physical pp. 27–28; its last-step contradiction assumes failure of `G(t)≥γ(0)/2` gives `G(t)<0`, and a displayed comparison with exponent `p−2<0` reverses the inequality direction. W25 has added a separate analytic supplement below which supplies the needed global-minimum argument; it is pending R01 review. Until that supplement is accepted, use this event estimate operationally only for `p=2` (direct Cauchy–Schwarz) and `p>2` (the separate Lemma 2.8 proof). For those ranges, taking `E={|Y_t|>λ}` gives the martingale-output weak-Lp distribution consequence. None of these is a p=1 Riesz-operator theorem.


### W25 supplement to the p<2 Lemma 2.7 step (pending R01)

This is an independent repair of the bound needed in the source proof, not a claim that the author's printed argument was correct. For `1<p<2`, define `γ(t)=exp(p t^(p−1)) integral_t^infinity exp(−p s^(p−1)) ds`, `γ₀=γ(0)`, and `G(t)=(t+γ(t))^p−t^p−p t^(p−1)γ(t)`. The needed special case of Lemma 2.7 is `G(t)≥γ₀/2` for `t≥0`.

1. By l’Hôpital applied to the tail integral, `γ(t) ~ t^(2−p)/(p(p−1))`; thus `γ(t)→infinity` and `γ(t)/t→0`. Convex Taylor remainder gives `G(t)≥p(p−1)/2·(t+γ(t))^(p−2)γ(t)^2`, whose right side tends to infinity like a positive constant times `t^(2−p)`. Hence `G(t)→infinity`.
2. At the boundary, `G(0)=γ₀^p≥γ₀/2`: the source formula gives `γ₀^(p−1)=Γ(p/(p−1))^(p−1)/p≥1/p>1/2` for `1<p<2` (the source checks the same gamma-function inequality on physical p. 27).
3. At an interior critical point, differentiating and using `1+γ'(t)=p(p−1)t^(p−2)γ(t)` shows `G'(t)=0` iff `(t+γ(t))^(p−1)−t^(p−1)=1/p`. Substituting this identity and the ODE gives `G(t)=(γ(t)−tγ'(t))/p`. Since `γ` is concave, `(γ−tγ')' = −tγ''≥0`, so this is at least `γ₀/p>γ₀/2`.

Continuity and `G(t)→infinity` imply that a global minimum occurs at `t=0` or at an interior critical point, proving the needed bound. SymPy checked the stationary algebra exactly and the p=3/2 boundary values in [`check_R01_boundaries.py`](../artifacts/L01_B3/math/check_R01_boundaries.py) and its [JSON output](../artifacts/L01_B3/math/check_R01_boundaries.json); the asymptotic and calculus argument above is the proof, not the computation. ## Riesz-transform applications and their exact range

### Manifolds

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 4–5):** if `L=−½ΣᵢXᵢ*Xᵢ+V` with `V≤0`, the second-order projection `S_A` defined in (1.9) obeys `||S_Af||_p≤||A||(p*−1)||f||_p`, `1<p<∞`. The theorem has no curvature hypothesis on the manifold, but it is a strong-Lp estimate, not weak type.
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 5–6):** for `L=Δ−∇φ·∇`, measure `dμ=e^{−φ}dvol`, and nonnegative Bakry–Émery curvature `Ric(L)≥0`, the first-order gradient Riesz transform satisfies `||R_L^0 f||_p≤2(p*−1)||f||_p`. Under `Ric(L)≥−a`, the shifted `R_L^a=∇(a−L)^{-1/2}` bound has the source’s exit-time factor; (1.17)–(1.18) give a piecewise refinement. These are strong bounds for `1<p<∞`.
- The authors explicitly state that weak-(1,1) inequalities for Riesz transforms on general nonnegative-Ricci manifolds were not known to them; the same page notes the infinite-dimensional Gauss-space weak-(1,1) problem was open (PDF p. 8). Do not upgrade their log or (p>1) results to either weak-(1,1) claim.

### Compact Lie groups, spheres, and finite-dimensional Gauss space

- **Compact Lie groups, vector form (Theorem 3.3, PDF pp. 34–35):** for a compact connected Lie group with bi-invariant metric and `Ψ(t)=(t+1)log(t+1)−t`. The source prints `K>2/π` although the vector remainder is divided by `K−1`; the tool restricts this vector-log estimate to `K>1` and preserves the source threshold as a discrepancy.
  \[
  \int_E|R^Gf|\le2K\int_G\Psi(|f|)+\frac{|E|}{K-1},\quad K>2/\pi;
  \qquad
  \int_E|R^Gf|\le2K_p\|f\|_p|E|^{1-1/p},\quad1<p<\infty.
  \]
  Here `R^Gf` is vector-valued with Euclidean norm. The weak-Lp consequence is for `p>1`; the log inequality is the endpoint-style estimate.
- **Compact Lie groups, one coordinate (Theorem 3.5, PDF pp. 35–36):** for a fixed direction `j`, the log inequality is `∫_E|R_jf|≤K∫_GΨ(|f|)+L(K)|E|` for `K>2/π`, and the `p>1` event inequality is `∫_E|R_jf|≤C_p||f||_p|E|^{1−1/p}`. These are scalar-coordinate statements, distinct from the vector theorem.
- **Spheres (Theorems 3.7–3.8, PDF pp. 38–41):** the article defines separate cylindrical and ball Riesz transforms. Directional `Q^c_{ℓm}` and `Q^b_{ℓm}` have the stated log and `p>1` inequalities. For the ball-type vector transform the constants explicitly carry `√(d−1)`; the cylindrical vector result is obtained by transfer from `SO(d)`. Preserve this normalization and the distinction between transforms.
- **Finite-dimensional Gauss space (Theorem 3.9, PDF pp. 42–45):** for `R^L=∇(−L)^{-1/2}`, where `L=Δ−x·∇` and `γ_d` is standard Gaussian measure, the source prints the log bound `∫_E|R^Lf|dγ_d≤2K∫Ψ(|f|)dγ_d+γ_d(E)/(K−1)` for `K>2/π`; the tool uses the safe range `K>1`. The event bound is `∫_E|R^Lf|dγ_d≤2K_p||f||_pγ_d(E)^{1−1/p}` for `p>1`. The proof first handles finite sums of Hermite polynomials by Poincaré limits from high-dimensional spheres, then uses density. This does not establish an infinite-dimensional weak-(1,1) theorem.

## Transfer boundary and tool use

The general method is: represent the target as a conditional expectation/projection of an (often differentially subordinate, sometimes orthogonal) martingale transform; apply the appropriate sharp Lp, Orlicz, or event-integral inequality; pass through conditional Jensen and the geometric/semigroup representation; preserve vector versus scalar norm and dimension factors. Every application depends on its own geometric, curvature, filtration, or Brownian representation hypotheses.

- The paper is relevant to endpoint-method calibration because it supplies logarithmic estimates and event inequalities for specified geometric Riesz systems. Until the independent p<2 supplement is accepted, the sharp event estimate is operationally used only for p≥2; vector-log estimates with a `K−1` remainder require K>1.
- It does **not** give weak-(1,1) for the general-manifold or infinite-dimensional Gauss cases discussed as open, and none of its `p>1` inequalities alone supplies a p=1 bound.
