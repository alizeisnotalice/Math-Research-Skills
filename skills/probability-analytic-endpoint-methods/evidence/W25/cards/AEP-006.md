## Results and proof chain

### Riesz transforms of finite measures (§1, printed pp. 257–262)

Let μ be a finite (bounded Radon) measure on ℝ^n, with Lebesgue decomposition (dμ=f,dx+dν), (f\in L^1), and ν singular. Write (u_0(x,y)) for the Poisson extension of μ to the upper half-space and (u_1,\ldots,u_n) for its Riesz-conjugate harmonic system. The scalar distribution used in the main theorem is

\[
N(\lambda)=\lambda\,m\!\left\{x:\left(\sum_{j=1}^n|R_j\mu(x)|^2\right)^{1/2}>\lambda\right\}.
\]

The paper first recalls the dimension-dependent weak-(1,1) upper bound (N(\lambda)\le C_n\|\mu\|). Its main theorem is printed with an **underlined** `lim`, denoting `liminf` here:
\[
\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}N(\lambda)\ge k_n\|\nu\|,
\]
where (k_n>0) depends only on (n). In dimension one it reports the stronger theorem of P. Jones (there identified as unpublished), using an ordinary `lim`:

\[
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda\,m\{|H\mu|>\lambda\}=\frac{2}{\pi}\|\nu\|.
\]

The source does not give explicit higher-dimensional (k_n) or (C_n). It also states a higher-dimensional Loomis-type estimate for a **positive singular** bounded measure ν:

\[
k_n\|\nu\|\le \lambda\,m\!\left\{x:\sum_{j=1}^n|R_j\nu(x)|>\lambda\right\}\le C_n\|\nu\|,\qquad \lambda>0.
\]

This all-threshold statement uses the sum of coordinate magnitudes, whereas (N(\lambda)) uses the Euclidean vector norm. The constants remain dimension-dependent. In dimension one, the paper separately cites Loomis's exact identity for a finite positive atomic measure μ:

\[
\lambda\,m\{|\widetilde\mu|>\lambda\}=\frac{2}{\pi}\|\mu\|,\qquad \lambda>0.
\]

The Jones and Loomis assertions are identified as cited results/comparisons; the present article does not supply their full proofs.

The lower-bound mechanism is a Poisson-maximal-function argument. The preliminary lemma on physical p. 3 has the same underlined `lim`, and asserts
\(\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda m\{u_0^*>\lambda\}\ge c_n\|\nu\|\), where (u_0^*(x)=\sup_{y>0}|u_0(x,y)|). After reducing to a positive singular measure supported in the unit cube, positivity of the Poisson kernel gives (u_0^*\ge A_n u^\#), where (u^\#) is a dyadic maximal density. A dyadic Calderón–Zygmund selection at level λ produces disjoint cubes with λ below their average density and at most (2^n\lambda); singularity implies the selected cubes capture the full singular mass. Summing their volumes yields the weak-tail lower bound.

For the main theorem, normalize ν to mass one and reduce to compact support. Choose ((n-1)/n<\alpha<1), put (p=1/\alpha), and form
\(F=(|u_0|^2+\cdots+|u_n|^2)^{\alpha/2}\) and its boundary expression
\(F_0=(|f|^2+|R_1\mu|^2+\cdots+|R_n\mu|^2)^{\alpha/2}\).
The chosen exponent makes (F) subharmonic; the stated boundary, boundedness, decay, and local (L^q) conditions allow harmonic majorization (F\le P.I.F_0). Split (F_0) at λ/2 and apply the Hardy–Littlewood weak-(L^1) inequality to its maximal function. Layer-cake integration of the high part bounds its contribution in terms of the high tails of (F_0), hence the vector Riesz distribution. Equation (1.3) on physical p. 4 uses overlined `lim` (limsup) for the upper-tail bound. This gives a bound for the tail of (MF_0). Since
\((u_0^*)^\alpha\le \sup_yF(\cdot,y)\le MF_0\), the preliminary lemma forces a positive dimension-dependent lower tail for the vector Riesz transform.

**Limit notation.** Preserve the source's distinct limit marks: the main theorem (physical p. 2), Poisson-maximal lemma (physical p. 3), and final lower-tail conclusion (physical p. 6) use underlined `lim`, i.e. `liminf`; the one-dimensional Jones comparison uses ordinary `lim`; equation (1.3) (physical p. 4) uses overlined `lim`, i.e. `limsup`, for the upper bound. No ordinary-limit-existence gap is asserted for the higher-dimensional theorem: it claims the lower-limit bound that the proof concludes.

### Martingale transforms (§2, printed pp. 262–263)

On a probability space with filtration Φ(_1\subset\Phi_2\subset\cdots\), the article assumes that for each (n), the increment space has a fixed finite real orthonormal family (r_1^{(n)},\ldots,r_p^{(n)}), bounded by a common constant, measurable at time (n), with conditional mean zero and conditional covariance
\(\mathbb E(r_j^{(n)}r_k^{(n)}\mid\Phi_{n-1})=\delta_{jk}\). It further assumes these functions span every martingale increment as
\(d_n=\sum_{j=1}^p a_{n-1}^{(j)}r_j^{(n)}\), with Φ(_{n-1})-measurable coefficients. This includes dyadic martingales with Rademacher increments.

For a constant complex (p\times p) matrix (M), the transform replaces each coefficient vector (a_{n-1}) by (Ma_{n-1}). The first theorem says: if (M_1,\ldots,M_k) have no common real eigenvector, an (L^1)-bounded martingale in this increment class is uniformly integrable iff each transformed martingale's terminal limit has tail (\mathbb P(|Y_\infty^{(j)}|>\lambda)=o(\lambda^{-1})) as λ→∞. The second theorem is one-way sufficient: if there are matrices (P_j) with \(\sum_jP_jM_j=I\), and each maximal transform (M_j^*X=\sup_n|(M_jX)_n|\) has tail (\mathbb P(M_j^*X>\lambda)=o(\lambda^{-1})), then (X) is uniformly integrable. The proof outline applies Good-λ inequalities to (P_jM_jX), reconstructs (X) through the matrix identity, obtains (\mathbb P(\sup_n|X_n|>\lambda)=o(\lambda^{-1})), and invokes the cited UI criterion. The second result is not stated as an equivalence.

## Transfer limits

- The paper concerns the classical Euclidean Riesz conjugate system of finite measures, Poisson extensions, and a particular martingale-transform class. It does not establish the corresponding estimates for arbitrary Calderón–Zygmund kernels.
- The higher-dimensional Riesz constants (k_n,C_n) are not computed; the exact (2/\pi) statements are one-dimensional and have distinct hypotheses (Jones asymptotic for the singular part; Loomis all thresholds for positive finite atomic measures).
- The all-λ higher-dimensional lower/upper estimate requires ν positive and singular and uses \(\sum_j|R_j\nu|\), not the Euclidean norm in the main theorem.
- This source does not repair or address the missing general-kernel angular-norm factors in CZ-03. It may be used for Riesz-specific singular-mass detection and its stated martingale interface only.
