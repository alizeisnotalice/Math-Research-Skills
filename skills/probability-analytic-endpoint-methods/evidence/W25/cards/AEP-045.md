## Model and sharp Bellman statement (physical pp. 2–7)

The martingale is real-valued and discrete-time. For a predictable sequence $\theta$ with values in $[0,1]$, a transform is $g_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\theta_k\,df_k$. The Haar model uses deterministic coefficients $a_k\in\mathbb R$ and selectors $\theta_k\in\{0,1\}$. Define $D=\{(x,y,F):F\ge|x|\}$, and $B(x,y,F)$ as the supremum of $\mathbb P(|g_n|\ge1)$ over finite Haar sums with initial values $f_0=x$, $g_0=y$, and $L^1$ cost $\|f\|_1\le F$; equivalently, the paper uses martingale pairs with $dg_n=\theta_n df_n$ for $n\ge1$. The relation is allowed to fail at $n=0$, so this is an “almost” transform when $x\ne y$ (physical p. 3).

Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 4) identifies this supremum with the explicit function $B$. On the region $x\ge0$, $y\le x/2$, the three cases are: $B=1$ on $A_0$; $B=F+y(y-x)$ on $A_1$; and
\[
B(x,y,F)=1+\frac{(x-2y-2+F)^2}{4(y+1)(x-y-1)}
\]
on $A_2$. The regions are defined in the introduction: $A_0$ has $y\ge1$, $y\le x-1$, or $F+y(y-x)\ge1$; $A_1$ has $x-1<y<1$ and $2(x-y)(1+y)-x\le F<1-y(y-x)$; $A_2$ has $x-1<y<1$ and $F<2(x-y)(1+y)-x$. Extend by $B(x,y,F)=B(-x,-y,F)=B(x,x-y,F)$. The formula is for a probability supremum at level 1 and only this positive-contraction transform model.

Theorem 2.1 (physical p. 7) is the upper-bound mechanism. For any $c>0$, if $f,g$ are martingales with $dg_n=\theta_n df_n$ for $n\ge1$, where $\theta_n\in[0,1]$, then
\[
\mathbb P(\sup_n|g_n|\ge1)\le c\,\|f\|_1+\mathbb E U^c(f_0,g_0).
\]
Here $\|f\|_1$ is the martingale $L^1$ size used in the paper (supremum over time; for the finite martingales in the proof this is the terminal $L^1$ norm). For fixed $c$, $U^c$ is concave along every direction $(1,\theta)$, $\theta\in[0,1]$ (Lemma 2.2, pp. 6–7), and dominates $1_{\{|y|\ge1\}}-c|x|$ (Lemma 2.1, p. 5). Therefore $U^c(f_n,g_n)$ is a supermartingale, giving the finite-horizon tail estimate; stopping at first passage and taking limits handles the maximal event. Theorem 1.1's exact sharpness is proved in §3 (physical pp. 8–13) by explicit finite martingale constructions and approximation of the optimizing parameters.

## One-sided sharp lower bound and Haar transfer (physical pp. 13–18)

Theorem 4.2 (physical p. 14; visually checked) assumes $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R$, $t\in[0,1]$, a real martingale $f$ with $f_0=\alpha$, and transform $g$ with predictable sequence $\varepsilon=(1,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\ldots)$, $\varepsilon_k\in[0,1]$ for $k\ge1$. If
\[
\mathbb P(\sup_{n\ge0}g_n\ge\beta)\ge t,
\]
then sharply
\[
\|f\|_1\ge |\alpha|\vee\left\{2\beta-\alpha-2\left[\beta^+(\beta-\alpha)^+(1-t)\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\]
This event is one-sided; it is not the event $\sup_n|g_n|\ge\beta$. The first transform coefficient is fixed at 1; later coefficients lie in $[0,1]$. The proof stops at $\beta'<\beta$, truncates at a finite horizon, rescales to the Bellman problem $B$, sends the scale to infinity, and then lets the probability/level approximations approach $t,\beta$ (physical pp. 14–16). Sharpness is established by cases; in the nontrivial case IV (pp. 16–17), a three-stage martingale reaches the formula with $\gamma=\beta-\sqrt{\beta(\beta-\alpha)/(1-t)}$ and a terminal 0–2 walk.

Theorem 4.3 (physical pp. 17–18) transfers the same bound to finite Haar sums: $n\ge1$, real coefficients $a_k$, selectors $\theta_k\in[0,1]$, and a one-sided set of measure at least $t$ for $\alpha+\sum_{k=1}^n\theta_ka_kh_k\ge\beta$. It bounds the $L^1$ norm of the unselected sum $\alpha+\sum_{k=1}^na_kh_k$ by the same expression and says the bound is sharp. The martingale-difference identification supplies the transfer.

## Limits and endpoint mapping

This is a direct sharp endpoint result for the stated scalar martingale/Haar models, not for an arbitrary Calderón–Zygmund operator, a Riesz transform, signed transforms, or a vector-valued output. The symmetric $\theta\in[-1,1]$ Bellman problem is explicitly referred to another source; do not extend the positive-contraction formula to that case. For C207 this is auxiliary endpoint calibration only: a Riesz transfer requires a separate representation preserving this exact weak event and $L^1$ input, which this paper does not provide.
