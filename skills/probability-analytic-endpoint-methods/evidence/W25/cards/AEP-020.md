## Framework and claims read from the source

Let ((P_t)) be a symmetric Markov semigroup with invariant measure (\mu), generator (L\le0), associated Cauchy semigroup (Q_t), and (C=-(-L)^{1/2}). On the paper’s pointwise-defined test algebra (\mathcal A),
\[
\Gamma(f,g)=\tfrac12\{L(fg)-fLg-gLf\},\qquad
2\Gamma_2(f,g)=L\Gamma(f,g)-\Gamma(f,Lg)-\Gamma(Lf,g).
\]
The intrinsic assumption is that (\Gamma_2\ge0) pointwise on (\mathcal A\times\mathcal A). The algebra is assumed bounded, dense in every finite (L^p), stable under (L) and (P_t), with the regularity/integrability conditions detailed in §3. “Good function” is a source-specific core condition: §3 reduces the proof to (f\in L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal A); the result is then extended to (D_p(L)) functions without invariant component by approximation. It is not merely a size bound.

**Theorem 2 (physical pp. 6–8; printed pp. 149–151):** If ((P_t)) is a diffusion semigroup, then for (1<p<\infty), on the initial test/core class,
\[
\|Cf\|_p\le c_p\|\sqrt{\Gamma(f)}\|_p,
\]
where (c_p) is independent of the particular semigroup. This direction uses diffusion continuity in the (\sqrt{\Gamma}) submartingale step.

**Theorem 7 (physical pp. 14–17; printed pp. 157–160):** Under the general symmetric-semigroup and (\Gamma_2\ge0) hypotheses, for a good function and (p>2),
\[
\|\sqrt{\Gamma(f)}\|_p\le c_p\|Cf\|_p.
\]
The constants are martingale-theory constants, not explicit sharp constants. §3 extends this estimate to the appropriate (D_p(L)) class with invariant component removed.

**§4.1 dual consequence (physical pp. 28–29; printed pp. 171–172):** For (1<p\le2), dualizing Theorem 7 gives (\|Cf\|_p\le c_q\|\sqrt{\Gamma(f)}\|_p), (q=p/(p-1)\). This is the opposite direction from the Riesz-transform upper bound obtained by putting (f=Vg). The source explains that dualizing Theorem 2 to reverse this inequality creates a weighted Sobolev duality issue; for general non-diffusion semigroups, full norm equivalence across all (1<p<\infty) cannot be expected.

**§4.3 (physical pp. 30–31; printed pp. 173–174):** A separate all-(p) equivalence is stated for certain diffusions with a representation (\Gamma(f,f)=\sum_i(H_if)^2), an algebra-preserving family (H_i), and a common nonnegative (h) satisfying ([L,H_i]=hH_i). The proof uses a submarkovian Littlewood–Paley–Stein function and commutation. This is an extra structural assumption, not a consequence of (\Gamma_2\ge0) alone.

## Proof chain and source sections

1. §1 (physical pp. 3–8) establishes the easy domination (C\lesssim\sqrt\Gamma), proves gradient and (\Gamma_2) inequalities, and treats Theorem 2 for diffusions using the positive submartingale (Z_t=\Gamma(Q_tf)), Doob and Lenglart–Lépingle–Pratelli. It explicitly explains why that step does not automatically cover arbitrary non-diffusions.
2. §2 (physical pp. 8–17) develops the harder reverse estimate. Brownian motion on the half-line killed at zero supplies Green/potential and conditional-expectation formulas; martingale projection and Burkholder estimates control four integral quantities (A_1,dots,A_4). The proof obtains inequalities (19\(_1\))–(19\(_5\)); the submartingale bracket gives (19\(_2\)), (19\(_4\)), the vertical martingale gives (19\(_3\)), and (\Gamma_2\ge0) supplies (19\(_5\)). Combining these controls yields Theorem 7 for (p>2).
3. §3 (physical pp. 18–27) supplies the algebra, generator-domain, analyticity, convergence, and integration-by-parts justification omitted in the formal proof. It defines the good core and extends the estimate by semigroup/resolvent approximation, excluding the invariant component where required.
4. §4 (physical pp. 28–31) contains dualization, higher iterated (\Gamma_n) estimates under positivity of all required iterates, the restricted diffusion norm-equivalence case, limitations, and final article text. No appendix; physical p. 31 is printed p. 174 and ends the contribution.

## C207 use and migration gap

For Euclidean space with (L=\frac12\Delta), (\Gamma(f,f)=\frac12|\nabla f|^2), (C=-(-L)^{1/2}); the factors cancel under (f=Vg), so (\sqrt{\Gamma(Vg)}=|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1/2}g|), the Euclidean vector Riesz norm. Theorem 7 gives a dimension-free but unspecified strong-(L^p) upper bound for (p>2), subject to its core/domain conditions. The (1<p\le2) dual consequence only controls (C) by the gradient, which after (f=Vg) is a lower bound on the Riesz norm, not the requested upper bound. All-(p) upper control needs an additional structure such as §4.3; neither route supplies C207’s best dimension-uniform constant or a matching sharpness argument. The read correction only repairs the Proposition 1 proof display; it does not change this transfer boundary.
