## Martingale-tree result and proof mechanism (physical pp. 2–4)

The underlying σ-finite measure space has a filtration by measurable partitions ℚ_n, each atom of positive finite measure; atoms refine to children (an atom may equal a child at the next level), and the tree ℚ generates the σ-algebra. The martingale differences and transform are
\[
\Delta_Pf=\sum_{Q\in\mathrm{ch}(P)}\mathbf1_Q(\langle f\rangle_Q-\langle f\rangle_P),\qquad Tf=\sum_{P\in\mathcal Q}\epsilon_P\Delta_Pf,\quad |\epsilon_P|\le1.
\]
A sparse operator is $Sf=\sum_{P\in S}\langle f\rangle_P\mathbf1_P$, where the total measure of the sparse children of each $P\in S$ is at most $\tfrac12\mu(P)$ (equations (2.2)–(2.3), physical p. 3).

Theorem 2.4 (physical p. 3) says: for $f\in L^1(\mu)$ supported on $Q_0\in\mathcal Q$ and a martingale transform $T$, there is an $f,T$-dependent sparse operator $S$ with
\[
\mathbf1_{Q_0}|Tf|\le C S|f|.
\]
The same statement is asserted for the maximal transform truncation $T_\sharp$ defined in (3.2). The constant is absolute for this tree model; no sharp endpoint constant is identified.

Theorem 3.1 (physical p. 4) is the weak-$L^1$ input for $T_\sharp$. In the proof of Theorem 2.4, form the exceptional set where either the martingale maximal function or $T_\sharp f$ exceeds a fixed multiple of the root average; weak-$L^1$ gives $\mu(E)\le\tfrac12\mu(Q_0)$. Maximal exceptional atoms provide the sparse children. Decompose $Tf$ into a controlled root term and localized transforms $T_Pf$, then recurse. This proves the pointwise sparse domination, not a new proof of the weak-$L^1$ input.

## Euclidean Dini-CZ result and endpoint interface (physical pp. 5–10)

For the Euclidean statement, $T:L^2(\mathbb R^d)\to L^2(\mathbb R^d)$ is bounded (the proof sets its norm to 1) and has an off-support kernel representation. Its modulus $\omega$ is monotone increasing and subadditive, the kernel satisfies the stated regularity in $x$ and the dual regularity in $y$, and
\[
\int_0^1\omega(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}<\infty.
\]
Theorem 4.2 (physical p. 5) states that for compactly supported $f\in L^1(\mathbb R^d)$, 
\(|Tf|\lesssim S|f|\), where $S$ is a sum of at most $3^d$ positive sparse averages, each relative to a dyadic grid. It also states the same domination for $T_\sharp$.

The truncation in equation (4.4) is the tail truncation
\[
T_\sharp f(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\left|\int_{|x-y|>\delta}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|.
\]
Lemma 4.3 (physical p. 6) imports the classical weak-$L^1$ bound for this maximal truncation under the same Dini-CZ class; the paper points to Stein rather than reproving that estimate. The proof of Theorem 4.2 then uses finitely many shifted dyadic grids (Lemma 4.5), a localized weak-$L^1$ stopping step (Lemma 4.7, physical p. 7), and recursion (physical pp. 8–10) to make the output sparse. For a target operator, verify the off-support representation, $L^2$ bound, both kernel regularity conditions, Dini integrability and the centered/tail truncation convention before transferring this method. The cited classical weak-$L^1$ truncation estimate and the weak-$L^1$ behavior of the resulting sparse operator remain explicit inputs if one wants to state a weak-$(1,1)$ corollary. This source does not provide a sharp endpoint constant.

The local Euclidean maximal truncation is adapted to a cube $P$: smooth cutoffs $\psi$ supported in doubled cubes, scales $0<s<t<\tfrac1{12}\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial P)$, and the difference $\psi((x-y)/t)-\psi((x-y)/s)$ (physical p. 6). Lemma 4.7 chooses dyadic children of total measure below $3^{-3d-3}|P|$ and obtains the recursive pointwise estimate $T_{\sharp,P}f\le C\langle|f|\rangle_P+\max_{Q\in\mathcal P(P)}T_{\sharp,Q}f$. The Dini integral controls oscillation between nearby points. This exact recursion is the proof bridge; its constants depend on dimension and the modulus/operator data.

## Source-to-target use and limits

For C207 this is an auxiliary endpoint-method template: it gives sparse domination, and the target may obtain a weak-$(1,1)$ estimate only after satisfying the Euclidean hypotheses and invoking the sparse-operator weak bound. It is not a proof for an arbitrary vector-valued Riesz transform or a sharp vector constant. Theorem 2.6's printed weighted exponent is quarantined above.
