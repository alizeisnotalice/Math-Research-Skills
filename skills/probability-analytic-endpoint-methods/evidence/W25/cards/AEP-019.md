## Model and exact claims

On ((-1,1)), for (\alpha,\beta\ge0), the Jacobi generator is
\[
Lf=(1-x^2)f''-[(\alpha+\beta+2)x+\alpha-\beta]f',
\qquad d\mu(x)=c(1-x)^\alpha(1+x)^\beta dx.
\]
The normalized Jacobi polynomials (J_n) satisfy (LJ_n=-\lambda_nJ_n), (\lambda_n=n(n+\alpha+\beta+1)). The source defines (C=-(-L)^{1/2}), (VJ_n=J_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}) for (n\ge1), (Hf(x)=\sqrt{1-x^2}f'(x)), and (\Gamma(f,g)=Hf\,Hg).

**Theorem 2 (physical pp. 8–10; printed pp. 136–138):** For every mean-zero polynomial (f) and (1<p<\infty), there are constants (k_p,K_p>0) such that
\[
k_p\|Cf\|_{L^p(\mu)}\le\|Hf\|_{L^p(\mu)}\le K_p\|Cf\|_{L^p(\mu)}.
\]
“Universal” means martingale-theory constants independent of the particular Jacobi semigroup; no explicit or sharp values are given. By density on the mean-zero subspace, (HV) is a strong-(L^p) Jacobi Riesz transform bound in this one-dimensional model.

**Theorem 4 (physical pp. 12–15; printed pp. 140–143):** Three Jacobi Hardy spaces, (H^1_{\max},H^{1\uparrow}_{\max},H^1_{an}), coincide with equivalent norms. For (f\in H^1), (HVf) is defined as the strong-(L^1) limit (\lim_{t\downarrow0}HVQ_tf), and (HV:H^1\to L^1) is bounded. The source explicitly does not establish (HV:H^1\to H^1), and says the duality statement ( (H^1)^*=\mathrm{BMO}) remains unresolved there. This is not an (L^1\to L^1) or weak-((1,1)) result.

## Proof chain and source sections

1. §I (physical pp. 3–6) constructs the Jacobi diffusion, spectral semigroups (P_t,Q_t), (V) on mean-zero polynomials, (H), and the identities (L=H^2-aH), ([L,H]=hH), (h=H(a)>0). These give subharmonicity/semigroup inequalities for (Hf).
2. §II (physical pp. 6–8) uses (Y=(X,B)) with independent Brownian motion of quadratic variation (2t), harmonic/Poisson extensions, martingale projections, Doob, Burkholder–Davis–Gundy, and Lenglart–Lépingle–Pratelli estimates.
3. §III (physical pp. 8–10) computes the horizontal martingale bracket and compares it with the vertical projection. This gives one inequality between (|Hf|_p) and (|Cf|_p); duality, mean-zero polynomial density, and the identity (\langle Hf,Hg\rangle=\langle Cf,Cg\rangle) give the reverse inequality. The source explicitly says this duality step does not extend to dimensions above one.
4. §IV (physical pp. 10–15) proves a subharmonicity lemma for ( (\Phi^2+\Psi^2+\varepsilon)^{p/2}) for some (p\in(0,1)), obtains maximal estimates, and identifies the probabilistic and analytic (H^1) norms by regularizing (Q_t), Doob/Fatou, and strong (L^1) convergence. Physical p. 16 is the complete reference list (printed p. 144).

## C207 use and migration gap

This is a scalar one-dimensional Jacobi diffusion result. It gives a useful martingale proof pattern for a Jacobi (HV) transform and an (H^1\to L^1) endpoint. It does not give the Euclidean (d)-dimensional vector Riesz norm, dimension-free sharp constants, or Lebesgue weak type ((1,1)). Do not identify its scalar (H)-transform with a vector (\ell_2)-valued Riesz transform.
