- **Role:** C120 matrix-only context for Brownian stochastic integrals and matrix martingale concentration. It does not give a tensor Banach type-2 estimate or a general discrete/noncommutative $L_p$ martingale theorem.

## Setting and exact statements

Let $B^1,\ldots,B^N$ be standard Brownian coordinates on a filtered probability space, and let
\[
X_t=\int_0^t\sum_{i=1}^N H_{i,s}\,dB_s^i,\qquad
\langle X\rangle_t=\int_0^t\sum_{i=1}^N H_{i,s}^2\,ds,
\]
where each $H_{i,s}$ is progressively measurable and symmetric, $X$ is the resulting matrix-valued local martingale, and
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\int_0^t\sum_iH_{i,s}^2\,ds<\infty
\]
almost surely for every $t>0$. The filtration may be richer than the Brownian natural filtration.

**Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 2).** For all $p\in\mathbb N^*$ and $t>0$,
\[
\left(\mathbb E\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|X_s\|^p\right)^{1/p}
\le C\sqrt{p+\log n}\left(\mathbb E\|\langle X\rangle_t\|^{p/2}\right)^{1/p},
\]
with universal $C$; the proof gives $C=12\sqrt{2\log2}$. This has $\sqrt{p+\log n}$ and puts the matrix operator norm of the *integrated* quadratic variation inside the $p/2$-moment.

**Theorems 1.2–1.3 (physical pp. 2–3).** Matrix Freedman states
\[
\mathbb P\{\exists t>0:\lambda_{\max}(X_t)\ge u,\ \|\langle X\rangle_t\|\le\sigma^2\}
\le n e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}.
\]
The good-$\lambda$ refinement is
\[
\mathbb P\{\exists t>0:\lambda_{\max}(X_t)\ge2u,\ \|\langle X\rangle_t\|\le\sigma^2\}
\le n e^{-u^2/(2\sigma^2)}
\mathbb P\{\exists t>0:\lambda_{\max}(X_t)\ge u\}.
\]

**Theorem 1.4 (physical p. 3).** For integer $p\ge1$,
\[
\mathbb E\|X_t\|_{S_{2p}}^{2}
\le(2p-1)\,\mathbb E\int_0^t
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^N H_{i,s}^2\right\|_{S_p}\,ds.
\]
The left side is the second moment of the Schatten-$2p$ norm, and the integrand is the first power of the Schatten-$p$ norm. The expectation, time integral and powers are placed as printed; this is not
\[
\mathbb E\left\|\int_0^t\sum_iH_{i,s}^2\,ds\right\|_{S_p}.
\]
Do not conflate this theorem with Remark 1 on physical p. 4, which separately uses $f=\|\cdot\|_{S_{2p}}^{2p}$ and states
\[
\bigl(\mathbb E\|X_t\|_{S_{2p}}^{2p}\bigr)^{1/p}
\le(2p-1)\int_0^t\bigl(\mathbb E\|\sum_iH_{i,s}^2\|_{S_p}^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}\,ds.
\]

For rectangular $n_1\times n_2$ integrands, Hermitian dilation gives Theorem 4.1 (physical p. 8): the same maximal spectral-norm form holds with dimension factor $\sqrt{p+2\log(n_1+n_2)}$ and
\[
\Lambda_t=\max\left\{
\left\|\int_0^t\sum_iH_{i,s}H_{i,s}^*\,ds\right\|,
\left\|\int_0^t\sum_iH_{i,s}^*H_{i,s}\,ds\right\|
\right\}
\]
in place of the self-adjoint quadratic variation.

## Proof route and scope

Theorem 1.4 follows from Itô's formula for
$f(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X^{2p})^{1/p}=\|X\|_{S_{2p}}^2$,
a Hessian estimate based on Lemma 2.1, Schatten Hölder, stopping the local martingale term, and Fatou. The differential estimate is $df(X_t)\le dM_t+(2p-1)\|\sum_iH_{i,t}^2\|_{S_p}dt$; taking expectations at localized stopping times and then Fatou yields the displayed second-moment bound. Rectangular estimates follow by Hermitian dilation.

For Theorem 1.2, the key supermartingale is
$\operatorname{Tr}\exp(X_t-\tfrac12\langle X\rangle_t)$.
Itô's formula and the trace-Hessian inequality show it is a nonnegative supermartingale; Chernoff gives a fixed-time tail, then stopping at either the eigenvalue or quadratic-variation threshold gives the all-time bound. For Theorem 1.3, stop at the first crossing of $u$, restart the Brownian stochastic integral, and combine Weyl's inequality with the increment quadratic-variation bound. Theorem 1.1 applies that good-$\lambda$ inequality after dilation, splits according to quadratic variation, integrates the tail, and chooses the paper's $\delta$ to absorb the maximal-probability term.

The theorem is for finite-dimensional symmetric Brownian stochastic integrals with the displayed trace integrability. Its rectangular extension needs both row and column quadratic variations. It does not by itself cover arbitrary discrete-time matrix martingales, infinite-dimensional von Neumann algebras, or tensor injective norms.

## R06 follow-up — exact Schatten powers

The original physical PDF p. 3 and its p. 4 proof were rendered and checked. Theorem 1.4 is the second-moment estimate displayed above; it does not raise either norm to the $p$-th power. Remark 1 on p. 4 is a distinct $2p$-moment inequality with a $1/p$ root and the time integral outside that root. The card and method reference preserve this distinction. The exact scalar Brownian diagnostic in `checks/maitre_2025_thm14_scalar_diagnostic-proof.md` falsifies the two erroneous power placements in simple cases; this finite check is not a proof of the matrix theorem.
