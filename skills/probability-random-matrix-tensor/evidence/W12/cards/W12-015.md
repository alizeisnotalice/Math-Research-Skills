# W12-015 — Tropp, user-friendly matrix tail bounds

- **Identity:** Joel A. Tropp, *User-Friendly Tail Bounds for Sums of Random Matrices*, arXiv:1004.4389v7 (2011), 32 pages.
- **Read:** Full PDF pp. 1–32. The main Gaussian/Rademacher, Bernstein, and rectangular-matrix statements are on pp. 3–5; the proof sections, applications, appendices, and references were also read.
- **Role:** Matrix concentration toolkit, especially the Euclidean matrix case and martingale extensions.

## Checked results

For fixed self-adjoint matrices (A_k) and independent standard Gaussian or Rademacher ξ(_k), Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 4) gives
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\sum_k\xi_kA_k\right)\ge t\right\}
\le d\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2\|\sum_k A_k^2\|}\right).
\]
Theorem 1.4 gives the matrix Bernstein tail for independent mean-zero self-adjoint (X_k) with (\lambda_{\max}(X_k)\le R), variance (\sigma^2=\|\sum_k\mathbb E X_k^2\|), and exponent −(t^2/[2(\sigma^2+Rt/3)]). Theorem 1.5 (PDF p. 5) extends Gaussian/Rademacher series to rectangular matrices with the maximum of the row and column square sums and dimension factor (d_1+d_2).

## Limits

The proof is the matrix Laplace-transform/Lieb approach and the norms are spectral/operator norms on Hilbert space. It is appropriate for (r=p=2); flattening an order-(r) tensor into a matrix does not preserve the injective ℓp norm without additional estimates.
