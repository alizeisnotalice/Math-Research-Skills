## Exact model and estimates

Let $X$ be a symmetric $n\times n$ matrix with $X_{ij}=b_{ij}g_{ij}$, where $b_{ij}\ge0$ and the $g_{ij}$, $i\ge j$, are independent standard Gaussians, with entries reflected symmetrically. Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 3) states for every $2\le p<\infty$
\[
\bigl(\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_p}^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}
\asymp
\left(\sum_i\left(\sum_jb_{ij}^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}
+\sqrt p\left(\sum_i\max_j b_{ij}^{p}\right)^{1/p},
\]
with universal comparison constants. The same theorem gives a more refined formula for the first moment $\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_p}$ using the row rearrangement $b^*$: permute rows and columns so that $\max_j b^*_{1j}\ge\cdots\ge\max_j b^*_{nj}$. Then
\[
\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_p}\asymp
\left(\sum_i\left(\sum_jb_{ij}^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}
+\max_{i\le e^p}\max_j b^*_{ij}\sqrt{\log i}
+\sqrt p\left(\sum_{i\ge e^p}\max_j(b^*_{ij})^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]
At $p=\infty$,
\[
\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_\infty}\asymp
\max_i\left(\sum_jb_{ij}^2\right)^{1/2}
+\max_{i,j}b^*_{ij}\sqrt{\log i}.
\]
The $e^p$ split and $\sqrt{\log i}$ placement above were checked against the rendered theorem; they are not inferred from the abstract. Corollary 2.2 (physical p. 8) gives the universal-constant two-sided moment proxy
\[
\bigl(\mathbb E\|X\|_{S_p}^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}
\asymp
\left(\sum_i\left(\sum_jb_{ij}^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}
+\sqrt p\left(\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}^{p}\right)^{1/p}
\qquad(2\le p<\infty),
\]
which is an equivalent form of the sharper theorem-level second term, with universal constants.

## Proof route and scope

For the Gaussian moment bound, expand $\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(X^{2p})$ over closed walks. Independence and centered Gaussian moments force each edge in a nonzero term to occur an even number of times. Group walks by graph shape, reduce the graph sum to tree shapes, and iteratively prune vertices; the key graph inequality (Proposition 2.5 / Theorem 2.8) is then combined with Hölder. Complex interpolation extends the relevant moment comparison to noninteger $p$. For the operator norm, the proof permutes rows/columns by maximal coefficient, separates a leading block from the tail, uses Gaussian maxima on the first $e^p$ rows and a high-moment estimate on the remainder, and supplies matching lower bounds from row variance and Gaussian maxima. The probabilistic formulation also gives the infinite-matrix bounded-operator dichotomy in Corollary 1.2.

Section 4 uses self-adjoint dilation and bipartite cycle shapes for non-symmetric matrices. Heavy-tailed extensions in §4.2 require moment-growth assumptions and use Gaussian scale-mixture/conditioning comparisons; bounded-entry extensions in §4.3 are one-sided upper estimates, not the two-sided Gaussian theorem. The independent-entry and Gaussian assumptions are essential to the stated theorem. Do not transfer it to an arbitrary correlated Gaussian tensor series or substitute a generic Banach type-2 constant.
