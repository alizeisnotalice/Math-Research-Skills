## Norms and theorem

PDF p. 3, Eq. (1), defines the full multilinear norm
\[
\|T\|_{I_p}=\sup_{\|x_1\|_p,\ldots,\|x_r\|_p\le1}T[x_1,\ldots,x_r],\qquad p\ge2.
\]
Definition 1.1 (PDF p. 4) defines (C_{r,p}(d)) as the least constant such that, for every (n) and deterministic tensors (T_i\in(\mathbb R^d)^{\otimes r}),
\[
\mathbb E\Big\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iT_i\Big\|_{I_p}^{2}
\le C_{r,p}(d)^2\sum_i\|T_i\|_{I_p}^{2},
\]
with independent Rademacher εi. Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 4) proves, for integer (r\ge2), (2\le p<\infty),
\[
d^{1/2-1/p}\lesssim_{r,p} C_{r,p}(d)
\lesssim_{r,p}d^{1/2-1/\max\{p,2r\}}\log d.
\]
The (r,p)-dependent constants are fixed-order constants. For (p\ge2r), the two dimension exponents match up to log (d). For (2\le p<2r), the upper estimate loses a fixed power of (d) relative to the target exponent.

## Transfer to C120 and norm guardrail

The source’s footnote to Eq. (1) and Proposition 4.15 (PDF p. 35) compare the full and diagonal norms for symmetric tensors. For any underlying vector norm,
\[
\|T\|_{\mathrm{sym}}\le\|T\|_{\mathrm{full}}\le (r^r/r!)\,\|T\|_{\mathrm{sym}}.
\]
Thus, for fixed order (r), the theorem transfers between the two versions with only an order-dependent factor. It is not an equality for (p\ne2) in general. The exact finite-dimensional counterexample in `checks/norms_lp-proof.md` distinguishes them.

The Rademacher (L_2) type bound controls the expected Gaussian series: conditioning on |g_i| and applying the type estimate gives its Gaussian (L_2) bound; conversely the standard Kahane–Khintchine comparison recovers Rademacher type from Gaussian first moments. Hence the polynomial exponents are the relevant C120 comparison.

## Proof chain and limits

The upper bound is proved by reducing the Gaussian series to a Gaussian process, estimating covering numbers for an ℓp ball in its induced metric, applying Dudley’s entropy integral, and bounding the variance parameters by dimension/order estimates (PDF pp. 39–40). The lower bound uses an explicit family of (d) tensors whose signed sum contains a normalized ℓp vector (PDF p. 40). The sharper variance-profile statement is Theorem 1.6, PDF p. 7.

Remark 1.3 (PDF p. 5) gives the separate matrix improvement (C_{2,p}(d)\lesssim\sqrt{\log d}\,d^{1-2/p}), which is better than Theorem 1.2 for (2\le p\le8/3); at (p=r=2), NCK gives (C_{2,2}(d)\lesssim\sqrt{\log d}). These are matrix-specific facts, not high-order tensor proofs.
