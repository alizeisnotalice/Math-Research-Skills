# W12-020 — Junge–Xu, noncommutative Burkholder/Rosenthal inequalities II

- **Identity:** Marius Junge and Quanhua Xu, *Noncommutative Burkholder/Rosenthal Inequalities II: Applications*, arXiv:0705.1952v1 (14 May 2007), 38-page PDF. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1952); local-file SHA-256 is recorded in `sources.json`.
- **Read:** All physical PDF pp. 1–38, including independence definitions and examples, Rosenthal proofs in both index ranges, matrix and maximal variants, nonfaithful case, Banach/operator-space applications, and references.
- **Role:** Strong (L_p) Rosenthal/Burkholder inequalities for noncommutatively independent sums and martingales. Useful background for row/column and maximal-function structure; not a cross-2 Orlicz modular result.

## Independence and Rosenthal statements

The ambient setting is a σ-finite von Neumann algebra (M) with normal faithful state φ, a φ-invariant subalgebra (N), and its state-preserving conditional expectation (E=E_N); (L_p) is Haagerup (L_p(M)) (physical pp. 3–5). The authors distinguish faithful independence over (N) from order independence: the former requires (E(xy)=E(x)E(y)) when (x) is in one subalgebra and (y) in the von Neumann algebra generated by the others; the latter requires the preceding generated-algebra expectation to agree with (E) on each later subalgebra. Centered order-independent variables form martingale differences. Classical, tensor, free, (q)-Gaussian, and group-algebra examples are given in Sections 1 (pp. 5–9).

Theorem 2.1 (physical pp. 9–12), for a finite mean-zero sequence (x_k\in L_p(M)) and (2\le p<\infty), compares \(\|\sum_k x_k\|_p\) with the maximum of the diagonal term \(\|(x_k)\|_{\ell_p(L_p)}\) and the conditioned column and row square-function norms. Under independence over (E), the maximum lies between \(c_p^{-1}\|\sum x_k\|_p\) and (2\|\sum x_k\|_p\); under order independence the lower comparison has the paper’s stated (c_p^2) factor. The conditioned square functions are
\[
\|(x_k)\|_{L_p(M,E;\ell_2^c)}=\left\|\left(\sum_k E(x_k^*x_k)\right)^{1/2}\right\|_p,
\quad
\|(x_k)\|_{L_p(M,E;\ell_2^r)}=\left\|\left(\sum_k E(x_kx_k^*)\right)^{1/2}\right\|_p,
\]
with the Haagerup-space extension specified in the paper when the displayed products are not directly in the (L_p) core. If the variables are not centered, Corollary 2.3 also includes \(\|\sum E(x_k)\|_p\).

For (1<p\le2), Theorem 3.2 (physical pp. 14–15) gives the dual form: the infimum of diagonal, conditioned-column, and conditioned-row norms over decompositions (x_k=x_k^d+x_k^c+x_k^r) is comparable to \(\|\sum_kx_k\|_p\); the theorem states lower factor (1/2) and upper factor (c_{p'}). Section 3 gives the proof via complemented centered subspaces and sum/intersection duality, rather than extending the (p\ge2) maximum unchanged.

## Matrix and maximal-function consequences

Theorem 3.3 (physical pp. 15–16) applies these results to finite matrices of independent centered entries (x_{ij}\in L_p(M)): for (p\ge2), the Schatten-valued norm of \(\sum_{ij}x_{ij}\otimes e_{ij}\) is equivalent to the maximum of the diagonal ℓ\(_p(L_p)\), column and row conditional square functions; for (1<p<2), it is equivalent to the infimum over a corresponding diagonal/column/row decomposition. This is a Schatten (S_p) result, not a bound on arbitrary tensor injective norms.

Theorem 4.6 (physical pp. 21–22) replaces the diagonal ℓ\(_p(L_p)\) term by the noncommutative (L_p(M;\ell_\infty)) maximal norm for centered independent sums when (2<p<\infty). The proof combines the ordinary Rosenthal inequality, interpolation between (L_p(\ell_\infty)) and (L_p(\ell_2)), and the row/column comparison. The displayed proof gives a constant with a singularity as (p\downarrow2) (bounded by a constant times (p) for (p\ge4)). Theorem 4.7 gives the analogous maximal diagonal term for bounded (L_p) martingales in the same strict (p>2) range.

## Limits for transfer

These are strong (L_p)-norm equivalences under stated independence/filtration assumptions. They are not modular Φ-moment statements for arbitrary Orlicz functions crossing index 2 and do not settle B172. A maximal (L_p(\ell_\infty)) diagonal norm is not the B171 simultaneous weak-Φ decomposition. The independent-entry matrix specialization does not transfer to a general correlated Gaussian tensor series or C120’s type-2 constant.
