## Setting and exact claims

The paper works with a tracial von Neumann algebra $(\mathcal M,\tau)$, finite-support sequences $x=(x_i)$, and coefficient variables $\xi_i$ in an auxiliary tracial algebra $\mathcal A$. Write
\[
G_x=\sum_i x_i\otimes\xi_i,\qquad
R_x=\sum_i x_i\otimes e_{1i},\qquad
C_x=\sum_i x_i\otimes e_{i1}.
\]
The corresponding row/column norms are $\|R_x\|$ and $\|C_x\|$; the decomposition functional is
\[
m_p(x)=\inf_{x=y+z}\left(\|R_y\|_p^p+\|C_z\|_p^p\right),
\]
with the paper's quasi-norm convention for $0<p<1$.

The hypotheses in Section 3 are: (i) the $\xi_i$ are orthonormal in $L_2(\mathcal A)$; (ii) for some $p\in(0,1]$, the lower Khintchine estimate
\[
m_p(x)^{1/p}\le B_p\|G_x\|_p
\]
holds for every finite sequence $x$. Theorem 3.1 (physical p. 13) then gives a constant $C_p=C_p(A_p,B_p)$ such that, for every $x$ and all $t\ge0$, there exist $y,z$ with $x=y+z$ and
\[
K_t(R_y,p,\infty)\le C_pK_t(G_x,p,\infty),\qquad
K_t(C_z,p,\infty)\le C_pK_t(G_x,p,\infty).
\]
Here $A_p$ is the constant in the preliminary K-functional equivalence (4), relating $K_t(x,L_p,L_\infty)$ to $\bigl(\int_0^{t^p}\mu_s(x)^p\,ds\bigr)^{1/p}$; it is not an additional upper Khintchine hypothesis. $B_p$ is the constant in the assumed lower Khintchine estimate (5). This yields Corollary 3.2 on $L_{1,\infty}$ and the stated extension to interpolation spaces; it is a K-functional conclusion with one decomposition controlling every $t$.

For Section 4, the paper assumes the $L_\infty$ upper Khintchine estimate
\[
\|G_x\|_\infty\le c_\infty\max(\|R_x\|_\infty,\|C_x\|_\infty).
\]
Theorem 4.2 (physical p. 21) gives an algebraic factorization for finite-support $x$: there are positive finite-support $\alpha,\beta$ and $u$ with
\[
x=\alpha u+u\beta,\qquad
s(\alpha)\le |(R_u)^*|\le1,\qquad s(\beta)\le|C_u|\le1.
\]
Theorem 4.4 (physical pp. 22–23) then gives, for every $t>0,p>0$,
\[
K_t(\alpha,p,\infty)+K_t(\beta,p,\infty)\asymp_c K_t(G_x,p,\infty),
\]
where $c$ depends on $p$ and the $L_\infty$ Khintchine constant. The proof's lower estimate uses the Ricard anticommutator/Mazur-map inequality, a conditional expectation $\mathrm{Id}\otimes\tau_{\mathcal A}$, and the support bounds; the upper estimate applies the $L_\infty$ Khintchine inequality to the factorization.

Theorem 5.1 (physical p. 24) assumes a filtration $(\mathcal M_n)$ with conditional expectations $E_n:\mathcal M\to\mathcal M_n$, and a finite bounded martingale $x$. It constructs $x=y+z$ with $y,z$ finite bounded martingales such that the *same* decomposition satisfies, for every $p>1$,
\[
\|R(dy)\|_p+\|C(dz)\|_p\le k_p\|x\|_p.
\]
The proof starts with Theorem 3.1 at $p=1$, real-interpolates its simultaneous K-functional bounds, applies the difference projections $\Delta_n$, and uses Stein's inequality and Rademacher unconditionality to compare $\|G_{dx}\|_p$ with $\|x\|_p$. The result is a strong-$L_p$ row/column split; it is not a $\Phi$-modular martingale theorem.

Section 6 gives counterexamples in $L_{2,\infty}$ (physical pp. 25–26). Proposition 6.1 uses Schur–Horn to prescribe singular-value distributions of $G_x,C_x,R_x$; the examples show neither usual row-plus-column nor row-intersection-column formula can serve as a general $L_{2,\infty}$ Khintchine formula.

## Proof route and endpoint boundary

For Theorem 3.1 the proof controls projected sequences via lower Khintchine estimates and K-functionals, then chooses a near-optimal decomposition; tensoring with diffuse $L_\infty[0,1]$ preserves the decomposition functional. For Theorem 4.2 an optimal $L_1$ decomposition exists by a weak-compactness argument; duality supplies $u$, and polar decompositions give the support constraints. Theorem 4.4 combines this factorization with a K-functional commutator estimate derived from Ricard's fractional-power/Mazur-map inequality. The martingale result is the interpolation/projection argument described above.

Although the paper's all-$t$ K-functional estimates resemble endpoint decompositions, B171 concerns a specific weak-$\Phi$ modular tail expression, while B172 concerns a specific cross-2 $\Phi$-moment Burkholder inequality. The assumptions, target quantities, and index ranges do not match merely by identifying K-functionals or substituting an $L_p$ norm. Do not claim this paper resolves either endpoint.
