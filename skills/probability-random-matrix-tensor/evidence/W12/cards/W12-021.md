# W12-021 — Randrianantoanina–Wu, noncommutative Orlicz martingale inequalities

- **Identity:** Narcisse Randrianantoanina and Lian Wu, *Noncommutative Burkholder/Rosenthal Inequalities Associated with Convex Functions*, arXiv:1506.04134v1 (12 June 2015), 32 PDF pages. Local title page and official arXiv metadata agree on identity.
- **Read:** All PDF pp. 1–32, including the martingale/Orlicz setup, interpolation preliminaries, Theorems 4.1 and 4.4 and their proofs, independent-sequence corollaries, concluding open questions, and references.
- **Role:** Main evidence for the split at Boyd indices (2) in noncommutative Φ-moment Burkholder/Rosenthal inequalities; Cited for B172 only as the historically stated 2015 endpoint question.

## Setting and theorem statements

Throughout the paper, (M) is a semifinite von Neumann algebra with normal faithful semifinite trace τ, and (M_*) is assumed separable. The filtration ((M_n)) is increasing with weak*-dense union and has trace-preserving normal conditional expectations (E_n). The Orlicz function Φ satisfies the Δ₂ condition; (p_\Phi,q_\Phi) are its Matuszewska–Orlicz indices. Martingale differences are (dx_n=x_n-x_{n-1}), with (x_0=0), and

\[
s_c(x)=\Big(\sum_n E_{n-1}|dx_n|^2\Big)^{1/2},\qquad
s_r(x)=\Big(\sum_n E_{n-1}|dx_n^*|^2\Big)^{1/2}
\]

on the (L_2(M)+M) core; for general (L_\Phi) martingales these are interpreted through the conditioned Orlicz-Hardy space embeddings described in Section 2 (PDF pp. 8–10).

Theorem 4.1 (PDF p. 18), for (1<p_\Phi\le q_\Phi<2), gives

\[
\tau\Phi(|x|)\asymp_\Phi
\inf_{x=x_c+x_r+x_d}\left\{
\tau\Phi(s_c(x_c))+\tau\Phi(s_r(x_r))+
\sum_n\tau\Phi(|dx_n^d|)\right\},
\]

where (x_c,x_r,x_d) lie in the column, row, and diagonal conditioned Hardy spaces. Theorem 4.4 (original PDF physical p. 23), for (2<p_\Phi\le q_\Phi<\infty), gives

\[
\delta_\Phi^{-1}M_\Phi(x)\le\tau\Phi(|x|)\le\eta_\Phi M_\Phi(x),
\qquad
M_\Phi(x)=\max\left\{\sum_{n\ge1}\tau\Phi(|dx_n|),\tau\Phi(s_c(x)),\tau\Phi(s_r(x))\right\}.
\]

Thus the diagonal contribution is the **sum** \(\sum_n\tau\Phi(|dx_n|)\), not \(\sup_n\tau\Phi(|dx_n|)\). This is also repeated in the historical Problem 6.2 on physical p. 30, which asks whether the upper inequality holds at lower Boyd indices and discusses replacing that sum by a noncommutative Φ-moment maximal function. The constants \(\delta_\Phi,\eta_\Phi\) depend on Φ; these are Φ-moment inequalities, not norm identities obtained by substituting \(\Phi(t)=t^p\) without retaining the index and martingale hypotheses.

## Proof route and endpoint scope

For the low-index theorem, the upper inequality follows by interpolation of the noncommutative (L_p) Burkholder inequalities. The reverse direction uses the singular-value/K-functional identity on ((L_1,M)), transfers through (L_p,L_q) J-functionals, discretizes the representation, applies the simultaneous three-part decomposition (Theorem 2.5), and uses the diagonal/column/row embeddings and Δ₂ control; the proof occupies pp. 18–23. For the high-index theorem, the lower inequality follows from Proposition 3.3 and the noncommutative Burkholder inequalities (physical p. 23). The upper inequality uses the complementary Orlicz function \(\Phi^*\), Proposition 2.3, the low-index Theorem 4.1 on a polar-linked dual martingale, and the relevant embeddings/duality argument (physical pp. 23–25). The printed formula on p. 23 itself is unambiguous: the diagonal term inside \(M_\Phi\) is \(\sum_{n\ge1}\tau[\Phi(|dx_n|)]\).

Problem 6.2 (physical p. 30) asks whether the upper inequality in Theorem 4.4 extends to all \(1<p_\Phi\le q_\Phi<\infty\), and gives the dual low-range question. The authors describe these as open even for independent sequences at the time of writing. The same page expressly repeats the diagonal **sum** and asks about replacing it by a “Φ-moment” maximal-function term. This is a historical statement only; it does not certify current open status. B171 is a separate weak-Φ endpoint clue and is not identified with Problem 6.2.
