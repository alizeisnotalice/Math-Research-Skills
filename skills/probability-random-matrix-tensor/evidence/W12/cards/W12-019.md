# W12-019 — Junge, Doob’s inequality for noncommutative martingales

- **Identity:** Marius Junge, *Doob’s Inequality for Non-commutative Martingales*, arXiv:math/0206062v1 (6 June 2002), 49-page PDF. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206062); local-file SHA-256 is recorded in `sources.json`.
- **Read:** All physical PDF pp. 1–49, including the Haagerup/Kosaki (L_p) preliminaries, conditional column spaces, duality/interpolation proofs, applications, and references.
- **Role:** Noncommutative Doob maximal factorization built from a dual positive-sequence inequality, conditional expectations, Hilbert (C^*)-modules and duality. It is background for noncommutative martingale maximal interfaces, not an Orlicz endpoint theorem.

## Setting and theorem statements

The paper works with a von Neumann algebra (N), Haagerup noncommutative (L_p(N)), a normal faithful state and a filtration (N_n\subset N) with state-preserving conditional expectations (E_n); in the tracial semifinite case the usual tracial (L_p) realization applies. The main “dual Doob” inequality, Theorem 0.1 (physical p. 3), says for (1\le p<\infty) and positive (x_n\in L_p(N)),
\[
\left\|\sum_n E_n(x_n)\right\|_p\le c_p\left\|\sum_n x_n\right\|_p.
\]
Theorem 0.2 (physical p. 3) derives a maximal factorization: for (1<p\le\infty) and (x\in L_p(N)),
\[
E_n(x)=a y_n b,\qquad a,b\in L_{2p}(N),\quad \|y_n\|_\infty\le1,\quad
\|a\|_{2p}\|b\|_{2p}\le c_{p'}\|x\|_p,
\]
where (p'=p/(p-1)). If (x\ge0), the positive version provides one (b\in L_p(N)_+) with (E_n(x)\le b) simultaneously for every (n). The maximal conclusion is for (p>1), even though the dual positive-sequence inequality includes (p=1).

## Proof chain

The introduction (physical pp. 1–4) explains why the naive operator supremum of (E_n(x)) is unavailable in general and formulates the dual inequality. Section 1 develops Haagerup (L_p), state-preserving expectations, analytic-core density and module estimates (pp. 5–8). Section 2 (pp. 9–22) constructs conditioned column (L_p)-spaces; Kasparov stabilization embeds them in vector-valued column spaces, yielding Cauchy–Schwarz and duality, while noncommutative Stein projections control the filtration-dependent sequence space.

For (1\le p\le2) (Section 3, pp. 22–34), the proof starts from an elementary (p=2) estimate, interpolates the dual positive-sequence inequality, identifies the ℓ\(_1\)-valued sequence norm and its dual factorization by Hahn–Banach/Grothendieck–Pietsch separation, then takes the adjoint map to obtain the maximal factorization. For (2\le p<\infty) (Section 4, pp. 34–41), a trace inequality for ordered positive operators and a Stein-inequality duality estimate prove the dual inequality; Lemma 3.9 transfers it back to Doob. This is a functional-analytic substitute for a stopping-time argument.

Section 5 (pp. 41–48) derives submartingale factorization, a square-function Doob decomposition estimate, vector-valued Doob, and applications to compact-group actions. These applications use the proved (L_p) factorization and martingale inequalities; the paper does not state a convex-Orlicz Φ-moment theorem.

## Limits for transfer

The result is a maximal factorization/order-majorant statement for (p>1), not a single row/column decomposition of martingale differences with a simultaneous weak-Φ tail estimate for every threshold. It therefore does not by itself solve the isolated B171 question. It also does not provide the cross-2 convex-Orlicz modular inequality asked in B172. No current open-status claim is made from this 2002 paper.
