# W12-013 — Gittens–Tropp, tail bounds for all eigenvalues of matrix sums

- **Identity:** Alex Gittens and Joel A. Tropp, *Tail Bounds for All Eigenvalues of a Sum of Random Matrices*, arXiv:1104.4513v2 (21 July 2011), 23-page PDF. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.4513); local-file SHA-256 is recorded in `sources.json`.
- **Read:** All physical PDF pp. 1–23, including minimax-Laplace derivations, Chernoff/Bennett/Bernstein theorems and proofs, both applications, supporting-lemma proofs, and references.
- **Role:** Matrix-specific concentration for each eigenvalue of a sum of independent self-adjoint matrices; useful context for C120 only in its Euclidean order-two matrix case.

## Main inequalities and assumptions

Eigenvalues are ordered decreasingly. Proposition 2.1 (physical p. 6) gives both Courant–Fischer formulas: (\lambda_k(A)=\min_{V\in V_{n,n-k+1}}\lambda_{\max}(V^*AV)=\max_{V\in V_{n,k}}\lambda_{\min}(V^*AV)). Theorem 3.1 (p. 6) therefore bounds the upper tail of one eigenvalue of any random self-adjoint (X):
\[
\mathbb P\{\lambda_k(X)\ge t\}\le\inf_{\theta>0}\min_{V\in V_{n,n-k+1}}
e^{-\theta t}\,\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}e^{\theta V^*XV}.
\]
Theorem 3.2 (p. 7) passes from independent self-adjoint summands (X_j) satisfying (\mathbb E e^{X_j}\preceq e^{A_j}) to a compressed trace-exponential bound. Theorem 3.3 (pp. 7–8), the minimax Laplace transform theorem, has (i) a bound when (\mathbb E e^{\theta X_j}\preceq e^{g(\theta)A_j}), and (ii) a sharper compression-aware version when the moment-generating bound depends on the isometry (V).

For independent positive-semidefinite (X_j\in M_n), Theorem 4.1 (pp. 8–10) bounds both tails of (\lambda_k(\sum_jX_j)). With (\mu_k=\lambda_k(\sum_j\mathbb EX_j)), and top/bottom invariant-subspace isometries (V_+,V_-), the upper tail has factor (n-k+1) and exponent \(\mu_k/\Psi(V_+)\); the lower tail has factor (k) and exponent \(\mu_k/\Psi(V_-)\), with the binomial Chernoff functions (e^\delta/(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}) and (e^{-\delta}/(1-\delta)^{1-\delta}), respectively. Here (\Psi(V)\) is an almost-sure upper bound for the largest eigenvalue of each compressed summand.

For centered independent self-adjoint summands with the same compression control, Theorem 5.1 (pp. 10–11) gives the (k)-th eigenvalue Bennett and Bernstein bounds in terms of \(\sigma_k^2=\lambda_k(\sum_j\mathbb E X_j^2)\) and (\Psi(V_+)\). The Bernstein exponent is \(-t^2/(2\sigma_k^2+2\Psi(V_+)t/3)\); split estimates show Gaussian decay for (t\le\sigma_k^2/\Psi(V_+)\) and exponential decay after that. Theorem 5.3 (pp. 11–13) replaces bounded increments by a matrix subexponential moment-growth condition (\mathbb E X_j^m\preceq(m!/2)B^{m-2}\Sigma_j^2) for all integers (m\ge2), yielding the corresponding unbounded Bernstein bound.

## Proof and applications

The minimax method first compresses (X) to a subspace selected by Courant–Fischer, applies Markov to the trace exponential, and then uses Lieb–Seiringer concavity of (A\mapsto\operatorname{tr}\exp(H+V^*(\log A)V)) to interchange conditional expectations with compression (Theorem 3.2). Matrix MGF lemmas then give Chernoff/Bennett/Bernstein forms by optimizing (\theta); lower-tail eigenvalue bounds follow by applying the upper-tail result to \(-X\).

Theorem 6.1 (physical pp. 13–14) applies the eigenvalue Chernoff bound to Bernoulli column subsampling of an (n\times r) matrix with orthonormal rows, with coherence-like (\tau_k=\min_{V\in V_{n,k}}\max_j\|V^*u_j\|^2\), and bounds each singular value. In Section 7, Theorem 7.1 (pp. 15–18) controls each sample-covariance eigenvalue for i.i.d. (N(0,C)) samples, (C\succ0), using the eigenvalue tail sums above and below (k); the proof compresses to fixed invariant subspaces (Lemma 7.3), invokes a rank-one Wishart moment bound (Lemma 7.4), then applies Theorem 5.3. Corollary 7.2 gives relative error estimates. Theorem 1.1 follows when \(\sum_{i>\ell}\lambda_i(C)=O(\lambda_1(C))\): (n=\Omega(\epsilon^{-2}\kappa_\ell^2\ell\log p)\) samples suffice, with high probability, for relative (1\pm\epsilon) accuracy of the top \(\ell\) eigenvalues.

## Limits for transfer

All claims concern finite-dimensional self-adjoint matrix sums and eigenvalues or matrix singular values. They do not establish C120’s type-2 estimate for general-order or non-Euclidean tensor injective norms, and the sample-covariance theorem is specific to its stated Gaussian/Wishart moment assumptions.
