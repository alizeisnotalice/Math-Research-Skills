# W12-008 — Vershynin, concentration inequalities for random tensors

- **Identity:** Roman Vershynin, *Concentration Inequalities for Random Tensors*, arXiv:1905.00802v5 (3 October 2025), 19-page local PDF. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00802); local-file SHA-256 is recorded in `sources.json`.
- **Read:** All physical PDF pp. 1–19, including the concentration preliminaries, maximal inequality, proofs of Theorems 1.3–1.4, conditioning application, final discussion, and references.
- **Role:** Concentration for *simple product tensors* built from independent factor vectors with independent coordinates. This is adjacent concentration input for Euclidean tensors; it does not cover symmetric powers of one vector or arbitrary correlated tensor series.

## Model and main statements

A simple order-(d) tensor is (X=x_1\otimes\cdots\otimes x_d\in\mathbb R^{n^d}), where the (x_k\in\mathbb R^n) are independent, and each factor has independent, centered, unit-variance coordinates. Theorem 1.3 (physical pp. 2–3) assumes those coordinates are almost surely bounded. For convex Lipschitz (f:\mathbb R^{n^d}\to\mathbb R), it states
\[
\mathbb P\{f(X)-\mathbb Ef(X)>t\}\le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{ct^2}{d n^{d-1}\|f\|_{\rm Lip}^2}\right),
\quad 0\le t\le2\bigl(\mathbb E|f(X)-f(0)|^2\bigr)^{1/2},
\]
where (c>0) depends only on the coordinate bound. The v5 theorem includes (f(0)) in its range condition; a footnote explains that an earlier published rendering omitted it.

Theorem 1.4 (physical p. 3) assumes instead that the coordinates are independent centered unit-variance subgaussian variables. For a Hilbert space (H), a linear map (A:\mathbb R^{n^d}\to H), and (0\le t\le2\|A\|_{\rm HS}),
\[
\mathbb P\{\|AX\|_H-\|A\|_{\rm HS}\ge t\}
\le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{ct^2}{d n^{d-1}\|A\|_{\rm op}^2}\right),
\]
with (c) depending only on the subgaussian norms of the coordinates. Both theorems have a wider range noted in Remark 1.5, but the displayed theorem ranges are the clean statements used here.

## Proof route and other results read

Sections 2–3 (physical pp. 7–11) prove an MGF estimate for centered subgaussian quadratic forms by separating diagonal and off-diagonal terms, controlling the latter by decoupling, and deduce an MGF bound for Euclidean functions. Lemma 3.2 is a maximal inequality for all partial products \(n^{-k/2}\prod_{i=1}^k\|x_i\|_2\), obtained by dyadic/binary interval decomposition and a union bound over the intervals.

For Theorem 1.3 (physical pp. 12–13), condition successively on the remaining factor vectors. The maximal product event bounds the Lipschitz constant of (f) as a function of each factor; convex concentration gives subgaussian bounds on the successive conditional-expectation increments. Lemma 4.1 combines these increments in an MGF estimate, and exponential Markov plus the theorem’s restricted range absorbs the exceptional maximal-event term. Theorem 1.4 (physical pp. 14–16) applies the Euclidean-function MGF estimate recursively to the tensor product and optimizes by Chernoff. Sections 5–6 establish distance-to-subspace and conditioning consequences; in particular, Corollary 6.2 assumes (\epsilon\gtrsim d^2\log n/n), (\epsilon\le1/2), and (m\le(1-\epsilon)n^d) for its lower singular-value statement.

## Limits for transfer

The product structure (x_1\otimes\cdots\otimes x_d) and independence across factors and coordinates are essential hypotheses. The article explicitly does not handle symmetric tensors (x^{\otimes d}). Its Euclidean concentration estimates do not prove C120’s Banach type-2 bound or cover a general correlated Gaussian tensor series. It concerns norm concentration, not Banach-space type constants.
