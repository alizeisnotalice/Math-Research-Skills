## Model and exact statements

The base model (2.1) is
\[
X=Z_0+\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i,
\]
where $Z_0$ is deterministic self-adjoint and the $Z_i$ are independent, centered, self-adjoint $d\times d$ random matrices. $G$ is the self-adjoint Gaussian matrix with the same mean and entry covariance as $X$. $X_{\rm free}$ is the covariance-matched semicircular-family model in a tracial $C^*$-probability space. The parameters used below are
\[
\sigma(X)=\|\mathbb E(X-\mathbb EX)^2\|^{1/2},\qquad
\sigma_*(X)=\sup_{\|v\|=\|w\|=1}
\bigl(\mathbb E|\langle v,(X-\mathbb EX)w\rangle|^2\bigr)^{1/2},
\]
\[
v(X)=\|\operatorname{Cov}(X)\|^{1/2},\qquad
R(X)=\max_i\|Z_i\|_{L_\infty}.
\]

**Theorem 2.6 (spectrum universality, physical p. 11).** For every $t\ge0$,
\[
\mathbb P\{d_H(\operatorname{sp}(X),\operatorname{sp}(G))>C\epsilon(t)\}\le d e^{-t},
\qquad
\epsilon(t)=\sigma_*(X)\sqrt t+R(X)^{1/3}\sigma(X)^{2/3}t^{2/3}+R(X)t,
\]
with universal $C$. Also
\[
\mathbb E d_H(\operatorname{sp}(X),\operatorname{sp}(G))
\lesssim \sigma_*(X)\sqrt{\log d}
+R(X)^{1/3}\sigma(X)^{2/3}(\log d)^{2/3}+R(X)\log d.
\]
The Hausdorff distance is genuinely two-sided here. Corollary 2.7 (physical p. 12) gives the corresponding upper-edge and operator-norm comparison; lower-edge results follow by applying it to $-X$.

**Theorem 2.16 (sharp matrix concentration, physical p. 14).** Combining Theorem 2.6 with the Gaussian/free comparison of the cited preceding work gives, for every $t\ge0$,
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
\operatorname{sp}(X)\subseteq \operatorname{sp}(X_{\rm free})+
\left[Cv(X)^{1/2}\sigma(X)^{1/2}(\log d)^{3/4}+\epsilon(t)\right][-1,1]
\right\}\ge1-2de^{-t}.
\]
The theorem also states the corresponding one-sided upper-edge probability and expected upper-edge bound. Its spectrum-set statement is a one-sided inclusion; it is not a Hausdorff theorem for the full spectra.

Other checked results include Theorem 2.9 (even normalized trace-moment universality, physical p. 12) and Theorem 2.10 (resolvent and $W^{5,1}$ functional-calculus universality, physical p. 12). The moment theorem assumes $p\in\mathbb N$, $2p\le q\le\infty$, and gives errors in terms of $R_q(X),\sigma_q(X)$; the resolvent theorem assumes bounded summands and $\Im z>0$. The unbounded versions are Theorems 2.8 and 2.11 and use truncation parameters.

## Proof route and limits

The universality proof develops a multivariate cumulant expansion for independent summands, then controls its higher-order trace terms using a high-order trace inequality and concentration of resolvent norms/statistics. For Theorem 2.6, Sections 6–7 first establish resolvent-moment universality, then convert resolvent control to spectral-support control with nets; the reverse Hausdorff direction is proved separately. Theorem 2.16 is obtained by a union bound with the Gaussian/free spectral comparison. Section 8 truncates unbounded summands, bounds truncation errors, and reapplies the bounded results. Section 9 supplies the application-specific reductions.

The results concern self-adjoint matrix sums. Non-self-adjoint singular values can be treated by Hermitian dilation; the paper explicitly does not bound complex eigenvalues of a non-self-adjoint matrix. The parameter $R(X)$ depends on the chosen independent-summand representation, while the covariance parameters do not. None of these matrix statements identifies spectral norm with an $\ell_p$ tensor injective norm outside the Euclidean order-two case.
