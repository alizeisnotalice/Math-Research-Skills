# W12-010 — Bandeira–Cipolloni–Schröder–van Handel, free probability II

- **Identity:** Afonso S. Bandeira, Giorgio Cipolloni, Dominik Schröder, Ramon van Handel, *Matrix Concentration Inequalities and Free Probability II: Two-Sided Bounds and Applications*, arXiv:2406.11453v2 (11 November 2025), 49 PDF pages. The local title page and official arXiv record agree on identity/version.
- **Read:** All PDF pp. 1–49, including Theorems 2.2–2.12, the ultracontractive and matrix-concentration proofs (Sections 4–7), all application proofs (Section 8), and references. Theorem 2.2 is stated on p. 8 and proved on pp. 26–28; Theorem 2.12 is stated on p. 12 and proved on pp. 32–39.
- **Role:** Two-sided matrix/free-model edge control and matrix applications. It does not extend tensor estimates outside the Euclidean order-two matrix setting.

## Exact setup and spectral comparison

The paper uses the jointly Gaussian matrix model (X=A_0+\sum_i g_iA_i), and its free counterpart (X_{\rm free}=A_0\otimes1+\sum_i A_i\otimes s_i) (PDF pp. 7–8). Its parameters are

\[
\sigma^2=\|\mathbb E(X-\mathbb EX)^*(X-\mathbb EX)\|\vee\|\mathbb E(X-\mathbb EX)(X-\mathbb EX)^*\|,
\quad v^2=\|\operatorname{Cov}(X)\|,
\quad \sigma_*^2=\sup_{\|u\|=\|w\|=1}\mathbb E|\langle u,(X-\mathbb EX)w\rangle|^2,
\quad \widetilde v^2=v\sigma.
\]

Theorem 2.2 (PDF p. 8) says for self-adjoint (X),

\[
\mathbb P\{d_H(\operatorname{sp}(X),\operatorname{sp}(X_{\rm free}))>
C\widetilde v(\log d)^{3/4}+C\sigma_*t\}\le e^{-t^2},\quad t\ge0.
\]

Corollary 2.3 (PDF pp. 8–9) gives the two-sided expectation and tail comparison for ‖X‖ and ‖Xfree‖, and for both edges when (X) is self-adjoint. Theorem 2.5 (p. 9) is a distinct quadratic comparison for (XX^*+B). For isotropic low-rank spikes, Theorem 2.7 (p. 10) assumes (\mathbb E(X-\mathbb EX)^2=I), (r=\operatorname{rank}(\mathbb EX)), and (\sigma_*\sqrt r\le1), and compares (\lambda_{\max}(X_{\rm free})) with (B(\lambda_{\max}(\mathbb EX))), where (B(\theta)=2) for θ≤1 and θ+θ⁻¹ for θ>1. Theorem 2.9 separately transfers edge stability into an eigenvector-overlap estimate.

## Application and proof boundaries

For Theorem 2.12 (PDF p. 12), partition \([d]=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_q\) with \(|C_k|>1\); take \(B\in M_q(\mathbb R)_{sa}\) with nonnegative irreducible entries and \(z\in\mathbb R^d\) normalized by \(\sum_{i\in C_k}z_i^2=|C_k|\). If \(\bar B\) is the block lift, the model is \(X=d^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(z)\bar B\operatorname{diag}(z)+X_\emptyset\), where \(X_\emptyset=-\operatorname{diag}(d^{-1}\bar B1_d)+G\), and \(G\) is symmetric with independent centered Gaussian entries below/on the diagonal and variances \(\mathbb E G_{ij}^2=(1+\mathbf 1_{i=j})\bar B_{ij}/d\). With \(c_k=|C_k|/d\), the outlier transition is determined by \(\lambda_{\max}(\operatorname{diag}(c)^{1/2}B\operatorname{diag}(c)^{1/2})\) crossing 1: below 1, \(\lambda_\emptyset=\lambda<1\); at 1 both equal 1; above 1, \(\lambda_\emptyset<\lambda=1\). Its proof (pp. 32–39) uses a finite-dimensional Lehner variational principle, reduction to an invariant algebra, perturbation estimates, optimality conditions, Perron–Frobenius positivity, and continuity. Section 3 applies the results to graph-label recovery, tensor PCA, block models, contextual SBM, and sample covariance, with proofs in Section 8 (pp. 39–46).

The tensor-PCA application is a matrix linearization for a symmetric Euclidean tensor model and a specified parameter regime; it is not a general tensor injective-norm type-2 estimate. These spectral-norm results identify C120 with a tensor problem only when the original problem has already reduced to the Euclidean matrix case.
