# W12-031 — Gaunt–Ouimet, exact integer Wishart-minor moments

- **Identity:** Robert E. Gaunt and Frédéric Ouimet, *On Wilks’ Problem: Exact Recursive Formulas via Stein’s Method for the Joint Moments of Disjoint Principal Minors of Wishart Random Matrices*, arXiv:2607.03984v1, 4 July 2026, 9 pages.
- **Read:** All PDF pp. 1–9. Theorem 3.1 is stated on PDF p. 4; its proof, including the degree-lowering argument and Eq. (3.8), occupies pp. 4–5. The numerical-validation design/results are in Section 4, pp. 6–8, followed by references.
- **Role:** New exact-computation progress for C205’s integer-exponent subfamily; not a sign proof of C205 and not a C206 event result.

## Exact theorem and proof chain

Let (p=p_1+\cdots+p_d), (W=(W_{ij})\sim W_p(\alpha,\Sigma)), (\alpha>p-1), and (n_i\in\mathbb N_0). Put
\[
P_n(S)=\prod_{i=1}^d|S_{ii}|^{n_i},\qquad m_n=\sum_i n_ip_i.
\]
For a polynomial (P), define the Wishart Stein operator
\[
(\mathcal R_{\alpha,\Sigma}P)(S)
=\alpha\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma\nabla P(S))
+2\,\mathrm{tr}(S\nabla\Sigma\nabla P(S)).
\]
Theorem 3.1 (statement on PDF p. 4; proof continues on pp. 4–5) states that ℛ lowers homogeneous polynomial degree by one and
\[
\mathbb E\prod_i|W_{ii}|^{n_i}
=\frac1{m_n!}(\mathcal R_{\alpha,\Sigma}^{m_n}P_n)(S),
\]
where the final polynomial is constant. The key one-step identity (Eq. (3.8), PDF p. 5) is (\mathbb E P(W)=m^{-1}\mathbb E(\mathcal R_{\alpha,\Sigma}P)(W)) for homogeneous degree (m\ge1); it follows from the Wishart Stein identity and Euler’s homogeneous-function identity. Iterating terminates after finitely many steps.

Theorem is valid for arbitrary finite (d), positive block sizes, all α(>p-1), and arbitrary Σ positive definite, but only nonnegative integer exponents. Remark 3.2 (PDF p. 6) says noninteger powers introduce inverse block matrices and destroy the finite polynomial degree-lowering recursion. Exact evaluation gives a route to test integer cases; it does not itself establish nonnegativity of the GPI difference for all parameters. The local proof was also checked computationally in MATLAB in the p=1 case: for α=2.5, Σ=1.7, and n=4, iterating the stated recursion gives 5191.35215625, matching the closed Gamma/Wishart moment to the printed precision. This finite scalar check is numerical corroboration only; the general result rests on the paper’s Stein identity and homogeneous-polynomial proof. The MATLAB script and captured output are `checks/wishart_scalar_recursion.m` and `checks/wishart_scalar_recursion.out`.
