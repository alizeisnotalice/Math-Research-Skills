# Matrix-exponential \(GL_n\) dimension defect

Source. Papas, *Ax-Schanuel for \(GL_n\)*, arXiv:1905.04364v2, 14 October 2021, physical pp. 1--2 and 33--35, SHA-256 `4470d1c7b06aa719e7d528368c0d807e292392ea1a021488e9d8dde1a35aaa16`.

Theorem 10.1 takes a weakly special, origin-containing algebraic \(U\subset\mathfrak{gl}_n\), sets \(X=\exp(U)\), and takes algebraic \(V\subset U\), \(Z\subset X\) containing \(0\) and \(I_n\). If \(C\) is a complex-analytically irreducible component of \(V\cap\exp^{-1}(Z)\) containing \(0\), and \(C\) is not contained in a proper weakly special subvariety of \(U\), then
\[
 \dim C\leq\dim V+\dim Z-\dim X.
\]

The source defines weakly special \(U\) through eigenvalue-block data, rational linear equations and a residual algebraic parameter variety. Its bi-algebraicity requires equality of the dimension of the exponential image and its Zariski closure. The origin and identity requirements address positive-dimensional components of \(\exp^{-1}(I_n)\).

The proof reduces to scalar Ax--Schanuel in the eigenvalue coordinates. A numerical matrix exponential, a finite eigenvalue approximation or an arbitrary invariant subspace does not establish weak specialness, bi-algebraicity or the analytic-component condition.
