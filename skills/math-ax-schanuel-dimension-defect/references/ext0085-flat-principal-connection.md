# Flat principal-connection dimension defect

Source. Blázquez-Sanz, Casale, Freitag and Nagloo, *A differential approach to Ax--Schanuel, I*, arXiv:2102.03384v5, 1 June 2026, physical pp. 1--2 and 16--17, SHA-256 `0c82e8ae9bb39d6f615bf84e02dbef1be7741859571aacfdc46f25eb015f55be`.

Theorem A takes a possibly irregular flat \(G\)-principal connection \(\nabla\) on \(P\to Y\), with sparse differential Galois group \(\operatorname{Gal}(\nabla)=G\). For an irreducible algebraic \(V\subset P\), a horizontal leaf \(L\), and an irreducible analytic \(\mathcal V\subset V\cap L\), the strict inequality
\[
 \dim \mathcal V < \dim V+\dim G
\]
forces the projection of \(\mathcal V\) to \(Y\) into a \(\nabla\)-special subvariety. The theorem defines this as a maximal irreducible locus on which the restricted differential Galois group is proper.

For a supplied \((G,G/B)\)-uniformization, Corollary B and Corollary 3.17 formulate the defect as \(\dim W<\dim U+\dim Y\) for a component \(U\) of its graph intersected with algebraic \(W\subset G/B\times Y\). These corollaries still require the stated irreducibility, graph intersection and sparse-group hypotheses.

The source identifies the nonalgebraic horizontal leaf and invokes its principal-connection construction. Any application must supply a proof that the differential Galois group equals the declared sparse \(G\), rather than infer it from numerical monodromy or a finite differential system.
