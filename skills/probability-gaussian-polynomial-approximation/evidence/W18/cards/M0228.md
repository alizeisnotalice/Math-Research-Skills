## Exact source results

- **Theorem 1 (p. 1; Ann. Probab. 27(4) (1999), 1922–1938).** Let `μ` be a centered Gaussian measure on a separable Banach space `F`; let `A⊂F` be symmetric, convex and closed; and let `P={x:|x*(x)|≤1}` be a symmetric strip with `μ(A)=μ(P)`. Then `μ(tA)≥μ(tP)` for `t≥1`, and `μ(tA)≤μ(tP)` for `0≤t≤1`. The statement is an equal-Gaussian-measure dilation comparison; it is not a pointwise or arbitrary-polynomial estimate.
- **Theorem 2 (p. 2).** In `R²`, let `A` have the special graph form under a symmetric, smooth, concave `f:(−w,w)→R`, symmetric and nonincreasing on `[0,w)`, with `f(x)→−∞` as `x→w−`. If `γ₂(A)=γ₂(P)` for a symmetric strip of half-width `p`, then `w(A) γ₂⁺(A) ≥ w(P) γ₂⁺(P)=√(2/π) p exp(−p²/2)`. Here `γ⁺` is the lower Minkowski Gaussian perimeter and `w(A)=sup{r:B(0,r)⊂A}`.
- **Theorem 3 (p. 2).** For any symmetric convex `A⊂Rⁿ` and equal-measure symmetric strip `P`, `w(A)γₙ⁺(A)≥w(P)γₙ⁺(P)`.
- **Corollary 1 (PDF p. 3; printed p. 1924).** Define the symmetric-strip profile from PDF p. 1 by
  `Ψ(x)=γ₁([-x,x])=(2π)^(−1/2)∫_{−x}^{x} exp(−y²/2)dy` for `x≥0` (and use the generalized inverse on `[0,1]`, with `Ψ⁻¹(1)=+∞`). If `μ` is centered Gaussian on a separable Banach space and `B` is convex, symmetric, and closed, then
  `μ(rB) ≥ Ψ((r/s) Ψ⁻¹(μ(sB)))`, for `r≥s>0`.
  In particular, for each `b<1` there is `C_b<∞`, depending only on `b`, such that `μ(tB)≤C_b t μ(B)` for `t∈[0,1]` whenever `μ(B)≤b`. The first inequality is the exact Corollary 1 statement; it is not `μ(rB)≥(r/s)μ(sB)` and `Ψ` is the symmetric-strip probability, not the one-sided Gaussian CDF `Φ`.
- **Corollary 3 (pp. 4–5).** For independent standard normals `g_i`, vectors `x_i` in a separable Banach space and an a.s.-convergent Gaussian series `S=Σx_i g_i`, `||S||_p≤(γ_p/γ_q)||S||_q` for `p≥q>0`, with `γ_p=(E|g_1|^p)^{1/p}`. This is a moment comparison for a Gaussian Banach-valued linear series.

## Proof chain and boundaries

The proof reduces the infinite-dimensional statement to canonical finite-dimensional Gaussian measure. For Theorem 3, slice `A` along a direction realizing its inradius; Ehrhard concavity turns the Gaussian measures of cross-sections into a concave profile. The resulting two-dimensional subgraph `B` has the same Gaussian measure as `A`/`P`. Gaussian isoperimetry applied to the cross-section neighborhoods gives `γ₂⁺(B)≤γₙ⁺(A)`, so Theorem 2 implies Theorem 3. For Theorem 1, differentiate the dilation comparison at `t=1`; a Euclidean ball of radius `(t−1)w(A)` around each point of `A` lies in `tA`, giving `γ'_A(1)≥w(A)γₙ⁺(A)`. The strip derivative is the explicit Gaussian density expression, so Theorem 3 gives the derivative comparison and hence the dilation comparison (the paper refers to [4] for this last equivalence).

The delayed proof of Theorem 2 occupies pp. 5–17. It develops Gaussian-tail inequalities (Lemmas 1–13), derives a one-dimensional derivative comparison, and finishes with tangent-line geometry and Gaussian isoperimetry. Several auxiliary numeric tables (pp. 9–10, 12, 16) are left for the reader to check; this card does not claim an independent verification of those finite table inequalities. No dimension-dependent constant is optimized for B167.

## B167 use

**Auxiliary background only.** The source compares Gaussian measures of dilated symmetric convex sets and moments of Gaussian Banach-valued linear series. Formal120 B167 minimizes `E[(|p(Z)|−1)^2]` over arbitrary real polynomials of degree at most `d`, all dimensions, under a variance constraint. No reduction from this nonlinear polynomial objective to the set-dilation or Banach-series comparison is proved here, so no B167 lower bound, optimizer, or asymptotic constant is imported.
