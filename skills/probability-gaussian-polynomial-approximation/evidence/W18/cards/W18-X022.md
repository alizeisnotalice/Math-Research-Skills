## General normal bound: Theorem 3.1, PDF p. 11

Let `Z~N(0,1)` and let `F∈D^{1,2}` satisfy `E F=0`. Put

`Γ_F = <DF,−DL⁻¹F>_H`,

where `L` is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator and `L⁻¹` is its inverse on centered variables. Then

- `d_W(F,Z) ≤ {E[(1−Γ_F)²]}^{1/2}`;
- `d_FM(F,Z) ≤ 4 {E[(1−Γ_F)²]}^{1/2}`.

If, in addition, the law of `F` is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, then

- `d_Kol(F,Z) ≤ {E[(1−Γ_F)²]}^{1/2}`;
- `d_TV(F,Z) ≤ 2 {E[(1−Γ_F)²]}^{1/2}`.

The AC hypothesis is required for the last two displayed bounds. Proof: `δD=−L` gives `F=−δDL⁻¹F`; Malliavin integration by parts yields `E[F f(F)]=E[f'(F)Γ_F]`. Subtract the Gaussian Stein identity, then use Cauchy–Schwarz and the test-function derivative bounds. The AC condition permits the nonsmooth indicator tests for Kolmogorov/TV distance.

## Fixed chaos: Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3, PDF pp. 12–13

For integer `q≥2`, let `F=I_q(f)` with symmetric kernel `f∈H^{⊙q}`. Proposition 3.2 gives `Γ_F=q⁻¹||DF||²_H` and the exact defect expansion

`E[(1−q⁻¹||DF||²_H)²] = (1−q!||f||²_{H^{⊗q}})² + q² Σ_{r=1}^{q−1}(2q−2r)!(r−1)!² binom(q−1,r−1)^4 ||f ˜⊗_r f||²_{H^{⊗2(q−r)}}`.

For a sequence `F_n=I_q(f_n)` with `E F_n²=q!||f_n||²_{H^{⊗q}}→1`, Theorem 3.3 states the equivalence of:

1. `F_n⇒Z~N(0,1)`;
2. `E F_n⁴→3`;
3. `||f_n⊗_r f_n||_{H^{⊗2(q−r)}}→0` for every `r=1,…,q−1`;
4. `||DF_n||²_H→q` in `L²(Ω)`.

The paper credits the normal-limit/fourth-moment/contraction equivalences to earlier work and the derivative-norm equivalence to another earlier result; its Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.1 give an alternate proof of the contraction-to-derivative-to-normal implication. No different kernel norm convention is introduced here: the multiple-integral isometry is written with the ordinary tensor norm and factor `q!`.

## Finite sums and Stein factors

Proposition 3.7 (PDF pp. 14–15) concerns `Z_n=Σ_{i=1}^s I_{q_i}(f_n^i)` with fixed distinct orders `2≤q_1<⋯<q_s`. If the total variance `E Z_n²=Σ_i q_i!||f_n^i||²` tends to 1 and, for every component `i`, all self-contractions `||f_n^i⊗_r f_n^i||→0` for `r=1,…,q_i−1`, then `Z_n⇒N(0,1)`; Theorem 3.1 supplies the associated distance bounds. The proof expands `Γ_{Z_n}` by the product formula, uses orthogonality and bounds mixed contractions by self-contractions. Remark 3.8 explicitly notes that the individual component variances need not each converge, provided the total variance and contraction conditions hold.

Remark 3.6 (PDF p. 13) derives a Stein factor `S(V)` for `Y=g(V)`, where `V` is a finite vector of iid standard Gaussians, `g` is smooth with subexponential growth together with its derivatives, `E Y=0`, and `E Y²=1`. Mehler’s formula yields `E[Y f(Y)]=E[S(V)f'(Y)]`; hence the normal Stein error is controlled through `S(V)−1`. This is a finite-dimensional Gaussian-functional identity, not a theorem for arbitrary input distributions.

## Centered-Gamma approximation: Theorem 3.11, PDF p. 17

For `ν>0`, let `F(ν)=2G(ν/2)−ν`, where `G(ν/2)` is Gamma with shape `ν/2`; the target is centered Gamma with support `(-ν,∞)`. If `G∈D^{1,2}`, `E G=0`, and the law of `G` is absolutely continuous, then

`d_{H₂}(G,F(ν)) ≤ K₂ {E[(2ν+2G−<DG,−DL⁻¹G>_H)²]}^{1/2}`,

with `K₂=max{1, 1/ν+2/ν²}`. If additionally `ν≥1` is an integer, then

`d_{H₁}(G,F(ν)) ≤ K₁ {E[(2ν+2G−<DG,−DL⁻¹G>_H)²]}^{1/2}`,

with `K₁=max{sqrt(2π/ν), 1/ν+2/ν²}`. The test classes `H₁,H₂` and their centered-Gamma restrictions are defined in §1; the integer condition belongs only to the `H₁` bound. The proof applies the centered-Gamma Stein equation and the nonnegativity property of the conditional Stein factor.

## Breuer–Major Berry–Esséen bound: Theorem 4.1, PDF p. 22

This is a **scalar Kolmogorov-distance bound**, not a multivariate Wasserstein theorem. Section 4.1 (PDF pp. 20–21) sets `V_{n,H}={n^H(B_{(k+1)/n}−B_{k/n}):0≤k<n}` for fractional Brownian motion of Hurst index `H`; its stationary increment correlation is

`ρ_H(k)=½(|k+1|^{2H}+|k−1|^{2H}−2|k|^{2H})`.

For fixed integer `q≥2`, the paper uses the scaled Hermite polynomial `H_q(x)=(-1)^q/q! · e^{x²/2}(d^q/dx^q)e^{−x²/2}`. Thus `Var(H_q(N))=1/q!` for `N~N(0,1)`, and the source records `q!H_q(h)=I_q(h^{⊗q})` for unit `h`. It defines

`σ_{H,q}²=(1/q!) Σ_{t∈Z} ρ_H(t)^q`,

and `Z_n=(σ_{H,q}√n)⁻¹ Σ_{k=0}^{n−1}H_q(n^H(B_{(k+1)/n}−B_{k/n}))`. The source notes `Σ_t |ρ_H(t)|^q<∞` exactly when `H∈(0,(2q−1)/(2q))`; under this condition `E Z_n²→1`.

Theorem 4.1 states `Z_n⇒N(0,1)` and, for every `n≥1`,

`sup_{z∈R}|P(Z_n≤z)−P(N≤z)| ≤ c_H ×`

`{ n^{-1/2}                    if H∈(0,1/2];`
`  n^{H−1}                    if H∈[1/2,(2q−3)/(2q−2)];`
`  n^{qH−q+1/2}               if H∈[(2q−3)/(2q−2),(2q−1)/(2q)) }`.

The endpoint overlaps are retained as printed; `q` is fixed, and the theorem labels its constant `c_H` (depending only on `H`). The proof writes `Z_n` as a multiple integral, expands the derivative defect using Proposition 3.2, and estimates its contraction terms `A_r(n)`. Lemma 4.3 gives the decay bound `ρ_H(k)≲|k|^{2H−2}`; the proof splits four-index sums into fifteen coincidence/order patterns (pp. 24–26) and obtains

`E[(q⁻¹||DZ_n||²−1)²] ≲ n^{-1}+n^{2H−2}+n^{2qH−2q+1}`.

Taking square roots and applying the scalar Kolmogorov estimate from Theorem 3.1 yields the three displayed rates. This theorem and proof are wholly within X022; they are distinct from X023’s §4.1 vector application.

## Use and boundary

**Auxiliary for B167.** The source gives Stein-factor bounds, fixed-chaos normal criteria, a finite-sum CLT implication, centered-Gamma approximation, and a scalar Berry–Esséen bound for a specific fractional-Brownian Hermite-increment sequence. B167 is instead a sharp optimization for the nonsmooth loss `E[(|p(Z)|−1)²]` over arbitrary bounded-degree Gaussian polynomials. No reduction from that objective to these Gaussian/Gamma distributional approximations is proved. Review remains pending.
