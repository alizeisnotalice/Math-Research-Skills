## Exact theorem and scope

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2).** For any degree-`d` real polynomial with `Var_{X~N(0,I_n)} f(X)=1`, all `t∈R` and `ε>0` satisfy
`P{|f(X)−t|≤ε} ≤ C_d ε^(1/c_d)`,
where the source gives `C_d=O(d)^d` and `c_d=O(d·4^d)`. The constants depend on `d`, not `n`. The exponent is much weaker than the `1/d` exponent in Carbery–Wright, so do not substitute this theorem for Carbery–Wright when the sharper exponent matters.

**Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2).** Let `D` on `R` have the paper's PSD anti-concentration property for `D−D`: for PSD `A` with `tr(A)=1`, `P_{x~D^n}(xᵀAx≤ε)≤C ε^c`. Then degree-`d` `f` normalized by `Var_{(dD)^n}f=1` obeys the same form of interval bound under `(dD)^n`, with constants depending on `d` and `c_d=O(d·2^{O(d)})`. Here `dD` denotes the sum of `d` iid `D` variables, not scalar multiplication; the Gaussian instance chooses `D=N(0,1/d)` so `dD=N(0,1)` and `D−D=N(0,2/d)`.

## Proof mechanism

The proof first treats homogeneous multilinear polynomials and repeatedly applies Cauchy–Schwarz/finite differences to pass to a set-multilinear polynomial on `d` blocks. **Lemma 4.1 (PDF p. 11)** gives the recursion `B_d^ML(ε)≤B_{d−1}^ML(sqrt ε)+2sqrt ε`, hence `B_d^ML(ε)≤2d ε^(1/2^(d−1))`. **PDF p. 12** contains Claim 4.2, the PSD quadratic small-ball base estimate, and the bridge toward the general-distribution result; it is not where Lemma 4.1 appears. Hermite decomposition and a component split reduce a general variance-one polynomial to the set-multilinear case. Section 5 carries the argument to distributions whose difference has PSD anti-concentration. The iterative squaring of the scale explains the very weak degree dependence.

## B167 migration boundary

This is a weaker interval-mass estimate for variance-one polynomials, not a bound for `E(|p(Z)|−1)²`. It may certify that a polynomial rarely falls in a short interval around a chosen `t`, but a new layer-cake/integration argument with the right global tails and normalization would still be required; even then its exponent is inferior to Carbery–Wright and cannot establish the sharp `d^(−1/2)` coefficient.
