## Conditional interface to B167

B167 asks for the infimum of `E[(|p(Z)|−1)^2]` over arbitrary real polynomials of total degree at most `d` in `Z~N(0,I_n)`, with `Var(p(Z))≥1`. MOO does **not** directly address this optimization problem. Its invariance results compare evaluations of a multilinear polynomial `Q` over independent coordinate ensembles, under one of the paper’s moment-matching and hypercontractivity hypotheses. For Theorem 3.18 one additionally needs `Var(Q)≤1`, `deg(Q)≤d`, each coordinate influence at most `τ`, and a `C^r` test `Ψ` with bounded `r`th derivative. Theorem 3.19 retains the model, degree, variance, and low-influence conditions for its distributional/smoothed comparisons; Theorem 3.20 has its own explicit hypotheses (Hypotheses 1, 3, or 4 and `E[Q²]≤1`, not Hypothesis 2). See the frozen W10 card for the exact rates, constants, and theorem-by-theorem proof routes.

A B167 application would first have to exhibit a valid independent-coordinate/multilinear representation in an allowed pair of ensembles, verify the relevant moment and hypercontractivity assumptions, degree/variance normalization, and every individual low-influence bound, then choose a permitted smooth or distributional comparison and separately control the threshold/absolute-value smoothing error. An arbitrary real Gaussian polynomial may be nonhomogeneous, non-multilinear, or have large coordinate influences; none of those hypotheses follows from `deg(p)≤d` and `Var(p)≥1`. The B167 loss is also not itself a smooth test function at zero.

## What this source can and cannot supply here

If that conditional reduction is proved for a particular structured polynomial, MOO can transfer the specified smooth-test expectations or distributional/noise-stability quantities between its allowed product ensembles. It supplies no unconditional lower bound for B167, no Hermite-tail theorem, no dimension reduction, and no sharp leading constant. Do not count it as support for arbitrary signed/nonhomogeneous/non-multilinear `p`, or infer a reduction from PTF sensitivity/noise stability to the B167 `L²` loss.
