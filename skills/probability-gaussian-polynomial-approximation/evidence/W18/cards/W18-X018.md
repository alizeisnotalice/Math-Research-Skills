## Gaussian theorem cards

The Gaussian domain is `R^n` with standard multivariate Gaussian input. For `X,Y` independent `N(0,I_n)`, the article's correlated perturbation is `Z=(1−ε)X+sqrt(2ε−ε²)Y`; thus `Corr(X_i,Z_i)=1−ε`. A PTF is a Boolean-valued sign of a polynomial.

- **Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 4):** for any degree-`d` **multilinear** PTF `f` over `R^n`, `GAS(f)≤O(d log n · n^(1−1/(2d)))`.
- **Theorem 1.5 (PDF p. 4, verbatim scope as printed):** for any degree-`d` **multilinear** PTF `f` and `0≤ε≤1`, the paper prints `GNS_ε(f)≤O(d log(1/ε) · ε^(1/(2d)))`. The paper does not qualify the endpoint by continuity.

**Source-statement boundary:** the printed range cannot be valid at large noise or at `ε=1`. For the linear halfspace `f(x)=sign(x₁)`, the exact disagreement under this convention is `acos(1−ε)/π→1/2` as `ε↑1`, while the displayed right side tends to zero. The proof in §4 chooses `t*=Θ(d√ε log^d(1/ε))` and establishes the stated anti-concentration/tail balance in the small-noise regime; it does not justify the printed large-noise/endpoint range. Therefore I retain the formula as the source's statement but mark its range as defective; I do not silently replace the theorem or claim a source-proved global correction. A global bound with a different logarithm would require a separate proof.

The same introduction contains separate Boolean-cube theorems. There the noise is independent coordinate flips, and the exponents/constants differ (e.g. Theorem 1.3 gives `O(2^{O(d)} ε^(1/(4d+2)) log(1/ε))`, with an alternative `O(ε^(1/2^d))` bound). Do not transplant cube probability or estimates onto the Gaussian pair.

## Proof mechanism and other sections

For small Gaussian noise, write the polynomial difference across the correlated pair, bound its L² size as `O(d√ε)`, and split on the event that the base polynomial is close to zero versus the difference being large. Carbery–Wright controls the first event; degree-`d` Gaussian hypercontractive tails control the second; choosing `t*=Θ(d√ε log^d(1/ε))` balances the two. This proof chain supports the small-ε estimate and not the p. 4 assertion all the way to `ε=1`. Gaussian average sensitivity uses a related anti-concentration argument and a scale decomposition. The Boolean sections use regularity, critical-index analysis, random restrictions and Fourier arguments; later sections relate average and noise sensitivity and derive agnostic-learning consequences by polynomial regression.

## B167 migration boundary

These estimates concern the sign of a multilinear polynomial and its stability under a correlated Gaussian perturbation, not the squared objective `E(|p(Z)|−1)²` for arbitrary degree-`≤d` (not necessarily multilinear) real `p` with variance at least one. No implication from the stated sensitivity to a sharp value of that expectation is proved. A transfer would require a new approximation/normalization result and explicit control of non-multilinear Hermite terms.
