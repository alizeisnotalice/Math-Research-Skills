## Exact theorem cards

- **Theorem 1.3 (PDF p. 3, Gaussian noise sensitivity):** for a degree-`d` PTF `f:R^n→{−1,1}` and `δ∈(0,1)`, `GNS_δ(f)=O_d(δ^(1/(2d+1)))`.
- **Theorem 1.4 (same page, Boolean noise sensitivity):** for a degree-`d` PTF on `{−1,1}^n` and independent bit-flip rate `δ∈(0,1)`, `NS_δ(f)=O_d(δ^(1/(4d+6)))`.
- **Theorem 1.6 (same page, Boolean average sensitivity):** `AS(f)=O_d(n^(1−1/(4d+3)))`; a separate elementary bound is `AS(f)≤3 n^(1−1/2^d)`.

For the Gaussian convention, `(X,Z)` is standard Gaussian with `Z=(1−δ)X+sqrt(2δ−δ²)Y` for independent standard `X,Y`; for the Boolean convention each coordinate is flipped independently with probability `δ`. Keep these as distinct probability spaces and noise definitions.

## Proof mechanism and version boundary

The Gaussian estimate combines Carbery–Wright anti-concentration near the zero level with Markov control of the polynomial difference under the correlated pair; optimizing the cutoff gives the `(2d+1)^{-1}` exponent. The Boolean arguments use random restrictions/critical-index structure and an invariance principle; the average-sensitivity proof also has an elementary combinatorial route. The paper derives learning consequences from noise sensitivity, but these are for PTF classes.

## B167 migration boundary

The results are sign-function stability bounds for correlated inputs, not direct lower/upper bounds on `E(|p(Z)|−1)²`. They do not yield B167's dimension-uniform sharp leading constant. A new bridge from the signed polynomial's absolute-value deviation to PTF noise/average sensitivity would need separate proof and would preserve this preprint version's hypotheses.
