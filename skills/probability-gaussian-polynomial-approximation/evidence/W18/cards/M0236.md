# M0236 — Kane, *The Gaussian Surface Area and Noise Sensitivity of Degree-d Polynomials*

- **Read and version:** Read the full 7-page conference manuscript. Its arXiv v1 stamp is `arXiv:0912.2709v1`, dated 14 Dec 2009; the PDF body is dated 14 Aug 2018. Preserve both printed dates as an unresolved discrepancy. The local PDF SHA-256 is `f6038d34f943a311b4b58e05832f4d89c15a9156b2f47890d6ad9a9247185bfc`; the source PDF is unchanged.
- **Definition (physical p. 2):** for independent standard Gaussian vectors `X,Y∈R^n`, set `Z=(1−ε)X+sqrt(2ε−ε²)Y` and `GNS_ε(f)=P[f(X)≠f(Z)]`. The coupling is real and has standard Gaussian marginals for `0≤ε≤2`, with `Cov(X,Z)=(1−ε)I`.
- **Theorem 1 (physical pp. 2–4):** the source prints `GNS_ε(f) ≤ (d/π) arcsin(sqrt(2ε−ε²))`, with small-noise asymptotic `d sqrt(2ε)/π` as `ε→0`, for degree-`d` PTFs `f=sgn(p)`. The displayed statement gives no explicit parameter interval. The proof-justified range for this upper bound is only `0≤ε≤1`; use it only on that range. The small-noise bound is asymptotically attained by the threshold of a product of `d` distinct homogeneous linear forms.
- **Why the range stops at 1:** on physical p. 3 the proof uses the principal angle `θ=arcsin(sqrt(2ε−ε²))` for the rotation argument. Then `cos θ=sqrt(1−(2ε−ε²))=|1−ε|`, which equals the coupling correlation `1−ε` only when `ε≤1`. For `ε>1`, the actual correlation angle is `arccos(1−ε)=π−arcsin(sqrt(2ε−ε²))`; the shorter principal angle in the displayed bound is not the same rotation. Indeed, for `f(x)=sgn(x)` and `ε=3/2`, the exact noise sensitivity is `arccos(−1/2)/π=2/3`, while the printed right side is `arcsin(sqrt(3/4))/π=1/3`. The source bound therefore must not be extrapolated beyond `ε=1`. A MATLAB numerical sanity check and the exact formulas are recorded in `evidence/W18/math/m0236_epsilon_domain_check.json`.
- **Theorem 2 (physical p. 2):** the Gaussian surface area of a degree-`d` PTF is at most `d/sqrt(2π)`.
- **Proof route:** rotate the Gaussian pair in its plane. For fixed `(X,Y)`, restrict `p` to the circle; a trigonometric polynomial’s zeros are controlled by a degree-`2d` algebraic polynomial, yielding at most `2d` sign changes. Averaging over the rotation proves Theorem 1 on the stated proof range. A radial comparison then gives the surface-area estimate.
- **Boundary:** these are PTF boundary/noise estimates, not bounds on `E[(|p|-1)^2]` and not a determination of B167’s sharp approximation constant.
