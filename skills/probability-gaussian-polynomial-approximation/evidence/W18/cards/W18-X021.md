## Fixed-chaos criterion: Theorem 4 (PDF pp. 6–8)

Let `F_k=I_q(f_k)` lie in a fixed Wiener chaos of order `q≥2` and suppose `E[F_k²]=q!||f_k||²→1`. The following are equivalent:

- `F_k ⇒ N(0,1)`;
- `E[F_k⁴]→3`;
- `||f_k⊗_r f_k||_{H^{⊗2(q−r)}}→0` for every `1≤r≤q−1`;
- `||DF_k||_H² → q` in `L²(Ω)`.

**Kernel norm convention (rendered PDF pp. 2 and 6).** The paper defines `H^{⊙q}` with the modified norm `||f||_{H^{⊙q},mod}=sqrt(q!)||f||_{H^{⊗q}}`; under this scaled convention `E[I_q(f)^2]=||f||_{H^{⊙q},mod}²`. In the ordinary tensor norm used in the statement above, the same isometry is `E[F_k²]=q!||f_k||_{H^{⊗q}}²`. The p. 6 identity for the Malliavin derivative is written with the modified norm, `E||DF_k||_H²=q||f_k||_{H^{⊙q},mod}²`; converted consistently to the ordinary tensor norm it is

`E||DF_k||_H² = q E[F_k²] = q q! ||f_k||_{H^{⊗q}}²`.

Thus the normalized sequence has derivative expectation tending to `q`, and Lemma 3 identifies convergence of `E||DF_k||⁴` to `q²` with the derivative-norm `L²` condition. Theorem 4 proves the characteristic-function direction: `δD=−L` and `LF_k=−qF_k` give `φ'_k(t)=−(t/q)E[e^{itF_k}||DF_k||²]`; the derivative condition forces any subsequential limit to solve `φ'(t)=−tφ(t)`, `φ(0)=1`. The contraction implication uses the displayed derivative-inner-product expansion (Lemma 2, p. 5), the product formula, and orthogonality. The scaled and ordinary symmetric-tensor norms are not interchanged without the factor `q!`.

## Multidimensional and projection-level results

For a fixed vector of chaos orders `q_1,…,q_m`, Theorem 7 (PDF pp. 9–11) gives a vector CLT under covariance convergence to the identity. Componentwise fourth-moment/contraction/derivative criteria are equivalent to joint standard Gaussian convergence. Lemma 6 controls cross terms: the mixed derivative inner product converges in `L²` to `√(q_iq_j)δ_{ij}`. Its proof reduces mixed contractions to self-contractions by Cauchy–Schwarz; when the orders agree, the full contraction is the covariance itself.

Theorem 8 (PDF p. 12) allows a general centered square-integrable sequence with chaos projections `J_qF_k`. Its assumptions are: (i) the high-order variance tail vanishes uniformly, `lim_{N→∞} limsup_k Σ_{q>N} E[(J_qF_k)²]=0`; (ii) each fixed-order projected variance tends to `σ_q²`; (iii) `Σ_q σ_q²=σ²<∞`; and (iv) for each fixed `q`, `||D(J_qF_k)||²→qσ_q²` in `L²`. Then `F_k⇒N(0,σ²)`. The proof first gets Gaussian limits for finitely many chaos levels, whose limit coordinates are independent, then removes the tail using the variance bound. The theorem does not require `F_k∈D^{1,2}`: the derivative assumptions concern projections only. Remark 9 gives one sufficient condition for (i), namely `sup_k E||DF_k||²<∞` when `F_k∈D^{1,2}`.

Section 6 (PDF pp. 13–15) applies this to odd powers of fractional-Brownian increments for `H<1/2`, proving joint convergence with the original fractional Brownian motion and an independent Brownian limit. Finite-dimensional convergence uses chaos projections and the ergodic theorem; tightness uses fixed-chaos moment equivalence.

## Gaussian-polynomial tool use and boundary

**Auxiliary for B167.** The derivative norm and projection theorem are useful diagnostics for normal limits of Gaussian polynomial sequences, with order, variance, and tail hypotheses explicit. They do not control `E[(|p(Z)|−1)²]` for an arbitrary polynomial in one Gaussian vector, and do not supply a dimension-uniform sharp constant or optimizer.
