## Theorem 2: structured Gaussian matrix norm

Let A=(a_ij) be a deterministic finite m-by-n matrix, let g_ij be independent standard Gaussians, and write G_A=(a_ij g_ij). Theorem 2 assumes p* and q are in [2,infinity), equivalently 1 < p <= 2 <= q < infinity. It gives

\[
\mathbb E\|G_A\|_{p\to q}
\asymp_{p,q}
\max_i\|(a_{ij})_j\|_{p^*}
+\max_j\|(a_{ij})_i\|_q
+\mathbb E\max_{i,j}|a_{ij}g_{ij}|.
\]

The third term is also equivalent to the deterministic deletion profile

\[
\max_{k\ge0}\inf_{|I|=k}\sqrt{\operatorname{Log} k}\,
\max_{(i,j)\notin I}|a_{ij}|,
\]

and to a two-sided row/column deletion form stated in the theorem, with the same sqrt(Log k) factor. On physical p.2 the convention is Log 0 = 1 and Log x = 1 vee ln x for x>0; thus k=0 means I is empty and the term is max_ij |a_ij|. For a finite m-by-n matrix, nonempty deletion sets have 0<=k<=mn. Here p* is the Hölder conjugate. The endpoint cases p=1 or q=infinity are handled separately by identities (1)–(2) in §1; they are not included in Theorem 2's hypothesis. The theorem’s constants depend only on p,q; the paper gives upper-constant bounds of order (p*∨q)^(5/2) when 1/p+1/q* >= 3/2 and (p*∨q)^(13/2) in the complementary range, while the first lower constant is independent of p,q.

Corollary 11 adds higher-moment and Gaussian tail estimates: the deterministic row/column/deletion scale is augmented in L_r by sqrt(r) max|a_ij|. The paper also treats Gaussian mixtures, symmetric Weibull entries with shape r <= 2, and more general independent entries, but these have their own hypotheses.

## Proof route

The upper estimate is assembled from Proposition 16 (dimension-dependent Gaussian matrix estimate), Proposition 17 (weighted decay of entries), and Proposition 19 (truncation/deletion of the largest entries to remove the dimension logarithm). The hard Proposition 16 proof splits into the range p*,q in [2,3] and its complement. It uses support-size decompositions of unit vectors, nets for sparse vectors, interpolation norms for small-coordinate vectors, Gaussian concentration, and an exponent-reduction argument. Proposition 17 decomposes A into block-diagonal bands; the block norm identity and Gaussian supremum estimates control the bands. The lower bound follows by testing row/column vectors and the maximum entry. The exact theorem gives equivalence up to p,q-dependent constants, not a sharp constant formula.

## B167 use and non-implication

Role: **auxiliary Gaussian matrix concentration only**. This is a result for the operator norm of a finite matrix with independent Gaussian entries and deterministic variance profile. B167 is an optimization over arbitrary real Gaussian-input polynomials and the residual ||p|-1|. No reduction from that polynomial objective to these matrix norms is proved, and matrix dimension dependence does not identify a polynomial-degree leading constant.

## Limits

The article’s exact domain is a matrix with independent Gaussian entries; its dependence on p,q and matrix dimensions is kept explicit. These conditions do not match a general polynomial of Gaussian coordinates or its sign-distance objective.
