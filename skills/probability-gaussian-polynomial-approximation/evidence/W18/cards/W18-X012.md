## Exact geometric theorem

**Theorem 15 (PDF p. 13, printed p. 12).** There is universal `C>0`. Let positive integers `d,k,m` be given and let `V` be a vector space of homogeneous degree-`d` real polynomials in `m` variables with codimension at most `k` in the full homogeneous degree-`d` space. For `δ,R>0`, define
`S={x∈R^m: ||x||₂≤R and |p(x)|≤δ||p||_{ℓ₂} for every p∈V}`,
where `||p||_{ℓ₂}` is **not** the raw monomial-coefficient norm: if `p(x)=<A_p,x^{⊗d}>` for the corresponding symmetric order-`d` tensor, then `||p||_{ℓ₂}:=||A_p||₂`. The induced inner product is rotationally invariant; for a monomial `x^α`, its squared norm is `α!/d! = 1/binom(d,α)`. If
`ε ≥ δ^[1/((C+1)d)] (2Rdkm)^[C/(C+1)]`,
then `S` admits an `ε`-cover of cardinality at most
`(2(R/ε) d k m)^[C² d² k^(1/d)]`.
The theorem is deterministic and geometric: `S` is a ball intersected with simultaneous near-zero constraints for a polynomial subspace. It is not a Gaussian probability or L² approximation estimate. The displayed degree, radius, dimension, codimension and exponent were visually checked on the original rendered p. 13.

## Proof chain and neighboring claims

The proof recursively splits coordinates `R^m=R^{m'}×R^{m−m'}`, covers the first coordinate block by small balls/cylinders, and studies restrictions `p(x,y)`. Lemma 11 supplies `|p(x)|≤||x||₂^d||p||_{ℓ₂}` and the Lipschitz estimate `|p(x)−p(y)|≤d max(||x||₂,||y||₂)^(d−1)||x−y||₂||p||_{ℓ₂}`. For a fixed cylinder, these estimates transfer smallness to the slice. A restriction map from a subspace homogeneous of degree 1 in the first block to degree `d−1` in the remaining variables is analyzed by singular values: good centers permit recursion, while bad centers are shown close to a subspace `H` with codimension at least `m'/2`. The contradiction uses a Gram determinant lower bound for polynomial products and an upper bound from their near-orthogonality, then recursion gives the stated cover bound. The recursive proof has dimension/codimension bookkeeping; no constant is sharpened for B167.

Theorem 25 gives an algorithmic version of the cover theorem. Proposition 26 applies it to heavy components of a finite moment decomposition after forming a quadratic form on degree-`d` polynomials and retaining all but the top `k` eigenvectors. Proposition 27 is a separate degree-one reduction. Later sections apply these tools to spherical Gaussian mixtures, positive ReLU/GLM combinations, mixtures of linear regressions and hyperplanes. Appendix A.1–A.4 supplies a codimension fact, the Lemma 11 tensor estimate, a product-inner-product claim, and KL/Pinsker/VC/Hermite background.

## B167 migration boundary

This source controls metric covers of **near-zero sets** in a bounded Euclidean ball for codimension-bounded spaces of homogeneous polynomials. B167 instead optimizes an expectation of `(|p(Z)|−1)²` over nonhomogeneous degree-`≤d` polynomials under standard Gaussian measure, with `Var(p)≥1`. No passage from the covering entropy here to that expectation, no sharp leading constant, and no full-dimensional matching construction are proved. Any adaptation would need a new truncation/normalization argument and quantitative control outside `||x||≤R`.
