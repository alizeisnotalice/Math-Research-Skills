## Theorem 1.3: two-sided Gaussian polynomial moments

Let G ~ N(0,I_n), and let f:R^n -> R be a polynomial of degree at most D. For every p >= 2, the paper proves

\[
C_D^{-1}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{\mathcal J\in\mathcal P_d}
p^{|\mathcal J|/2}\|\mathbb E D^d f(G)\|_{\mathcal J}
\le \|f(G)-\mathbb E f(G)\|_p
\le C_D\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{\mathcal J\in\mathcal P_d}
p^{|\mathcal J|/2}\|\mathbb E D^d f(G)\|_{\mathcal J}.
\]

Here \(\mathcal P_d\) is the set of partitions of the d derivative slots and \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal J}\) is the associated tensor partition norm defined in §2. The same theorem gives, for t > 0,

\[
C_D^{-1}e^{-C_D\eta_f(t)}
\le \mathbb P(|f(G)-\mathbb E f(G)|\ge t)
\le C_D e^{-\eta_f(t)/C_D},
\qquad
\eta_f(t)=\min_{1\le d\le D}\min_{\mathcal J\in\mathcal P_d}
\left(\frac{t}{\|\mathbb E D^d f(G)\|_{\mathcal J}}\right)^{2/|\mathcal J|}.
\]

Constants C_D depend on degree D only; no sharp asymptotic dependence on D is supplied. The general upper estimate is Theorem 1.2: the input vector must satisfy the family of Sobolev inequalities (3), with growth C(p) <= L p^gamma, 1/2 <= gamma <= 1. The bound involves the D-th derivative tensor norms and expected lower derivative tensors. Gaussian log-Sobolev gives gamma = 1/2. Do not transfer the Gaussian two-sided polynomial conclusion to arbitrary measures.

## Proof dependency and mechanism

Theorem 1.2 is proved by iterating the assumed Sobolev inequality along independent copies, reducing the fluctuation to a decoupled derivative chaos, and applying Latała’s Gaussian-chaos moment estimate; Markov optimization gives the tail form. For Theorem 1.3, the upper bound follows from Theorem 1.2 under Gaussian log-Sobolev. For the lower bound, §4 uses Kwapień’s decoupling/reduction for tetrahedral polynomials, expands a general Gaussian polynomial in multivariate Hermite polynomials, and approximates those Hermite products by tetrahedral polynomials built from many independent Gaussian copies. The limit preserves expected derivative tensors and transfers the tetrahedral lower-moment estimate. Thus the equivalence is only up to constants depending on D, not a leading-constant theorem.

Theorem 1.1 is the homogeneous tetrahedral Gaussian-chaos estimate used in this route. Section 5 proves Theorem 1.4 for independent sub-Gaussian coordinates by decoupling, symmetrization, contraction, and comparison of powers of sub-Gaussian variables to Gaussian products. Its random-graph cycle applications retain constants depending on fixed graph/degree parameters.

## B167 use and non-implication

Role: **auxiliary moment/tail structure only**. The formal B167 objective is E[(|p(G)|-1)^2], the L2 squared distance from p(G) to its sign-valued threshold, subject to a variance normalization. Theorem 1.3 controls centered moments of polynomial p; applying it to |p| is invalid because |p| need not be a polynomial or smooth at its zero set. The theorem gives neither a quantitative bridge from derivative partition norms to this sign-distance objective nor matching asymptotic extremizers. Degree-dependent C_D cannot identify the sharp D^(-1/2) leading constant.

## Limits

This is a finite-degree moment equivalence. Constants depend on degree, and the sign-distance functional is not among the polynomial observables treated by the theorem. It is useful for locating possible intermediate moment estimates, but closes no B167 interface by itself.
