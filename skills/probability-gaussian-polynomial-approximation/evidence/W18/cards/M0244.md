## Scope and exact hypotheses

The article studies noise stability of measurable sets/functions under a correlated standard Gaussian pair `(X,Y)∈R^n×R^n`, with `X,Y~N(0,I_n)` and `E[X_iY_j]=ρ 1{i=j}`. The principal functional is Borell's `J_ρ` and the deficit `δ=J_ρ(Ef,Eg)-E_ρ J_ρ(f(X),g(Y))`, for measurable `f,g:R^n→[0,1]`. It is a Gaussian isoperimetric/noise-stability paper, not a theorem about approximating `sgn(p)` by degree-`d` polynomials.

## Results and proof cards

- **Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 5; journal p. 437).** Put `m=Ef(1−Ef)Eg(1−Eg)`. For `0<ρ<1`, there are a common vector `a∈R^n` and scalar offsets `b,d` such that each of the two L¹ distances from `f,g` to their corresponding Gaussian-CDF threshold profiles with slope `a` is at most
  `C(ρ)m^{−C(ρ)} δ^{(1/4)((1−ρ)(1−ρ²)/(1+3ρ))}`.
  This is a near-equality statement with volume dependence through `m` and constants depending on `ρ`; it is not dimension-free evidence for a B167 lower bound or its sharp asymptotic constant. Formula and exponent were visually checked on the rendered original page.
- **Theorem 1.5 (PDF p. 6; journal p. 438).** For every `η>0` there are `ρ₀<1` and `C(η)` such that if `ρ₀<ρ<1`, `Ef=1/2`, and `f:R^n→[0,1]` is measurable, then some `a∈R^n` satisfies
  `E|f(X)−Φ(<a,X>)| ≤ C(η) (δ(f,f)/sqrt(1−ρ))^{1/4−η}`.
  The small-deficit/high-correlation regime and the explicit `η` loss are part of the assertion. Do not silently remove them.
- **Borell inequality derivative (Lemma 2.2, PDF pp. 12–14).** For OU-smoothed `v_t,w_t`, the interpolation remainder has derivative
  `dR_t/dt = ρ/[2π sqrt(1−ρ²)] E_ρ[ exp(−(v_t²+w_t²−2ρv_tw_t)/(2(1−ρ²))) |∇v_t−∇w_t|² ]`.
  The positive Gaussian kernel and squared gradient difference are the rigidity mechanism: equality forces the gradients to agree and hence the smoothed profiles to be affine with common slope. The calculation is coupled with OU semigroup interpolation and limiting approximation of indicators.
- **Equality/robustness proof (PDF pp. 14–21 and 22–27).** Equality uses positivity of the derivative integrand, full support of the conditional Gaussian law, and semigroup inversion to identify parallel half-space extremizers. Quantitative stability then combines Gaussian Poincaré/Hermite control, reverse Hölder estimates and time-reversal/semigroup comparisons. Section 6 tracks improved dependence on `ρ`; sections 7–8 transfer the method to robust Majority-Is-Stablest and spherical settings, with separate domain assumptions.

## B167 migration boundary

B167 asks for the sharp leading asymptotic constant of `inf E(|p(Z)|−1)^2` over all dimensions and real `deg p≤d` with `Var(p(Z))≥1`. This paper supplies stability of **noise-stability extremizers** and a threshold-profile comparison under a noise-stability deficit. It supplies neither the Hermite-tail estimate in the B167 lower-bound argument nor a reduction from the B167 objective to its deficit. The bridge would need a separate theorem relating polynomial variance/degree and `E(|p|−1)^2` to the paper's `δ(f,g)` with constants uniform in dimension and degree; no such interface is proved here.
