## Exact source results

Write `γ_p=(E|g|^p)^(1/p)` for `g~N(0,1)`. The coefficients are rearranged so `|a₁|≥…≥|aₙ|`.

- **Proposition 1 (p. 2).** If `X_i` are independent symmetric, logarithmically concave-tail random variables with `EX_i²=1`, then for `p≥3`, `||Σa_i ε_i||_p ≤ ||Σa_iX_i||_p ≤ ||Σa_i E_i||_p`, where `ε_i` are Rademachers and `E_i` are symmetric exponentials of variance one.
- **Theorem 1 (p. 4).** For `p≥2`,
  `γ_p(Σ_{i=1}^n a_i²)^(1/2) ≥ ||Σ_{i=1}^n a_i ε_i||_p ≥ ||Σ_{i=⌈p/2⌉}^n a_i E_i||_p ≥ γ_p(Σ_{i=⌈p/2⌉}^n a_i²)^(1/2)`.
  The proof of the middle bound is double induction on `k` and `n`; it starts from the characteristic-function comparison for `2≤p≤4`, then uses the two scalar second-derivative inequalities (5)–(6).
- **Theorem 2 (p. 5).** Under Proposition 1's assumptions and sorted coefficients, for `p≥3`,
  `max{γ_p(Σ_{i≥⌈p/2⌉}a_i²)^(1/2), ||Σ_{i<p}a_iX_i||_p} ≤ ||Σ_{i=1}^n a_iX_i||_p ≤ γ_p(Σ_{i≥⌈p/2⌉}a_i²)^(1/2)+||Σ_{i≤2k}a_iX_i||_p`, `k=⌈p/2⌉−1`.
- **Corollary 3 (p. 6).** With the same assumptions as Theorem 2, for `p≥3`,
  `| ||Σ_i a_iX_i||_p − γ_p||a||₂ | ≤ p||a||_∞`.
  This is an additive approximation with error proportional to the largest coefficient. It becomes informative relative to the Gaussian term only when `p||a||∞` is small on that scale.

## Proof mechanism and scope

The paper first compares Rademacher and exponential characteristic functions after sorting the coefficients. For `p∈[2,4]`, a fractional-moment integral formula converts that comparison to a moment inequality. The induction for larger `p` uses integration-by-parts identities for the variance-one symmetric exponential law and a second-derivative lower bound for a Rademacher shift. Corollaries split the largest roughly `p/2` coordinates from the Gaussian-like remainder. The final corollary follows from Proposition 1 and those two corollaries, with the exact coefficient `p`.

The result is for **linear sums** of independent symmetric variance-one variables with logarithmically concave tails. It does not directly apply to a polynomial in a shared Gaussian vector, to dependent monomials, or to `E[(|p(Z)|−1)²]`.

## B167 use

This is useful as a narrowly scoped warning/reference for Gaussian moment approximation of independent sums. It cannot justify Gaussian approximation for a general low-degree polynomial or the absolute-value objective. No matching B167 lower or upper constant follows without an additional reduction, which this source does not provide.
