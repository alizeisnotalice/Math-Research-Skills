## Theorem 3.5 (PDF pp. 9–10): multivariate Malliavin–Stein bound

Let `C` be a positive-definite symmetric `m×m` matrix, `Z∼N_m(0,C)`, and `F=(F₁,…,F_m)` a centered vector with each `F_i∈D^{1,2}`. Define the random matrix

`Φ_{ij}(DF)=<DF_i,−DL⁻¹F_j>_H`.

Then

`d_W(F,Z) ≤ ||C⁻¹||_{op} ||C||_{op}^{1/2} [ E ||C−Φ(DF)||_{HS}² ]^{1/2}`.

The covariance matrix must be positive definite in this theorem. The proof solves the multivariate Stein equation `g(x)−Eg(Z)=<x,∇u(x)>−<C,Hess u(x)>_{HS}` and proves `sup_x||Hess u(x)||_{HS}≤||C⁻¹||_{op}||C||_{op}^{1/2}||g||_{Lip}`. Integration by parts with `δD=−L`, followed by the chain rule, identifies the error as the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing of the Hessian with `C−Φ`; Cauchy–Schwarz yields the displayed square root. The result extends the one-dimensional Malliavin–Stein identity to an arbitrary positive-definite Gaussian covariance.

For `F_i=I_{q_i}(f_i)`, Corollary 3.6 (p. 10) substitutes `−L⁻¹F_j=F_j/q_j`, giving a squared-error sum with entries

`C(i,j)−q_j⁻¹<DF_i,DF_j>_H`.

Lemma 3.7 (pp. 10–12) expands the variance of these derivative inner products by the multiple-integral product formula. It bounds mixed contractions using Cauchy–Schwarz between self-contractions; unequal orders also leave an endpoint term involving the lower-order kernel norm and a contraction of the higher-order kernel. Theorem 3.9 (p. 12) gives the Peccati–Tudor vector CLT for fixed orders: if covariances converge to positive-definite `C`, componentwise Gaussian convergence (equivalently the fourth-moment/self-contraction criteria) implies joint convergence to `N_m(0,C)`. Proposition 3.10 (p. 13) records Wasserstein metrization via those derivative conditions.

## Applications and assumptions

Theorem 4.1 (PDF p. 14) concerns normalized partial sums of the **standard qth Hermite polynomial of fractional-Brownian unit increments**, not arbitrary functionals of Hermite rank `q`. It defines `H_q(x)=(-1)^q e^{x²/2}(d^q/dx^q)e^{−x²/2}` and, for integer `q≥2` and `H<1−1/(2q)`,

`S_n(t)=(σ√n)⁻¹ Σ_{k=0}^{⌊nt⌋−1} H_q(B_{k+1}−B_k)`, `t≥0`.

**Printed-normalization issue.** The source literally prints `σ²=q! Σ_{r∈Z}ρ_H(r)²` on p. 14. Here `ρ_H(r)` is the ordinary covariance/correlation of unit fBm increments, defined on p. 13. Preserve this as the source’s printed formula, but do not treat it as a verified general-`q` normalization. For standard Hermite polynomials, if `(X,Y)` are standard Gaussian with correlation `ρ`, the exact identity is `E[H_q(X)H_q(Y)]=q!ρ^q`; hence the covariance-derived long-run variance is `q!Σ_rρ_H(r)^q`, not `q!Σ_rρ_H(r)^2` unless `q=2`. For `q=3`, it is `6ρ_H(r)^3`. The source’s own range includes `H=3/4` when `q=3` (`H<5/6`), but `ρ_H(r)~(3/8)|r|^{-1/2}` there: `Σρ_H(r)^2` diverges while `Σρ_H(r)^3` converges. Thus the p. 14 printed denominator is incompatible with the usual variance normalization for allowed `q=3,H=3/4`; do not silently repair it or claim the stated general-`q` normalized limit/rate without resolving this source discrepancy. The `q=2` specialization is consistent. A finite truncation check is saved at `evidence/W18/reader_batches/L02_gaussian_B12/math/check_x023_hermite3_normalization.py`; it is illustrative numerical evidence only, while the covariance identity and asymptotic comparison give the mathematical diagnosis.

Theorem 4.1 claims finite-dimensional convergence of `S_n` to standard Brownian motion and states that, for fixed `0=t_0<t_1<⋯<t_d`, the Wasserstein error of the normalized increment vector is at most `c` times `n^{-1/2}` for `H∈(0,1/2]`, `n^{H−1}` for `H∈(1/2,(2q−3)/(2q−2)]`, and `n^{qH−q+1/2}` for `H∈((2q−3)/(2q−2),(2q−1)/(2q))`; `c` depends on `d,H` and the fixed time grid, not on `n`. Its proof represents these specific Hermite increments as multiple integrals and uses contraction bounds and off-diagonal covariance estimates before invoking Corollary 3.6 and Lemma 3.7. Section 4.2 (pp. 15–16) gives a finite-Gaussian-vector second-order Poincare estimate under absolute-continuity and subexponential-growth assumptions on the functions and first derivatives. The core multivariate Theorem 3.5 and its positive-definite covariance hypotheses are unaffected by the §4.1 normalization issue.

## Gaussian-polynomial tool use and boundary

**Auxiliary for B167.** The source provides quantitative multivariate Gaussian approximation when a polynomial vector is represented by fixed-order Wiener integrals and its Malliavin covariance is close to `C`. B167 supplies neither a Gaussian-limit premise nor a covariance-defect bound; it asks for a sharp global infimum of a nonsmooth scalar loss. The theorem does not provide that infimum or its leading constant.
