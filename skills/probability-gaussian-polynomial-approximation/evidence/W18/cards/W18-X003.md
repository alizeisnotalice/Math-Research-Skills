## Gaussian Hermite-tail theorem

Use standard Gaussian measure and the orthonormal multivariate Hermite basis. The paper writes
\[
W^j[f]=\sum_{|\beta|=j}\widehat f(\beta)^2,\qquad
W^{>k}[f]=\sum_{j>k}W^j[f].
\]

**Theorem 1.3 (physical p. 2; introduction).** For \(f:\mathbb R^d\to\{-1,1\}\) with \(\operatorname{Var}[f]\ge\Omega(1)\),
\[
\sum_{|\beta|\ge k}\widehat f(\beta)^2\ge\Omega(k^{-1/2}).
\]
The general statement is **Theorem 2.9 (physical p. 5)**: for every Boolean-valued \(f\),
\[
W^{>k}[f]\ge\Omega(\operatorname{Var}[f])\,k^{-1/2}.
\]
The constant is implicit.

## Proof chain for Theorem 2.9

1. **Rotation sensitivity (physical pp. 3–5).** The Hermite expansion gives
   \[
   RS_f(\epsilon)=\frac12\sum_{j\ge0}(1-\cos^j\epsilon)W^j[f].
   \]
   Theorem 2.5 (physical p. 4) lower-bounds \(RS_f(2\epsilon)\) by \((2\epsilon/\pi)\operatorname{Var}[f]\) for the admissible rotation parameters stated there.
2. Apply the paper's \(\ell=2\) subadditivity inequality, then split the Hermite levels at \(j\le1/(4\epsilon^2)\). On this low range the proof uses
   \[
   \tfrac12(1-\cos^j(2\epsilon))\ge\tfrac34j\epsilon^2,\qquad
   1-\cos^j(\epsilon)\le\tfrac12j\epsilon^2.
   \]
   Subtracting the low-level contribution leaves a constant fraction of the sensitivity lower bound in the high-level tail.
3. Choose an admissible \(\epsilon\asymp k^{-1/2}\). This yields the stated \(\Omega(\operatorname{Var}[f]/\sqrt{k})\) bound. The paper notes that a halfspace through the origin is sharp up to constant factors.

**Discrete-cube transfer (Theorem 3.2, physical pp. 7–8).** By the MOO invariance principle, a corresponding Fourier-tail bound holds for Boolean-cube functions under the paper's low-degree individual-influence condition, stated in terms of \(\operatorname{Inf}^{\le k}_i(f)\), variance, and an exponentially small-in-\(k\) threshold. This is a conditional transfer, not a statement for arbitrary Boolean functions.

**Bourgain-type alternative (Theorem 3.19, physical pp. 12–13).** Under the paper's noisy-influence assumptions, the authors give a bound of order
\[
\Omega\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Var}[f]}{\sqrt{k}\log^{3/2}k}\right)
\]
without the same invariance transfer.

## Derived B167 consequence — not a theorem stated by the source

Let \(p\) be a real polynomial of degree at most \(d\) under standard Gaussian measure, with \(\operatorname{Var}[p]\ge1\), and set \(f=\operatorname{sgn}p\). A nonzero polynomial's zero set has Gaussian measure zero. Put
\[
\delta=\mathbb E[(|p|-1)^2]=\|p-f\|_2^2.
\]
Centering is an orthogonal projection, so \(\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[p]}\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[f]}+\sqrt{\delta}\). If \(\delta\le1/4\), then \(\operatorname{Var}[f]\ge1/4\). Since \(p\) has Hermite degree at most \(d\),
\[
\delta\ge W^{>d}[f]\ge c\,\operatorname{Var}[f]/\sqrt d\ge c/(4\sqrt d).
\]
If \(\delta>1/4\), the same order lower bound follows for \(d\ge1\) after reducing the constant. Thus the paper supports only an \(\Omega(d^{-1/2})\) lower-order obstruction for B167; it does not determine the sharp leading constant.
