## Setup and exact results

The source studies `ξ=(ξ₁,…,ξₙ)` with independent subgaussian coordinates, `Var(ξ_i)≥1`, and coordinate subgaussian constants at most `β` (defined by `||ξ_i||_(2k)≤β||g||_(2k)` for each integer `k≥1`). Random symmetric polytopes are `B=absconv{ξ_j:j≤N}`.

- **Theorem 2.5 (p. 8).** For a nonzero `n×n` matrix `A`, every `y∈Rⁿ`, and the above independent-coordinate assumptions,
  `P(|Aξ−y|≤||A||_HS/2) ≤ 2 exp{−(c₀/β⁴)(||A||_HS/||A||_op)²}`, with universal `c₀>0`.
  The center `y` is arbitrary; the radius is exactly half the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. This is a linear-image small-ball bound, not polynomial anti-concentration.
- **Proposition 2.6 (p. 8).** For a standard Gaussian vector `θ`, the paper gives the stronger small-scale estimate `P(|Aθ−y|≤α||A||_HS)≤exp{κ log(α)(||A||_HS/||A||_op)²}` for `α∈(0,α₀)`, with universal constants. Unlike the subgaussian bound, `α` can make the probability arbitrarily small.
- **Theorem 4.2 (pp. 13–14).** For symmetric convex `K⊃B₂ⁿ`, bounds `P(Aξ∈α||A||_op√n K)` through a Euclidean covering of `K`; `α` depends on `β`, volume radius `V_K`, and stable-rank ratio `a(A)=||A||_HS/||A||_op`. See the displayed formula in the full text for the explicit `η=a(A)²/(nβ⁴)` and logarithmic factor.
- **Theorem 2.3 (pp. 6–7, proof pp. 16–20).** Under `N,M>μ₃n` and balance condition (2.5), the two independent polytopes have `d(B, B̃)≥(1/4)α n` with probability at least `1−2(M+N+3)e^(−μ₅n)`, where `α=c₁ ln(N/n)·ln(M/n)^(−μ′)` and constants depend on `β` as stated.
- **Theorem 2.4 (p. 7, proof pp. 21–26).** For `N≥μ₆n² ln n`, with probability at least `1−(N+1)e^(−2n)`, every projection of rank between `n/4` and `3n/4` has operator norm on `B` at least `α√n`, where `α=c₂ ln(N/n)^(−μ′)` and the constants depend on `β` as stated.

## Proof chain and assumptions that matter

For Theorem 2.5, take an independent copy `ξ′` and set `θ=ξ−ξ′`; symmetrization bounds the square of the desired small-ball probability by `P(|Aθ|≤||A||_HS)`. **Gram-direction note (PDF p. 10):** the source literally writes `B=AAᵀ=(b_ij)` and then `|Aθ|²=⟨Bθ,θ⟩`. Under the standard column-vector convention for `Aθ`, the identity uses `B=AᵀA`; the printed `AAᵀ` instead corresponds to the row-vector convention (or is a transpose slip). A limited corrected derivation under column vectors is to set `B=AᵀA`, so `|Aθ|²=θᵀBθ`, `B` is PSD, `b_ii=Σ_j a_{ji}²≥0`, and `tr(B)=||A||_HS²`. Then `Eθ_i²=2 Var(ξ_i)≥2` gives `Σ_i b_ii Eθ_i²≥2||A||_HS²`; split the lower-tail event into an off-diagonal quadratic form and a negative deviation of the weighted diagonal squares. The matrices `AᵀA` and `AAᵀ` have the same nonzero eigenvalues and the same Hilbert–Schmidt/operator norm bounds relevant to the subsequent exponent. This flags a convention/transpose issue in this proof display; it does not by itself refute Theorem 2.5. The off-diagonal form is controlled by Hanson–Wright after symmetrization, and the diagonal term by the one-sided exponential-moment lemma using nonnegative weights and fourth moments, yielding exponents of order `β⁻⁴ (||A||_HS/||A||_op)²`. Independence of coordinates is essential; p. 12 gives the common-scale Gaussian mixture counterexample, which has a large atom at zero despite marginal variance/subgaussian control.

The convex-body estimate covers `K` by Euclidean balls and unions Theorem 2.5 over the cover. The polytope results then use nets of operators, a high-probability Euclidean inball for the random polytope, and (for projection norms) a weak-dependence decoupling lemma. These geometric conclusions concern random normed spaces and linear operators.
