## Model and universality

For independent centered variance-one coordinates `X_i`, a homogeneous sum is

`Q_d(N,f,X)=Σ_{1≤i₁,…,i_d≤N} f(i₁,…,i_d) X_{i₁}⋯X_{i_d}`,

where `f` is symmetric and vanishes on diagonals. Then `E Q_d=0` and `Var(Q_d)=d!||f||²`. The influence is `Inf_i(f)=(d−1)!⁻¹ Σ_{i₂,…,i_d} f(i,i₂,…,i_d)²`; the sum of influences is `||f||²/(d−1)!`.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 5) says that for finitely many such sums with degrees `d_j≥2`, sizes `N_n^{(j)}→∞`, bounded Gaussian-counterpart variances, and target covariance matrix `V` with nonzero diagonal, Gaussian-counterpart convergence to `N_m(0,V)` is equivalent to convergence for **every** independent centered variance-one input sequence with uniformly bounded third absolute moments, in the multivariate Kolmogorov distance. It does not require positive-definite `V` or a low-influence assumption for this equivalence. Theorem 1.8 (p. 8) gives the even-degree centered chi-square criterion: with variance tending to `2ν`, the moment combination `E Q⁴−12E Q³→12ν²−48ν` corresponds to convergence to `Σ_{j=1}^ν(G_j²−1)` and to the middle contraction approaching `c_d f`, `c_d=4(d/2)!³/(d!)²`, while all other self-contractions vanish. These conclusions are specific to the symmetric diagonal-free homogeneous-sum setup.

## Low-influence replacement and quantitative bounds

Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 18–19) states a Lindeberg replacement principle. If `max_i Inf_i(f_n)→0`, then Kolmogorov-distance replacement holds under `sup_i E|X_i|^{2+ε}<∞`; for identically distributed inputs it also gives weak convergence under the stated unit-variance assumptions. For `β=sup_i E|X_i|³<∞`, a thrice differentiable test function satisfies the finite-`n` replacement bound

`||φ'''||∞ (30β)^d d! sqrt(max_i Inf_i(f))`.

The square root on the maximum influence is present in item (3) of Theorem 4.1 on the rendered original PDF p. 18; the corresponding coverage note and source patch were corrected to preserve it.

Theorem 5.1 (PDF p. 21) combines this replacement with the Gaussian fixed-chaos fourth-moment bound. For centered independent variance-one `X_i`, `β=sup_i E[X_i⁴]<∞`, `α=max{3,β}`, symmetric diagonal-free `f`, `E Q_d(X)²=1`, and `||φ'''||∞≤B`, it gives

`|Eφ(Q_d(X))−Eφ(Z)| ≤ B(30β)^d d! (max_i Inf_i(f))^{1/2}`

`+ C_* √((d−1)/(3d)) [ √|E(Q_d(X)^4)−3| + 4√2·144^{d−1/2} α^{d/2} √(d d!) (max_i Inf_i(f))^{1/4} ]`,

where `Z∼N(0,1)` and `C_*` is the dimension/order-dependent Stein constant in equation (3.1). The proof splits the error into non-Gaussian replacement and Gaussian-chaos approximation, then bounds the fourth-moment replacement by a one-coordinate Lindeberg estimate. This is a smooth-test bound; the displayed exponents are as printed and were visually verified. Proposition 5.4 separately smooths Lipschitz tests to get a Wasserstein bound under its small-error condition.

Theorem 6.1 (PDF pp. 24–25) gives the analogous even-degree centered chi-square bound, requiring bounded fourth moments, integer `ν≥1`, variance `2ν`, and test functions with `||φ||∞,||φ'||∞≤1` and bounded third derivative. Its error uses the third/fourth-moment combination and the maximum influence. Sections 7.1–7.2 extend replacement and Gaussian-chaos contraction bounds to vectors; the proof uses Gaussian interpolation, integration by parts, and smoothing of convex-set indicators. Section 8 (PDF pp. 34–38) supplies the Malliavin–Stein calculations behind these estimates.

## Gaussian-polynomial tool use and boundary

**Auxiliary for B167.** This is a core source for when a low-influence Gaussian homogeneous sum can be replaced by non-Gaussian inputs or approximated by a Gaussian/chi-square law. B167 permits arbitrary real polynomials of degree at most `d`, any dimension, and has no low-influence or asymptotic-normality premise. The homogeneous-sum theorem does not bound `E[(|p(Z)|−1)²]` or determine its sharp asymptotic constant. A decomposition plus a proven low-influence reduction would be needed before applying these estimates.
