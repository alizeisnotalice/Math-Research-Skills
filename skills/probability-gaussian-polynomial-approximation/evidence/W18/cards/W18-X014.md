## Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 1)

As printed, for `X~N(0,I_n)`, every **nonnegative degree-two** real polynomial `f:R^n→R` and every `ε>0` satisfy
`P{f(X)≤ε E f(X)} ≤ (2e ε)^(1/2)`.
The result is dimension-free, relative to the mean, and near zero. Since the displayed event and normalization use `E f(X)`, the zero polynomial cannot be included; for a degree-two nonnegative polynomial that is not identically zero, full support of Gaussian measure gives `E f(X)>0`. “Nonnegative” and degree two are part of the source scope. The polynomial is represented as `f(x)=[1;x]^T Q[1;x]` with `Q⪰0`; `E f(X)=q_11+tr(Q_22)`. The source does not assert the result for signed polynomials or arbitrary degrees.

**Separate elementary extension (not Theorem 1.2's wording):** the same displayed inequality also holds for every nonzero nonnegative polynomial of degree at most two. The only such degree-0 or degree-1 polynomials are positive constants. For `f≡c>0`, the left side is 0 if `ε<1`, and 1 if `ε≥1`, while the right side exceeds 1 in the latter range. The zero polynomial remains excluded.

## Proof chain

Lemma 2.1 completes the square in the Gaussian integral and uses the PSD Schur complement to show for `λ>0`
`E exp(−λ [1;X]^TQ[1;X]) ≤ det(I+2λQ_22)^(−1/2)`.
Lemma 2.2 combines this with a determinant/trace bound and Chernoff to control the lower tail in terms of `tr(Q_22)`. The remaining linear-block-dominant case is separated and handled by a one-dimensional noncentral-Gaussian small-ball estimate (Lemma 2.3), then a perturbation parameter is sent to zero; Lemma 2.4 completes the reduction. This yields the displayed factor `sqrt(2eε)`. The proof is a special low-degree argument, not an upgrade of the general Carbery–Wright theorem.

## B167 migration boundary

B167 permits signed degree-`≤d` polynomials and asks for `E(|p(Z)|−1)²` under `Var(p(Z))≥1`. This result only bounds lower-tail mass for nonnegative quadratics relative to their mean. It neither controls `|p|` near one nor supplies the high-degree / arbitrary-sign dimension-uniform estimate or sharp constant.
