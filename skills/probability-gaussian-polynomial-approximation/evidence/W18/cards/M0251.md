## Theorem 1: one-sided comparison for Gaussian maxima

Let X,Y be d-dimensional Gaussian vectors with the same mean vector mu and positive marginal standard deviations. Put sigma_i = sqrt(Var(X_i) wedge Var(Y_i)) and

\[
\Delta_{ij}=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)-\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)}{\sigma_i\sigma_j},\quad
\Delta_+^{\rm cross}=\max_{i<j}(\Delta_{ij})_+,\quad
\Delta^{\rm diag}=\max_i|\Delta_{ii}|.
\]

For d >= 2, Theorem 1 (physical p.3, printed p.5596) bounds the **one-sided** difference

\[
\mathbb P(\max_iY_i\le t)-\mathbb P(\max_iX_i\le t)
\le \sqrt{4\Delta^*\log d},
\quad
\Delta^*=\frac{\Delta_+^{\rm cross}+\Delta^{\rm diag}}2
+\frac{\max_{i\le j}|\Delta_{ij}|}{2\log d}.
\]

Its second bound is

\[
\left(2\frac{\Delta_+^{\rm cross}\vee\Delta^{\rm diag}}{1-\rho^*}
+\frac{\Delta^{\rm diag}}2\right)
\min\{2\log d,(\nu_*(t)+1)^2\},
\]

where rho* is the largest absolute off-diagonal correlation among the coordinates of X or Y and nu*(t)=1 \vee max_i |t-mu_i|/sigma_i. The numerator of the first term is 2(Delta_cross^+ vee Delta_diag) exactly as printed; the vee and the 1 vee in nu* were checked on rendered physical p.3. Under Slepian covariance ordering, the error vanishes and the classical inequality is recovered.

## Other anti-concentration theorems and constants

Theorem 2 (physical p.5, printed p.5598) bounds a weighted first-derivative sum for the joint Gaussian CDF under explicit monotonicity conditions on the weight h. Setting h=1 yields Corollary 1: for d >= 2 and all marginal standard deviations sigma_i >= sigma > 0, the density of max_i X_i is at most sqrt(2 log d)/sigma. If |t-mu_i| >= a for all i, its density is at most (2/a) log d.

Theorem 3 (physical p.7, printed p.5600) bounds weighted off-diagonal second derivatives. The weights are alpha_(i,j)=2 tan(theta_(i,min)/4)|det Sigma^(i,j)|^(1/2). The resulting bound is the minimum of a logarithmic term and (a_2^*(x)+sqrt(2))^2; a signed diagonal/off-diagonal Hessian bound is also stated. The proof uses Gaussian conditional regression, pairwise rank events and bivariate Mills-ratio integrals. Section 3 interpolates covariance matrices and smooths the maximum to prove the comparison theorem.

For distinct-coordinate derivatives, Theorem 5 (physical p.13, printed p.5606) states: for every m<d there is a finite C_m depending only on m. For positive weights b_(i_1: i_m), ordered as b_(1) <= b_(2) <= ..., it proves

\[
\sup_x\sum_{i_{1:m}\in[d]^m_<}
\frac{\alpha'_{i_{1:m}}}{b_{i_{1:m}}}|G_{i_{1:m}}(x)|
\le C_m\max_{1\le k\le d}\frac{(1+\sqrt{2\log k})^m}{b_{(k)}}.
\]

Here G_(i_1: i_m) is the mixed partial derivative of the joint CDF, and alpha' is the square-root determinant of the m-coordinate covariance matrix times the product of one-sided threshold factors t_(i_1:j) built from successive partial correlations. The theorem needs the relevant m-coordinate covariance matrices to be nonsingular so these factors are defined; invertibility of the full d-by-d covariance matrix is not required.

Theorem 6 (physical p.14, printed p.5607) replaces alpha' by alpha'' expressed with sparse minimum eigenvalues. In particular, writing lambda_min,j for the minimum eigenvalue across all j-coordinate correlation submatrices,

\[
\sup_x\sum_{i_{1:m}\in[d]^m_<}
\left(\prod_{j=1}^m\sigma_{i_j}\right)|G_{i_{1:m}}(x)|
\le
\frac{C_m(1+\sqrt{2\log d})^m}
{\lambda_{\min,m}\sqrt{\lambda_{\min,m-1}\cdots\lambda_{\min,2}}}.
\]

Theorem 7 (physical p.15, printed p.5608) handles the repeated-index third derivative:

\[
\sup_x\sum_{i=1}^d\sum_{j=1}^d\sum_{k=1}^d
\sigma_i\sigma_j\sigma_k|G_{i,j,k}(x)|
\le\frac{C_3(1+\sqrt{2\log d})^3}
{\lambda_{\min,3}\sqrt{\lambda_{\min,2}}}.
\]

The paper explicitly leaves the general repeated-direction higher-order extension beyond scope; the displayed repeated-index result is third order only.

## Proof chain

Theorem 5 standardizes and shifts the Gaussian coordinates, applies Gram–Schmidt regression to obtain independent conditional residuals, and partitions a rank event so that its probabilities sum to at most one. A carefully chosen positive-threshold region lower-bounds the probability of the rank event in terms of alpha' and the joint density. Ordering the user weights b then yields the logarithmic factor (1+sqrt(2 log k))^m. Lemma 1 writes the determinant of each correlation submatrix as a product of conditional-variance factors. The sparse eigenvalue bound gives alpha'' <= 5^((m-1)/2) alpha', proving Theorem 6 from Theorem 5. Theorem 7 differentiates the conditional-density identity and controls the resulting regression coefficients by the sparse eigenvalues for subsets of size two and three.

## B167 use and non-implication

Role: **auxiliary Gaussian anti-concentration/comparison only**. The input is a finite Gaussian vector and the object is its joint CDF or coordinatewise maximum. B167 is a polynomial optimization under a Gaussian input; its scalar p(G) is generally not the maximum of a Gaussian vector. No map from polynomial roots/derivatives to these CDF derivative bounds is proved, and the paper gives no sharp constant for E[(|p|-1)^2].
