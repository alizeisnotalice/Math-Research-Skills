## Theorem 1: two-sided decoupled-chaos moment estimate

Theorem 1 (physical p.3) is printed for independent symmetric variables X_i^j, i<=n, j<=d, with unit variance and logarithmically convex tails. That phrase is internally ambiguous in this source. The Introduction on physical p.2 defines N_i(t)=-ln P(|X_i|>=t) to be convex, which makes survival log-concave, yet in the same paragraph says the class includes heavy-tailed Weibull variables with P(|X|>=t)=exp(-t^r), 0<r<1; there N(t)=t^r is concave, not convex. The abstract also calls the tails logarithmically concave.

The order-one input that the proof actually cites resolves the intended class as log-convex survival, equivalently N_i(t)=-ln P(|X_i|>=t) concave: HMO, Theorem 1.1, defines P(|X_k|>=t)=exp(-N_k(t)) with N_k concave, N_k(0)=0, and proves the order-one estimate for p>=2 with absolute constants. HMO explicitly includes Weibull exponents 0<r<=1. M0242 Theorem 3 on physical p.6 quotes this same logarithmically-convex-tail class for independent symmetric variables. Thus the supported reading of Theorem 1 below is conditional on the HMO convention N_i concave; the printed opposite convexity in M0242 p.2 is retained as a source defect, not silently corrected. Do not extend it to the Gaussian/log-concave-survival class. For this supported reading, let X_i^j, i<=n, j<=d, be independent symmetric variables with N_i^j concave and E|X_i^j|^2=1. For any coefficient array a=(a_(i_1,...,i_d)) and p>=2,

\[
\left\|\sum_{i\in[n]^d}a_i\prod_{j=1}^dX^j_{i_j}\right\|_p
\asymp_d
\sum_{I\subset[d]}\sum_{J\in\mathcal P(I^c)}p^{|J|/2}
\left(\sum_{i_I}\|(a_i)_{i_{I^c}}\|_J^p\prod_{j\in I}\|X^j_{i_j}\|_p^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]

Here P(I^c) partitions the Gaussian-like coordinate slots, and ||.||_J is the partition contraction norm defined on physical p.2. Constants in both directions depend only on chaos order d, not on n, p, or the array. The variables are independent across both i and chaos slot j; this theorem is for the decoupled multilinear form.

Remark 1 transfers the comparison to a nonhomogeneous tetrahedral polynomial in independent symmetric inputs by Kwapień’s decoupling and degree decomposition; the constants depend on total degree d. Theorem 3 on physical p.6 is the order-one input: for independent symmetric variables in the HMO log-convex-survival class (N_i=-ln survival concave),

\[
\left\|\sum_i X_i\right\|_p\asymp
\left(\sum_i\mathbb E|X_i|^p\right)^{1/p}
+\sqrt p\left(\sum_i\mathbb E X_i^2\right)^{1/2},\qquad p\ge2,
\]

with universal constants. Corollary 2 gives an upper moment/tail bound when independent centered inputs satisfy ||X_i^j||_p <= A p^(1/r), 0 < r <= 1.

## Proof dependencies and limitations

Proof of Theorem 1: apply the HMO order-one estimate conditionally and induct on d, replacing one slot at a time by independent standard Gaussians with matching L_p comparison; then use Latała’s two-sided Gaussian-chaos estimate (Theorem 6) for the remaining Gaussian slots. Decoupling statement (2) transfers between homogeneous tetrahedral and independent-slot chaoses. The corollary for moment-dominated inputs uses symmetrization, comparison with symmetric Weibull variables and Markov’s inequality. Constants C(d) are order-dependent, not sharp leading asymptotic constants.

## Source inconsistency and exact HMO boundary

This source has more than an abstract wording mismatch. Its title, keywords and Theorems 1/3 label the inputs logarithmically convex tails; its abstract says logarithmically concave tails; physical p.2 explicitly defines N=-ln survival as convex; but the same Introduction includes heavy-tailed Weibull survival exp(-t^r), 0<r<1, for which N=t^r is concave. These conditions cannot all describe one class. I checked the cited original HMO paper, P. Hitczenko, S. J. Montgomery-Smith and K. Oleszkiewicz, Moment inequalities for sums of certain independent symmetric random variables, Studia Math. 123 (1997), 15–42, DOI 10.4064/sm-123-1-15-42, at the author-hosted full-text URL https://stephenmontgomerysmith.github.io/preprints/weibulls.pdf. Its Theorem 1.1 defines logarithmically convex tails by survival exp(-N) with N concave and N(0)=0; for independent inputs and p>=2 it gives universal-constant equivalence between the sum Lp norm and (sum ||X_i||_p^p)^(1/p) + sqrt(p)(sum ||X_i||_2^2)^(1/2). It identifies symmetric Weibull exponents 0<r<=1 as included. M0242 Theorem 3's formula is exactly this order-one input for independent symmetric variables; Corollary 4 further requires variance-one inputs and compares weighted sums to Gaussian sums. The proof of M0242 Theorem 1 uses this HMO input conditionally, then induction and Gaussian-chaos comparison. Accordingly, this card records only the HMO-supported N-concave/log-convex-survival interpretation as the usable conditional scope and explicitly preserves M0242 p.2's opposite sign as an unresolved source error. It gives no license to replace the assumption with Gaussian or log-concave survival tails.

## B167 use and non-implication

Role: **auxiliary chaos-moment comparison**. It supplies order-dependent two-sided norms for decoupled/tetrahedral chaoses and an exponential/Weibull comparison. B167 concerns arbitrary real polynomials under standard Gaussian input and the non-polynomial residual ||p|-1|. This result neither estimates that residual nor identifies its sharp degree asymptotic; log-convex-tail hypotheses are not a substitute for the B167 objective.
