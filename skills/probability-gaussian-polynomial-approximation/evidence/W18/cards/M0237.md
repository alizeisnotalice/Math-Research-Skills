# M0237 — Kane, *A Structure Theorem for Poorly Anticoncentrated Gaussian Chaoses*

**Source/version.** Daniel M. Kane, arXiv:1204.0543v2, FOCS 2012 version, 57 physical pages. The first page matches the title and author. The header labels the arXiv version 2012-08-15, while the manuscript body says “September 12, 2018”; this internal date discrepancy is retained. All physical pp. 1–57 were read.

## Structural and invariance results

- **Carbery–Wright input (physical p. 2).** For degree-d p under standard Gaussian X, P(|p(X)|<=epsilon ||p||_2)=O(d epsilon^(1/d)). The exponent is sharp for powers of a linear form, so the paper does not assume a bounded density for arbitrary Gaussian polynomials.
- **Theorem 1, diffuse decomposition (physical pp. 3–4).** For positive epsilon,c,N and integer d>0, every degree-d polynomial p has a degree-d polynomial p' such that ||p-p'||_2 < O_(c,d,N)(epsilon^N)||p||_2, and p' has an (epsilon,epsilon^(-c))-diffuse decomposition of size O_(c,d,N)(1). A diffuse tuple (q_1,...,q_m) is normalized by E|q_i(X)|^2<=1 and satisfies P(|q_i(X)-a_i|<=epsilon for every i)<=epsilon^m N for every a in R^m; N is outside the exponent. Thus the theorem’s choice N=epsilon^(-c) gives box mass at most epsilon^(m-c), not epsilon^(mN). The paper says the size bound is effective but may be Ackermann-scale; polynomial size is conjectured by the author, not proved.
- **Theorem 32, diffuse invariance (physical pp. 34–37).** For a degree-d multilinear polynomial with a (tau,N,m,epsilon)-regular decomposition, Bernoulli and Gaussian cdf values differ by O_(d,m)(tau^(1/5) N log(tau^(-1))^(dm/2+1) + epsilon^(1/d) log(epsilon^(-1))^(1/2)). This is conditional on the decomposition, multilinearity/influence controls, and the stated parameter range; it is not a general PTF bound without the regularity/decision-tree step.
- **Theorem 34, diffuse regularity (statement physical p. 36; proof pp. 43–45).** A Bernoulli-input degree-d polynomial admits a decision-tree reduction of depth O_(c,d,M)(tau^(-1) log(tau^(-1))^(O(d))). With probability at least 1-tau, a random leaf is either low variance relative to its squared L2 norm or has a regular diffuse decomposition with the theorem's parameter bounds.

## PTF applications and scope

Theorem 46 (physical pp. 48–51) gives Boolean noise sensitivity O_(c,d)(delta^(1/6-c)); Theorems 47–48 give Boolean and Gaussian average sensitivity O_(c,d)(n^(5/6+c)). Their proofs combine diffuse invariance, decision-tree regularity, anti-concentration, and comparison to Gaussian noise sensitivity. Sections 5 and 8 also give Gaussian- and Bernoulli-input PRG applications. These are degree-dependent structural bounds, not a sharp Gaussian polynomial/sign L2-approximation constant.
