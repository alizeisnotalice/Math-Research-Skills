## Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3): fixed-order fourth-moment theorem

Let `H` be a separable Hilbert space, `X` an isonormal Gaussian process over `H`, and fix an integer `q≥2`. For symmetric kernels `f_k∈H^{⊙q}`, put `F_k=I_q^X(f_k)` and assume

`E[F_k²]=q! ||f_k||²_{H^{⊗q}} → 1`.

The following are equivalent as `k→∞`:

1. `E[F_k⁴]→3`;
2. for each `r=1,…,q−1`, `||f_k ⊗_r f_k||²_{H^{⊗2(q−r)}}→0`;
3. `F_k` converges in distribution to `N(0,1)`.

The order `q` must stay fixed, and the variance normalization is part of the result. Corollary 2 (same page) says that a single nonzero normalized integral of fixed order `q≥2` is not Gaussian. Its short proof uses the contraction identity tested against `v⊗v`: vanishing contraction for every `v` forces every partial kernel contraction to vanish, hence `f=0`.

## Proof mechanism and applications

The paper first proves a Brownian iterated-integral version (Proposition 3, PDF pp. 3–10). For ordered kernels on the simplex it adds an equivalent condition on time-integrated adapted-slice contractions. In the direction from this condition to the CLT, Dambis–Dubins–Schwarz writes the terminal multiple integral as a Brownian motion at its quadratic-variation clock. The product formula expands that clock into the deterministic variance plus multiple-integral terms; stochastic Fubini identifies their kernels with the slice-contraction expressions. The reverse implication from the fourth moment expands `I_q(f)^2` by the product formula. Orthogonality makes the nonnegative contraction terms visible, while permutation counting handles the symmetrization. The Brownian formulation is transferred to arbitrary separable `H` in §2.2 (PDF pp. 10–11) by an isometry into Brownian `L²`; it is not a claim that the proof uses moments alone.

Section 3.1 (PDF pp. 11–15) applies the criterion to weighted quadratic functionals of fractional Brownian motion for `H>1/2`, including a near-critical Gaussian limit and a distinct noncentral limit as the weight parameter tends to infinity. Section 3.2 (pp. 15–17) treats Brownian-sheet quadratic functionals. These applications explicitly check contraction norms rather than follow from the theorem without model-specific work.

## Gaussian-polynomial tool use and boundary

**Auxiliary for B167.** This gives a sharp diagnostic for Gaussian convergence of sequences lying in one fixed Wiener chaos. B167 asks for the infimum of `E[(|p(Z)|−1)²]` over arbitrary degree-at-most-`d` polynomials and all dimensions; it does not assume an asymptotically Gaussian sequence. The theorem gives neither that objective's lower bound nor its leading constant. A reduction from the nonsmooth loss to these contraction conditions remains unproved.
