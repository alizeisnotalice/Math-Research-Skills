## Invariance theorems

The paper works with multilinear polynomials over independent orthonormal ensembles, with matching moments and hypercontractivity. For a Boolean cube Fourier expansion, the influence is
\[
\operatorname{Inf}_i(Q)=\sum_{S\ni i}\widehat Q(S)^2,
\]
and low-degree influence restricts this sum to \(|S|\le d\).

- **Theorem 3.18 (§3.4; physical p. 21, printed p. 313).** Suppose one of Hypotheses 1–4 holds; \(\operatorname{Var}[Q]\le1\), \(\deg(Q)\le d\), and \(\operatorname{Inf}_i(Q)\le\tau\) for every coordinate. For a \(C^r\) test function \(\Psi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) with \(|\Psi^{(r)}|\le B\), the expectation difference between the two ensemble evaluations is at most \(\epsilon\). Under the general Hypothesis 1,
  \[
  \epsilon=\frac{2B}{r!}\,d\,\eta^{-rd}\tau^{\,r/2-1}.
  \]
  The paper gives sharper/specialized forms under Hypotheses 2–4; in particular for the Rademacher-versus-Gaussian setting (Hypothesis 4, \(r=4,\eta=3^{-1/2}\)) the bound is \(B\,10^d\tau\). Moment matching and hypercontractivity are assumptions, not consequences of low influence.
- **Theorem 3.19 (§3.5; physical PDF p. 26, printed p. 318; statement begins on physical p. 25).** Under Hypothesis 1, 2, 3, or 4, with \(\operatorname{Var}[Q]\le1\), \(\deg(Q)\le d\), and \(\operatorname{Inf}_i(Q)\le\tau\) for all \(i\), the theorem gives \(L^1\), Lévy-distance, and squared-distance-to-\([0,1]\) expectation comparisons with rates \(O(\epsilon^{1/r})\), \(O(\epsilon^{1/(r+1)})\), and \(O(\epsilon^{2/r})\), respectively. If \(\operatorname{Var}(Q)=1\), the distribution-function bound is \(O(d\,\epsilon^{1/(rd+1)})\). The constants in the first three comparisons depend only on \(r\). The theorem defines its own \(\epsilon\): under Hypothesis 1, \(d\eta^{-rd}\tau^{r/2-1}\); under Hypothesis 2, \(30^d\beta^d\tau^{1/2}\); under Hypothesis 3, \((10\alpha^{-1/2})^d\tau^{1/2}\); under Hypothesis 4, \(10^d\tau\). In particular, these are not obtained by carrying over the derivative bound \(B\) from Theorem 3.18. Retain the model, degree, variance, influence and ensemble assumptions.
- **Theorem 3.20 (§3.5; physical PDF p. 27, printed p. 319).** Assume Hypothesis 1, 3, or 4 (not Hypothesis 2) and \(\mathbb E[Q^2]\le1\). Smoothing \(Q\) by \(T_{1-\gamma}\) removes the bounded-degree requirement from applications, provided its low-degree influences satisfy
  \[
  \operatorname{Inf}^{\le \log(1/\tau)/K}_i(Q)\le\tau\le\tfrac12
  \]
  and \(\tau\le\exp(-CK/\gamma)\). Here \(K=\log(1/\eta)\) under Hypothesis 1, \(K=\log(2/\alpha)\) under Hypothesis 3, and \(K=1\) under Hypothesis 4. The smoothed evaluations have Lévy distance and the stated \(\zeta\)-functional discrepancy at most \(\tau^{\Omega(\gamma/K)}\). An alternative applies without smoothing when all high-degree variance tails satisfy the theorem’s bound. Low-degree influence is not the same as a small maximum Fourier coefficient.

## Applications and obstruction

- **Theorem 4.4 (§4.2; physical p. 34, printed p. 326).** For \(f:\Omega_1\times\cdots\times\Omega_n\to[0,1]\) with every atom in every coordinate space of probability at least \(\alpha\), put \(K=\log(2/\alpha)\). Under the theorem’s low-degree-influence condition \(\operatorname{Inf}^{\le\log(1/\tau)/K}_i(f)\le\tau\) and \(\tau\le K^{-CK}\), its noise stability is at most that of the Gaussian threshold of the same mean plus
  \[
  O\!\left(\frac{K}{1-\rho}\frac{\log\log(1/\tau)}{\log(1/\tau)}\right).
  \]
  In the balanced Boolean-cube case the Gaussian benchmark is \(\frac14+\frac{1}{2\pi}\arcsin\rho\), Sheppard’s formula. This is the Majority Is Stablest transfer; it is not an unconditional comparison for arbitrary Boolean functions.
- **Theorem 4.9 (§4.3; physical pp. 37–39, printed pp. 329–331).** The “It Ain’t Over Till It’s Over” prediction theorem extends the reveal operator to finite product spaces. Under atom lower bounds and low-degree-influence assumptions, after independent random revelation the conditional expectation remains away from both 0 and 1 with high probability, with explicit coupled parameter conditions in the theorem. Keep the reveal model and low-degree hypotheses; this is not a claim for arbitrary observation schemes.
- **Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.3 (§5; physical pp. 40–41, printed pp. 332–333).** An odd Gaussian sign function and a sequence of completely symmetric odd Boolean functions with coordinate influences \(O(n^{-1/2})\) have combined Fourier/Hermite mass on levels \(1\) and \(3\) approaching at least \(0.75913\), exceeding the majority benchmark \(2/\pi+1/(3\pi)\). Thus the proposed stronger extremality of majority for the sum of the first few levels is false. This does not contradict Majority Is Stablest, which concerns the full noise-stability functional under its stated hypotheses.

## Proof route and scope

Theorem 3.18 uses a Lindeberg replacement: replace one ensemble coordinate at a time, Taylor-expand the smooth test, cancel terms by matching moments, bound the remainder with hypercontractivity, and sum influence powers. Theorem 3.19 smooths nonsmooth tests and uses Gaussian small-ball estimates; Theorem 3.20 truncates high degrees after noise smoothing. The applications then transfer to Gaussian space and invoke Borell’s Gaussian noise-stability inequality, or transfer to the discrete reveal setting.

This source supplies a conditional invariance/noise-stability interface. It does not identify Fourier degree, Fourier-\u21130 support, Fourier-\u21131 norm, Fourier dimension, or “depends on every coordinate” as interchangeable quantities. For polynomial threshold questions, explicitly prove the low-influence/matching-moment/hypercontractivity hypotheses and separately bound threshold anti-concentration.
