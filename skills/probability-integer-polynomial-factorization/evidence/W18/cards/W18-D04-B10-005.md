## Exact setting and results

The ambient multiplicative monoid is `N0[x]^*={f∈Z[x]: f≠0 and every coefficient is a nonnegative integer}`. Divisibility in this monoid (`|_N`) is strictly distinguished from divisibility in `Z[x]` (`|_Z`). For example, `x^3+1=(x+1)(x^2−x+1)` in `Z[x]`, but the second factor is not in `N0[x]`; this does not constitute a factorization in the studied monoid.

- **Lemma 2.3 (physical p. 5).** Given finitely many nonconstant atoms `f_i∈N0[x]^*\{x}` of degree at least one, put `n=max_i deg(f_i)`. Choose distinct primes `p,q` with `q≥max_i f_i(1)`; since coefficients are nonnegative integers, `q` exceeds each coefficient. Set `z(x)=Σ_{k=0}^{2n}a_kx^k`, with `a_{2n}=q(p+1)`, `a_n=−p`, and `a_k=pq` for all other k. The polynomial is outside `N0[x]^*`, has degree `2n`, and is irreducible in `Z[x]` by Eisenstein at p: every lower coefficient is divisible by p, the leading coefficient is not, and the constant is not divisible by p². For any `f_i=Σ_{j=0}^d b_jx^j`, `f_i z` has at most one negative summand `−p b_j` in each coefficient; the other convolution summands include a nonnegative term at least `pq b_0`, `(p+1)q b_d`, or `pq b_d`. The condition `q≥f_i(1)≥b_j` makes these sums nonnegative. The article then derives the single-atom statement: for an atom `f≠x`, there are `h∈N0[x]^*` of positive degree and `z∉N0[x]^*` with `f∤_N h` and both `fz,hz∈N0[x]^*`.
- **Proposition 2.4 (physical p. 5; source proof).** A nonconstant atom other than x is not a prime element of `N0[x]^*`; hence the only prime elements are positive prime integers and x. Lemma 2.3 supplies `h,z` with `f∤_N h` and `fz,hz∈N0[x]^*`. Put `g=fz`. Then `gh=(fz)h=f(zh)`, with quotient `zh∈N0[x]^*`, so `f|_N gh`. Also `f∤_N h` by Lemma 2.3, and `f∤_N g`: any quotient in `N0[x]` would, by cancellation in `Z[x]`, have to equal z, contrary to `z∉N0[x]`. This is the product and quotient used in the source; it does not require `hz²∈N0[x]`.
- **§6 algorithm (physical pp. 13–14; source statement and safe derived use).** For primitive `f∈N0[x]^*`, let `α(f)` be its largest coefficient, `a=α(f)+1`, and `b=f(a)`. Since `α(f)<a`, the coefficients of `f` are the base-a digits of `b`. The source prints `η(a,b)=max{n∈N:a^n<b}` and uses `deg f=η(a,b)`; this misses the exact-power boundary (e.g. `f=x²,a=2,b=4` gives printed η=1 although `deg f=2`). The all-boundaries degree formula is the derived `d=floor(log_a b)=max{n∈N0:a^n≤b}`, which follows from `a^d≤b<a^{d+1}` for a degree-d polynomial with digits below a. The source then enumerates integer factorizations `b=∏b_j`, reconstructs base-a candidate factors, and prints at step (5) “if `α(g)≤a`, then `g=f`.” That non-strict digit bound is insufficient: on physical p. 13 take `f=1+x³+x⁴+x⁵`, `a=2`, `b=57=3·19`; the printed η values are `η(2,57)=5`, `η(2,3)=1`, `η(2,19)=4`. The candidate `(1+x)(1+x+x⁴)=1+2x+x²+x⁴+x⁵` has value 57 at 2, degree 5, and `α(g)=2≤a`, yet is not f. Thus the printed candidate-acceptance step and degree identity are isolated as source-local boundary issues; this finite counterexample does not assert that the paper's broader results are false. For a safe derived enumeration, use the corrected degree `floor(log_a b)` (including monomial cases), reconstruct candidates, and verify the exact polynomial product equals f before accepting. Exact SymPy checks are saved in `math/b10_exact_checks.json`. Example 6.1 gives `1+x+…+x^5=(x+1)(x^4+x^2+1)=(x^3+1)(x^2+x+1)`.

## Use boundary for A175

This is a concrete method for **integer-coefficient** nonnegative polynomial factorization and monoid divisibility. It does not infer that a factor with arbitrary real nonnegative coefficients is integral, rational, or in `N0[x]`. Therefore the source is auxiliary to A175 unless the integer-coefficient subcase is independently established. The worked example distinguishes two valid `N0[x]` factorizations from a merely `Z[x]` ring factorization.
