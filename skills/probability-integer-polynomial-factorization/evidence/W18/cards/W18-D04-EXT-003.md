## Exact statements

Let `p` be the smallest prime dividing `n`, and let `q₁,…,q_k` be the other **distinct** primes dividing `n`. **Conjecture 1 (physical p. 2)** says the lowest-degree monic polynomial with nonnegative coefficients divisible by `Φ_n(x)` is `1+x^{n/p}+⋯+x^{(p−1)n/p}`, with uniqueness up to scalar in the corresponding minimization. The paper says the general case remains open and states skepticism that the conjecture always holds.

**Theorem 1 (physical p. 3; rendered).** The conjecture holds when `n` is even, when `n` is a prime power, or when
`2/p > 1/q₁+⋯+1/q_k`.
The proof section assumes `n` squarefree; the introduction explains reduction to the squarefree part. It also notes that all cases with at most three distinct prime factors follow, and that exactly two prime factors had already been covered by de Bruijn.

**Problem 1 and Theorem 2 (physical pp. 15–18; Theorem 2 begins p. 16).** For positive rationals `P>Q_i`, `z=(Q₁,…,Q_k)`, and `h∈R`, define `G⁺={x∈Z^k:h−P<⟨x,z⟩<h}` and `G⁻={x∈Z^k:h<⟨x,z⟩<h+P}`. The problem asks for a periodic zero-sum function, constant on equal-`⟨x,z⟩` levels, positive on `G⁺`, negative on `G⁻`, and zero elsewhere. **Theorem 2** proves such a function exists under the strict additional inequality `2P>Q₁+⋯+Q_k`. Taking `P=n/p`, `Q_i=n/q_i` gives Theorem 1’s prime-sum condition.

## Proof dependencies and mechanism

Section 2 (physical pp. 4–6) writes coefficient vectors divisible by `Φ_n` as a vector space `V_n`; for squarefree `n=pq₁⋯q_k`, CRT identifies them with arrays whose generators are coordinate fibers. The orthogonal complement is the zero-sum-array space. Lemma 1 (p. 5) uses Farkas’ lemma to equate the conjectured minimality with a certificate in `V_n^⊥`, zero on the special index set `H` and positive on `F⁺`; Lemma 2 records a weaker lower-degree certificate.

Section 3 (physical pp. 7–13) constructs certificates in the illustrative cases `n=35` and `105` by decomposing layers and building zero-sum arrays. Section 4 (pp. 14–16) reduces the general certificate to Problem 1 by CRT and periodic projection. For Theorem 2, let each lattice point’s cell be its centered unit cube. The proof defines upper/lower extremal slab positions `α,β`; the strict inequality gives `β−α>0`. It treats the cases where the central lattice level `I={x∈Z^k:⟨x,z⟩=h}` is nonempty or empty. In each case, three parallel hyperplanes and the two equal-thickness slabs `R⁺,R⁻` are chosen so that the function
`f(x)=vol(cell(x)∩R⁺)−vol(cell(x)∩R⁻)`
has the required signs, support, and zero sums along every integer coordinate line. Rationality of `z` ensures the required positive separation/choice of a small `ε` in the empty-slice case. Corollary 1 then identifies Theorem 1. The paper explicitly says the general minimal-degree problem remains open outside proved cases; Theorem 2’s auxiliary Problem 1 is hard for `k≥3` and the paper lists parameters with no solution, without claiming these disprove Conjecture 1.

## W18/A175 use and limits

**Relevant cyclotomic positivity/degree constraint, not a factorization classification.** This gives a rigorous constraint on the degree of nonnegative-coefficient multiples of `Φ_n` under a precise prime-sum condition, and a geometric/Farkas certificate construction. It can inform root-of-unity divisibility analysis of integer mask polynomials. It does not classify arbitrary convolution factors and does not prove or refute A175’s arbitrary finite-support probability-factorization claim. Do not drop the strict inequality or present the conjecture as proved generally.
