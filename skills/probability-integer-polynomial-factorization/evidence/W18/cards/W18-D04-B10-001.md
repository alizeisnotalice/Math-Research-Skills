## Model and exact interfaces

Definition 2.1 (PDF p.4): a probability polynomial is (P(z)=\sum_{n=0}^d q_nz^n) with (q_n\ge0) and (P(1)=1). A positive factorization (Definition 2.2) is a product of nonconstant probability polynomials; “positive” here means nonnegative coefficients, not strictly positive coefficients. This is exactly the PGF representation of independent finite-support nonnegative-integer random variables. Deterministic monomial shifts are treated as trivial unless stated otherwise; the paper normally assumes (P(0)>0).

Definition 2.3 defines refinement by splitting one or more factors into positive probability-polynomial factors. The factorization poset is finite, but need not be a lattice: Example 2.4 and Proposition 2.5 (PDF pp.4–5) give two incomparable maximal atomizations of the uniform polynomial (1+z+\cdots+z^5):
\[
(1+z)(1+z^2+z^4)=(1+z+z^2)(1+z^3).
\]
The factors in this example happen to have 0–1 coefficients; this is an example, not a general theorem about factors of 0–1 polynomials.

## Theorem/proof cards

### Theorem 3.2 — entropy dominance and equality (PDF pp.6–7)

For a positive factorization (P=P_1\cdots P_m), let independent (X_j) have PGFs (P_j), and (X=\sum_jX_j). Then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m H(X_j)-H(X)=H(X_1,\ldots,X_m\mid X)\ge0.
\]
Equality holds iff the support addition map ((x_1,\ldots,x_m)\mapsto\sum_jx_j) is injective on (\prod_j\operatorname{supp}(X_j)). Proof: independence gives joint entropy (\sum H(X_j)); (X) is a deterministic function of the tuple, so the entropy chain rule gives the identity. Zero conditional entropy is equivalent to the tuple being determined by the sum. Splitting a factor is exactly such an independent sum, so factorization entropy is monotone under refinement. Iteration and finiteness imply at least one maximal positive atomization maximizes the factorization entropy; the maximizing factorization need not be unique and equality in the displayed inequality is a property of a particular factorization, not equivalent to global optimality (see §6, PDF pp.14–15).

### Proposition 4.1 / Corollary 4.2 — support obstruction (PDF p.8)

If (P=QR) with nonnegative coefficients, then \(\operatorname{supp}P=\operatorname{supp}Q+\operatorname{supp}R\), the Minkowski sum. Proof is coefficientwise: the coefficient at (z^n) is \(\sum_{i+j=n}q_ir_j\), a sum of nonnegative terms, and is positive exactly when at least one support pair adds to (n). Thus a support not admitting a nontrivial Minkowski decomposition forces positive irreducibility. In particular (a+bz^N) for (a,b>0,N\ge1) is a positive atom.

### Theorem 4.3 / Corollary 4.4 — real-rooted and Hurwitz-stable sectors (PDF p.9; quartic details pp.12–14)

For degree (N) probability (P) with positive constant and leading coefficients, real-rootedness, all roots negative, and being a Poisson-binomial PGF are equivalent; specifically
\[
P(z)=\prod_{j=1}^N(1-\nu_j+\nu_jz),\quad0<\nu_j<1,
\qquad S_{\rm fac}(P)=\sum_j[-\nu_j\log\nu_j-(1-\nu_j)\log(1-\nu_j)].
\]
Proof: nonnegative coefficients rule out positive real roots; normalize each negative-root linear factor at (z=1). The reverse implication is immediate. For a positive Hurwitz-stable polynomial, Corollary 4.4 states every positive atom has degree one or two, hence entropy maximization can be restricted to factors of degree at most two. The proof groups real negative roots and nonreal conjugate pairs into positive linear/quadratic factors; positive quadratics either split into two positive linears or are atoms.

For a strictly positive quartic (P=\sum_{i=0}^4p_iz^i), the quartic Routh–Hurwitz conditions are (p_3p_2>p_4p_1) and (p_3p_2p_1>p_4p_1^2+p_3^2p_0); with positive coefficients the second implies the first. Under normalized Lebesgue/Dirichlet(1,…,1) measure on the probability simplex, Proposition 5.3 proves the Hurwitz-stable region has volume \(\nu_4=1/6\). The argument normalizes independent unit-rate exponentials (p_i=X_i/\sum X_j), integrates first over (X_2), then (X_0,X_4), and obtains
\[
\nu_4=\mathbb E\frac{X_1X_3}{(X_1+X_3)^2}=\mathbb E[U(1-U)]=1/6,
\quad U=X_1/(X_1+X_3)\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1].
\]
Since every such quartic is positively reducible, the full factorable-quartic volume is at least (1/6). The larger estimate \(\mu_4\simeq0.43\) is a Monte Carlo estimate only, not a theorem (Remark 5.4 and §7).

### Theorem 4.5 — local stability of a positive factorization (PDF p.10)

Fix ordered degrees (d_i>0) summing to (N), and let each (P_i) have strictly positive coefficients and satisfy (P_i(1)=1). If (P=P_1\cdots P_m) and the factors are pairwise coprime over \(\mathbb R[z]\), the differential of the multiplication map on the product of the corresponding open probability simplices is invertible. Therefore every sufficiently close degree-(N) probability polynomial has a unique nearby positive factorization with that same ordered degree profile.

Proof mechanism: tangent polynomials \(\dot P_i\) satisfy \(\deg\dot P_i\le d_i\), \(\dot P_i(1)=0\). A kernel vector obeys \(\sum_i\dot P_i\prod_{j\ne i}P_j=0\). Modulo (P_i), pairwise coprimality gives (P_i\mid\dot P_i); degree forces \(\dot P_i=c_iP_i\), and evaluation at 1 gives (c_i=0). Domain and target dimensions both equal (N), so the derivative is invertible; the inverse function theorem and strict positivity give the neighborhood statement. It is local and fails to address collisions/common roots or boundary coefficients.

### Exact low-degree criteria (PDF pp.11–13)

* Proposition 5.1 (degree exactly two): under the source's standing positive-endpoint assumption, \(P(z)=c+bz+az^2\) has \(a>0,\ c>0,\ b\ge0,\ a+b+c=1\). On this nondegenerate quadratic model, positive factorability iff \(b^2\ge4ac\), the real-rooted quadratic condition. Do not extend the criterion literally to boundary coefficient vectors such as \(a=b=0,c=1\): that is the constant polynomial, outside the degree-two/nonconstant-factor model, although the formal inequality reads \(0\ge0\). Under uniform simplex measure, the factorable fraction is \(\mu_2=1/3\): in \((a,b)\) coordinates with \(c=1-a-b\), the criterion region has area \(\int_0^1[(1-a)-2(\sqrt a-a)]\,da=1/6\), whereas the simplex area is \(1/2\).
* Proposition 5.2: for a strictly positive cubic (P=p_0+p_1z+p_2z^2+p_3z^3), positive factorability iff (p_1p_2\ge p_0p_3). Proof: a factorization into a linear and quadratic with nonnegative coefficients gives the inequality by coefficient multiplication. Conversely (g(s)=P(-s)) has (g(0)>0), while (g(p_2/p_3)=p_0-p_1p_2/p_3\le0); hence a root (-s), (0<s\le p_2/p_3), and division by (z+s) yields a quadratic quotient with nonnegative coefficients. After normalization this is a positive factorization. Symmetry of simplex coordinates gives factorable probability \(\mu_3=1/2\).
* Proposition 5.3: the quartic lower bound \(\mu_4\ge1/6\) and proof given above.

## A175 use and non-transfer

Direct reusable interface: encode a finite-support integer law by its normalized PGF; independence becomes ordinary polynomial multiplication. The support Minkowski identity, atom criterion, entropy identity, and exact low-degree tests are valid in that nonnegative-coefficient model. For A175, the uniform-on-support law corresponds (after normalization) to a 0–1 mask polynomial, but none of these results proves that arbitrary nonnegative-real factors of a 0–1 product have 0–1 coefficients, or that an arbitrary A175 factorization is real-rooted/Hurwitz-stable. The degree-two/three criteria apply only in their stated degrees; volume calculations and random sampling do not decide the unrestricted conjecture.
