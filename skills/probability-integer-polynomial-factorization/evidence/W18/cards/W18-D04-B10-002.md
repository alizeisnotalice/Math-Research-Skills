## Definitions and polynomial interface

The probability portion is §3 (PDF pp.22–44); §§1–2 on stochastic matrices/quantum channels were also read but are not needed for A175.

Definitions 42–43 (PDF p.25): (n)-decomposable means a sum of (n) independent nonconstant random variables, which need not be identically distributed. (n)-divisible means the summands are iid. For a finite law on the integers, shift the support so its minimum is 0. Its characteristic/PGF polynomial is (f_D(x)=\sum_i p_i x^i), normalized at 1; the paper works with nonnegative polynomials modulo positive scalar multiples.

Proposition 50 and Lemma 51 (PDF pp.27–28) state the exact interface:
\[
D\text{ is }n\text{-divisible}\iff f_D=g^n\text{ for some nonnegative polynomial }g;
\quad D\text{ is }n\text{-decomposable}\iff f_D=\prod_{i=1}^n g_i
\]
for nonnegative polynomials, with scaling normalized at (x=1). The proof uses coefficient convolution/PGF multiplication and the iid versus non-iid distinction. This is finite-support polynomial factorization; it does not assert that factors of a 0–1 polynomial are themselves 0–1.

## Main computational results

### Theorem 54 — source claim: finite-law iid divisibility is in P (PDF pp.28–29)

The paper states that deciding (n)-divisibility is in P. By Lemma 51 it seeks (g\ge0) with (g^n=f). Its proof expands the formal (n)-th root as a Taylor series with a remainder and tests whether the remainder vanishes; for rational coefficients it alternatively proposes polynomial factorization (e.g. LLL), recombining linear factors and checking coefficient signs. The result is for **iid repeated-factor divisibility**, not arbitrary decomposition into possibly different factors.

Representation caveat: the article presents finite distribution coefficients as real numbers but the usual Turing-machine meaning of polynomial time requires an encoding and precision model. The rational-coefficient route supplies a conventional exact-input setting; do not silently read the stated complexity as a bit-complexity theorem for arbitrary real inputs.

Lemma 58 / Theorem 60 (PDF pp.29–30): the coefficient-vector ℓ∞ weak-membership variant of divisibility is in P. The proof pads degree to a multiple of (n), compares coefficients of (g^n) with an ε interval, and recursively maintains allowed intervals for successive nonnegative coefficients of (g); an empty interval returns NO. The separate weak formulation uses distance in the paper’s coefficient ℓ∞ norm, not total variation unless a conversion is supplied.

### Lemmas 68, 70 and Theorem 77 — general decomposability hardness (PDF pp.33–40)

Lemma 68 places the paper’s discrete decomposability decision problems in NP via factor-table witnesses, coefficient convolution, and polynomial-time verification in its encoding model. Lemma 70 claims NP-hardness even when the two factors have equal support cardinality. Theorem 77 deduces NP-hardness of unrestricted two-factor finite-distribution decomposability.

Reduction proof as presented:

1. Start from Even Subset Sum: (|S|=N) even; is there (|T|=N/2) with \(|\sum_{t\in T}t-\sum_{s\in S\setminus T}s|<\ell\)? Appendix Lemma 92 (pp.45–46) pads zeros to reduce Subset Sum to this variant. Lemma 93 supplies elementwise scaling/translation invariance.
2. The main text maps (s_i) to small real (b_i), defines (g(b_i,x)=x^2+b_ix+1), and (f_B=\prod_i g(b_i,x)). For sufficiently small (|b_i|), each quadratic is irreducible over ℝ[x]. Therefore unique factorization implies every polynomial factor is a product over a subset of these irreducible quadratics.
3. For a subset (T\subset B), (f_T=\prod_{t\in T}(x^2+tx+1)=\sum_jc_jx^j). The proof expands coefficients by degree in the small scale δ; the leading terms of each even coefficient are positive, while the leading term of each odd coefficient has the sign of the subset sum. It bounds higher-order terms by coefficients of ((x^2+\delta x+1)^{|T|}), showing those terms are dominated for sufficiently small δ. This sign criterion is intended to make a factorization into equal-degree halves equivalent to a balanced positive/negative subset split.
4. Appendix Lemmas 96–97 (pp.46–47) connect a special Subset Sum threshold to Partition, including an ε-perturbed variant; Theorem 77 then reduces that hard instance to general distribution decomposition.

**Two source-print discrepancies on the central reduction page (physical p.35).** They are preserved as printed and isolated from the mathematical inference:

* In the physical-p.35 displayed affine map (7), the Even Subset Sum input was introduced on p.34 as \((S,\ell)\), but the formula uses \(S'\) in the centering average and denominator; \(S'\) is not defined in this local reduction. Preserve that source notation gap. For the factor-two diagnosis below only, take the natural intended identification \(S'=S\). Then \(b_i=a(s_i-\frac1{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}s)+\frac{a\ell}{2|S|}\), so \(\sum_i b_i=a\ell/2\). The page nevertheless asserts \(\mathrm{EvenSubsetSum}(S,\ell)=\mathrm{EvenSubsetSum}(B,a\ell)\) and says YES is equivalent to a balanced partition with both half-sums positive. If the total is \(T=a\ell/2>0\), both halves are positive exactly when their absolute difference is less than \(T\), not \(a\ell\); this is the factor-two mismatch under \(S'=S\). For example, with \(S=\{0,1\}\), \(\ell=3/2\), \(a=1/10\), this natural reading gives \(B=\{-1/80,7/80\}\), total \(3/40\). The original instance is YES and the claimed rescaled difference is \(1/10<3/20=a\ell\), but one singleton half-sum is negative. Replacing the displayed additive shift by \(a\ell/|S|\) would repair the total under this interpretation; that is a derived repair, not the source formula.
* On the same page, for \(T\subset B\) with \(n:=|T|\) and \(\Sigma:=\sum_{t\in T}t\), the source prints \(c_1=n\Sigma\) for the coefficient of \(x\) in \(f_T=\prod_{t\in T}(x^2+tx+1)\). Direct expansion gives \(c_1=\Sigma\). The p.37 first-order expression \(c_{j,1}=\binom{n-1}{(j-1)/2}\Sigma\) confirms this at \(j=1\). Thus the printed identity has an extra factor \(n\); its sign conclusion remains unchanged for \(n>0\). The saved finite check tests this identity for one concrete three-element set; it does not replace the general coefficient calculation.

These detected factor/sign-display issues mean the reduction is not copied as a clean certificate without repair. The polynomial correspondence and the paper’s theorem statements remain useful, but the source reduction should be checked/normalized before relying on its detailed proof as an A175 result. The problem class is the full nonnegative-factor class, not the special 0–1 target subclass.

## A175 boundary

Direct use: translate independent finite-support integer sums into multiplication of nonnegative PGFs and keep distinct-factor decomposability separate from iid divisibility. This helps formulate exact finite searches and prevents confusing “(f=g^n)” with “(f=gh)”. The article does not prove a structural theorem for 0–1 products and does not imply that arbitrary real factors of a 0–1 polynomial are Boolean masks. Its complexity results do not settle Green’s question about uniformity of the factors, especially with support gaps.
