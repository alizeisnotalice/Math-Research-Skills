# M0149 — Ghidelli, *Progress on the Unfair 0-1-Polynomials Conjecture Using Linear Recurrences and Numerical Analysis*

**Source/version.** Luca Ghidelli, arXiv:2209.09843v1 (2022), 46-page local PDF. Physical PDF p. 1 matches the title, author, and arXiv version. The complete physical text pp. 1–46 was read; local file and SHA-256 are in `sources.json`.

## Exact result and proof route

- **Theorem 1.2 (physical pp. 5–6).** Let P,Q in R[x], R=PQ, every coefficient of R belong to {0,1}, and R(0)=1. If P(x)=x^5+a x^2+1 with 0<a<1, then Q has a negative coefficient. Equivalently, no nonnegative-coefficient cofactor Q gives a 0–1 product in this family. The stronger first-zero form in **Theorem 1.3** says: if N=min{n>=4:c_n=0}, or N=infinity if no such zero exists, some b_n<0 for n<=N+8.
- **Recurrence.** Writing Q=sum b_n x^n and R=sum c_n x^n gives c_n=b_n+a b_(n-2)+b_(n-5), with negative-index coefficients zero. The proof first forces the recurrence while product coefficients remain 1. In §2.2–2.3, a resultant computation checks the needed nonvanishing for 11<=n<=10000, so the paper reports c_n=1 through that finite range. This PARI/GP computation is author-reported, not independently rerun here; it is not a proof for all n.
- **Large-parameter branch.** For a>=0.005, the recurrence is represented using roots of the auxiliary polynomial P-tilde(x)=x^5+a x^3+1, not the factor P(x)=x^5+a x^2+1. Root-modulus and coefficient estimates force a negative coefficient after the first zero.
- **Small-parameter branch.** For 0<a<0.005, root-of-unity perturbation expansions and Taylor bounds control the recurrence near the first zero N and force b_(N+8)<0. The explicit error bounds and endpoint estimates occupy §§4–5.

## Scope and audit caveats

This is a theorem for one trinomial factor family, not the unrestricted unfair 0–1-polynomial conjecture or arbitrary integer-valued convolutions. The computation through 10,000 is finite and author-reported. There is one internal sign contradiction in the physical-p. 41 proof of Corollary 5.9: after the p. 40 derivative argument establishes that |alpha(t)| and |gamma(t)| decrease, the constant root-product identity |alpha(t)| |beta(t)|^2 |gamma(t)|^2=1 forces |beta(t)| to increase, as the corollary statement also says; one sentence on p. 41 instead says it decreases. The next sentence and subsequent estimates use the increasing direction. Isolate this proof-line issue for review; it does not by itself refute the theorem.
