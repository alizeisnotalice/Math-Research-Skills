## Three 0–1 polynomial results

- **First question (physical pp. 2–6; printed pp. 357–361).** The recursively defined sequence starts with `f_0=1` and chooses the least `k_j>deg f_{j−1}` for which `f_j=x^{k_j}+f_{j−1}` is reducible. The authors establish that the sequence terminates at `f_7=1+x^3+x^{15}+x^{16}+x^{32}+x^{33}+x^{34}+x^{35}`. For `g=f_7`, they rule out reciprocal irreducible factors of `x^n+g` using the fixed polynomial `g\tilde g−x^{deg g}`, then bound non-reciprocal factors for `n≥83` using Lemma 2.1’s `w\tilde w=f\tilde f`, equal-term-count criterion and a smallest-exponent argument. They explicitly supplement this with a computational check for `1≤n≤82`; that finite computation is part of the article’s argument and is not a general irreducibility theorem beyond this sequence.
- **Theorem 3.1 (physical pp. 6–8; printed pp. 361–363).** If `S` is a set of positive integers such that `1+Σ_{e∈U}x^e` is reducible for every nonempty finite `U⊆S`, then `|S|≤2`. Equivalently, for any distinct positive integers `a,b,c`, at least one of the three trinomials `1+x^a+x^b`, `1+x^a+x^c`, `1+x^b+x^c` is irreducible over `Q`. Proof: Ljunggren–Tverberg implies each reducible trinomial has a cyclotomic factor; the sparse bound of Filaseta–Schinzel reduces its order to primes 2 and 3; a unit-circle relation `1+z_1+z_2=0` forces `{z_1,z_2}={ζ_3,ζ_3²}`; 3-adic valuations and parity of the 2-adic exponents then make three simultaneous trinomial relations impossible. These cited lemmas are used with their stated hypotheses.
- **Theorem 4.1 (physical pp. 8–14; printed pp. 363–369).** Let `(a_j)_{j≥0}` be a bounded sequence of nonnegative integers with `a_0≠0`. If infinitely many positive `j` satisfy `a_{4^j}/a_0` is **not** `4r^4` for any `r∈Q`, then there is an infinite set `S⊂Z_{>0}` such that every finite `T⊂S` gives `a_0+Σ_{t∈T}a_t x^t` irreducible over `Q`. In the proof, `b_j=a_{4^j}`; boundedness and the hypothesis yield one nonzero value `b` repeated infinitely often with `b/a_0` not four times a rational fourth power. The exponents are chosen recursively among those `4^k` with `b_k=b`. For every subset of the already chosen exponents, Schinzel’s Lemma 4.1 gives a threshold after which the next polynomial is either irreducible or has an irreducible reciprocal factor. The proof excludes such reciprocal factors for large exponents: they divide a fixed polynomial `g\tilde g−b²x^{deg g}`, so there are only finitely many non-cyclotomic candidates, and a fixed non-cyclotomic factor can divide at most one `bx^n+g` because differences divide `x^{n'−n}−1`. Cyclotomic factors are excluded by Lemma 4.6. Taking the maximum of finitely many thresholds lets every subset extension remain irreducible. This is an existence proof; the thresholds are not made effective here.

## Lemma 4.6: cyclotomic orders for 4-power exponents

**Statement (physical pp. 10–12; printed pp. 365–367).** Let `r≥2`, let each `c_j` be a nonzero integer, and let `0≤k'_1<⋯<k'_r`. If
`F(x)=c_0+Σ_{j=1}^r c_j x^{4^{k'_j}}`
has a cyclotomic factor `Φ_m(x)`, then `m | 2^{2k'_1+1}`. For `k'_1=0`, this says that any cyclotomic divisor index is 1 or 2. The statement is about integer coefficients and this exact **nested-power** exponent pattern, not arbitrary exponents divisible by four.

The proof first factors out the smallest power by setting `ξ=ζ_m^{4^{k'_1}}`, reducing to a polynomial whose nonconstant exponents are `1,4^{k'_2-k'_1},…,4^{k'_r-k'_1}`. Mann’s theorem on **minimal** vanishing sums of rational multiples of roots of unity first implies `m` squarefree. If `m` is even, substituting `x↦−x` reduces to an odd squarefree order. For odd `m`, summing the Galois conjugates over units modulo `m` gives
`0=c_0φ(m)+μ(m)Σ_{j=1}^r c_j`,
so `Σ_{j=1}^r c_j=−c_0 μ(m)φ(m)` (multiplication by `φ(m)`, not division). Averaging instead over the subgroup of square units and using that its size is `φ(m)/2^ℓ` gives the algebraic integer `μ(m)/2^ℓ`, impossible for `ℓ≥1`. Thus odd `m` must be 1; the even case reduces to `m=2`. This proof chain is visible on rendered pp. 10–12. The authors note (p. 14) the consequence that a 0–1 polynomial with at least three terms and all exponents powers of 4 has no cyclotomic factor; this consequence is still only for that structured exponent class.

## A175 interface and limitation

The article gives exact irreducibility and cyclotomic-obstruction methods for special integer 0–1 polynomials. Theorem 4.1 constructs a structured family of rationally irreducible masks; Theorem 3.1 applies only when **every** nonempty finite subset of one exponent set gives a reducible mask. Neither condition is present in A175. More importantly, irreducibility over `Q` does not rule out a factorization over `R` with nonnegative coefficients. A175’s probability factors may have arbitrary real coefficients, so none of these results proves factor integrality/rationality or resolves the uniform-convolution question.
