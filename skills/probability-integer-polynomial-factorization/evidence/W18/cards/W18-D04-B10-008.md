## Definition, theorem statements, and proof chain

All groups are finite and nontrivial unless a theorem includes the trivial base case. For an ordered tuple of subsets `(H_1,…,H_k)` of G, the multiplication map sends `(h_1,…,h_k)` to `h_1⋯h_k`. It is a **uniform factorization** if every `g∈G` has the same number t of preimages; t is its multiplicity. If every H_i is a subgroup, it is a uniform group factorization; if each is cyclic, it is a uniform cyclic group factorization. Order matters for tuples of length at least three.

- **Sampling consequence (Introduction, physical pp. 1–2).** If each independent `h_i` is uniform on H_i and every group element has exactly t representations, then `P(h_1⋯h_k=g)=t/∏|H_i|=1/|G|`, since counting all tuples gives `t|G|=∏|H_i|`. Thus the product is exactly uniform; uniqueness is unnecessary. This consequence uses the finite group operation and subgroup-supported uniform inputs.
- **Lemma 2.3 (physical pp. 3–4).** If G has uniform group factorization `(H_1,…,H_k)` of multiplicity t and each H_i has a uniform cyclic group factorization of multiplicity t_i, refining each subgroup factor gives a uniform cyclic group factorization of G with multiplicity `t∏t_i`. Proof partitions representations by the original factor tuple; for each tuple the refined multiplicity is `∏t_i`.
- **Lemma 2.5 (physical p. 4).** For a homomorphism `f:G→G′`, with `t_i=|ker(f)∩H_i|`, if `(f(H_1),…,f(H_k))` uniformly factors G′ with multiplicity t′, then `(H_1,…,H_k,ker f)` uniformly factors G with multiplicity `t′∏t_i`. Proof maps preimages to quotient representations and each fiber has size `∏t_i`.
- **Proposition 2.6 (physical p. 4).** A cyclic group G has a proper uniform group factorization iff `|G|` is not a prime power; in that case one exists with cyclic subgroup factors and multiplicity one. The prime-power obstruction uses a generator that cannot lie in a product of proper subgroups; the construction factors the order into coprime integers and applies the Chinese remainder theorem.
- **Theorem 3.3 (physical p. 5).** Every finite solvable group admits a uniform cyclic group factorization. A subnormal series with abelian factors is used; each abelian quotient is a direct product of cyclic groups, cyclic quotient factors are lifted to cyclic subgroups, and Lemma 3.1 lifts uniform group factorizations. Lemma 2.3 then refines recursively along the series.
- **Theorem 3.4 (physical pp. 5–6).** For each n, these are equivalent: all groups of order at most n admit uniform cyclic group factorizations; all non-prime-power-cyclic groups up to n admit proper uniform group factorizations; all non-prime-power-cyclic simple groups up to n admit proper uniform group factorizations. Proof is induction on n, using the proper factorization for a simple group, quotient lifting through maximal normal subgroups, and refinement by Lemma 2.3. This is a reduction theorem; it does not assert the open universal simple-group premise.

## A175 transfer boundary

The group theorem is a useful exact-uniform sampling construction for finite groups. However, a group product computes modulo the group law: for cyclic `C_M`, exponent sums wrap modulo M. It does not itself give a uniform ordinary integer convolution or a product identity in `Z[x]`. Moreover, the factors here are uniform subgroup-set measures (0–1 indicators before normalization), not arbitrary nonnegative real PGF factors. A transfer requires a no-wrap support embedding and an exact measure/normalization correspondence.

## Exact boundary calculation

`math/b10_exact_checks.py` checks `C_6` with subgroups `{0,3}` and `{0,2,4}`: each residue has one modular representation, and the ordinary sums are also distinct in this selected example. It contrasts two copies of all of `C_3`: every residue has multiplicity 3 modulo 3, whereas ordinary integer-sum coefficients are `[1,2,3,2,1]`. This exact finite example demonstrates that uniform modular multiplicity alone does not establish uniform no-wrap convolution.
