## Definitions and exact theorem scope

Translate a finite `A⊂Z` to assume `A⊂Z_{≥0}` and write its mask polynomial `A(x)=Σ_{a∈A}x^a`. Let `S_A` be the set of prime powers `s` for which `Φ_s(x)|A(x)`. The source's conditions are

- `(T1)  A(1)=∏_{s∈S_A} Φ_s(1)`.
- `(T2)  If s₁,…,s_m∈S_A are powers of distinct primes, then Φ_{s₁⋯s_m}(x)|A(x)`.

**Theorem A (physical p. 2, stated again p. 4).** `(T1)+(T2)` are sufficient for `A` to tile `Z` by translates, for every finite `A`.

**Theorems B1 and B2 (physical p. 2; detailed statements p. 5–6).** If `A` tiles `Z`, then `(T1)` holds. If `A` tiles `Z` and `#A` has at most two prime factors (source wording), then `(T2)` also holds. Consequently, under that cardinality restriction, tiling is equivalent to `(T1)+(T2)`. The article explicitly says it is unknown whether `(T2)` is necessary for every finite tile; this work does not settle the general Coven–Meyerowitz necessity question.

## Proof chain

§1 (physical pp. 3–4) supplies the cyclotomic identities, periodicity of finite-set tilings, and Lemma 1.3’s equivalences between a tiling, a complete set of residues in a period, a mask-polynomial congruence modulo `x^n−1`, and complementary cyclotomic divisibility. Lemma 1.4 reduces a common gcd of support coordinates while tracking `S_A`, `(T1)`, and `(T2)`.

For Theorem A (physical pp. 4–5), the authors construct a periodic complement whose mask polynomial meets the cyclotomic-divisor criterion in Lemma 1.3; `(T1)` controls the count of factors and `(T2)` excludes the required mixed-prime factors from the wrong side. For B1, a period-`n` tiling and the partition of nontrivial divisors of `x^n−1` between the two masks yield the product identity at `x=1` (physical p. 5). For B2 (physical pp. 6–7), periodicity and reductions (Lemmas 2.2–2.4, including Sands’ prime-divisor alternative for periods with at most two prime factors) arrange a complement inside `kZ`. Lemma 2.5 decomposes the tile into residue-class fibers and reduces to smaller tiles; induction and the cyclotomic identity in Lemma 1.1(7) give `(T2)`. The proof depends on the stated small-prime-factor hypothesis.

§3 (physical pp. 8–10) provides alternate proofs of Tijdeman’s and Sands’ results. §4 (physical pp. 10–11) develops a recursive structure description in the at-most-two-prime-factor case and includes examples; the references finish on p. 12.

## W18/A175 use and limitations

**Relevant structural bridge, not a probability-factor theorem.** The mask-polynomial/cyclotomic formulation connects unique additive tilings by finite sets to polynomial divisibility, and can organize special 0–1 support cases of integer convolution. It does not prove that arbitrary nonnegative-real factors of a uniform finite-support probability generating function are 0–1 or integer, and its `(T2)` necessity conclusion is restricted as above. Do not use Theorem A as a general necessary-and-sufficient test for all finite tiles.
