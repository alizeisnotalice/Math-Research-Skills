## Models and exact claims

For a die with outcomes in `{0,…,n−1}`, Behrends encodes its law by a probability polynomial `P(x)=Σ_{j=0}^{n−1} p_j x^j`, `p_j≥0`, `P(1)=1`; independence turns addition into multiplication. The target in Problem 1 is uniform on the **whole interval** `{0,…,n+m−2}`. After scaling by endpoint coefficients, its polynomial equation is `A(x)B(x)=1+x+…+x^{n+m−2}`, where `deg A=n−1`, `deg B=m−1`, leading coefficients 1, and all coefficients are nonnegative. Internal zero coefficients are allowed, so factor supports may have gaps; the model still fixes an interval target and factor degrees/endpoints.

Lemma 2.1 (printed pp. 18–19; PDF physical pp. 4–5) proves two statements. For either the geometric target or its square, nonnegative real factors with the indicated degrees have palindromic coefficients. For the unsquared target, every factor coefficient is in `{0,1}`. The proof first identifies all roots of each factor as nontrivial roots of unity; real coefficients pair conjugate roots, and on the unit circle conjugation is inversion, forcing reciprocal symmetry. For the `{0,1}` conclusion, product coefficients give `0≤a_j,b_j≤1`; if the first strictly fractional coefficient is `a_{j0}`, the coefficient equations inductively force `b_{j0}` strictly between 0 and 1, while the top-degree coefficient and palindromicity imply `1 ≥ b_{m−1}+b_{m−1−j0}a_{j0}=1+b_{j0}a_{j0}>1`, a contradiction.

Thus Problem 1 becomes a unique additive representation: if `Π_A={j:a_j=1}` and `Π_B={j:b_j=1}`, every integer in `{0,…,n+m−2}` has exactly one representation `u+v`, `u∈Π_A`, `v∈Π_B`.

Theorem 3.1 (printed pp. 20–23; PDF physical pp. 6–9) classifies all such **canonical-order** `s`-factor families. Write `k₀+1=g₁⋯g_t` with each `g_i≥2`. For the mixed-radix blocks
`Π_τ=(g_{τ−1}⋯g₁){0,…,g_τ−1}` and `k_τ=(g_τ−1)g_{τ−1}⋯g₁`, choose a partition `Δ₁,…,Δ_s` of `{1,…,t}` into nonempty parts, with no two adjacent indices in any one part and increasing least indices. Then `Π_σ=Σ_{τ∈Δσ}Π_τ`, `k_σ=Σ_{τ∈Δσ}k_τ` is a family giving unique sums of `{0,…,k₀}`; every canonical-order family is obtained uniquely this way. Proof: recursively take the longest initial interval `{0,…,r}` in the first factor; Lemma 3.2 forces `r+1 | k₀+1` and the other factor to be the corresponding arithmetic progression. Lemma 3.3 shows all new block starts and the complementary factor are divisible by `r+1`; divide by it and apply induction. The reverse recursion recovers the unique mixed-radix list and partition. For two factors, Corollary 3.4 gives the alternating recursion explicitly.

Problem 2 fixes two fair `k`-sided dice as the target: `A(x)B(x)=(1+x+…+x^{k−1})²`, with both factor degrees `k−1`, nonnegative real coefficients, and excludes the trivial factors. Theorem 4.2 (printed pp. 26–27; PDF physical pp. 12–13) says a nontrivial equal-degree factorization exists exactly for `k∉{1,…,9,11}` (equivalently `k=10` or `k≥12`). For `k≥12`, it selects adjacent conjugate root-pairs, transfers one quadratic cyclotomic factor between squared factors, and uses Lemma 4.1’s coefficient recurrence/bound `|c_j|≤φ(η)` to ensure the perturbed coefficients stay nonnegative; the criterion is `|φ(η)|,|φ(η′)|≤1/|η−η′|`. It handles `k=12,…,15` directly and `k≥16` by a cosine mean-value estimate. `k=10` has a separate explicit root-pair choice. For excluded `k`, factors of the target must be allocated among the two degree-`k−1` factors; the paper illustrates the obstruction at `k=8` and says the finite cases are checked (with a computer as a reliability check). Do not interpret the recurrence criterion alone as the whole necessity proof.

Theorem 4.2 does **not** classify unequal factor degrees `n,m`, `n+m=2k`; the paper reports computational examples there but says no general proofs are known. Section 5 further states that for each number `r` of dice there is `K_r≤(r−1)16` such that for every `k≥K_r`, a sum of `r` identically sided, strictly dishonest dice can simulate the sum of `r` fair `k`-sided dice; the finitely many smaller `k` are checked separately. This is not a classification of arbitrary probability-polynomial factors.

## A175 use and gap

This is the batch’s most direct source for a **restricted** A175 subproblem: nonnegative real probability polynomials whose product is uniform on a full integer interval; Problem 1 even allows factor supports with internal gaps, and Lemma 2.1 forces their rescaled coefficients to be binary. It supplies a concrete algebraic route from convolution to unique sumsets and a complete mixed-radix classification in that interval-target case. It does not settle arbitrary finite target support with gaps, nor arbitrary factor degrees for the square target. A175 allows both summands and the sum to have general finite supports; replacing that by `{0,…,n−1}`, `{0,…,m−1}`, or a full interval target would be an extra hypothesis.

Two source-level cautions: (i) the displayed range in Problem 2 on **printed p. 16** (PDF physical p. 2) says `j=0,…,2n−2` despite the immediately preceding condition `n+m=2k` and the target square of a `k`-interval. Treat the consistent target equation and Theorem 4.2 as governing; flag the range as a likely typographical error, rather than silently using it. (ii) The square-target proof’s use of complex roots does not transfer to arbitrary target polynomials without suitable root structure.
