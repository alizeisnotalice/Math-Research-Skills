## Exact setting and theorem

The source works on a **finite cyclic group** `Z_M`, with addition modulo M. For `f:Z_M→C`, the mask polynomial is `F(X)=Σ_{z∈Z_M} f(z)X^z`, understood as a cyclic representative modulo `X^M−1`; the unnormalized Fourier transform is `f̂(ξ)=Σ_z f(z)e^(−2πizξ/M)`. A step class is `R_m={z∈Z_M:gcd(z,M)=m}` for `m|M`, and step functions are constant on each such class. Functional pd-tiling assumptions include nonnegativity, normalization at zero, the convolution identity, and positive-definiteness as defined in the article; Theorem 1.6's pair has the stated functional-pd property.

- **Theorem 1.6 (physical p. 7).** For primes `p<q<p²`, with `M=p^4q²`, there is a pair of step functions `(f,g)` on `Z_M` forming a functional pd-tiling, whose mask polynomials both satisfy **period-relative** (T1) and both fail period-relative (T2). Here, as defined on physical p. 3 for functions on `Z_M`, the prime-power signature only ranges over `p^a|M`; this is not a claim of ordinary T1/T2 for every cyclotomic divisor of the polynomial representatives. The authors explicitly state the examples cannot both originate from complementary sets; they leave open whether they can help build a proper set-tiling counterexample.
- **Construction and proof (physical pp. 15–17).** The authors set `f=Σ c_m 1_{R_m}`, `g=Σ ν_m 1_{R_m}`. The table rows are indexed by `m=M/p^α` (`α=0,…,4`) and columns by `m=M/q^β` (`β=0,1,2`); entry `(M/p^α,M/q^β)` is the value on `R_{M/(p^αq^β)}`. With `d=2pq−p²−q`, nonzero f entries are `(α,β)=(0,0):1`, `(0,2):(q−p)/φ(q²)`, `(1,1):1/φ(q)`, `(1,2):(q−p)/φ(q²)`, `(2,0):(q²−pq+p²−q)/(pφ(q²))`, `(2,1):(p²−q)/(pφ(q²))`, `(3,0):(q−p)/(pφ(q))`, `(4,2):1/(p²φ(q))`. Nonzero g entries are `(0,0):1`, `(0,1):p(q−p)/d`, `(1,0):qφ(p)/d`, `(2,2):1/d`, `(3,1):q/(pd)`, `(3,2):φ(p)/(pd)`, `(4,0)=(4,1):(q−p)/(pd)`; all other table entries are zero. The condition `p<q<p²` gives nonnegativity.
- Using the explicit Fourier transform formula for step classes, the source verifies both functions are Fourier eigenfunctions with eigenvalue `√M=p²q`; their supports intersect only at the identity. Thus `f̂(k)ĝ(k)=M·1_{k=0}`, equivalent to cyclic convolution `f*g=1_{Z_M}`. At zero, both transforms equal `p²q`, giving the period-relative (T1) mass. The period-relative prime-power signatures are `{p,p^4,q}` for F and `{p²,p³,q²}` for G; each has `Φ_s(1)` product `p·p·q=p²q`. Positive mixed-order coefficients `c_{M/(pq)}>0` and `ν_{M/(p²q²)}>0` show `Φ_{pq}∤F` and `Φ_{p²q²}∤G`, so each violates period-relative (T2).
- **Exact instance checked.** For `(p,q)=(3,5)`, `M=2025`, `d=16`; all rational table entries are nonnegative. `math/b10_exact_checks.py` checks the exact Ramanujan-sum DFT identity at all 2,025 frequencies, support intersection, T1 mass, and the two positive mixed-order witnesses. This calculation corroborates that finite instance; it is not the general proof.

## A175 transfer limit

This is a sharp counterexample to extending set-tiling T1/T2 behavior to the paper's wider **finite cyclic functional-pd** class. It is not an ordinary independent-sum factorization on `Z` without wraparound. A transfer requires an explicit no-wrap lift and a match between the source's cyclic PD/convolution hypotheses and the target probability-polynomial conditions. It does not resolve the 0–1 factor question.
