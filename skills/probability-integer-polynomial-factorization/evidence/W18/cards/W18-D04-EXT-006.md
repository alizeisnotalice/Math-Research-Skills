## Results and proof mechanisms

- **Theorem 1.1 / Smyth (physical p. 2).** For `P∈Z[x]` not self-reciprocal with `P(0)P(1)≠0`, `M(P)≥θ_0`, where `θ_0` is the real root of `x^3−x−1`. This is a cited known theorem, not proved here. The paper also records Schinzel's bound for monic integer polynomials all of whose roots are real and with `P(0)P(1)P(−1)≠0`.
- Write `L(P)=Σ_j|a_j|`, and let roots be counted with multiplicity. **Proposition 3.1 (physical pp. 7–8)** assumes `P∈Z[x]` is monic, self-reciprocal of degree `2n`, and does not vanish at any root of unity. If `P(μ)=0` and `ω^m=1` or `ω^m=−1`, `m∈N*`, then for `μ∈R` or `|μ|=1`, `|μ−ω|≥m^{-1}2^{1−n}M(P)^{-m/2}`; for `μ∉R` and `|μ|≠1`, `|μ−ω|≥m^{-1}2^{1−n/2}M(P)^{-m/4}`. The no-root-of-unity hypothesis makes the proof's integer product `∏_k(1−μ_k^m)` nonzero. **Proposition 3.2 (physical pp. 8–9)**: if `P∈Z[x]` is monic, irreducible, self-reciprocal of degree `d`, and `M(P)>1`, then for `ω^m=±1`, for each `ε>0` and all sufficiently large `d`,
  `|ω−μ| ≥ exp(−(π√m/8+ε)√(d log d log M(P)))`;
  when `μ` is nonreal and `|μ|≠1`, the constant `π√m/8` improves to `π√m/16`.
- **Theorem 3.3 (physical p. 9).** For `P∈C[x]` degree `d≥1`, a zero `μ` of multiplicity `m_μ`, and `|ω|=1`,
  `|μ−ω| ≥ d^{-1}( e^{-1}|P(ω)|/L(P) )^{1/m_μ}`.
  If all coefficients are positive, take `ω=1` and `P(1)=L(P)` to get `|μ−1|≥d^{-1}e^{-1/m_μ}`. This bound comes from a Jensen estimate in the disk of radius `1/d` centered at `ω`; the rendered page confirms the exponent `1/m_μ` and the factor `e^{-1}`.
- **Theorem 4.1 (physical pp. 14–16; rendered pp. 14–15).** For monic `P∈C[x]`, degree `d≥2`, with `m≥1` real zeros and `P(0)P(1)P(−1)≠0`, the exact lower bound is
  `M(P) ≥ [ ( |P(1)P(−1)|^{1/m} + sqrt(4^{d/m}|P(0)|^{2/m}+|P(1)P(−1)|^{2/m}) ) / 2^{d/m} ]^{m/2}`.
  Equality occurs exactly for `P=(z−i)^{d1}(z+i)^{d2}(z−a)^{d3}(z+a)^{d4}(z−a^{-1})^{d5}(z+a^{-1})^{d6}`, `a>1`, with nonnegative integer exponents meeting the degree/root constraints. The positive-real-root variant assumes at least one positive root and `P(0)P(1)≠0`, with its corresponding formula and equality form `(z−a)^{d1}(z−a^{-1})^{d2}`, `a>1`. Proof forms `Q=P(0)^{-1}PP^*`, groups real roots outside `[-1,1]` and one representative from conjugate reciprocal pairs, and applies a concavity/product inequality; the equality characterization tracks equality in these steps.
- **Theorem 5.3 / Corollary 5.4 (physical pp. 17–18; rendered and formula-checked on both pages).** Let `c>1` solve `c log c=1+c` (`c≈3.594`). For monic `P∈C[x]` of degree `d` with `P(0)P(1)P(−1)≠0`, Theorem 5.3 (5.4) states
  `|P^{-1}(0)∩R| ≤ 2√c (d log(M(P)^2/|P(0)|))^{1/2} + (c+1)log(M(P)^2/|P(0)|) + (log c)^{-1}log(1+d^2) + (log c)^{-1}log( L(P)^2/(2|P(1)|^2) + L(P)^2/(2|P(−1)|^2) )`.
  The leading term is `2√c sqrt(d log(M(P)^2/|P(0)|))`; the logarithm is inside the square root, and the Mahler ratio uses `|P(0)|`. For integer `P∈Z[x]` with the same three nonvanishing conditions, Corollary 5.4 states
  `#(P^{-1}(0)∩R) ≤ 2√(2c d log M(P)) + 2(c+1)log M(P) + (log c)^{-1}log(1+d²) + 2(log c)^{-1}log L(P)`.
  If additionally `log L(P)≤c_1√(d log M(P))`, then for each `ε>0`, eventually this is at most `(c_2+ε)√(d log M(P))`, with `c_2=2(√(2c)+c+1+(log c)^{-1}c_1)`. The proof applies Theorem 5.3 to an endpoint-normalized polynomial and uses the Mahler/length inequalities; the growth condition on `L(P)` is an extra hypothesis, not automatic.
- **Theorem 6.4 and (6.2) (physical pp. 20–21; rendered both pages).** If `P∈Z[x]` is monic irreducible of degree `d`, `0<log M(P)≤φ(d)` and `φ(d)=o(d/log d)`, then for each `ε>0`, for all sufficiently large `d`,
  `#(P^{-1}(0)∩{|x−1|<1}) ≥ ( (2/π)log((1+√5)/2)−ε ) √( d/(log d log M(P)) )`.
  Consequently, with `K(P)=min{#(roots in |x−1|<1), #(roots in |x+1|<1)}`,
  `log M(P) ≥ ( (2/π)log((1+√5)/2)−ε )² d/(K(P)² log d)`.
  The proof on pp. 20–21 combines Lemma 6.1's small-value/height estimate, Theorem 6.3's root separation, and a count of roots in the two unit-centered disks; the asymptotic assumption `φ(d)=o(d/log d)` and irreducibility are used. This is an asymptotic theorem and does not cover arbitrary integer polynomials.

## A175 interface and limitation

The paper supplies zero-distance, real-root-count, and Mahler-measure bounds once a polynomial and its arithmetic hypotheses are specified. It does not show that arbitrary nonnegative real factors of a 0–1 polynomial are integer, monic, irreducible, self-reciprocal, or small-Mahler-measure; several theorems require endpoint nonvanishing, and Theorem 6.4 additionally requires irreducibility and a degree-asymptotic small-height condition. Use as auxiliary constraints only after those properties have independently been established.
