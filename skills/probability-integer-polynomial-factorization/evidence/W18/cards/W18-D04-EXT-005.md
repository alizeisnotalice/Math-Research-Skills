## Main statements and proof mechanisms

- **Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 2).** For any sequence `P_n∈Z_n^s(D,M)`—degree `n`, integer coefficients, simple zeros in the closed unit disk `D`, and leading coefficient bounded by fixed `M`—the normalized zero counting measures `τ_n=n^{-1}Σ_k δ_{α_{k,n}}` converge weak-* to normalized arclength on the unit circle. Thus the arithmetic mean of the zeros tends to zero. The theorem is presented as a consequence of the more general results in §2.
- Define the generalized Mahler measure for compact `E⊂C` with logarithmic capacity `cap(E)=1` by `M_E(P_n)=|a_n| exp(Σ_{α_{k,n}∈Ω_E}g_E(α_{k,n},∞))`, where `Ω_E` is the unbounded component of `C\E` and `g_E` its Green function with pole at infinity.
- **Theorem 2.1 (physical pp. 4–5).** Let `P_n∈Z[z]`, degree `n`, with simple zeros, and let `E` be compact with `cap(E)=1`. Then `(M_E(P_n))^{1/n}→1` iff all three hold: (i) `|a_n|^{1/n}→1`; (ii) `lim_{R→∞}lim_{n→∞}(∏_{|α_{k,n}|≥R}|α_{k,n}|)^{1/n}=1`; (iii) `τ_n⇒μ_E`, the equilibrium measure of `E`. For `E=D`, the paper's Remark allows condition (ii) to be replaced by existence of one `R>1` for which the displayed product to power `1/n` tends to 1. This is an iff characterization, not merely a sufficient condition.
- **Theorem 2.2 / Corollary 2.3 (physical pp. 5–6).** If `E` is compact with `cap(E)=1`, `P_n∈Z_n(E,M)`, and every irreducible integer factor appearing along the sequence has multiplicity `o(n)`, then `τ_n⇒μ_E`. For `E=D`, weak-* convergence also forces every fixed positive zero moment `n^{-1}Σ_k α_{k,n}^m→0`. The sublinear multiplicity condition is essential to the stated theorem; it is not a claim for arbitrary multiplicity.
- **Theorem 2.4 (physical pp. 5–6).** For regular compact `E` of capacity one and integer polynomials with simple zeros, `(‖P_n‖_E)^{1/n}→1` is equivalent to the generalized Mahler-measure limit and to the three conditions of Theorem 2.1. Regularity cannot be dropped: the paper gives `E=D∪{2}`, `P_n=z^n−1` as a counterexample to the analogous claim for irregular `E`.
- **Theorem 3.1 (physical pp. 7–8).** If `φ:C→R` is `A`-Lipschitz and supported in `|z|≤R`, and `P_n∈Z[z]` has degree `n`, simple roots, and nonzero leading coefficient, then for `n≥55`
  `|n^{-1}Σ_k φ(α_{k,n})−∫φ dμ_D| ≤ A(2R+1) sqrt(log(max(n,M(P_n)))/n)`.
  The proof invokes the potential-theoretic energy estimate of Theorem 4.2 and bounds the Dirichlet energy of the test function; Corollaries 3.2–3.3 specialize this to means and growth. For a real segment `[a,b]` of length 4, Theorem 3.4 gives the corresponding estimate with equilibrium measure `μ_[a,b]`, support centered at `(a+b)/2`, factor `A(3R+1)`, and `n≥25`.
- **Theorem 4.2 (physical pp. 14–16; rendered p. 15).** For a polynomial with simple zeros and a compactly supported continuous `φ` with finite Dirichlet integral `D[φ]`, smoothing each root to uniform measure on a radius-`r` circle gives
  `|∫φdτ_n−∫φdμ_D| ≤ ω_φ(r) + sqrt(D[φ]/(2π)) ( (2/n)log M(P_n) − (1/n²)log|a_n²Δ(P_n)| − (1/n)log r + 4r )^{1/2}`.
  The mechanism is: compare point masses with circle averages (modulus of continuity), express the potential of each circle as `−log max(r,|z−α|)`, apply Green's identity to the zero-mass signed measure, then use Cauchy–Schwarz and an energy bound involving Mahler measure, discriminant, `r`, and `4r`. The exact displayed inequality is image-checked on the source page; no discriminant estimate is claimed when zeros are not simple.
- **Theorem 4.3 (physical pp. 18–20).** For compact capacity-one `E` bounded by finitely many piecewise smooth curves/arcs, the analogous discrepancy inequality uses `M_E(P_n)` and a Green-function boundary error near `E`; the theorem assumes continuous compactly supported `φ` with finite Dirichlet integral. The proof is explicitly sketched and cites a potential-theory boundary-jump formula; it is not a self-contained derivation of every auxiliary boundary result.

## A175 interface and limitation

This paper controls zero distributions and asymptotic heights/norms for specified **integer** polynomial sequences. If a finite-support probability problem is already encoded by a specified integer polynomial sequence, the equidistribution/height statements can provide a conditional asymptotic obstruction. A175 assumes arbitrary finite supports with gaps and nonnegative real factors; no integer-polynomial sequence, bounded height, simple-root condition, capacity-one set, or asymptotic degree family is given. None of the results forces hypothetical factors to be integer, cyclotomic, or uniform. This is auxiliary analytic context only.
