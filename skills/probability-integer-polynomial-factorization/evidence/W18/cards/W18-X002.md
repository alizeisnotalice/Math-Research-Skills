# W18-X002 — Hasanbegović, *Structural Results on the Unfair 0–1 Polynomial Conjecture*

**Source.** Emina Hasanbegović, arXiv:2611.14596v1, submitted 2026-11-10; the PDF title page is dated 2026-11-13. The D02 full PDF has nine physical pages, and title, author, version, and abstract match the official arXiv record. Its SHA-256 and path are recorded in `sources.json`.

## Definitions, normalization caveat, and exact theorem

A polynomial is 0–1 if all its coefficients lie in \(\{0,1\}\). On physical PDF pp. 1–2, the paper defines a factorization \(f=gh\) using nonnegative-real-coefficient factors and says fairness requires both factors to be 0–1; it does not state a monic-factor or reciprocal-rescaling convention. Physical p. 2 then asserts the raw coefficients of \(g,h\) lie in \([0,1]\), which is false without normalization. For example,
\[
1+x+x^2+x^3=(2+2x)(1/2+x^2/2).
\]
The factors on the right are nonnegative but have coefficients outside \([0,1]\). Rescaling them reciprocally gives the monic factors \((1+x)(1+x^2)\), whose coefficients are 0–1. Thus this source's coefficient lemmas and fairness theorem can be transferred to A175 only in the standard **monic-factor model** (equivalently, after positive reciprocal rescaling of each nonzero factor to make both factors monic). In that model, nonnegativity and the leading coefficient 1 of \(f\) imply each factor coefficient is at most 1 by comparing the product coefficient at the opposite factor's leading degree; if \(f(0)=1\), the two constant coefficients are also 1. This normalization argument is a derivation for the intended model, not an assumption printed in the source. Do not apply the paper's literal raw-factor wording to arbitrary scalings.

For nonzero 0–1 polynomials \(v,w\), with \(v\) fair in the same monic-normalized model, the source states on physical PDF p. 5:
\[
 n\ge\max\{\deg(w)+2\deg(v),\,2\deg(w)\}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 f(x)=x^{n-\deg(w)}w(x)+v(x)\text{ is fair}.
\]
This is a sufficient condition for a separated two-block family, not a resolution of the unrestricted conjecture. The printed range includes the degenerate case \(v=w=1,n=0\), for which the displayed polynomial is \(f=2\), not 0–1. For A175, require \(n\ge1\), or explicitly verify that the two shifted supports are disjoint and that \(f\) itself is 0–1 before applying the result. The source's theorem and proof are otherwise kept conditional on its normalized-factor model; no claim is made for arbitrary raw-scaled factors.
## Proof mechanism

In the monic-normalized model, write a hypothetical nonnegative factorization as \(f=gh\), with coefficients \(b_i,c_i\in[0,1]\); physical p. 2 states this bound without stating the needed normalization. For a normalized 0–1 polynomial with constant term 1, the endpoint coefficients of both factors equal 1. **Lemma 1 (physical p. 3)** restricts nonzero factor coefficients to the support exponents of \(f\); **Lemma 2 (p. 3)** rules out a pair with one coefficient equal to 1 and the other positive; **Lemma 3 (p. 3–4)** says the first half-pair must be preceded by a double-pair. **Theorem 1 (p. 4)** then shows, in this normalized model, that a factorization with no positive-index double-pair is fair.

For Theorem 4, **Lemma 5 (physical pp. 6–7)** uses \(n\ge2\deg(w)\) to show one factor has no coefficients in the shifted high-degree block, so no double-pair can occur there. In the low block, the coefficient equations match those of \(v\), except possibly at the boundary \(n=\deg(w)+2\deg(v)\). Fairness of \(v\) excludes double-pairs below that boundary; Lemma 5 and the leading-coefficient constraints exclude the remaining boundary double-pair. Theorem 1 then implies fairness, subject to the normalization and nondegeneracy conditions above. The reciprocal-polynomial symmetry and the corollary for either block fair are given on physical p. 8.
