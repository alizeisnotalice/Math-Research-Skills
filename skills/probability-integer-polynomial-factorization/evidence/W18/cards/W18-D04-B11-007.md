## Model and notation

For m=∏_(i=1)^r p_i^(a_i), with distinct primes p_i, let G=⟨z⟩ be cyclic of order m and fix a primitive mth root ζ. The evaluation homomorphism φ:ZG→Z[ζ], φ(z)=ζ has kernel the integer group-ring relations among mth roots; vanishing sums with repetitions and nonnegative integer multiplicities are elements of NG∩ker(φ), where NG is the group semiring. Their weight is augmentation ε(x), and support size is ε_0(x). “Minimal” means not a sum of two nonzero elements of NG∩ker(φ), equivalently the represented positive vanishing sum has no proper nonempty vanishing subsum. The theorem is in characteristic zero and integer coefficients; it does not cover arbitrary nonnegative real coefficients.

## Theorem and proof cards

### Theorem 2.2 — integral kernel generators (PDF pp.4–5)

Let P_i be the unique subgroup of G of order p_i. The general ideal identity is
\[
\ker(\phi)=\sum_{i=1}^r ZG\,\sigma(P_i).
\]
Here \(\sigma(P_i)=\sum_{g\in P_i}g\), the prime-cycle relation. **Scope correction to the printed v1 clause:** the theorem display also prints \(\ker(\phi)=\mathbb Z\,\sigma(P_1)\) when r=1, and its proof on p.5 equates \(\mathbb ZG\sigma(P_1)\) with this scalar subgroup. For general \(G=C_{p^a}\), that scalar-only equality omits coset translates; the correct r=1 case of the general ideal identity is \(\mathbb ZG\sigma(P_1)\). The scalar formula holds on the squarefree core \(G_0=P_1\) of order p. In C_4, both 1+z² and z+z³ vanish at a primitive fourth root, but z+z³ is not a scalar multiple of 1+z². This is a scope/printing issue in the local preprint; the general ideal formula and Theorem 3.1's coset decomposition are used below. Proof of the ideal formula: induct on r. For one prime, Φ_(p^a)(z)=σ(P_1), so \(ZG\Phi_m(z)=ZG\sigma(P_1)\). For r≥2, split G=H×H′ of coprime orders, use integral bases of the free abelian rings ZH, ZH′ adapted to the kernels of their evaluation maps, and identify
\[
\ker(\phi)=\ker(\psi\otimes\psi')=
\ker(\psi)\otimes\mathbb ZH'+\mathbb ZH\otimes\ker(\psi').
\]
This proves generation of all integer linear relations by prime-cycle relations using addition, subtraction, and rotation. The paper immediately warns that this signed generation statement does not mean each nonnegative vanishing sum decomposes into positive prime cycles.

### Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 — reduction to squarefree order (PDF pp.6–7)

If G is cyclic of order m=∏p_i^(a_i), let G_0 be its unique subgroup of squarefree order ∏p_i. Theorem 3.1 decomposes any x∈NG∩ker(φ) into coset translates of elements of NG_0∩ker(φ). Corollary 3.2: if the vanishing sum is minimal, only one coset component occurs, so after a rotation all roots in it have squarefree order. Proof: write x=∑_j g_j z_j along cosets of G_0; positivity of x forces each z_j nonnegative, and the relation condition forces each z_j into the kernel. Minimality rules out two nonzero components.

### Theorem 3.3 — nonnegative-kernel description on the squarefree core (PDF pp.7–8)

On \(G_0\), the squarefree core from Theorem 3.1, for r=1,
\[
\mathbb NG_0\cap\ker(\phi|_{\mathbb ZG_0})=\mathbb N\,\sigma(P_1).
\]
For r=2,
\[
\mathbb NG_0\cap\ker(\phi|_{\mathbb ZG_0})=\mathbb NP_1\,\sigma(P_2)+\mathbb NP_2\,\sigma(P_1).
\]
Proof on G_0: write x in P_2-coset layers x_0+⋯+x_(p_2−1)g^(p_2−1), with x_i∈\mathbb NP_1. Linear disjointness of the prime cyclotomic fields forces all φ(x_i) equal. Pick a layer of minimum augmentation; the one-prime kernel description on P_1 shows every layer differs from it by a nonnegative integer multiple of σ(P_1), yielding the displayed positive decomposition. For general prime-power exponents, Theorem 3.1 restores the omitted cosets:
\[
\mathbb NG\cap\ker(\phi)=\sum_j g_j\bigl(\mathbb NG_0\cap\ker(\phi|_{\mathbb ZG_0})\bigr),
\]
where \(g_j\) run through representatives for G/G_0. Thus only the squarefree-core formulas are used directly; the printed Theorem 3.3 line is not read as a scalar/core-free formula for general G. Corollary 3.4 remains valid for general m with at most two distinct prime divisors because a minimal relation occupies one coset after Theorem 3.1 and hence rotates to the squarefree core.

### Theorem 4.8 — lower bound for asymmetric minimal relations (PDF pp.11–12)

For any minimal x∈NG∩ker(φ), either x is symmetric, meaning a translate of σ(P_i) and weight p_i, or r≥3 and
\[
\epsilon(x)\ge\epsilon_0(x)\ge p_1(p_2-1)+p_3-p_2>p_3,
\]
where p_1<p_2<p_3 are the three smallest distinct prime divisors of m. Proof chain:

1. Corollary 3.2 reduces to squarefree order.
2. Theorem 4.1 compares nonnegative x,y with equal φ-images when ε_0(x)≤p_1−1: either y≥x coefficientwise or ε_0(y)≥(p_1−ε_0(x))(p_2−1). Its proof is induction on the number of prime factors, splitting group-ring elements by the largest-prime subgroup; linear disjointness gives equal images of the coefficient layers, and the two cases yield the coordinatewise alternative or the lower support bound.
3. Apply that comparison to the prime layers of a minimal kernel element. If one layer vanishes, minimality reduces to fewer primes; if the small-layer alternative holds, positivity forces a symmetric prime cycle; otherwise the other alternative gives the displayed lower bound. The last strict inequality follows from p_1(p_2−1)+p_3−p_2−p_3=(p_1−1)(p_2−1)−1>0 for distinct primes p_1<p_2.

Corollary 4.9 gives the corresponding low-support ideal inclusion. The lower-bound proof is specific to nonnegative integer group-ring coefficients and support size; it is not a statement about arbitrary positive-real coefficient vectors.

### Main Theorem 5.2 — possible weights (PDF pp.12–13)

With N denoting nonnegative integers, for every m=∏p_i^(a_i),
\[
W(m)=\mathbb N p_1+\cdots+\mathbb N p_r.
\]
Here W(m) is the set of weights of vanishing sums of mth roots with repetitions allowed; 0 is included for the empty sum. Proof: decompose any nonnegative kernel element into minimal elements. Symmetric ones have weights p_i. For asymmetric ones, Theorem 4.8 gives
\[
\epsilon(x)\ge p_1(p_2-1)+p_3-p_2>(p_1-1)(p_2-1),
\]
so the elementary Frobenius bound (Lemma 5.1) puts the weight in \(\mathbb Np_1+\mathbb Np_2\), hence in the full semigroup. Conversely, each p_i is the weight of a prime cycle and disjoint multiset union adds weights.

This is a classification of which total weights can occur. It does not say that every concrete positive vanishing sum is a positive sum of translated prime cycles.

### The concrete failure of positive prime-cycle decomposition (PDF pp.5–6, 13)

Example 2.5 constructs, for distinct primes p,q,ℓ, an asymmetric minimal vanishing sum
\[
(\alpha+\cdots+\alpha^{p-1})(\beta+\cdots+\beta^{q-1})
+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{\ell-1}=0,
\]
where α,β,γ have orders p,q,ℓ. Minimality is proved by examining any vanishing subsum: if its γ-part is empty, linear disjointness of Q(α),Q(β) forces all terms empty; if nonempty, linear disjointness of Q(γ) from Q(α,β) forces the whole γ-cycle, then the whole αβ part. Its weight is (p−1)(q−1)+(ℓ−1), and it is not a prime cycle.

Remark 5.3 makes the obstruction explicit in G=C_30. With t,h,g the subgroups' generators of orders 2,3,5, respectively,
\[
x=t(h+h^2)+g+g^2+g^3+g^4\in NG\cap\ker(\phi),\qquad \epsilon(x)=6.
\]
There cannot be a representation x=z_1σ(P_1)+z_2σ(P_2)+z_3σ(P_3) with z_i∈ZG and ε(z_i)≥0. Taking augmentation gives 6=2ε(z_1)+3ε(z_2)+5ε(z_3); thus only the all-order-2 or all-order-3 alternatives are possible. The first would imply xt=x, the second xh=x, and both invariances fail. Therefore even the stronger signed-kernel-generator formulation with nonnegative augmentations fails; in particular, a decomposition into positive translated prime cycles is not guaranteed. The exact finite check in math/b11_exact_checks.run.json uses exponents {5,6,12,18,24,25}, verifies Φ_30 divisibility, and finds no proper nonempty vanishing submultiset.

Theorem 6.5 (PDF pp.14–15) further classifies the asymmetric minimal relations attaining the smallest possible support/weight \((p_1−1)(p_2−1)+(p_3−1)\): up to similarity, they are the above product-cycle construction \(x(G)=\sigma(P_1^*)\sigma(P_2^*)+\sigma(P_3^*)\). It is a uniqueness statement at the lower-bound equality case, not a classification of all minimal relations.

Theorem 7.1 (PDF p.17) applies the weight theorem to characteristic-zero finite-group representations: if χ(g) is an integer for an element of order m, then t=χ(1)+|χ(g)| belongs to the prime-divisor semigroup when χ(g)≤0; for χ(g)>0 and odd t, t is at least the least odd prime divisor of m. This application retains the stated integer-character and characteristic-zero hypotheses.

## Integer-coefficient and cyclic boundary

The core theorem concerns nonnegative integer multiplicities on the finite group of mth roots, equivalently NG∩ker(φ). It gives no classification for arbitrary nonnegative-real coefficients: the proof uses integer group rings, finite supports with integer augmentation, and decomposition into nonnegative integral minimal elements. Root evaluation \(P(\zeta_m)=0\) for P∈Z[X] is equivalent to \(\Phi_m\mid P\) in Z[X], but the theorem concerns the residue class in ZG and its integer coefficient vector. Passing from an ordinary PGF factorization to a group-ring element requires an identified modulus/order and integral coefficients. Cyclic reduction of exponents is modulo X^m−1; an ordinary polynomial convolution can be identified with the cyclic one only with a proved period/quotient relation or a no-wrap condition.

## A175 use and unresolved interface

Auxiliary lemma: once a finite integer-coefficient root-of-unity relation or exact cyclotomic divisibility condition is available, Theorem 5.2 restricts its possible total coefficient weight, and Theorem 4.8 rules out small-support asymmetric minimal relations. The A175 inputs, however, are arbitrary normalized nonnegative-real PGFs for finite-support integer-valued laws. No integerization, rationality, root-of-unity vanishing condition, or cyclic tiling representation is supplied by A175. Lam–Leung therefore does not show that an A175 factor has integer coefficients, 0–1 coefficients, or a prime-cycle decomposition.
