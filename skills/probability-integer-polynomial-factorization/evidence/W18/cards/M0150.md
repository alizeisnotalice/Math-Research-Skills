# M0150 — Hare, *Computational Progress on the Unfair 0–1 Polynomial Conjecture*

- **Read:** full 14-page arXiv v1 text, `arXiv:2307.07363v1` (2023-07-14). This is the read preprint; the published article appeared online in *Experimental Mathematics* in 2025, so do not conflate versions or pagination.
- **Exact setup (PDF pp. 1–2):** a 0–1 polynomial `C(x)` is monic. A candidate factorization is `C=A B`, where A and B are monic real polynomials with nonnegative coefficients. The conjecture asks whether both factors must have only 0–1 coefficients. The introduction gives the equivalent independent finite-support integer convolution formulation.
- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2):** a polynomial `a(x)` is a factor of an unfair 0–1 polynomial if and only if its reciprocal `a*(x)` is. This is a symmetry reduction, not a classification theorem.
- **Algorithmic proof route:**
  1. §2 (PDF pp. 3–4) encodes cofactor coefficients as `0`, `1`, or undecided `*`, and propagates constraints along multiplication-table diagonals, each of which must sum to 0 or 1.
  2. §3 (pp. 4–6) branches recursively on undecided cofactor coefficients.
  3. §4 (pp. 6–9) adds polynomial identities and strict inequalities, reduces modulo Gröbner bases, and uses real quantifier elimination to reject infeasible branches.
  4. §5 (pp. 9–11) enumerates candidate factor shapes through degree 15. The paper reports 7,141,686 candidate factors; 7,130,374 excluded by trinary propagation, another 7,438 by recursive propagation, and 2,900 symbolically, leaving 975 unresolved. These reported totals differ arithmetically by one, so this card preserves the paper’s table values without silently reconciling them. The paper reports Maple 2023 on four machines, a four-hour cap per QE test, and a cofactor-degree bound of 200.
- **Conclusion scope:** the work is a finite computational elimination with explicit caps. The 975 unresolved cases and bounded search do not prove the unrestricted conjecture.
- **Relaxation boundary:** §6 (PDF pp. 11–13) defines `α`-unfair factors with coefficients in `[-α,1+α]`; this permits negative coefficients. The ε-constructions therefore are not counterexamples to the nonnegative-factor conjecture.
- **Target interface:** candidate-specific exact semialgebraic feasibility is useful for bounded-degree searches. A global conclusion still needs a degree-independent argument or an explicit counterexample.

**Physical-page coverage:** pp. 1–2 title, setup, equivalent convolution problem, Theorem 1.1 and algorithm overview; pp. 3–4 §2 trinary coefficient propagation and diagonal constraints; pp. 4–6 §3 recursive branching; pp. 6–9 §4 Gröbner-basis and real quantifier-elimination pruning; pp. 9–11 §5 finite search, degree-15 table and computational caps; pp. 11–13 §6 ε-relaxation with negative coefficients; pp. 13–14 conclusion and references.
