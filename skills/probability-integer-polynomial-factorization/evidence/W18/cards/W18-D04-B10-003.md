# W18-D04-B10-003 — Reddy, 0–1 multiples of cyclotomic polynomials

**Identity/read evidence.** A. Satyanarayana Reddy, “The lowest degree 0,1-polynomial divisible by cyclotomic polynomial,” arXiv:1106.1271v2 (2011-11-15), 7 physical pages. Read every page including references; SHA-256 `65d8b28e479dc8a6dc93e794ededaeffa5229e714924001aa862d4eeae86d7e1`. Formula-heavy pp.3–6 were rendered and checked.

## Scope and definitions

For a fixed even (n), the article studies 0–1 polynomials (f) for which (f(\zeta_n)) is a minimal vanishing sum of distinct (n)-th roots of unity. Such an (f) has Φ_n dividing (f) and degree at most (n-1). The equivalence relation identifies rotations of a vanishing sum; (H_n) consists of lowest-degree representatives of these classes. The question in the article is a *conditional cyclotomic-mask minimum-degree problem*, not a general factorization theorem.

## Results with proof mechanism

### Lemma 2.1 — flat cyclotomic case (PDF p.3)

Let (n=2p_1p_2\cdots p_k), where (2=p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_k) are distinct primes, (k\ge1), and suppose Φ_n is flat. Write Φ_n as the difference (f_1-f_2) of its positive- and negative-coefficient parts, with disjoint exponent sets. Then
\[
\Phi_n^T(x)=f_1(x)+x^{n/2}f_2(x)
\]
is a 0–1 multiple of Φ_n and its value at ζ_n is a minimal vanishing sum. Its degree is φ(n)−1+n/2 if (k) is odd, and φ(n)−p_1+n/2 if (k) is even. Proof: since ζ_n^{n/2}=−1, Φ_n(ζ_n)=0 implies Φ_n^T(ζ_n)=0; flatness makes the shifted sign-separated support a 0–1 mask. Minimality follows by splitting a hypothetical proper vanishing subsum and comparing the low/high exponent halves against the degree φ(n) of Φ_n.

### Lemma 2.2 — (n=2p) (PDF pp.4)

For odd prime (p), (H_{2p}=\{g_0,g_1\}), with (g_1=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}x^{2i}=\Phi_{2p}^T(x)). The proof starts from Φ_{2p}(x)=Φ_p(−x), splits any candidate exponent set below/above (n/2), and uses degree (<φ(n)) to show the difference polynomial must equal Φ_n^T.

### Theorem 2.3 — proved degree comparison for (n=2pq) (PDF pp.5–6)

Let (p<q) be odd primes and (n=2pq). The theorem states:

1. (\deg(x^i\Phi_n^T)>\deg(\Phi_n^T)) for all rotations (i\ne0,n/2-p).
2. (\deg(\Phi_n^T)=\deg(x^{n/2-p}\Phi_n^T)=n/2+\varphi(n)-p=n-2p-q+1.

The proof uses Lemma 2.4: for exponent sets (E_A,E_B) in the Lam–Leung decomposition of Φ_{pq}, consecutive gaps are at most (p+q). It proves this by parity-splitting indices (ip+jq) and constructing a nearby exponent in the opposite parity class, with increment at most (p+q). These gap bounds force a large gap between the high- and low-exponent blocks of a nontrivially rotated Φ_n^T; the exceptional rotation (n/2-p) ties. This establishes minimality **within the rotation class** for Φ_n^T; it does not compare every possible minimal vanishing sum class. The printed theorem writes the shift as (x^i\Phi_n^T(x)) without displaying a reduction modulo (x^n-1); read this as the cyclic exponent rotation already defined via evaluation at ζ_n. If read as ordinary polynomial multiplication, the stated degree equality in part 2 cannot hold. This notation issue is preserved rather than silently normalized.

### Conjecture (PDF p.6; not proved)

For (n=2pq), the paper conjectures the lowest degree of a member of (H_n) with at least three terms is
\[
\min\{n-2q,\;n-2p-q+1\}.
\]
It conjecturally identifies (g_1) as the minimizer when (2p<q+1), Φ_n^T when (2p>q+1), and both in the tie case. The article explicitly presents this as a conjecture; Lemma 2.5 is only a possible aid. For (n=30), (p=3,q=5), it prints the tied candidates (x^{20}+x^{10}+1) and (x^{20}+x^{19}+x^{18}+x^8+x^7+x+1).

## A175 transfer boundary

Auxiliary only. It can support a degree or support obstruction after an A175 target/factor has independently been proved to have Φ_n as a divisor, be 0–1, have the minimal-vanishing-sum property, and meet the stated arithmetic restrictions. It supplies no reason that an arbitrary nonnegative real factor of a uniform finite-support PGF vanishes at a root of unity, is a 0–1 mask, or is divisible by a cyclotomic polynomial. Do not promote the conjectured minimum-degree formula to a theorem.
