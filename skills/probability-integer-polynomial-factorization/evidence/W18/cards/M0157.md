## Definitions, theorems, and proof dependencies

For finite `A⊂Z` and a tiling `A⊕B=Z` with unique representations, `B` is periodic. Write its least positive period as `P(B)`. Define `T(D)` as the maximum least period among tilings with a finite tile `A⊂{0,…,D}`. Lemma 1 identifies a tiling with least period `M` with a nonperiodic cyclic tiling `A⊕B̃=Z_M`, where `B̃` is the period cell.

Theorem 1 (physical p. 2) gives absolute constants `c₁,c₂,c₃>0` with `c₁D²≤T(D)≤c₂ exp(c₃√D·log D·√(log log D))` for `D>1`; proof is split into Theorems 2 and 3. The two square roots in the upper exponent are on `D` and on `log log D` separately.

**Lower bound.** Theorem 2 (physical pp. 2, 4–6) constructs, for `D>10⁵`, distinct primes `p,q∈[D/200,D/50]`, `M=30pq`, and the `3×5` rectangle in `Z_{3p}×Z_{5q}×Z₂`. Start with the product tiling, then shift one lower-layer column and one upper-layer row. This destroys both nontrivial coordinate periods; the resulting complement is nonperiodic. Under the explicit CRT isomorphism `ψ(i,j,k)=i(10q)+j(6p)+k(3pq) mod M`, the tile lies in `[0,20q+24p]⊂[0,44D/50]`. Hence `M≥30D²/40000`; for bounded small `D`, reduce the absolute constant. The constructed tile has size 15. This yields a quadratic lower bound on possible least tiling periods, not a factorization theorem for probability laws.

**Upper bound.** Theorem 3 (physical pp. 3, 6–8) states: if `A⊕B=Z`, `A⊂{0,…,D}` and `D<M`, then `P(B)≤c₂ exp(c₃√D·log D·√(loglogD))`. Reduce by Lemma 1 to a nonperiodic cyclic tiling `A⊕B̃=Z_M`. For the distinct cyclotomic divisors `Φ_{s_i}` (`s_i>1`) of `A(x)`, the degree bound gives `Σφ(s_i)≤deg A≤D`.

**Source-level coefficient error on the proof (printed/physical pp. 7–8).** On p. 7, inequality (5) is `φ(n)≥(e^{−γ}−ε)n/loglog n` for large `n`. Rearranging yields `n≤[1/(e^{−γ}−ε)]φ(n)loglog n`. But the next step on p. 8 writes a coefficient `(e^{−γ}+ε)` in front of `φ(s_i)loglog s_i` and then derives `Σs_i≤(e^{−γ}+2ε)DloglogD`; that is not the reciprocal coefficient and does not follow from (5). Treat that displayed coefficient and its claimed exact constant as a source error, not as a valid estimate.

**Independent coarse repair and valid scope.** Fix `ε>0` and choose `n₁` so (5) holds for `n>n₁` and `loglog n>0`. Then `C_ε=1/(e^{−γ}−ε)` is finite, and for `s_i>n₁`, `s_i≤C_εφ(s_i)loglog D`; the finitely many `s_i≤n₁` contribute at most a fixed constant. Thus `Σs_i≤C DloglogD` for a (different) constant `C`, using `Σφ(s_i)≤D`. Since the `s_i` are distinct positive integers, `Σs_i≥k(k+1)/2`, hence `k≤C'√(DloglogD)`. Put `t=∏s_i`; then `t≤(C DloglogD)^k≤exp(C''√D·logD·√(loglogD))`. Every `Φ_{s_i}` divides `x^t−1`; the tiling identity forces `x^M−1 | (x^t−1)B̃(x)`, so `t` is a period of `B̃`. Nonperiodicity forces `M≤t`. Therefore the **stated order/exponent with unspecified absolute constants** follows after this coarse repair, but the source's displayed `e^{−γ}+2ε` coefficient is unsupported. Remark 4 records a stronger `exp(c√(DlogD))` estimate from Ruzsa/Konyagin–Łaba; this paper cites, but does not prove, that improvement.

Theorem 4 (physical pp. 4, 8–9): if `0∈A∩B`, `A⊕B=[n]` and `max A>max B`, then `A` is periodic modulo `n`. It imports Long’s factor-replacement theorem (Theorem A): a finite cyclic interval factorization descends to `E⊕D=[n/m]` with either `A=mE⊕[m], B=mD`, or the roles reversed. A minimal-counterexample induction descends to `n/m` and lifts the period. Corollary 1: when `n>2D` and `A⊂[0,D]`, the complement contains a smaller-period sub-tiling of `[t]` for some proper divisor `t|n`.

## A175 use and gap

The useful bridge is the exact distinction between (a) unique additive set tilings and (b) convolution of probability laws. Theorems 2–4 analyze integer sets, cyclic complements, and period constraints; they do not show that probability-polynomial factors have 0–1 coefficients. Use as structural context only after a separate argument has converted a coefficient factorization to a set tiling. Its period bounds concern least periods and a diameter parameter `D`; they provide no conclusion for arbitrary nonnegative real convolution factors.
