## Definitions and theorem scope

An `N`-spectrum of `A(x)∈Z[x]` is a distinct set `{θ₁,…,θ_{N−1}}` such that `A(e^{2πi(θ_j−θ_k)})=0` for all distinct `j,k`, with `θ₀=0`. For a nonnegative-integer-coefficient polynomial representing a finite set, (T) means that its set tiles `Z`; (S) means it has a spectrum of size `A(1)`. The paper recalls Coven–Meyerowitz conditions: `S_A` consists of prime powers `p^α` with `Φ_{p^α}|A`; (T1) is `A(1)=∏_{s∈S_A}Φ_s(1)`; (T2) requires `Φ_{s₁⋯s_k}|A` whenever the `s_i` are prime powers for distinct primes.

Theorem 1.4 (physical pp. 2, 5–6): for **irreducible** `A(x)` with nonnegative integer coefficients, (T), (S), and (T1)+(T2) are equivalent; these hold exactly when `A(x)=1+x^{p^{α−1}}+⋯+x^{(p−1)p^{α−1}}` for a prime `p` and `α≥1`. For (T)→classification, (T1) and irreducibility force `A=Φ_{p^α}`. For (S)→classification, orthogonality of the spectral matrix yields a root sum identity; Galois averaging and Newton identities force equal gaps among the `N` exponents; all roots are roots of unity and irreducibility identifies the cyclotomic polynomial. This is a theorem about **irreducible integer polynomials**, not arbitrary real-coefficient probability factors.

For product polynomials `A(x)=∏_{i=1}^N A_i(x)`, `A_i(x)=1+x^{m_i}+⋯+x^{m_i(n_i−1)}`, Theorem 1.5 (physical pp. 3, 10–12) treats `N=2`: (T), (S), and (T1)+(T2) are equivalent to at least one divisibility condition `n₁ | m₂/(m₁,m₂)` or `n₂ | m₁/(m₁,m₂)`. The factors are finite arithmetic-progression mask polynomials; the total product must have 0–1 coefficients to represent a set tile in the stated conjectures.

Theorem 1.6 (physical pp. 3, 6–10) treats every `N≥2` for this same special product form. After relabeling, the condition is
`n_i | (m_{i+1}/d_{i,i+1},…,m_N/d_{iN})` for `i=1,…,N−1`, where `d_{ij}=(m_i,m_j)`; this is equivalent to (T) and (T1)+(T2), and implies (S) via the cited results. For the proof of (T)→divisibility, assume `(m_1,…,m_N)=1`, lift a tiling of `Z` to a box tiling in `Z^N`, and let `W={w∈Z^N:⟨w,m⟩=0}` be the kernel. Keller’s cube-tiling theorem forces every nonzero vector in the normalized `W` to have a nonzero integer coordinate. A failed divisibility ordering yields a cycle of indices and a kernel vector violating this condition; induct on `N` to get the full ordering. Conversely, cyclotomic divisibility is exactly `Φ_s|A_i ⇔ s|m_i n_i` and `s∤m_i`; valuation comparisons under the ordering give (T1) and mixed-prime (T2). The implication (T1)+(T2)→(T) is cited to Coven–Meyerowitz, not reproved here. Theorem 4.3 p. 8 contains the main equivalence; its proof spans §§4.1–4.3, physical pp. 7–10.

## A175 use and gap

Useful arithmetic references for cyclotomic divisibility and special **set-mask** factorizations; the `N=2` arithmetic-progression case gives a clear divisibility test. It does not classify arbitrary factorizations of a uniform-support polynomial with nonnegative real coefficients, nor does it show that such factors have integer/0–1 coefficients. A175’s finite supports may have gaps and the target support may have gaps; those generalities are outside Theorems 1.4–1.6. The three-prime tile result W18-X016 is also not subsumed by this product-of-progression theorem.
