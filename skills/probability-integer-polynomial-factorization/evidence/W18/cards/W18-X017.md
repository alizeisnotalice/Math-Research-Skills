## Model and algorithmic theorems

For finite sets `A,B` in an abelian group, `G=A⊕B` means every group element has exactly one representation as `a+b`; this is a **set tiling/group factorization**, not probability convolution. The paper focuses on finite abelian groups and `Z_N`. It recalls periodicity of finite integer tilings and encodes a cyclic set by its 0–1 mask polynomial; tiling is equivalent to `|A||B|=N` and cyclotomic zero sets covering all nontrivial `N`-th roots.

Theorem 2.1 (physical p. 5): for a finite integer set `A⊂Z` of diameter `D`, there is an algorithm to test Coven–Meyerowitz (T1),(T2) in time polynomial in `D`; by the cited ≤2-distinct-prime-factor theorem, this decides whether `A` tiles `Z` in that cardinality range. It computes cyclotomic polynomials up to degree `D`, finds prime-power cyclotomic divisors of the mask polynomial, checks (T1), then checks all mixed-prime products required by (T2). The number of combinations is at most `D` by the degree bound `(N₁+1)…(N_k+1)−1≤D`; this is the paper’s stated complexity model, not a bit-complexity analysis.

Theorem 3.1 (physical p. 6) gives the local cyclic version for finite `A` and positive `N`: local conditions `(T₁^N),(T₂^N)` imply `A` tiles `Z_N`; a tiling implies `(T₁^N)`; the global CM conjecture would imply the local conjecture; if `|A|` has at most two distinct prime factors, a tiling implies both local conditions; and the local conditions can be decided in time polynomial in `N`. This decides tiling in `Z_N` when `|A|` has at most two distinct prime factors.

Theorem 4.1 (physical pp. 6–7) constructs arbitrarily large cyclic groups with at least `exp(C√N)` distinct nonperiodic complements for a tile, illustrating that full enumeration can become intractable. It adapts the rectangle/shift construction with `N=2·3·5·p·q`. It is a lower bound on the number of tiling complements, not an algorithmic complexity theorem.

## Heuristic enumeration and proof limits

§5 describes listing nonperiodic tilings of `Z_144`. The “fill-out” procedure recursively extends a packing complement `P`: for each uncovered point `x`, count `r(P,x)`, the number of nonoverlapping new translates that cover it; select a point with minimal `r`, branch over its possible translates, and backtrack when `r=0`. A deterministic tie-break is specified. The authors explicitly provide **no rigorous complexity guarantee** for this procedure. They filter cyclotomic prime-power assignments using (T1),(T2), group candidates by Fourier zero sets, then run fill-out; one exceptional case is discharged by Sands’ theorem and known `Z_72` classifications. The article reports a full classification for `Z_144`, but does not print every generated tiling; supporting online documentation is cited.

Remark 5.1 restricts where their method works: it relies on tiling implying local (T2), in particular when `N` has at most two distinct prime factors, or has three distinct prime factors with one occurring to the first power. This cannot justify enumeration for arbitrary cyclic groups or prove the general CM conjecture. The `Z_144` computation is a bounded case study.

## A175 use and gap

This paper supplies an implementation blueprint for exact cyclotomic tests **after** the object is a finite set mask polynomial and the CM conditions apply. It does not test whether an arbitrary nonnegative-real polynomial factor of a uniform probability-generating polynomial must itself have a special form. Its `0–1` coefficients, unique set representations, finite cyclic group, and ≤2-prime algorithm hypotheses are all extra structure absent from general A175. Use the polynomial factor examples from M0156/Raikov for the probability model; use this paper only for a distinct set-tiling computational route. Finite computations do not establish a general probability theorem.
