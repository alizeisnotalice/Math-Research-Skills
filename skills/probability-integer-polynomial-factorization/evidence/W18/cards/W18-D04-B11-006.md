## Exact model

Fix M=∏_(i=1)^K p_i^(n_i), with distinct primes. Polynomials are elements of Z[X]/(X^M−1); their coefficients define integer-weighted multisets in Z_M. For a set A⊂Z_M, the mask is A(X). A⊕B=Z_M requires both masks be 0–1 and A(X)B(X)=[M]_X modulo X^M−1. Div(A)={gcd(a−a′,M):a,a′∈A}; it includes M from a=a′. Theorem 2.5 (Sands, recast on PDF p.9) is the exact tiling test: A⊕B=Z_M iff |A||B|=M and Div(A)∩Div(B)={M}.

The article uses (T1),(T2) for the cyclotomic tiling conditions. Its stated scope is finite cyclic integer tilings, weighted masks, and structural reductions. It does not establish the unrestricted Coven–Meyerowitz theorem for arbitrary many prime factors; §9 explicitly leaves reduction questions and several conjectures open. The paper cites the later three-odd-prime result as a separate follow-up.

## Theorem and proof cards

### Proposition 3.4 — standard-complement equivalence for (T2) (PDF pp.10–11)

For a tiling A⊕B=Z_M, construct A^flat by replacing each prime-power cyclotomic factor Φ_(p_i^α) dividing A with the standard factor Φ_(p_i)(X^(M_i p_i^(α−1))), where M_i=M/p_i^(n_i). Lemmas 3.2–3.3 show A^flat has the same prime-power cyclotomic divisors as A and satisfies (T2). Proposition 3.4 proves the following equivalent conditions:

1. Div(A^flat)∩Div(B)={M};
2. A^flat⊕B=Z_M;
3. B satisfies (T2);
4. |B∩(x∗A^flat)|=1 for every x∈Z_M.

Proof: (1)⇔(2) is Sands divisor exclusion; (3)⇔(2) follows from the exact standard mask factorization and the cyclotomic divisors of its factors; (2) gives at most one intersection in (4), and tiling with −B gives at least one; failure of (1) creates two distinct points in the same intersection. Example 3.6 shows Div(A^flat)⊆Div(A) is not necessary, even when A,B both satisfy (T2). Corollary 3.8: if A⊕B is (T2)-equivalent to A^flat⊕B, then both A and B satisfy (T2). This is a conditional reduction, not a general proof of (T2).

### Theorem 4.4 — box-product characterization (PDF pp.13, 17–18)

For N|M, the N-box A^N[x]=(A_m^N[x])_(m|N) counts weighted points a by gcd(x−a,N)=m. Its inner product (Definition 4.2, PDF p.12) is
\[
\langle \mathbf A,\mathbf B\rangle=\sum_{m|N}\frac{A_mB_m}{\phi(N/m)}.
\]
If A⊕B=Z_M, then for every N|M and x,y∈Z_M,
\[
\langle A^N[x],B^N[y]\rangle=\frac{|A||B|}{N}=\frac{M}{N};
\]
in particular the value is 1 for N=M. Conversely, if A,B⊂Z_M, |A||B|=M, and this M-box product is 1 for every a∈A,b∈B, then A⊕B=Z_M.

Proof of the forward implication: Theorem 4.7 plus the tiling root condition Φ_d|AB for every nontrivial d|M kills all nonconstant Fourier terms, leaving d=1 and the value |A||B|/N. For the converse, sum the assumed pairwise products over a∈A,b∈B; combine Theorem 4.7's nonnegative Fourier-energy identity with the d=1 term |A|²|B|²=M². All other cyclotomic energy terms must vanish, so for each nontrivial d|M at least one of A,B vanishes on every primitive dth root; the standard finite-group Fourier criterion gives the tiling.

### Theorem 4.7 — Fourier identity and proof chain (PDF pp.14–18)

For integer-coefficient polynomials A,B,C,D modulo X^N−1 and their N-boxes,
\[
\langle A^N[C],B^N[D]\rangle=
\frac1N\sum_{d|N}\frac1{\phi(d)}
\left(\sum_{\zeta:\Phi_d(\zeta)=0}A(\zeta)\overline{C(\zeta)}\right)
\left(\sum_{\zeta:\Phi_d(\zeta)=0}B(\zeta)\overline{D(\zeta)}\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]
For C=A,D=B this becomes
\[
\langle A^N[A],B^N[B]\rangle=
\frac1N\sum_{d|N}\frac{E_d(A)E_d(B)}{\phi(d)},\qquad
E_d(A)=\sum_{\zeta:\Phi_d(\zeta)=0}|A(\zeta)|^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]
The identity is for masks modulo X^N−1; it allows arbitrary integer coefficients and weighted multisets. Proof: express gcd-class indicators as Ramanujan sums G_ξ(N/m), apply the finite DFT, and use Proposition 4.9. That proposition proves
\[
\sum_{v|N}\frac{G_\xi(v)G_{\xi'}(v)}{\phi(v)}
=0\quad\text{if }\gcd(\xi,N)\ne\gcd(\xi',N),
\]
and equals N/φ(N/d) when both gcds equal d. Multiplicativity reduces this orthogonality to prime powers, where the three cases ν≠ν′, ν=ν′<α, and ν=ν′=α are computed explicitly (pp.15–17). No positivity of A,B,C,D is needed for Theorem 4.7; positivity of E_d(A),E_d(B) in the set specialization is what supports the converse in Theorem 4.4.

### Proposition 5.5 — cyclotomic divisibility by cuboid tests (PDF pp.20–21)

For an integer-weighted multiset A in Z_N, Φ_N(X)|A(X) iff A_N^N[Δ]=0 for every classic N-cuboid Δ. Such a cuboid has alternating ±1 vertices and mask X^c∏_(p_j|N)(1−X^(ρ_jN/p_j)), with gcd(ρ_j,p_j)=1. The evaluations are the associated box counts with those signed weights.

Forward proof: Lemma 5.3 applies Theorem 4.7 to A and the product ΔT; each cyclotomic order dividing N is covered by a divisor of A, the template T, or a cuboid side factor. Converse: induction on the number of prime divisors of N, starting with prime powers; split into prime layers, use the cuboid tests to show layer differences are N′-null, apply induction, and recombine as a linear combination of N-fibers. The proof permits integer coefficients and uses the exact cyclic quotient; it is not a test of arbitrary real-coefficient factors.

### Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.2 — subgroup reduction (PDF pp.23–24)

Let M=∏p_i^(n_i), A⊕B=Z_M, and suppose A⊂p_iZ_M. If (T2) holds for both A′ and B′ in every smaller tiling A′⊕B′=Z_(M/p_i) with |A′|=|A| and |B′|=|B|/p_i, then A and B satisfy (T2). Proof: decompose B into its p_i residue slices B_ν; A′ tiles each smaller residue-class group with each B_ν. Cyclotomic orders transform by τ(s)=s if p_i∤s and τ(s)=p_i s if p_i|s, so the assumed (T2) for each smaller tile lifts to A and B. Corollary 6.2: (T2) follows when every prime i≥3 divides at most one of |A|,|B|, by induction and Tijdeman's cyclic-factorization theorem. This is conditional on the subgroup structure/reduction hypotheses.

### Theorem 6.5 — slab-reduction equivalences (PDF p.28)

Assume A⊕B=Z_M and Φ_(p_i^(n_i))|A. Then these are equivalent:

1. For every translate A′ of A, the slab A′_(p_i) tiles with B in Z_(M/p_i).
2. For each d with p_i^(n_i)|d|M, either Φ_d|A or ∏_(j=1)^(n_i)Φ_(d/p_i^j)|B.
3. For each m with p_i^(n_i)|m|M, m∈Div(A) implies m/p_i∉Div(B).

Here A′_(p_i) is the low-coordinate slab defined in (6.2); it has |A|/p_i elements. Proof of (1)⇔(3) uses Sands divisor exclusion in the smaller group and checks that both projected objects are sets; (1)⇒(2) follows from Lemma 6.4 and cyclotomic divisibility; (2)⇒(1) uses Theorem 2.5 and the cyclotomic criterion for the reduced masks. Corollary 6.7 propagates (T2) if the smaller slab tilings satisfy (T2). The theorem is a criterion; it does not assert that every tiling admits a slab reduction.

### Lemma 8.7 and Lemma 8.10 — fiber shifting under cofiber hypotheses (PDF pp.37–40)

Lemma 8.7: if A⊕B=Z_M has the specified (P_A,P_B)-cofibered structure, shifting one A-cofiber by M/p_i^β for β∈P_B preserves the tiling and preserves the (T2)-status of A (the two A-tiles are (T2)-equivalent). Proof checks every cyclotomic order s|M in three cases according to whether the p_i-exponent is above the shift scale, belongs to P_B, or belongs to P_A; the corresponding fiber-chain factors divide the translated mask or B, so all zeros of AB remain and the prime-power/T2 divisors of A are unchanged.

Lemma 8.10 (PDF pp.38–39; the hypothesis is a single M-box coordinate product, not an inner product). Assume A⊕B=Z_M. In part (i), if x,y∈Z_M, x∉A, and for some 0<γ≤n_i
\[
A^M_{M/p_i^γ}[x]B^M_{M/p_i^γ}[y]=\phi(p_i^γ),
\]
then there are disjoint P_A,P_B with P_A∪P_B={1,…,γ−1} and
\[
\{M/p_i^\alpha:\alpha\in P_A\}\subseteq\operatorname{Div}(A),\qquad
\{M/p_i^\beta:\beta\in P_B\}\subseteq\operatorname{Div}(B).
\]
Here A_{x,y},B_{y,x} are the saturating sets from Definition 7.2. Choose maximal subsets A_0⊂A_{x,y}, B_0⊂B_{y,x} such that every two distinct points in either root subset satisfy gcd(a−a′,M)=M/p_i^γ (respectively gcd(b−b′,M)=M/p_i^γ); one has size 1 and the other size p_i−1, and
\[
A_{x,y}=\bigcup_{a\in A_0}F(a),\qquad B_{y,x}=\bigcup_{b\in B_0}G(b),
\]
where F(a),G(b) are respectively P_A- and P_B-fiber chains rooted at a,b. In part (ii), suppose x∈Z_M\A, A_x⊂\ell_i(x), and for some 0<γ≤n_i the same single-coordinate identity
\[
A^M_{M/p_i^γ}[x]B^M_{M/p_i^γ}[b]=\phi(p_i^γ)\quad\text{for every }b\in B.
\]
Then (A,B) has a (P_A,P_B∪{γ})-cofibered structure, with A_x a P_A-cofiber at distance M/p_i^γ from x. The proof builds maximal separated root subsets and fiber chains level by level; divisor exclusion assigns each lower level to one side, and the coordinate-product bound is (p_i−1)p_i^(γ−1)=φ(p_i^γ), with equality forcing the full chains. Part (ii)'s “for every b∈B” and line-containment assumptions are essential to the stated conclusion. Corollary 8.11 is a special (1,2)-cofiber consequence.

## Open statements and boundary

Section 9 (PDF pp.41–45) labels the main extensions as open: strong/weak subgroup or slab reductions in general, whether cofiber obstructions exhaust cases, and several saturating-set/subspace conjectures. In particular, this article develops methods and conditional reductions; it does not prove (T2) for all cyclic tilings or the general Coven–Meyerowitz conjecture. The specific result for odd periods p_1²p_2²p_3² is attributed to a separate follow-up [24], not proved here.

## A175 use and unresolved interface

Auxiliary exact method: if an instance is already a 0–1 tiling in a specified finite cyclic group, Theorem 2.5 gives a divisor-exclusion test; Proposition 3.4 turns (T2) into a standard-complement tiling criterion; Theorems 4.4/4.7 and Proposition 5.5 provide box/Fourier/cuboid tests; Theorem 6.5 gives a conditional slab-reduction criterion. A175 instead starts with arbitrary finite-support integer-valued independent laws and nonnegative-real normalized PGFs, asks whether a uniform-on-support sum forces uniform factors, and allows support gaps. One must first prove that the PGF factorization can be represented by integer 0–1 cyclic masks with a valid period; this article does not integerize real factors or supply a no-wrap bridge from ordinary convolution to cyclic tiling. Its results therefore do not resolve the A175 factorization question.
