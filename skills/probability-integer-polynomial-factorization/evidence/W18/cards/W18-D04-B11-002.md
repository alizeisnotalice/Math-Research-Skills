## Exact divisibility criterion

- Write `P(z)=a_0 z^m+a_1z^{m−1}+⋯+a_m`, with `a_j=0` outside `0≤j≤m`. **Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 2; notation clarified p. 3).** For `N∈N` with `φ(N)≤m`, `Φ_N(z)|P(z)` iff for every `h∈{0,…,N−1}`
  `Σ_{d|N} μ(d) Σ_{0≤j≤m, j≡ h−Σ_{p|d}(N/p) (mod N)} a_j = 0`.
  If `N` is even it suffices to check `h=0,…,N/2−1`. The inner sum is over coefficient **indices** `j` in the authors’ descending-coefficient convention, not exponents `m−j`. This is an exact necessary-and-sufficient test for a *specified* polynomial and *specified* order `N`; coefficients may be complex. Rendered p. 2 confirms the formula, `φ(N)≤deg P`, and the even-`N` half-range.
- **Proof chain (pp. 10–16).** Lemma 2.8 (p. 11) proves `z^n−1|P` iff each coefficient-index residue sum `Σ_{j≡r (mod n)}a_j` is zero, by evaluating at all `n`th roots and inverting the nonsingular Vandermonde matrix. Lemma 2.9 (pp. 12–13) specializes this to `Φ_p(z^s)|P`: the sums in residue classes `h (mod ps)` and `h−s (mod ps)` agree for every `h`; for `p=2`, only `0≤h<s` need be checked. For square-free `N`, the proof of Theorem 1.1 (pp. 13–15) inducts on `ω(N)`, uses `Φ_N(z)=∏_{d|N}(z^{N/d}−1)^{μ(d)}`, and applies the induction hypothesis to `(z^{sN}−1)P(z)`; the nontrivial coprimality step shows `z^{sN/d}−1` and `Φ_N(z^{sp})` have no common root for `d>1`. Finally `Φ_N(z)=Φ_{γ(N)}(z^{N/γ(N)})` reduces general `N` to its square-free kernel, and the even case halves the residue range. The displayed shifts and congruences on rendered pp. 12–15 were checked.

## Additional coefficient and Ramanujan-sum methods

- **Theorem 1.3 (p. 6; proof p. 17).** If `p` is prime and `p∤m`, the coefficient of `z^{(p−1)φ(m)−k}` in `Φ_{mp}(z)` is
  `a_{mp}(k)=Σ_{s=0}^{⌊k/p⌋} a_m(s) H_{k−sp}(\vec ζ_m^*)`, `0≤k≤(p−1)φ(m)`;
  here `H_r` is the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial evaluated on the vector of primitive `m`th roots. The proof uses `Φ_m(z^p)=Φ_{mp}(z)Φ_m(z)` and the generalized Vieta identity (Lemma 2.6, p. 10). If `p|m`, Remark 1.2 (pp. 6–7) instead gives `Φ_{mp}(z)=Φ_m(z^p)` and the coefficient insertion/zero pattern.
- **Theorem 1.4 (p. 7; proof pp. 17–18).** For distinct primes `p,q`, every coefficient of `Φ_{pq}` lies in `{−1,0,1}`. Its proof identifies these coefficients with complete homogeneous symmetric functions on nonprimitive `pq`th roots and counts a unique CRT-compatible index in the relevant interval; it is not an assertion for general cyclotomic indices.
- **Corollary 1.2 and Appendix A (pp. 3–4, 15–23).** Applying Theorem 1.1 to Tóth’s Ramanujan-sum polynomial gives weighted identities. In particular, for each `d_1|γ(n)`, the printed identity is `μ(d_1) Σ_{d|n,d≠d_1} μ(d)c_n(Σ_{p|d_1}n/p−Σ_{p|d}n/p)=n−φ(n)` (p. 3). The rest gives further vanishing identities in `h`, an elementary alternate proof of this identity in Appendix A.1 (pp. 18–22), and the `ω(n)=2` specialization in A.2 (pp. 22–23). Full appendix and declarations/license/references (pp. 23–24) were read.

## A175 interface and limitation

If an exact coefficient vector for a proposed probability factor is available and one wants to test whether it has a specified cyclotomic divisor, Theorem 1.1 gives a finite coefficient-sum certificate, provided `deg P≥φ(N)`. It does **not** find an unknown factor, prove that a factor has rational or integer coefficients, or show that any factor is cyclotomic. Although the theorem permits complex coefficients, exact use still requires exact coefficients and a chosen `N`; the coefficient-recursion formulas concern constructing cyclotomic polynomials, not factoring an arbitrary real nonnegative probability polynomial.
