## Core definitions and statements

**Theorem/Definition 1 (physical pp. 2–3).** For a nonzero `f(q)=Σ_{k=0}^n c_kq^k` with `c_k∈Z_{≥0}`, the following are equivalent: (i) every complex root is zero or a root of unity; (ii) all roots have modulus at most one and the leading coefficient equals the gcd of all coefficients; (iii) `f` is a positive integer times a product of cyclotomic polynomials and powers of `q`; (iv) a rational product `αq^β ∏_{j=1}^m [a_j]_q/[b_j]_q`, with positive integers `a_j,b_j`, `α>0`, `β≥0` (the reduced multisets give uniqueness when disjoint); (v) after normalizing coefficients to a law, an equality in distribution of independent discrete uniforms, `X+U_{b_1}+⋯+U_{b_m}=β+U_{a_1}+⋯+U_{a_m}`; and (vi) the scaled characteristic function has zeros only at rational arguments. The theorem is a characterization of cyclotomic generating functions (CGFs), not of arbitrary probability-generating factors.

**Cumulants (Theorem 9, physical p. 10; formula (2.5), visually checked at p. 13).** For a CGF with rational form `∏[a_k]_q/∏[b_k]_q`, its `d`th formal cumulant is `κ_d=(B_d/d)(Σa_k^d−Σb_k^d)` under the paper’s Bernoulli-number convention; the mean is `(1/2)Σ(a_k−b_k)` and variance `(1/12)Σ(a_k²−b_k²)`. The source also gives characteristic-function factorization by sinc ratios and explicit cyclotomic cumulants.

**Theorem 5 (physical p. 7; rendered).** For `f_N(q)=∏_{a∈a(N)}[a]_q/∏_{b∈b(N)}[b]_q∈Φ⁺`, the associated CGF variables are asymptotically normal if
`limsup_N Σ_{b∈b(N)}(b²−1)/Σ_{a∈a(N)}(a²−1)<1`
and
`lim_N Σ_{a∈a(N)}(a/max a(N))⁴=∞`.
The strict variance-ratio gap matters; the adjacent source text notes that a limiting ratio of one can involve major cancellation and is not covered by the conclusion.

**Theorem 37 (physical pp. 23–24; rendered p. 23).** For numerator-only products `f_N=∏_{a∈a(N)}[a]_q`, with every `a≥2`, (i) the same fourth-power/max condition implies asymptotic normality; (ii) when cardinalities are bounded and `max a(N)→∞`, convergence of standardized variables is equivalent to pointwise convergence of the rescaled multisets `a(N)/||a(N)||₂` in the paper’s extended finite-`ℓ` sense, with standardized generalized-uniform-sum limit; (iii) if both cardinality and maximum are bounded, convergence occurs iff the multiset is eventually constant. This is not an arbitrary quotient theorem by itself.

**Theorem 53 (physical pp. 35–36; displayed result visually checked at p. 36).** For a weighted polynomial ring `k[x_1,…,x_m]`, `deg x_j=b_j>0`, and a homogeneous regular sequence `θ_1,…,θ_m` with `deg θ_j=a_j`, the quotient is finite-dimensional and has Hilbert series `Hilb(R;q)=∏[a_j]_q/[b_j]_q∈Φ⁺`; the stated converse is that if this product is the Hilbert series in (6.1), then `θ_1,…,θ_m` is a regular sequence. This is algebraic context, not a probability-factorization result.

## Proof mechanisms and qualifications

Section 3 proves Theorem/Definition 1 by cyclotomic factorization, Möbius inversion on q-integers, and Kronecker’s theorem; §3.2 expands coefficients of integer-q-integer ratios by multiplying `(1−q^i)^{-M_i}` series; §§3.3–3.5 derive polynomiality/positivity constraints, majorization conjectures, and fixed-denominator structure. For Theorem 5, the proof writes independent uniform sums `A_N,B_N` for numerator/denominator and uses `X_N+B_N=A_N`, the cumulant criterion of Theorem 37, standardized characteristic functions, and Lévy’s continuity theorem (physical pp. 24–26). Theorem 37’s proof uses standardized cumulants and the Hwang–Zacharovas fourth-cumulant criterion; its limit classification invokes the cited Billey–Swanson limit-law theorem. Theorem 53 relies on regular sequences/HSOP equivalence for Cohen–Macaulay polynomial rings and the Hilbert-series exact-sequence argument; the surrounding source states the six equivalent dimension/regularity conditions in Lemma 52.

The paper itself notes at physical p. 28 that the cited Hwang–Zacharovas Lemma 2.8 is incorrectly stated for all real `t`; the proof step used there requires `t≥0`. It uses the needed nonnegative range. This source-level citation caveat is not silently generalized.

## W18/A175 use and limits

**Relevant cyclotomic/PGF structure source.** Theorem/Definition 1 gives a precise route from a nonnegative-integer polynomial with cyclotomic roots to a sum-of-uniforms identity, useful when an A175 candidate is independently shown to be a CGF. It does not show that an arbitrary uniform finite-support factorization has cyclotomic roots or that its factors are nonnegative-integer polynomials. The CLT results concern CGF families and cannot be transferred to arbitrary factors without establishing the CGF hypotheses.
