## Exact theorem and proof

The article defines reducibility in the class `P` of real-coefficient polynomials with nonnegative coefficients: a polynomial is reducible in `P` if it is a product of two positive-degree polynomials in `P`. Its theorem (physical p. 1; repeated in the French résumé, p. 4) is that for **every prime** `p`,
`1+x+⋯+x^{p−1}=(1−x^p)/(1−x)`
is irreducible in `P`.

Preliminary lemma (physical pp. 1–3): if `1+x+⋯+x^{m−1}=A(x)B(x)` with nonnegative real coefficients, and factors normalized to constant coefficient 1, then every coefficient of both factors is either 0 or 1. The proof uses the coefficient matrix `a_i β_j`: each antidiagonal sum is exactly 1, so all coefficients lie in `[0,1]`; a diagonal containing a product entry 1 forces that pair of coefficients to equal 1 and all other entries on the diagonal to vanish. Induction over diagonals, using symmetry of the finite coefficient matrix, forces the remaining coefficients to be binary.

For prime `p`, suppose both factors have positive degree. The lemma makes their coefficient sums at `x=1` integers `r,s≥2`. Since `(1+⋯+x^{p−1})|_{x=1}=p`, one has `p=rs`, impossible for prime `p`. This proves the theorem. The argument covers two factors; it is not a classification of arbitrary target polynomials.

## Identity/version boundary and A175 use

This is **Raikov’s** Russian note, not the unavailable Krasner–Ranulac French note M0152. Raikov’s own p. 1 footnote identifies the Krasner–Ranulac note as a separate, “somewhat more general” proof that he saw after submitting his note. Thus W18-X013 must not be relabeled M0152 or used to claim that the missing paper was read.

For A175 this directly excludes a factorization of the prime-length interval polynomial into two nonconstant nonnegative-real factors. After normalizing the probability polynomial by `p`, the same exclusion applies to representing the uniform law on `{0,…,p−1}` as a convolution of two nondegenerate interval-supported laws (with polynomial factor degrees summing to `p−1`). It says nothing for composite interval length, a target support with gaps, or an arbitrary finite-support target. The proof’s `x=1` integer-factor argument relies on the binary-coefficient lemma specific to a geometric sum.
