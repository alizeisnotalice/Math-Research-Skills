## Exact result

**§3 theorem (physical p. 3; printed p. 63; unnumbered).** Let `F` be an infinitely divisible real-line distribution with Lévy–Khintchine representation (1). If there are constants `k>0` and `c>0` such that `M'(x)>k` almost everywhere on at least one of `(-c,0)` or `(0,c)`, then `F` has a convolution divisor that is not infinitely divisible. In particular the paper names Pearson Type III and every non-normal stable distribution. This is a theorem about distributions on `R` and infinite divisibility, not finite-support integer distributions or 0–1 mask-polynomial factors.

The source uses `m∈R`, `σ²≥0`, and a real function `M` nondecreasing on each side of zero, with `M(−∞)=M(+∞)=0` and `∫_{−1}^{1} x² dM(x)<∞`, in
`log φ(t)=mit−(1/2)σ²t²+∫(e^{itx}−1−itx/(1+x²))dM(x)` (display (1), physical pp. 2–3). The representation is stated to be unique.

## Proof mechanism and boundary

By symmetry it suffices to take the positive interval. The paper extracts an infinitely divisible divisor `G` with `m=σ=0` and `M'(x)=k` on `(0,c)`, zero elsewhere. It splits its exponent using `a₁,a₂` on `(0,c)`: on the central interval `((1/2−ε)c,(1/2+ε)c)`, `a₁=−ε`, `a₂=1+ε`; off it, `a₁=1`, `a₂=0`, for `0<ε<1/2`. Then `a₁+a₂=1`, and the factor defined with `a₂≥0` is infinitely divisible.

For sufficiently small `ε`, the paper proves the other factor is a probability law by expressing its c.f. through the series of convolution powers `β_n` of the signed density `β₁=ka₁` (physical pp. 4–5). It gives the support and size bounds and the transform identity for `β_n`; on p. 5 it notes that `β₁` is negative only on the short central interval, while `β₂` converges uniformly to the triangular convolution profile as `ε→0`. Thus for small `ε`, `β₂,β₃≥0`; the displayed estimate `β₁+β₂/2!+β₃/3!≥0` and `β₄=β₂*β₂`, `β₅=β₃*β₂` seed the recurrence `β_n=β_{n-2}*β₂` for all `n≥6`, giving nonnegativity of the complete series. It then rules out infinite divisibility: uniqueness of the Lévy–Khintchine representation would force its Lévy density to be `k(1−a₂)`, which equals `−kε` on the central interval, contradicting nonnegativity of the Lévy measure. No discrete-support reduction is given.

**§4 consequence (physical p. 5; printed p. 65).** Invoking Khintchine’s theorem that a non-infinitely-divisible distribution has an indecomposable divisor, the paper concludes that each `F` meeting §3’s condition is divisible by a product of an infinite sequence of indecomposable distributions.

## W18/A175 use

**Background boundary only; exclude from the A175 qualifying-source count.** The source clarifies the distinction between infinite divisibility and ordinary convolution decomposability for real-line laws. It supplies no theorem on finite integer supports, uniform sums, probability-generating polynomials, or nonnegative/0–1 factor coefficients. Do not transfer the §3 conclusion to A175.
