# W18-D04-EXT-004 — Monic polynomials in Z[x] with roots in the unit disc

**Source/version.** Pantelis A. Damianou, arXiv:1507.02419v1 (5 physical pages; arXiv submission 9 July 2015), journal reference *American Mathematical Monthly* 108 (2001), 253–257. The local PDF is the complete arXiv v1, SHA256 `e420bb0e8ed5ee55f29b4d7e64b5e70591613a1d3dc5ccfb0ff72491856a7d50`. All five physical pages, including references, were read.

## Results and proof chain

- **Kronecker Theorem 1 (physical pp. 1–2).** If a monic `f∈Z[z]` has all roots in `|z|≤1`, every nonzero root `λ` satisfies `|λ|=1`. The proof first bounds the integer coefficients by Vieta's formulas: `|a_{n-j}|≤binom(n,j)`, so there are finitely many such degree-`n` polynomials. For `f(z)=∏(z−z_j)`, form `f_k(z)=∏(z−z_j^k)`. Newton identities show each `f_k∈Z[z]`; all its roots stay in the disk. Finiteness gives `f_j=f_k` for some `j<k`, hence the two powered root multisets agree under a permutation `σ`. If `m` is the order of `σ`, iterating the matched-root identity gives `λ^{j^m}=λ^{k^m}` (as displayed on physical p. 2), so `λ^{k^m-j^m}=1`; for `λ≠0`, this is a root-of-unity relation and therefore `|λ|=1`.
- **Theorem 2 (physical pp. 3–4).** Every such polynomial has the form `z^k ∏_{r≥1} Φ_r(z)^{e_r}`, where `k,e_r` are nonnegative integers with finite support and `Φ_r` is the `r`th cyclotomic polynomial. This follows from Theorem 1 and factorization over `Q`: every nonzero root is a root of unity, so its minimal polynomial is cyclotomic. Conversely, every displayed product has all roots in the closed disk.
- The degree-counting generating function is `∏_{r≥0}(1−x^{φ(r)})^{-1}=Σ_{n≥0}k(n)x^n`, with the paper's convention `φ(0)=φ(1)=1`; the `r=0` factor counts `z`, and `r=1,2` count `Φ_1,Φ_2`. The note gives a recurrence/partition method, `k(1),…,k(10)=3,9,19,43,81,159,277,501,831,1415`, and `k(100)=13445370780675`. These are counts of canonical cyclotomic products, not a formula for arbitrary integer-polynomial factors.

## A175 interface and limitation

Theorem 2 is useful only after a factor is known to be **monic integer** and all of its roots lie in the closed unit disk. A175 gives nonnegative real probability-generating factors, and neither integrality/monicity nor a root-location hypothesis of this kind follows from the problem statement. This paper therefore supplies a conditional classification tool, not a bridge from a hypothetical real factor to a cyclotomic product and not a resolution of A175.
