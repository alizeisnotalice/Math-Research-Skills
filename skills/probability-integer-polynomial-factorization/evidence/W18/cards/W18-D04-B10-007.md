## Exact setting and results

The paper studies cyclic convolution and rational/nonnegative weak complements for finite set tiles. For `A⊂Z_M`, a normalized nonnegative weak complement is `f:Z_M→R_{≥0}` with `f(0)=1` and `1_A*f=1_{Z_M}`; the canonical mask polynomial is reduced modulo `X^M−1`. It carefully distinguishes **ordinary** T1/T2 of a rational polynomial, which see all prime-power cyclotomic divisors, from **period-relative** T1/T2, which see only orders dividing the chosen M.

- **Lemma 2.1 (physical p. 5).** If `A⊂Z_M` is nonempty and `b:Z_M→Z_{≥0}` is integer-valued with `1_A*b=1`, then b is an indicator. Fix `a0∈A`; for every x, `1=(1_A*b)(x+a0)≥b(x)≥0`, and integrality forces `b(x)∈{0,1}`. Clearing a rational denominator gives a multi-tiling instead, so the lemma does not make rational weights Boolean.
- **Remark 2.5 (physical p. 6).** The tile `{0,3}⊂Z_6` has normalized rational weak complement `(1,3/4,1/4,0,1/4,3/4)` of mass 3. Its cyclic mask vanishes at orders 2 and 3 but not 6; this is a direct boundary example showing the integer-mask allocation argument cannot be transferred to rational weights without the T1 hypothesis.
- **Theorem 1.4 (physical pp. 3, 8–13; full statement at p. 3 and proof §4).** Suppose `A⊕B=Z_M`, `N=∏_{i=1}^k p_i^{α_i}|M` is mixed (`k≥2`, distinct primes), `Φ_N|A(X)`, and none of its constituent prime-power factors `Φ_{p_i^{α_i}}` divides `A(X)`. Then for some `q≥1`, either `q=1` or q is a power of a prime dividing N, there is `P∈Q[X]` with nonnegative coefficients, `P(0)=1`, and `A(X^q)P(X)≡U_{qM}(X) mod(X^{qM}−1)`. P satisfies ordinary T1 and fails T2; each coefficient denominator has prime divisors only among primes dividing M; the dilated set qA is a tile of cardinality |A| and its ordinary T2 status agrees with A.
- **Proof chain for Theorem 1.4 (physical pp. 8–13).** Lemma 3.1 constructs a pure-order cuboid filter `R_{N,M}` with coefficients in `{−1,0,1}`, zero mass and Fourier support exactly at order N; if `Φ_N|A`, then `1_A*R=0`. Its negative support has size `s=2^{k−1}M/N`. If the original complement B does not vanish at N, translate B to include zero and take q=1. Otherwise choose a prime p|N and q=p^h≥max(3,s+1); stack q translates of B in distinct residue layers of `Z_{qM}` with shifts chosen to give enough positive autocorrelation at all negative filter positions. Add `H(X)=X R_{N,M}(X^q)` to the normalized autocorrelation. The convolution with `A(X^q)` vanishes; layer-pair counting absorbs every negative coefficient, retaining `f(0)=1`, mass and weak tiling. The perturbation changes only Fourier order qN, retaining prime-power zeros and removing the mixed zero. Lemma 4.1 then transfers rational coefficient mass from exponent x to x+D by adding `(X^{x+D}−X^x)/(ℓm)`, for odd prime ℓ, `mF∈Z[X]`, `deg F<D`, `0<x<D`, and `[X^x]F>0`; subtracting at x and adding at x+D preserves the cyclic coefficient function modulo D. In Theorem 4.2 the chosen `x=1`, `m=K` and ℓ divides M, controlling denominators. The source states all ordinary/period-relative T1/T2 conversions and the qA boundary explicitly.
- **Theorem 5.1 explicit example (physical pp. 14–16).** `A*={0,12,18,24,36}⊂Z_180`, `T={0,1,5,8,10,21}⊂Z_30`, `Q` is T's cyclic autocorrelation mask, `H=X(1−X^{12})(1−X^{18})U_5(X^{36})`, `W=Q U_6(X^{30})+H`, and `F=W/6`. The set tile has subgroup complement `5Z_180`; construction layers are shifts `(0,0,1,3,6,10)`. Here `A*=6A_0`; this explicit example uses composite dilation 6 with a tailored six-layer construction, while general Theorem 4.2 guarantees `q=1` or a prime power dividing N. The signed filter repeats `(1,0,−1,−1,0,1)`; source's difference counts at negative sites are all at least one, hence W is coefficientwise nonnegative. The proof gives `F(0)=1`, `F(1)=36`, cyclic convolution identity, ordinary prime-power signature `{2,3,4,9}`, and `Φ_36∤F`, so ordinary T1 holds and T2 fails. At order 36, the printed remainder is `W≡20X−10X^7 mod Φ_36`, nonzero. The cyclic coefficient function is **not positive definite**, since `w_1=2` and `w_179=1` for W, so it is not even. Do not cite this as a PD counterexample.
- **Theorem 1.5 (physical p. 3; proof Proposition 6.2).** The fixed 30-element tile `T={0,2,3,4,6}+30{0,1,2,3,4,5}⊂Z_180` satisfies ordinary T1/T2 and has unsupported divisor `Φ_6`. For every `L≥2`, any rational nonnegative P with `P(0)=1`, ordinary T1, and `T(X)P(X)≡U_L(X) mod(X^L−1)` must satisfy ordinary T2. Its hypotheses include this specific fixed T and all L; it is not an unrestricted statement for arbitrary A or weak complement period.
- **Appendix A alternative (physical pp. 22–24).** This independent construction uses `Z_M×Z_r` with a new prime `r>2`, `r∤M`; it is not the prime-preserving Theorem 4.2 construction. Under (A.1), `A⊕B=Z_M`, A satisfies T2, mixed `N=∏_{i=1}^k p_i^{α_i}|M` with `k≥2`, `Φ_N|A` and `Φ_N|B`, and every constituent `Φ_{p_i^{α_i}}|B` (the extra cardinality clause in “unsupported divisor” is not assumed), set `D=rM`, `K=r|B|`, `A↑=A×{0}`, `B↑=⋃_{j∈Z_r}(B+t_j)×{j}`. The normalized autocorrelation `g=K^{-1}1_{B↑}*1_{−B↑}` has mass K and tiles with A↑. Put `H(x,j)=R_{N,M}(x)1_{j=1}` and `f=g+εH`. Theorem A.1 assumes rational `ε>0` with `g(x,1)≥ε` at every negative filter site `x∈S^-`; then f is nonnegative, normalized at the identity and complements A↑, with period-relative prime-power zero set `S^*_{B}∪{r}`, period-relative T1 and a T2 failure at N. A↑ retains ordinary T1/T2, while f is not PD: `f(0,1)−f(0,−1)=ε`. Lemma A.2 gives shifts with `g(x,1)≥1/r` when `r−1≥|S^-|`, so one may choose any rational `0<ε≤1/r`, for example `ε=1/K`; Corollary A.3 uses the coefficient-transfer lemma to obtain an ordinary rational-polynomial representative with ordinary T1 and the same T2 failure. These hypotheses and the extra prime are essential to this appendix route.

## Exact model boundary for A175

These results address cyclic mask polynomials and ordinary rational polynomial representatives in a set-tiling framework. Group-ring identities are modulo `X^M−1`, permit wraparound, and differ from a no-wrap product in `Z_{ge0}[X]`. The explicit constructed rational weak complements are not positive definite; an ordinary probability generating polynomial also has total mass normalized to one and may have arbitrary real coefficients. To use this source in A175, one must separately prove a no-wrap lift, the relevant tile/unsupported-divisor hypotheses, and any needed positive-definiteness or coefficient-type conditions. It neither proves that arbitrary real factors are rational/integer nor settles A175.
