## Model and exact result

The paper studies **sets** `A,B⊂Z_M` forming a cyclic unique tiling `A⊕B=Z_M`, equivalently their 0–1 mask polynomials multiply to `1+X+⋯+X^{M−1} mod (X^M−1)`. Its main theorem (Theorem 1.2, physical p. 3) is:

> If `M=p_i²p_j²p_k²` for three distinct odd primes and `A⊕B=Z_M` with `|A|=|B|=p_i p_j p_k`, then **both** `A` and `B` satisfy the Coven–Meyerowitz condition (T2).

Here `S_A` is the set of prime powers `p^α` with `Φ_{p^α}|A(X)`; (T2) says that for every choice of prime powers from distinct primes in `S_A`, the cyclotomic polynomial of their product divides `A(X)`. The quantifiers do not include arbitrary `M`, prime-exponent vectors other than the specified `(2,2,2)`, a prime equal to 2 (all three primes must be odd), unequal complement cardinalities, or arbitrary coefficient polynomials. The paper’s introduction states that this was the three-distinct-odd-prime case not covered by the known ≤2-prime results at the time.

Corollary 1.3 (physical p. 4) combines Theorem 1.2 with external spectral-set results to obtain tiling-implies-spectrum for `Z_M` and for the corresponding union of unit intervals in `R`. It is a corollary in that restricted cyclic setting, not a proof of the full Fuglede conjecture.

## Proof architecture and dependencies

The proof is a long finite-group combinatorial/cyclotomic classification, not a probabilistic convolution argument. §2 (physical pp. 4–11) sets up weighted multisets/mask polynomials modulo `X^M−1`, CRT coordinate grids, divisor exclusion, standard tiling complements, box products and cuboid balances, subgroup reductions, saturating sets, fibers/cofibers, and the fiber-shifting lemma. Several tools are specialized from the authors’ companion work [23] and earlier literature; they are imported as lemmas rather than all reproved from first principles. Proposition 2.1 (p. 6) identifies a standard complement with the other factor satisfying T2; Theorem 2.6 (pp. 9–10) is a separate reduction equivalence, and Corollary 2.7 is the slab-reduction criterion.

§3 (pp. 11–13) states Theorems 3.1–3.2 and sketches the proof. In the unfibered case, Theorem 3.1 reduces by fiber shifts to a standard tile `A♭=Λ`, then Proposition 2.1/Corollary 2.2 yields T2 for both factors. In the fibered case, Theorem 3.2 either makes the same standard reduction or reduces to subgroup containment (Theorem 2.5) or to the hypotheses of the separate slab-reduction Corollary 2.7 (which invokes Theorem 2.6(ii)); each route yields T2. §4 (pp. 13–17) assembles divisor, cyclotomic, plane-bound, saturation, and fibering lemmas. §§5–8 (pp. 17–45) classify and resolve unfibered grids: diagonal boxes, extended corners, structures with missing top differences (odd and even cases), and the remaining corner configurations. §9 (pp. 45–70) treats fibered grids. It separates intersection patterns of `I,J,K`, proves Propositions 9.2–9.4 through lemmas about cuboid balance, fiber directions, cyclotomic divisibility, and lower-scale reductions. §10 acknowledgments and references occupy pp. 70–71.

The oddness and exact square-period/cardinality assumptions are used in the lemmas (for example, several missing-difference and plane arguments explicitly require odd primes); they cannot be dropped on the strength of this theorem. The cited T2-to-standard-complement equivalence and other companion results are proof dependencies, not new consequences established in every generality here.

## A175 interface: explicit non-implication

This source is auxiliary structural context only. Its factorization is a **unique tiling of a finite cyclic group by 0–1 sets**, with coefficients read modulo `X^M−1`. A175 instead asks whether two arbitrary finite-support integer-valued random variables with **nonnegative real probability coefficients** and independent sum uniform on its support must each be uniform; their support sets may have gaps, and no cyclic complement or 0–1 coefficient assumption is given. Converting A175 into this theorem would require additional results proving a set-tiling/cyclic structure and matching its highly specific period and cardinality hypotheses. No such interface is proved here, so neither T2 nor the spectral corollary solves A175.
