## Objects and results

- A **finite group factorization** `A⊕B=G` means each `g∈G` has a unique representation `g=a+b`, `a∈A,b∈B`. The article studies rational periodic tile sets `S=Z^n+A` and spectra of bounded measurable sets tiling with `S`. A finite-group complement and a spectral set are distinct structures; the article links them only through stated Fourier criteria and its geometric tiling hypotheses.
- **Theorem 1.1 (physical pp. 2–6).** For a rational periodic tile `S=Z^n+A`, a rational periodic frequency set is a universal spectrum precisely when the associated finite frequency representatives satisfy the spectral condition for every finite-group complement factorization `A⊕B=G` (with the canonical box attached to `B`). The statement is an iff criterion for universal spectra in this rational-periodic setting; it is not a theorem about arbitrary polynomial factorization over `Z`.
- **Theorems 2.1–2.2 and Corollary 2.3 (physical pp. 7–12).** The introduction calls the last result Theorem 2.3, but the body labels it Corollary 2.3. Fourier orthogonality/completeness gives criteria connecting a bounded measurable tile to finite representative sets. In the canonical-box formulation, `Ω=[0,1]^n+B` and `Λ=Z^n+Γ` are spectral exactly when `|Γ|=|B|` and every nonzero difference in `Γ−Γ` lies among the nontrivial zeros of the finite Fourier transform/mask associated with `B`. The proofs factor the Fourier transform into lattice and finite-representative terms, and use orthogonality plus cardinality/completeness.
- **Theorem 3.1 (physical pp. 12–15).** A geometrically bounded measurable set tiling by a rational periodic tile partitions, up to null sets, into finitely many translated canonical box pieces associated with finite complements `A⊕B_j=G`. This finite decomposition and the Fourier criterion yield the universal-spectrum characterization of Theorem 1.1.
- **Finite cyclic group results (§4, physical pp. 15–20).** The paper reviews Sands' classification of “good” finite abelian groups and proves a strong Tijdeman property for finite cyclic good groups (Theorem 4.1). The listed good cyclic orders have restricted prime-divisor forms; the first cyclic nongood example is `Z_72`. This classification is invoked only in the stated finite cyclic setting. It does not assert all finite abelian groups have the property; examples such as `(Z_4)^3` illustrate higher-rank limits.
- **One-dimensional conclusion (§5, physical pp. 21–25).** The proof uses induction on finite cyclic factorizations and the spectral criterion. Under the paper's stated strong Tijdeman hypothesis, compact one-dimensional translational tiles are spectral; the earlier good-group criterion supplies universal spectra for the corresponding rational periodic tile sets. The conditional assumption remains part of the conclusion.

## A175 interface and limitation

The relevant algebra is in the finite group ring / quotient by relations such as `X^N=1`, with **0–1 set masks** whose coefficients encode unique complements. A175 is an exact identity of probability-generating polynomials in ordinary `Z[x]` (or real polynomials) with nonnegative real coefficients; its finite supports may have gaps, and it supplies neither 0–1 factor masks nor a cyclic modulus and unique-sum complement. An identity modulo `X^N−1` does not imply equality in `Z[x]`. For example, in `Z_4`, `A={1,3}`, `B={0,1}` are complementary, so `(x+x^3)(1+x)≡1+x+x²+x³ mod (x^4−1)`, while the ordinary product is `x+x²+x³+x⁴`, not `1+x+x²+x³`. This source therefore provides only an auxiliary Fourier/set-tiling framework after a separate bridge to a finite cyclic group has been proved.
