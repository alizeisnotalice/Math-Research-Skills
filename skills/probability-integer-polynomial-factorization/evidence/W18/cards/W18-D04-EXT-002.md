## Exact theorem and assumptions

**Theorem (physical p. 1; printed p. 186; unnumbered).** If `g∈Z[z]` is monic, `g(0)≠0`, and is not a product of cyclotomic polynomials, and `k` is its number of nonzero coefficients, then
`M(g) ≥ 1 + 1/(a exp(b k^k))`,
where the paper states absolute constants `a≤13911` and `b≤2.27`. Mahler measure is `M(g)=|a_0|∏_i max(1,|α_i|)` for leading coefficient `a_0` and roots `α_i` (for the monic case the leading factor is one). The title-page abstract says this sharpens an earlier sparsity-dependent bound. The theorem is specifically for sparse **integer-coefficient monic polynomials** with nonzero constant term; it is not a lower bound for arbitrary real-coefficient probability factors.

## Proof chain

The proof occupies physical pp. 2–10. The source introduces a multivariate sparse polynomial `F(z)=Σ_{j∈J}a(j)z^j`, directional substitutions/derivatives, and **extended cyclotomic polynomials** `Φ_m(z_1^{v_1},…,z_N^{v_N})` with coprime integer exponents.

- **Lemma 1 (physical pp. 2–3; printed 187–188)** gives linearly independent exponent-difference vectors when a cyclotomic factor divides a directional specialization, under a non-cyclotomic residual-factor condition and the same-term-count hypothesis. Its proof tracks multiplicities through the derivative and rules out the one-dimensional difference-set case by contradiction.
- **Lemma 2 (physical p. 4; printed 189)** is a geometric/lattice bound: if the non-extended-cyclotomic residual specialization has more than half its degree accounted for by ordinary cyclotomic factors (with multiplicity), then a nonzero integer vector annihilating the direction has a bounded height. It cites the earlier proof with a modified degree parameter.
- **Lemma 3 (physical pp. 4–5; printed 189–190)** recursively constructs an integer matrix factorization of an exponent vector with controlled matrix height and no short nonzero integer annihilator. The minimal-dimension contradiction uses a basis of the integer lattice orthogonal to a proposed short vector (Cassels’ geometry-of-numbers result is cited).
- **Lemma 4 (physical p. 5; printed p. 190)** supplies a rational approximation/lattice decomposition with controlled integer-vector length and denominator.
- **Main induction (physical pp. 6–10; printed pp. 191–195)** proceeds on the number `k` of nonzero terms. Smyth’s result reduces to a product of reciprocal polynomials, possibly with a factor `z−1`. The authors embed the exponent pattern into a multivariate polynomial, split off all extended-cyclotomic factors, and control the residual degree using Lemmas 1–4. If the residual contains enough ordinary cyclotomic degree, Lemma 2 yields a short relation and the cited earlier Mahler-measure decomposition argument produces a smaller-term polynomial; if the distinguished variable is absent, Jensen’s formula integrates out one variable and induction applies to a polynomial with fewer terms. The recurrence estimates on pp. 6–7 and the two cases on pp. 8–10 yield the displayed absolute constants.

The proof cites prior Mahler-measure arguments and Smyth’s reciprocal reduction; this card does not reprove those imported results or claim the theorem was independently reproduced.

## W18/A175 use and limits

**Adjacent sparse-polynomial method; exclude from direct A175 qualifying-source count unless the reviewer finds a specific transfer.** It demonstrates a quantitative arithmetic tool for non-cyclotomic sparse integer polynomials. A175 concerns factorization of a uniform finite-support probability generating function into nonnegative real factors, where integrality, monicity, nonzero constant term, and non-cyclotomic hypotheses are not guaranteed. No transfer from this Mahler bound to arbitrary probability factors is established here.
