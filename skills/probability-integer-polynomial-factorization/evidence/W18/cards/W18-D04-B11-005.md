## Model and definitions

All masks are ordinary integer polynomials of finite subsets, but a tiling assertion is in a specified finite cyclic group. Write [L]_X=1+X+⋯+X^(L−1). For A⊂Z_M, its mask is A(X)=∑_{a∈A}X^a, with representatives chosen in [0,M−1]; A⊕B=Z_M means every residue has exactly one representation a+b mod M, equivalently A(X)B(X)≡[M]_X mod X^M−1.

For the ordinary mask polynomial of the chosen representatives, the full prime-power signature is
\[
S_A=\{p^\alpha:p\text{ prime},\ \alpha\ge1,\ \Phi_{p^\alpha}\mid A(X)\}.
\]
It is not restricted to exact prime-power factors of M. The paper uses the Coven–Meyerowitz conditions
\[
\text{(T1)}\quad |A|=\prod_{s\in S_A}\Phi_s(1),\qquad
\text{(T2)}\quad \Phi_{s_1\cdots s_k}|A(X)
\]
for every finite selection of pairwise coprime s_i∈S_A. A divisor Φ_s|A, s>1, is unsupported when every prime dividing s divides |A|, but Φ_{p^α}∤A for every exact prime-power factor p^α||s. In the finite-cyclic application, unsupported orders under discussion satisfy s|M; this is the condition that makes the unsupported-divisor statement independent of the chosen representatives. It does not truncate the full ordinary-mask signature S_A above.

## Theorem and proof cards

### Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 — square-period three-prime classification (PDF p.2)

Let p<q<r be distinct primes, N=pqr, M=N², and
\[
\mathcal E(p,q,r)=\{A\subset Z_M: |A|=N,\ \Phi_{d^2}|A\ \text{for every }d|N,\ d>1,\ \Phi_N|A\}.
\]
Every A∈E(p,q,r) is confined to one residue class modulo r, and
\[
\mathcal E(p,q,r)\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r\in\langle p,q\rangle:=\{ap+bq:a,b\in Z_{\ge0}\}.
\]
Corollary 1.2 says there is a cyclic tiling A⊕B=Z_(pqr)² with unsupported Φ_pqr for A iff the same semigroup condition holds. In every such tiling |A|=|B|=pqr, S_A={p²,q²,r²}, S_B={p,q,r}, both tiles satisfy (T1),(T2), and A is confined modulo r.

Proof dependencies: §2 proves the smoothing/Apéry identity for the two-prime polynomial and records that for arbitrary coprime a,b>1 the natural polynomial is (1−X)(1−X^(ab))/((1−X^a)(1−X^b)), not necessarily Φ_ab. §3 obtains the necessary-and-sufficient square-signature CRT normal form. §4's two-cylinder alternative and §5's integer-capacity inequalities rule out all three low functions being nonconstant, giving confinement modulo at least one prime. Theorem 6.1 then characterizes confinement modulo a specified prime s by s belonging to the semigroup generated by the other two primes. With p<q<r, confinement modulo p would require p∈⟨q,r⟩, impossible since q>p; confinement modulo q would require q∈⟨p,r⟩, which forces the coefficient of r to be zero and q to be a multiple of p, also impossible. Thus the confined prime is r. For sufficiency, Theorem 7.1 supplies an explicit cyclic tile.

### Theorem 3.1 — square-signature normal form (PDF pp.5–6)

Under M=p²q²r², N=pqr, use CRT Z_M≅Z_(p²)×Z_(q²)×Z_(r²) and split each coordinate into high and low digits. A size-N set has all seven square-signature zeros iff it has the unique form
\[
A=\{(pu+f(v,w),\,qv+g(u,w),\,rw+h(u,v)):(u,v,w)\in[0,p-1]\times[0,q-1]\times[0,r-1]\},
\]
where f,g,h take low-digit values modulo p,q,r, respectively, and for every two distinct high triples at least one coordinate has both different high digits and equal low digits (condition (K)). Every such A tiles with the standard complement
\[
B_\square=[0,p-1]\times[0,q-1]\times[0,r-1]
\]
in CRT coordinates. The additional condition Φ_pqr|A, hence A∈E(p,q,r), is exactly
\[
\sum_{u,v,w}\zeta_p^{f(v,w)}\zeta_q^{g(u,w)}\zeta_r^{h(u,v)}=0.
\]
Proof: construct the N×N character matrix indexed by the high-digit grid and A. The square-order zeros make its rows orthonormal; since it is square, its columns are too. Row/column orthogonality yields the normal form and (K); conversely (K) yields column orthogonality. The complement is the product of complete low-digit intervals. The order-pqr character factors through the low digits and gives the final scalar equation.

### Theorem 5.3 and Theorem 6.1 — confinement and arithmetic criterion (PDF pp.9–10)

Theorem 5.3: every A∈E(p,q,r) is confined modulo at least one of p,q,r, equivalently at least one of f,g,h in the normal form is constant. Its proof combines Lemma 4.1 (if none is constant, the coordinate-separation condition forces three disjoint support cylinders) with Lemma 4.2 (low-character vanishing then gives positive integers n,m,k,a,b,c,d satisfying p²n+q²m+r²k=pqr, 1≤a<p, 1≤b<q, c,d≥1, c+d≤r, and (p−1)n≤bc, (q−1)m≤ad, (r−1)k≤(p−a)(q−b)). Lemma 5.1 gives 1−α−β−γ≥2αβγ under the three capacity bounds; Lemma 5.2 proves the resulting integer constraints impossible. The rendered PDF p.9 shows the exact contradiction
\[
4(p-1)(q-1)(r-1)\le pqr+2(pq+pr+qr),
\]
whose difference is positive for p≥5, with separate p=2,3 cases. Thus the low-order zero is essential; Remark 5.4 gives an explicit square-signature example satisfying (K) but with nonzero low character sum.

Theorem 6.1: for any three distinct primes p,q,r, with no ordering assumption, there exists a member of E(p,q,r) confined modulo r iff r∈⟨p,q⟩. Necessity: translate h to zero; by Lemma 3.2, in each fixed w-layer one low vector is constant. Set W_ij=# {(u,v,w):f(v,w)=i,g(u,w)=j}. The row/column sums are multiples of p,q; character-basis independence in Q(ζ_pq) makes W additive. Subtracting a minimum entry t∈⟨p,q⟩, row/column differences are multiples of p,q, so the total mass yields r=p(v−c)+q(u−d) with nonnegative coefficients. Sufficiency is the specialization L=r, e=1 of Theorem 7.1. Corollary 6.3 gives the full layer form: after translation h=0, every layer has f_w or g_w constant, and the sum of the layer character products vanishes.

Corollary 6.4 (PDF p.11) also gives gcd(M,A−A)=r, |Stab(A)| divides pq, and after A=rC, the quotient C⊂Z_(M/r) is primitive, has signature {p²,q²,r}, satisfies (T2), and has unsupported Φ_pq. Complements split independently into r slices D_j, each complementing C. These are exact structural conclusions in this three-prime square-period model only.

### Theorem 7.1 and Theorem 7.4 — Apéry construction and all complements (PDF pp.12–14)

Let Q=pq, integers m≥1,e≥0, L=r^m∈⟨p,q⟩, h=r^e, K=QhL, M=QK. Then
\[
A(X)=[Q]_{X^K}[L]_{X^h}\Phi_Q(X^h),\qquad
B_0(X)=[h]_X[Q]_{X^{hL}}
\]
are 0–1 masks giving A⊕B_0=Z_M, with |A|=QL, |B_0|=Qh, and exact signatures
\[
S_A=\{p^2,q^2\}\cup\{r^{e+1},\ldots,r^{e+m}\},\qquad
S_{B_0}=\{p,q\}\cup\{r,r^2,\ldots,r^e\}.
\]
Consequently each of Φ_(Qr^j), 0≤j≤e, is unsupported for A; the e=0 second signature union is empty. Proof mechanism: the support of [L]_(X^h)Φ_Q(X^h) is h Ap(⟨p,q⟩;L)⊂[0,K); its Q-translates by K are disjoint in [0,M). The Apéry smoothing identity and a telescoping geometric-series identity give the ordinary polynomial identity
\[
A(X)B_0(X)=[M]_X\Phi_Q(X^h).
\]
For any integer polynomial R, [M]_XR(X)≡R(1)[M]_X mod X^M−1, and Φ_Q(1)=1; hence this is a cyclic tiling, not an ordinary product identity A(X)B_0(X)=[M]_X. Factoring Φ_Q(X^(r^e))=∏_(j=0)^e Φ_(Qr^j)(X) and evaluating at X=1 gives the stated exact signatures and unsupported orders.

Theorem 7.4 (PDF p.14) classifies every complement of this fixed A, not just B_0:
\[
B=\{t+h a_t+hLj+K\ell_{t,j}:0\le t<h,\ 0\le j<Q\},
\quad a_t\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\},\quad \ell_{t,j}\in\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}.
\]
All parameters are independent; the count is L^h Q^(Qh). Every complement satisfies (T1),(T2), has exact signature S_(B_0), and the gcd in Z[X] of all complement masks is B_0(X). Proof: quotient by the K-period subgroup; in each residue t mod h, Lemma 7.3 says complements of the Apéry core in Z_(QL) are exactly a_t+L{0,…,Q−1}; each projected point has an independent lift Kℓ_(t,j). The resulting B is a complete residue system mod Qh, proving its signature by cardinality at X=1 and its cyclotomic zeros.

Corollary 7.6 yields, for every p<q<r, an unsupported Φ_pqr at period p²q²r³; the square period (pqr)² is possible exactly under the semigroup condition. For fixed admissible m, the construction permits arbitrarily many unsupported scales while |A|=pqr^m stays fixed.

### Proposition 8.1 — sharp least cyclic periods (PDF p.15)

Among cyclic tilings with an unsupported Φ_s, s|M, the least period is M=180. If s has three distinct prime factors, the least period is 900, and the least |A| is 30. Proof: each prime of an unsupported order needs both an excluded prime-power level and a different supported level, so occurs in M to exponent at least two; the two-prime CM necessity and two-prime unsupported-divisor exclusion rule out periods with only two prime factors. The examples p,q,r=2,3,5 attain the lower bounds. For the square-period core C_5={0,2,3,4,6}, 1+X²+X³+X⁴+X⁶=Φ_5(X)Φ_6(X); the lifts have periods 180 and 900. The explicit CRT construction on PDF p.16 gives an aperiodic A,B_square tiling of Z_900. The 180 example A⊕B_0=Z_180 has |A|=30, |B_0|=6, signature {4,5,9}, unsupported Φ_6; the 900 square example has {4,9,25} and unsupported Φ_6,Φ_30. Appendix A pages 17–18 classifies layer choices at 900 and provides exact enumeration counts, but the theorem proofs do not rely on numerical enumeration.

## Integer-coefficient, cyclic, and no-wrap boundary

This paper studies 0–1 masks and exact cyclotomic divisibility in finite cyclic integer tilings. It does not show that arbitrary nonnegative-real PGF factors have integer or 0–1 coefficients, nor that an A175 ordinary convolution factorization induces a finite cyclic tiling. To transfer a cyclic identity to an ordinary polynomial product, one needs an explicit no-wrap embedding (for chosen nonnegative representatives, max A+max B<M is sufficient) or an independent argument handling reduction modulo X^M−1. The paper's examples genuinely use wrap: the exact finite check in math/b11_exact_checks.run.json finds maximum representative sums 181>180, 909>900, and 1669>900. Hence those examples prove cyclic convolution identities, not ordinary polynomial identities. The three-prime results are not a proof of the general Coven–Meyerowitz conjecture; all classification statements above retain the stated period/signature hypotheses.

## A175 use and unresolved interface

Conditional auxiliary method: if a candidate instance is already represented by 0–1 masks A,B in a specified finite cyclic group and has the three-prime square-period hypotheses above, Theorem 1.1 classifies its confinement and existence criterion, while Theorems 7.1 and 7.4 give a construction and all complements in the stated Apéry family. The original A175 asks about two arbitrary finite-support integer-valued independent laws with normalized nonnegative-real PGFs and a uniform sum over its support. To use this paper, one must first prove a finite cyclic integer-mask representation with a compatible period and prove that the ordinary product/convolution corresponds to the cyclic one. Neither integerization of real factors nor a no-wrap/quotient bridge is supplied here.
