# Positive example: preserve support gaps

Suppose $X,Y$ are independent and $X+Y$ is uniform on a finite support $S\subset\mathbb Z$. Translate both summands so their minima are zero. Form their probability-generating functions $P_X,P_Y$, and let $N=|S|$. Then $C=N P_XP_Y=\sum_{s\in S}z^s$ is a monic 0–1 polynomial. Its top coefficient is 1 because the maximum sum has a unique pair of support points. Normalizing each factor by its own leading coefficient gives monic nonnegative factors $A,B$ with $AB=C$. This converts the probability question to the unfair 0–1 polynomial conjecture without filling holes in either support.

For a bounded candidate $A$, the convolution equations are diagonal sums of $a_i b_j$. A propagation or quantifier-elimination certificate can exclude only the encoded candidate under its degree and branch bounds. State those bounds with the result.
