## Definitions and results

For $p\ge1$, the paper says $X$ satisfies $\mathrm{SMP}_p(\kappa)$ if, whenever $T\subset\mathbb R^d$ has $|T|\ge e^p$ and
$$
\|\langle t-s,X\rangle\|_p\ge A\quad (s\ne t\in T),
$$
then
$$
\mathbb E\sup_{t,s\in T}\langle t-s,X\rangle\ge\kappa A.
$$
It satisfies $\mathrm{SMP}(\kappa)$ if this holds for all $p\ge1$.

- **Theorem 3.6 (PDF pp. 10–11; printed pp. 260–261).** For an arbitrary log-concave vector $X$, $p\ge1$, and a finite $T$ with pairwise $L_p$ separation at least $A$, the same lower bound holds with universal $\kappa>0$ provided the cardinality is at least $\exp(\exp(p))$. The extra cardinality exponent is part of the general result.
- **Corollary 3.8 (PDF pp. 11–12; printed pp. 261–262).** If $X$ is isotropic log-concave, then for any weights $a_i$ and $p\ge1$,
  $$
  \left(\mathbb E\max_{i\le d}|a_iX_i|^p\right)^{1/p}
  \le C\left(\mathbb E\max_{i\le d}|a_iX_i|+\max_{i\le d}\|a_iX_i\|_p\right).
  $$
  This is a weighted coordinate $\ell_\infty$ statement, not the arbitrary-norm conjecture.
- **Theorem 4.3 (PDF pp. 14–17; printed pp. 264–267).** For an unconditional log-concave $X$ and $p\ge1$, the universal Sudakov lower bound holds when $|T|\ge e^{p^2}$ and the same pairwise $L_p$ separation holds.
- **Corollary 5.3 (PDF p. 19; printed p. 269).** Densities proportional to $\exp[-\varphi(\|x\|_r)]$, for $1\le r\le\infty$ and nondecreasing convex $\varphi$, satisfy SMP with a universal constant; this includes rotationally invariant log-concave vectors.

The article also states Conjecture 1.6: every log-concave vector has SMP with a universal constant. The results above establish it only for the stated classes or weakened cardinality thresholds.

## Proof dependencies read

The initial lemmas reduce to symmetric vectors and show stability under linear maps. Symmetric one-dimensional log-concave moment regularity controls changes in moment order. For general log-concave measures, the proof uses exponential concentration, a truncation inequality for finite families of linear forms, and a lower bound on maxima of sufficiently many uncorrelated coordinates; a geometric decomposition then yields Theorem 3.6. The unconditional theorem compares moments and suprema to Rademacher and exponential product vectors, with a sparse-support reduction before applying the independent-coordinate Sudakov inequality. For invariant densities, radial decomposition $X=RZ$ (uniform $Z$ on the unit body) and a comparison of radial moments transfer SMP from a product reference law. Chaining in Section 6 gives the moment corollary; it retains a logarithmic factor in the general SMP case.

## Transfer limit

These estimates concern separated linear forms, weighted coordinate maxima, or special invariant/unconditional classes. Corollary 3.8 does not permit replacing an arbitrary norm by a weighted coordinate maximum. None of these conclusions proves C153 for arbitrary log-concave laws and arbitrary norms or C154's every-set $Z_p$ enlargement.
