## Euclidean strong/weak moment comparison

For a finite-dimensional Euclidean random vector $X$, the paper defines
$$
\sigma_p(X)=\sup_{|z|\le1}(\mathbb E|\langle z,X\rangle|^p)^{1/p}.
$$
For $p>0$, $m=\lceil p\rceil$, and $\lambda\ge1$, condition $H(p,\lambda)$ requires that for every linear map $A:E\to\mathbb R^m$ for which $Y=AX$ is nondegenerate, there is a gauge $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb R^m$ with $\mathbb E\|Y\|<\infty$ and $(\mathbb E\|Y\|^p)^{1/p}\le\lambda\mathbb E\|Y\|$ (Definition 4.1, PDF p. 4).

**Theorem 4.2 (PDF p. 5).** If $X$ satisfies $H(p,\lambda)$, then
$$
(\mathbb E|X|^p)^{1/p}\le c\{\lambda\mathbb E|X|+\sigma_p(X)\},
$$
with universal $c$. The proof first uses a Gaussian minimax/separation argument to find a linear functional with controlled positive-part moment relative to the gauge mean; conditioning on a Gaussian matrix and selecting its directional gauges turns the H-condition into the displayed Euclidean bound.

**Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.3 (PDF p. 7).** If $X$ is centered and $(-1/r)$-concave in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, $r>1$, then for $0<p<r$ it satisfies $H(p,\lambda(p,r))$, with
$$
\lambda(p,r)=c\left(\frac r{r-1}\right)^3\left(\frac r{r-p}\right)^4.
$$
The paper combines this with Theorem 4.2. Corollary 5.3 states that every log-concave random vector in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space and every $p>0$ satisfy the same comparison with a universal constant $C$.

**Theorem 6.2 and Theorem 6.3 (PDF p. 12).** For centered $n$-dimensional $(-1/r)$-concave $X$, $r>1$, and $1\le p<\min\{r,n/2\}$,
$$
(\mathbb E|X|^{-p})^{-1/p}\ge C_4(p,r)\{\mathbb E|X|-C\sigma_p(X)\},\quad
C_4(p,r)=c\left(\frac{r^2}{(r-p)(r-1)}\right)^{-3}\left(1+\frac c{r-p}\right)^{-1/p}.
$$
For $0<p<1$, the lower bound is $c_0(1-p)(r-1)\mathbb E|X|/r$. Markov's inequality gives Theorem 6.3: if additionally $\mathbb E|X|\ge2C\sigma_p(X)$, then for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$,
$$
\mathbb P(|X|\le\varepsilon\mathbb E|X|)\le(2C_4(p,r)^{-1}\varepsilon)^p.
$$

## Scope and interface

These are Euclidean norm estimates. The paper's $\sigma_p$ uses the Euclidean dual unit ball; none of the cited results asserts C153 for arbitrary norms. The negative-moment and small-ball theorems additionally require centering, the stated $r,p,n$ range, and the mean-to-weak-moment condition for Theorem 6.3. The contribution is useful for Euclidean moment and small-ball interfaces, not for C154's all-set $Z_p$-dilation.
