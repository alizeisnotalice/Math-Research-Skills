## Main statements

For a random vector $X$, define $\Lambda_X(y)=\log\mathbb E e^{\langle y,X\rangle}$ and $\Lambda_X^*(x)=\sup_y(\langle x,y\rangle-\Lambda_X(y))$. IC($\beta$) means the infimum-convolution inequality holds with cost $\Lambda_X^*(\cdot/\beta)$; convex IC restricts test functions to convex functions bounded below.

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 3): every symmetric real random variable with log-concave tails, i.e. $t\mapsto-\log\mathbb P(|X|\ge t)$ convex, satisfies convex IC($\beta$) for $\beta\le1680e$. Tensorization yields Corollary 2.2 for symmetric vectors with independent coordinates having log-concave tails.

Theorem 2.4 (PDF pp. 4–5): if a symmetric vector $X\in\mathbb R^n$ has $\alpha$-regular moments and satisfies convex IC($\beta$), then for every norm and $p\ge2$,
$$
\|\|X\|-\mathbb E\|X\|\|_p\le C\alpha\beta\,\sigma_{\|\cdot\|,X}(p),
$$
where $C=4\sqrt{2}e<16$. The moment regularity convention here is $\|\langle X,\theta\rangle\|_p\le\alpha(p/q)\|\langle X,\theta\rangle\|_q$ for $p\ge q\ge2$.

Corollary 2.5 (p. 4) consequently gives for symmetric independent-coordinate vectors with log-concave tails, every norm and $p\ge2$,
$$
\|\|X\|\|_p\le\mathbb E\|X\|+D\sigma_{\|\cdot\|,X}(p).
$$
The first-moment coefficient is exactly 1. From the displayed theorem constants one can take $D=(4\sqrt{2}e)(1680e)$; the paper states the looser numerical bound $D<70223$.

## Proof mechanism and limitations

The one-dimensional theorem uses a characterization of convex weak transport/convex ICI by one-dimensional tail costs, then applies log-concavity of the tails to compare the optimal cost with the Legendre transform. Tensorization gives the product result. The moment theorem combines convex ICI with $\alpha$-regularity and a concentration-to-moment argument. The paper explicitly gives an $\alpha$-regular symmetric variable without log-concave tails for which no finite optimal convex IC constant exists, so regular moments alone do not replace its tail hypothesis.

The result concerns independent coordinates, not an arbitrary dependent log-concave vector, and the displayed moment corollary only covers $p\ge2$. It neither proves C153 in full nor the measure expansion C154.
