## Main results

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent, centered random variables with finite moments, and assume that for a fixed $\alpha<\infty$,
$$
\|X_i\|_{2q}\le\alpha\|X_i\|_q\quad\text{for every }q\ge2\text{ and }i\le n.
$$
**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 1)** states that for every $p\ge1$ and nonempty $T\subset\mathbb R^n$,
$$
\left(\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}\left|\sum_{i=1}^nt_iX_i\right|^p\right)^{1/p}
\le C(\alpha)\left[\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}\left|\sum_{i=1}^nt_iX_i\right|+
\sup_{t\in T}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^nt_iX_i\right\|_p\right].
$$
The constant depends only on the moment-regularity parameter $\alpha$.

**Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 1–2)** is a converse in the iid case: if iid $X_i$ satisfy the displayed weak/strong comparison uniformly for all $p,n,T$ with constant $L$, then $\|X_1\|_{2p}\le\alpha(L)\|X_1\|_p$ for all $p\ge2$. The proof notes it is enough to assume the comparison for $T=\{\pm e_j:1\le j\le n\}$.

Corollary 1.3 converts the moment comparison to a tail comparison with constants depending on $\alpha$; Corollary 1.4 gives $p$-to-$q$ growth for suprema with exponent $\max\{1/2,\log_2\alpha\}$ (PDF pp. 2, 11–13). The paper states that this exponent is optimal (Remark 1.5, p. 2).

## Proof chain read

Section 2 first proves the result for unconditional index sets and symmetric independent variables. A power transformation reduces the moment-regularity assumption to the regular-growth case, then a Bernoulli comparison and symmetrization remove the restrictions on $T$ and on symmetry. Proposition 3.1 controls the random-sign conditional moment using the Hitczenko Rademacher norm estimate, truncation at a level $a$ determined by the first $p$ rearranged coefficients, and Talagrand comparison for Bernoulli processes; its two pieces are bounded by the expected supremum plus the weak moment (PDF pp. 6–9). Theorem 1.1 follows by integrating over signs and applying the unconditional-set result to auxiliary index sets (PDF pp. 9–11). For Theorem 1.2, choose $n=\lfloor(4L)^{2p}\rfloor+1$ and $A=n^{1/p}\|X_1\|_p$; tail integration compares the maximum of $n$ iid copies above $A$ to $\|X_1\|_{2p}$, and the assumed inequality forces the desired regularity (PDF pp. 13–14). The printed proof concludes that one may take $\alpha(L)=O(L^2)$.

## Transfer limit

Independence, centering and the coordinate-wise moment condition are essential hypotheses. The authors explicitly distinguish this from the dependent log-concave-vector problem. This result does not prove arbitrary-norm C153 for general log-concave laws and has no direct C154 enlargement conclusion.
