## Interpolating functional inequality and concentration

For a probability measure $\mu$ and nonnegative $f\in L^2(\mu)$, the paper defines
$$
\operatorname{Var}^{(p)}_\mu(f)=\mathbb E_\mu f^2-(\mathbb E_\mu f^p)^{2/p},\qquad 1\le p<2.
$$
As $p\uparrow2$, this functional divided by $2-p$ tends to $\tfrac12\operatorname{Ent}_\mu(f^2)$. The property $I_\mu(a)$ with constant $C$ is the inequality $\operatorname{Var}^{(p)}_\mu(f)\le C(2-p)^a\mathcal E(f)$ for every allowed nonnegative smooth $f$ and every $p\in[1,2)$; $a=0$ and $a=1$ recover the Poincaré and log-Sobolev endpoints up to the paper's stated normalization.

**Proposition 1 (PDF p. 9, tensorization).** If each coordinate measure $\mu_i$ satisfies $I(a)$ with the same $C$, then the product measure $\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\mu_i$ satisfies it with energy $\mathcal E(f)=\mathbb E\|\nabla f\|_2^2$. The proof first establishes subadditivity of $\operatorname{Var}^{(p)}$ on products using the convexity class $\Phi$ and conditional expectations, then sums the coordinate inequalities.

**Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 9–11).** Suppose $a\in[0,1]$, $C>0$, and the Euclidean-gradient inequality
$$
\mathbb E_\mu f^2-(\mathbb E_\mu f^p)^{2/p}\le C(2-p)^a\mathbb E_\mu\|\nabla f\|_2^2
$$
holds for every smooth $f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)$ and every $p\in[1,2)$. Then every 1-Lipschitz $h$ (Euclidean metric) has $\mathbb E_\mu|h|<\infty$, and for a universal $K>0$,
$$
\mu(h-\mathbb E_\mu h\ge t\sqrt C)\le
\begin{cases}e^{-Kt^2},&0\le t\le1,\\ e^{-Kt^{2/(2-a)}},&t\ge1.\end{cases}
$$
The proof specifies $K=1/3$. It applies the functional inequality to $e^{\lambda h/2}$, iterates an exponential-moment estimate, optimizes $p,\lambda$ in the two regimes, and removes boundedness by Lipschitz truncation. Applying the theorem to $-h$ gives the corresponding lower-tail estimate.

**Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 11–18, sharpness example).** For $a\in[0,1]$, let $r=2/(2-a)$ and $c_r=[2\Gamma(1+1/r)]^{-1}$. The product generalized-Gaussian law on $\mathbb R^n$ with density
$$
\mu_r(dx)=c_r^n\exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|^r\right)dx
$$
satisfies $I(a)$ with a universal constant independent of $a$ and $n$, for every smooth nonnegative $f$ and $p\in[1,2)$. Proposition 1 reduces this to one dimension; a quantile transport between the symmetric exponential and generalized-Gaussian laws transfers a weighted inequality with weight $\max(1,|x|^a)$. The later proposition gives examples showing the concentration exponent's order cannot generally be improved.

## Proof coverage and transfer limits

The functional inequality is an input in Theorem 1, not a conclusion for all log-concave measures. The theorem controls Euclidean Lipschitz functions and does not furnish a dimension-free comparison for arbitrary norms (C153), nor a $Z_p(\mu)$-shaped enlargement for every Borel set (C154). Keep the precise law and energy inequality as hypotheses when using it.
