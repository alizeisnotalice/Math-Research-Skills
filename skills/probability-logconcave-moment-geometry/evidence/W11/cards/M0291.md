# M0291 — An Almost Constant Lower Bound of the Isoperimetric Coefficient in the KLS Conjecture

**Source and read evidence.** Yuansi Chen, *Geometric and Functional Analysis* 31 (2021), no. 1, pp. 34–61, DOI [10.1007/s00039-021-00558-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-021-00558-4). The local 28-page PDF is the complete published article (physical pages 1–28, printed pages 34–61), SHA-256 `2f456fd3b39ce0de579b2601cf49244d395fdfca950d3a0924603c652e23b7df`; it was read through references. Write $\psi(p)$ for the Cheeger isoperimetric coefficient of a log-concave density $p$ on $\mathbb R^d$, and let $\rho(p)=\|\operatorname{Cov}(p)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. **Theorem 1 (PDF p. 4, equation (5))** states that a universal $c>0$ exists such that, for every log-concave density $p$ in $\mathbb R^d$ and every integer $\ell\ge1$,
$$
\psi(p)\ge\frac{1}{[c\ell(\log d+1)]^{\ell/2}\,d^{16/\ell}\,\sqrt{\rho(p)}}.
$$
The displayed dimension factor is $d^{16/\ell}$; the numerator of the exponent is 16 and its denominator is $\ell$. In particular, choosing $\ell=\left\lceil\sqrt{\log d/\log\log d}\right\rceil$ for sufficiently large $d$ gives a lower bound of the form
$$
\psi(p)\ge\frac{1}{d^{c'(\log\log d/\log d)^{1/2}}\sqrt{\rho(p)}}
$$
for a universal $c'>0$. This is not dimension-free KLS; the covariance scale and slowly varying dimension loss remain.

## Proof mechanism

The proof first establishes the same bound for compactly supported log-concave densities (Lemma 1) by induction on $\ell$. Eldan stochastic localization tilts the density by a Gaussian factor; its mean/covariance SDE and martingale evolution of a half-mass set control how much boundary measure survives while the Gaussian component grows. An isoperimetric bound for densities more log-concave than a Gaussian then transfers back to the initial density. The spectral-norm potential is controlled over two time intervals using Lemmas 6–8; their tensor estimates are supplied by Lemmas 9–13, including log-concave third-moment bounds and Brascamp–Lieb/Hessian-Poincaré estimates. Lemma 2 performs the inductive improvement, and Lemmas 1–2 yield Theorem 1. To extend from compact support, the paper truncates to a ball retaining a fixed large fraction of the mass and compares the resulting Cheeger coefficient. Appendix A verifies the stochastic differential identities and existence/uniqueness steps. The method is stochastic localization plus a dimension-dependent induction; it does not prove a universal KLS constant.
