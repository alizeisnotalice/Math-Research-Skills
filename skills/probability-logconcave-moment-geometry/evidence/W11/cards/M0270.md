## Main result and assumptions

For every convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, **Theorem 1.1** gives a convex body $T\subset\mathbb R^n$ such that
$$
d(K,T)<1+\varepsilon,\qquad L_T<\frac{c}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},
$$
where $d$ is the paper's translation-and-dilation geometric distance (bounded above by Banach–Mazur distance), and $c$ is universal. **Corollary 1.2** concludes $L_K<Cn^{1/4}$ by taking $\varepsilon=n^{-1/2}$ and using the Paouris stability lemma proved in Section 4. **Corollary 4.3** transfers this bound to the isotropic constant $L_f$ of every integrable log-concave function $f$.

## Proof dependencies read

The proof first associates to a log-concave $f$ the ray-integral body
$$
K_{n+1}(f)=\left\{x:(n+1)\int_0^\infty f(rx)r^n\,dr\ge f(0)\right\}.
$$
Ball's convexity lemma makes this a convex body; the indicator identity $K_{n+1}(1_K)=K$, pointwise comparison of densities, and polar-coordinate moment identities show that if $f$ varies by at most a factor $m^n$ on $K$, then a translate of $K_{n+1}(f)$ is within factor $m$ of $K$ and its isotropic constant is universally comparable to $L_f$.

For the perturbation, define $F_K(x)=\log\bigl(|K|^{-1}\int_K e^{\langle x,y\rangle}dy\bigr)$. The gradient maps onto $\operatorname{int}K$, its Hessian is the covariance of the exponentially tilted uniform law on $K$, and the measure with density $\det\nabla^2F_K$ is transported by $\nabla F_K$ to Lebesgue measure on $K$. A volume lower bound for the polar of $\operatorname{conv}(K,-K)$ therefore yields a point in a prescribed small polar dilation where the tilted law has small covariance determinant. Its density $e^{\langle x,y\rangle}1_K(y)$ has oscillation at most $e^{\pm\varepsilon n}$; the ray-integral body then supplies $T$ with the asserted distance and $L_T<c/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. The corollary uses Paouris concentration to compare isotropic constants of sufficiently close bodies.

## Interface and limits

This is a convex-body perturbation and slicing/isotropic-constant result. It does not give a dimension-free arbitrary-norm moment estimate for a fixed law (C153), nor an all-set enlargement by the moment body $Z_p(\mu)$ (C154). Its corollary retains an $n^{1/4}$ dependence. The local manuscript's relationship to the published pagination remains unverified.
