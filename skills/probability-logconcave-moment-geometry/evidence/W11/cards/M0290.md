## Definitions and separate theorems

For a random vector with finite first moments, the paper sets
$$
t(k,X)=\inf\left\{t>0:\frac1t\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb E\left[|X_i|\mathbf1_{\{|X_i|\ge t\}}\right]\le k\right\},\qquad
t^*(q,X)=\inf\left\{t>0:\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb P(|X_i|\ge t)\le q\right\},\quad 0<q<n.
$$
Write $k\text{-max}_i|X_i|$ for the random $k$th largest coordinate modulus.

- **Theorem 2 (PDF p. 2).** If, for some $\alpha\ge1$, the pairwise tail condition
  $$
  \mathbb P(|X_i|\ge s,|X_j|\ge t)\le\alpha\,\mathbb P(|X_i|\ge s)\mathbb P(|X_j|\ge t)
  \quad(i\ne j,\ s,t>0)
  $$
  holds, then for $1\le k\le n$,
  $$
  c(\alpha)k t(k,X)\le \mathbb E\max_{|I|=k}\sum_{i\in I}|X_i|\le2k t(k,X),
  \qquad c(\alpha)=\bigl(36(5+4\alpha)(1+2\alpha)\bigr)^{-1}.
  $$
  This decorrelation theorem is **only in M0290**; it is not a result from M0274.
- **Theorem 3 (PDF p. 2).** For log-concave $X$ with uncorrelated coordinates, the same expectation of the sum of the largest $k$ moduli lies between $c k t(k,X)$ and $2k t(k,X)$, with universal $c>0$.
- **Theorem 4 (PDF p. 3).** Assume $X$ is a mean-zero log-concave vector in $\mathbb R^n$ with uncorrelated coordinates and $1\le k\le n$. Then
  $$
  \mathbb E[k\text{-max}_i|X_i|]\ge \tfrac12\operatorname{Med}(k\text{-max}_i|X_i|)\ge c\,t^*(k-\tfrac12,X).
  $$
  If in addition $X$ is unconditional, then
  $$
  \mathbb E[k\text{-max}_i|X_i|]\le C\,t^*(k-\tfrac12,X).
  $$
- **Theorem 5 (PDF p. 3).** Under the same **mean-zero, log-concave, uncorrelated-coordinate** hypotheses and $1\le k\le n$,
  $$
  \mathbb P\!\left(k\text{-max}_i|X_i|\ge C\,t^*(k-\tfrac12,X)\right)\le1-c,
  $$
  and
  $$
  \mathbb E[k\text{-max}_i|X_i|]\le C\,t^*\!\left(k-\tfrac12 k^{5/6},X\right).
  $$
  The $k^{5/6}$ term is a **subtraction inside the argument of $t^*$**, not a multiplicative factor on the resulting bound. $c,C$ denote positive universal constants whose values may change.
- **Corollary 6 (PDF p. 4; proof via Lemma 24, pp. 23–24).** For isotropic log-concave $X$ and $1\le k\le n/2$,
  $$
  \mathbb E[k\text{-max}_i|X_i|]\sim t^*(k,X)\sim t(k,X),\qquad
  c\frac{k}{n}\le\mathbb E[k\text{-min}_i|X_i|]=\mathbb E[(n-k+1)\text{-max}_i|X_i|]\le C\left(\frac{k}{n}+n^{-1/6}\right).
  $$
  If $X$ is additionally unconditional, then $\mathbb E[k\text{-min}_i|X_i|]\sim k/n$. All comparisons here require isotropy and the stated $k$-range.

## Proof chain read

Theorem 2 is reduced to a second-moment lower bound for truncated coordinate sums, using the pairwise tail condition and a layer-cake identity (PDF pp. 11–14). Theorem 3 follows by symmetrization, normalization of coordinate $L_2$ scales, and a dimension-dependent exponential-concentration estimate for projections (PDF pp. 9–11). For the lower estimate in Theorem 4, the paper sets $t^*=t^*(k-1/2,X)$, splits indices according to whether their marginal exceedance probability at $t^*$ is small or large, and combines a lower-order-statistic proposition with a log-concave one-dimensional small-ball estimate (PDF pp. 17–18). For the upper estimates, the same type of split is used: the unconditional case follows by log-concavity of the law of coordinate moduli and an order-statistic tail comparison (PDF pp. 19–21). The general case uses the probability threshold $9/10$, an estimate for the lower order statistics, and induction on $k$ to absorb the exceptional high-probability coordinates; this proves the expectation bound at the shifted parameter in Theorem 5, not at a multiplicatively rescaled $t^*$ (PDF pp. 20–23). Lemma 24 supplies the isotropic comparisons used in Corollary 6 (PDF pp. 23–24).

## Scope and transfer limit

These are coordinate/order-statistic conclusions under the exact assumptions above. The general estimate is an expectation bound with a shifted $t^*$ index. None implies arbitrary-norm C153 or C154's full $Z_p(\mu)$ expansion for every Borel set. Do not transplant Theorem 2's $c(\alpha)$ into M0274 or omit the mean-zero and uncorrelated-coordinate assumptions in Theorems 4–5.
