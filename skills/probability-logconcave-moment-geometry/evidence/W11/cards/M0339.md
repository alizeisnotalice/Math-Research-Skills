# M0339 — A reverse entropy power inequality for log-concave random vectors

**Source and read evidence.** Keith Ball, Piotr Nayar and Tomasz Tkocz, *Studia Mathematica* 235 (2016), no. 1, pp. 17–30, DOI [10.4064/sm8418-6-2016](https://doi.org/10.4064/sm8418-6-2016). Its first page identifies the published journal article; publisher metadata matches title, authors, volume, issue and pages.

For a real random variable with density, write $S(X)=-\int f\log f$. In this paper this is finite for log-concave $X$. For symmetric log-concave $X\in\mathbb R^n$, define
$$
N_X(v)=\begin{cases}\exp S(\langle v,X\rangle),&v\ne0,\\0,&v=0.\end{cases}
$$
Homogeneity follows from $S(aY)=S(Y)+\log|a|$, but the authors pose the triangle inequality for $N_X$ itself as Conjecture 1.

**Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 4–5; printed pp. 20–21).** There is a universal $\kappa$ and one can take $\kappa=1/5$ such that every symmetric log-concave $X\in\mathbb R^n$ and all $u,v\in\mathbb R^n$ satisfy
$$
e^{\kappa S(\langle u+v,X\rangle)}\le e^{\kappa S(\langle u,X\rangle)}+e^{\kappa S(\langle v,X\rangle)}.
$$
Thus $N_X^\kappa$ is a $\kappa$-seminorm; the paper does not prove that $N_X$ is a norm. Equivalently, for a symmetric log-concave pair $(X,Y)\in\mathbb R^2$, $e^{\kappa S(X+Y)}\le e^{\kappa S(X)}+e^{\kappa S(Y)}$. The equivalent Theorem 2 assumes $S(X)=S(Y)$ and states, for $0\le\theta\le1$,
$$
S(\theta X+(1-\theta)Y)\le S(X)+\kappa^{-1}\log(\theta^\kappa+(1-\theta)^\kappa),\qquad \kappa=1/5.
$$

**Proof chain and separate auxiliary result.** Proposition 1 (PDF p. 4) gives the coarser $e^{S(X+Y)}\le e(e^{S(X)}+e^{S(Y)})$: use $-\log\|f\|_\infty\le S(f)\le1-\log\|f\|_\infty$, then Ball's theorem that $\|z\|_w=(\int w(tz)dt)^{-1}$ is a norm for an even log-concave joint density $w$; apply its triangle inequality to the diagonal direction and convert back to marginal/sum maxima. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are equivalent by rescaling the marginals. For small $\theta$, Proposition 3 bounds the derivative of $S(\theta X+(1-\theta)Y)$ using Lemma 1's slice integral estimate; Lemmas 2–3 control one-dimensional log-concave slices and their mean/quantile. For larger $\theta$, Proposition 1 supplies a constant additive entropy bound. The resulting scalar inequality is checked at $\kappa=1/5$. Section 3.5's trapezoid-convolution construction proves Proposition 2: for each $0<\lambda_0<1/[2(2+\sqrt2)]$ it gives a symmetric continuous finite-variance $X$ with $S(X_{\lambda_0})>S(X_{1/2})$; it refutes that universal maximization guess, not Conjecture 2 in the log-concave case.

**Scope.** This is an entropy seminorm result for one-dimensional projections of a centrally symmetric log-concave vector. It does not give a $Z_p(\mu)$ covering or enlargement result, a norm-moment inequality, or the all-Borel-set assertion C154.
