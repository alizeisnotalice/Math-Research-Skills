## Proportional sections and centroid-body entropy

For a log-concave measure $\mu$, $Z_q(\mu)$ is defined by $h_{Z_q(\mu)}(y)=\|\langle y,X\rangle\|_{L_q(\mu)}$. **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3)** assumes $\mu$ isotropic log-concave and fixes $1\le\alpha<2$. If $0<\varepsilon<1$ and $q\le\sqrt{\varepsilon n}$, some $k\ge(1-\varepsilon)n$ and $F\in G_{n,k}$ satisfy
$$
P_F(Z_q(\mu))\supseteq c(2-\alpha)\varepsilon^{1/2+2/\alpha}\sqrt q\,B_F.
$$
For $2\le q\le\varepsilon n$, such $k,F$ also satisfy
$$
P_F(Z_q(\mu))\supseteq \frac{c_1(2-\alpha)\varepsilon^{1/2+2/\alpha}}{L_{\varepsilon n}}\sqrt q\,B_F
\supseteq \frac{c_2(2-\alpha)\varepsilon^{1/4+2/\alpha}}{n^{1/4}}\sqrt q\,B_F,
$$
with absolute positive constants as displayed. The page image confirms $k\ge(1-\varepsilon)n$, the exponent $1/2+2/\alpha$, and denominator $L_{\varepsilon n}$. The proof in §5 combines Pisier's $\alpha$-regular ellipsoid theorem, centroid-body information and circumradius estimates for proportional sections of $\alpha$-regular bodies.

**Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 3–4).** Under the same isotropic log-concave and $1\le\alpha<2$ assumptions, for $q\le\sqrt n$ and
$$
1\le t\le\min\left\{\sqrt q,\ c_1(2-\alpha)^{-1}(n/q^2)^{(\alpha+4)/(2\alpha)}\right\},
$$
$$
\log N(\sqrt qB_2^n,tZ_q(\mu))\le c(\alpha)\frac n{t^{2\alpha/(\alpha+4)}}\max\left\{\log\frac{\sqrt{2q}}t,\log\frac1{(2-\alpha)t}\right\},
\quad c(\alpha)\le C(2-\alpha)^{-2/3}.
$$
For the second range $2\le q\le n$, the source states $1\le t\le\min\{\sqrt q, c_2(2-\alpha)^{-1}L_n(n/q)^{(\alpha+4)/(2\alpha)}\}$ and
$$
\log N(\sqrt qB_2^n,tZ_q(\mu))\le c(\alpha)L_n^{2\alpha/(\alpha+4)}\frac n{t^{2\alpha/(\alpha+4)}}\max\left\{\log\frac{2q}{t^2},\log\frac{L_n}{(2-\alpha)t}\right\}.
$$
Here $c(\alpha)$ is as above and $c_2$ is absolute. The formula and both range restrictions were visually checked on PDF p. 4. The proof uses Theorem 1.1 and an entropy-extension/duality argument.

**Theorems 1.3–1.4 (PDF p. 4).** For isotropic log-concave $\mu$, $M(Z_q(\mu))\le C(\log q)^{5/6}/q^{1/6}$ when $1\le q\le n^{3/7}$, and the second range $L_n^2\log^2q\le q\le\sqrt{L_n}n^{3/4}$ has upper bound $C L_n^{1/3}(\log q)^{5/6}/q^{1/6}$. For symmetric isotropic convex $K$, Theorem 1.4 gives $M(K)\le C L_n^{1/4}(\log n)^{5/6}/(L_K n^{1/8})$. These are geometric estimates in their stated $q,n,L_n,L_K$ regimes.

## Scope and interface

The conclusions concern isotropic log-concave measures, Euclidean projection, covering numbers and mean norm of centroid bodies. They do not imply C153 for arbitrary norms or C154's uniform all-set dilation by $Z_p(\mu)$. In particular the inradius bound is for an existing proportional projection/subspace, not arbitrary-set enlargement.
