## Product-space inequalities

Let $(E_i,\mathcal E_i,\mu_i)$ be probability spaces with measurable singletons, $E=\prod_{i=1}^nE_i$, and $\mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_i$. For $0<\alpha<1$, the paper constructs sequential transition kernels that retain mass at the starting coordinate with probability $\lambda_i$ and move according to a measure absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu_i$. With the cost
$$
c_\alpha(\ell)=\frac{\alpha(1-\ell)\log(1-\ell)-(1-\alpha\ell)\log(1-\alpha\ell)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)},\quad0\le\ell\le1,
$$
and $c_\alpha(\ell)=+\infty$ for $\ell>1$, define $Q_\alpha f(x)$ as the infimum of expected $f(Y)$ plus the sum of these coordinate move costs over the admissible sequential kernels. **Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 4)** proves for $\zeta_1,\zeta_2\ll\mu$ that the associated transport cost satisfies
$$
C_\alpha(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)\le\frac1\alpha D(\zeta_1\|\mu)+\frac1{1-\alpha}D(\zeta_2\|\mu).
$$
This yields the dimension-free functional inequality (3): for bounded measurable $f$,
$$
\left(\int e^{\alpha Q_\alpha f}\,d\mu\right)^{1/\alpha}
\left(\int e^{-(1-\alpha)f}\,d\mu\right)^{1/(1-\alpha)}\le1.
$$

The paper also defines $R_\alpha$ using the density-dependent cost $d_\alpha$, the Legendre dual of
$$
d_\alpha^*(h)=\frac{\alpha e^{(1-\alpha)h}+(1-\alpha)e^{-\alpha h}-1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.
$$
**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 4)** gives the analogous inequality with $Q_\alpha$ replaced by $R_\alpha$ for bounded measurable $f$. The two operators and costs are distinct; the theorem does not identify them with the usual Euclidean quadratic infimum convolution.

## Proof chain read

In one dimension the transport construction couples the common mass of two densities along the diagonal and moves only their excess mass. The resulting cost is bounded by the two relative entropies. In products, sequential conditional couplings are linked coordinate by coordinate, and the entropy chain rule tensorizes the estimate, proving Theorem 1.2. The $Q_\alpha$ inequality follows by the entropy variational principle. The $R_\alpha$ inequality is proved by induction: the one-dimensional step uses quantile rearrangement, a truncation level and Legendre duality; composition of coordinate operators and concavity of negative-order $L^q$ norms gives the product step.

For suprema $Z=\sup_{t\in F}\sum_iX_{i,t}$ of independent processes, choosing a maximizing index $\tau(X)$ converts increments into coordinate changes, so the functional inequality yields Laplace and deviation bounds (Section 3). For example, when every $X_{i,t}\in[0,1]$, Corollary 3.2 gives $\log\mathbb E e^{\lambda Z}\le\mathbb E Z(e^\lambda-1)$ for every real $\lambda$.

## Interface and limits

The assumptions are product structure and the stated measurability/kernel conditions; individual factors need not be log-concave. Applications to convex functions or empirical suprema require their own coordinate increment bounds. This does not provide a concentration theorem for an arbitrary dependent log-concave law and does not yield C154's all-set enlargement by $Z_p(\mu)$.
