# M0272 — Geometry of log-concave ensembles of random matrices and approximate reconstruction

**Source and version.** Radosław Adamczak, Rafał Latała, Alexander E. Litvak, Alain Pajor and Nicole Tomczak-Jaegermann, *Comptes Rendus Mathématique* 349 (2011), nos. 13–14, pp. 783–786, DOI [10.1016/j.crma.2011.06.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2011.06.025); arXiv:1103.0401. The local file is a complete seven-page author manuscript (SHA-256 `ea5394b349e2d8b157a23efd573807b44f82c1c2bb4b50ba408b8feb67f83a7e`), read through author information. Let $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\in\mathbb R^N$ be independent isotropic log-concave rows of an $n\times N$ random matrix $\Gamma$. For $T\subset S^{N-1}$ define
$$
\Gamma_k(T)^2=\sup_{y\in T}\sup_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},\,|I|=k}\sum_{i\in I}|\langle Y_i,y\rangle|^2,
\qquad \Gamma_{k,m}=\Gamma_k(U_m),
$$
where $U_m$ is the set of at-most-$m$-sparse unit vectors.

**Theorem 6 (PDF p. 5).** For $1\le n\le N$, $k\le n$, $m\le N$, and $t\ge1$, there is an absolute $C$ such that
$$
\mathbb P(\Gamma_{k,m}\ge Ct\lambda)\le \exp\!\left(-\frac{t\lambda}{\sqrt{\log(3m)}}\right),\qquad
\lambda=\sqrt{\log\log(3m)}\,\sqrt m\log(eN/m)+\sqrt k\log(en/k).
$$
The source says the threshold is optimal up to the factor $\sqrt{\log\log(3m)}$ and that unconditional rows remove this factor by another cited result.

**Theorem 7 (PDF p. 5).** For $0<\theta<1$ and integers $1\le n\le N$, let $\Gamma$ be an $n\times N$ random matrix with independent isotropic log-concave rows. There is $c(\theta)>0$ such that the restricted-isometry parameter obeys $\delta_m(\Gamma/\sqrt n)\le\theta$ with the paper's stated “overwhelming probability” whenever
$$
m\log^2(2N/m)\log\log(3m)\le c(\theta)n.
$$
The note does not give a numerical value for $c(\theta)$ in this statement. Its preceding Lemma 1 reduces empirical covariance/RIP control on finite $T$ to $\Gamma_k(T)$, where $k$ is the largest integer satisfying $k\le(\Gamma_k(T)/B)^2$.

## Proof chain and scope

The note develops deviation estimates for coordinate projections of an isotropic log-concave vector (Theorems 2–3), upper-order coordinate statistics (Theorem 4), and weighted sums (Corollary 5). For Theorem 6 it adjusts a chaining argument to reduce to a range controlled by a threshold $k'$, invokes Theorem 2 in that reduction, and combines Corollary 5 with a second chaining step. Theorem 7 follows by combining the deviation bound with the finite-net RIP reduction. Several supporting estimates are cited or summarized rather than proved in this short note; the authors point to a companion work for proofs and related results. I therefore record the stated implications and proof dependencies, not a claim that the short note contains every underlying derivation.

This is a theorem for independent isotropic log-concave **rows** and sparse Euclidean directions. It is not a general arbitrary-norm moment theorem (C153) or an all-set $Z_p$ enlargement theorem (C154).
