## Definitions and exact open problem

For a probability measure $\mu$ with finite $p$-moments, $Z_p(\mu)$ is the origin-symmetric convex body with support function

$$
h_{Z_p(\mu)}(v)=\left(\int|\langle v,x\rangle|^p\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p},
$$

equivalently $Z_p(\mu)=\{x:|\langle v,x\rangle|^p\le\int|\langle v,y\rangle|^p\,d\mu(y)\ \forall v\}$ (Definition 1, PDF p. 2). For isotropic $\mu$, $Z_2(\mu)=B_2^n$. Moment growth is $\alpha$-regular if $\|\langle v,X\rangle\|_p\le\alpha(p/q)\|\langle v,X\rangle\|_q$ for all $p\ge q\ge2$ and $v\in\mathbb R^n$; log-concave laws are 3-regular, symmetric log-concave laws 1-regular (Definition 2, p. 2).

Definition 4 (p. 3) calls $\mu$ CI($\beta$) when

$$
\forall p\ge2,\ \forall A\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n),\quad
\mu(A)\ge\tfrac12\Longrightarrow 1-\mu(A+\beta Z_p(\mu))\le e^{-p}(1-\mu(A)).
$$

The equivalent all-set form (Remark 5, p. 3; by the cited Proposition 2.7) is

$$
\mu(A+\beta Z_p(\mu))\ge\min\{\tfrac12,e^p\mu(A)\}.
$$

The conjecture immediately following Remark 5 asks for an absolute $\beta$ for every symmetric log-concave probability measure. This is C154 exactly: all $p\ge2$, all Borel $A$, symmetric log-concave $\mu$, and the full moment body $Z_p(\mu)$.

## What is known in this contribution

- Theorem 6 (PDF p. 3): CI($\beta$) with absolute $\beta$ holds for (i) symmetric product log-concave measures, (ii) uniform measures on $\ell_p$ balls for every $1\le p\le\infty$, and (iii) rotationally invariant log-concave measures. These are proper subclasses, not a proof for all symmetric log-concave measures.
- Proposition 7 (pp. 4–5): every centered log-concave probability measure in $\mathbb R^n$ satisfies CI($\beta$) with $\beta\le C\sqrt n$. Proof: affine invariance reduces to isotropic position. Lemma 8 uses a 1/2-net in the polar moment body and Chebyshev to show $\mu(10\lambda Z_p)\ge1-\lambda^{-p}$ for $p\ge n,\lambda\ge1$. Lemma 9 turns any symmetric convex $K$ with $\mu(K)\ge1-e^{-p}$ into $\mu(A+9K)\ge\min\{\tfrac12,e^p\mu(A)\}$, using Borell tails. Combining gives $90e$ when $p\ge n$; moment regularity extends it with $180e\sqrt n$ when $p\ge\sqrt n$; Paouris Euclidean concentration and $Z_p\supset Z_2=B_2^n$ handle $2\le p\le\sqrt n$, yielding the dimension bound. This applies to centered, possibly nonsymmetric, log-concave laws but the constant is not dimension-free.
- The proposed route to $\beta\le C n^{4/9}(\log n)^{1/3}$ assumes the unproved estimate $\mu((C n/p)Z_p)\ge1-e^{-p}$ for $2\le p\le n$; the chapter notes it can show this in the unconditional case. Do not cite this conditional calculation as an established general theorem.
- In the discussion around Conjecture 12 (pp. 7–9), C153 is stated for arbitrary log-concave $X$, arbitrary norm, all $p\ge1$, with an absolute constant. Paouris' Euclidean result is a theorem; Theorem 14's $\ell_r$ result has a factor $C_r$, $r<\infty$; neither resolves the arbitrary-norm conjecture. The paper explicitly observes that any finite-dimensional space embeds isometrically in $\ell_\infty$, so the missing case is the uniform-$\ell_\infty$ endpoint.
- For isotropic unconditional vectors, Theorem 19 gives a bound involving $\mathbb E\|Y\|$ for an independent product exponential vector $Y$; Corollary 20 replaces this by $C\log n\,\mathbb E\|X\|$. Neither is dimension-free C153. Corollary 21 covers normed spaces with bounded nontrivial cotype constant. Proposition 22 covers densities proportional to $e^{-\varphi(\|x\|_r)}$, constants depending on $r$.

## Proof dependency map / safe transfer

CI($\beta$) implies Euclidean exponential concentration for isotropic log-concave measures, hence Cheeger/Poincaré inequalities with comparable constants; the reverse requires log-concavity plus the relevant regularity/transport steps (the contribution cites Latała–Wojtaszczyk Corollary 3.14). For C154, preserve symmetry, the quantifier over every Borel set, every $p\ge2$, and the $Z_p(\mu)$ shape. A Euclidean-ball KLS estimate alone loses the direction-dependent moment geometry. For C153, preserve arbitrary norm and covariance; a result for isotropic $X$ in a fixed norm class is not invariant under whitening the law while keeping that norm unchanged.
