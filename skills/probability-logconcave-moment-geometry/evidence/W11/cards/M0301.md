**Source and read evidence.** Yin Tat Lee and Santosh S. Vempala, FOCS 2017, pp. 998–1007, DOI [10.1109/FOCS.2017.96](https://doi.org/10.1109/FOCS.2017.96). ## Exact conclusion

Write $\psi_p$ for the KLS constant in the paper's reciprocal-expansion convention and $A=\operatorname{Cov}(p)$. **Theorem 8** states, for every log-concave density $p$ on $\mathbb R^n$,
$$
\psi_p\le C\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)\bigr)^{1/4}
$$
with universal $C$. For isotropic $p$, this is $\psi_p\le Cn^{1/4}$. **Corollary 9** gives the same $O(n^{1/4})$ upper order for the isotropic/slicing constant and thin-shell constant. **Corollary 10** gives $O^*(n^{2.5})$ ball-walk mixing from a warm start for an isotropic log-concave density.

## Proof chain read

It suffices by the log-concave isoperimetric profile to consider a set $E$ of initial probability $1/2$. The stochastic localization process has $dc_t=dW_t+\mu_tdt$ and
$$
p_t(x)\propto e^{\langle c_t,x\rangle-t\|x\|_2^2/2}p(x),
$$
so $p_t$ is a density-valued martingale and its covariance $A_t$ obeys the cubic-moment SDE in Lemma 20. A Brownian time-change bounds the probability that $\int_0^T\|A_s\|_2ds$ is small from below, which keeps the mass of $E$ between $1/4$ and $3/4$. The Gaussian factor in $p_T$ gives expansion $\Omega(\sqrt T)$ by localization and Brascamp–Lieb, hence $\psi_p=O(T^{-1/2})$ (Lemma 24).

To obtain a usable $T$, the proof controls $\Phi_t=\operatorname{Tr}(A_t^2)$. One-dimensional log-concave moment bounds control the drift and quadratic variation through third moments; applying Itô's formula to $-(\Phi_t+n)^{-1/2}$ and a Brownian maximal bound gives $\sup_{t\le c_1/\sqrt n}\Phi_t\le8n$ with probability at least $0.99$ when initially $\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)=n$ (Lemma 28). Thus $\|A_t\|_2\le\sqrt{8n}$ on this interval, and Lemma 24 yields the isotropic $n^{1/4}$ estimate. The general-covariance statement follows by the paper's affine rescaling. The slicing and thin-shell corollaries use the established KLS-to-thin-shell/slicing comparisons; the walk bound uses the standard KLS mixing implication.

## Interface and limits

The result controls KLS/isoperimetric, thin-shell and sampling quantities. It does not bound $\|X\|_{L_p(\|\cdot\|)}$ for every norm with a dimension-free coefficient, and it does not prove C154's every-set $Z_p(\mu)$ enlargement. The isotropic bound retains $n^{1/4}$.
