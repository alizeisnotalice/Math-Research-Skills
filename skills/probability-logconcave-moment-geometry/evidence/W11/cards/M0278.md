## Euclidean log-concave moments and coordinate tails

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 12).** For every $N$-dimensional log-concave vector $X$ and every $p\ge1$,
$$
(\mathbb E|X|^p)^{1/p}\le C\left\{(\mathbb E|X|^2)^{1/2}+\sup_{t\in S^{N-1}}(\mathbb E|\langle t,X\rangle|^p)^{1/p}\right\},
$$
where $C$ is universal. This is a Euclidean-norm theorem. The authors explicitly note that the analogous assertion for arbitrary norms is an open problem in their discussion; it does not settle C153.

For isotropic log-concave $X\in\mathbb R^N$, **Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 14)** states that for $p\ge2$ and $t\ge C\log(Nt^2/\sigma_X(p)^2)$,
$$
\mathbb E\{t^2N_X(t)\}^p\le(C\sigma_X(p))^{2p},\qquad
N_X(t)=\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf1_{\{X_i\ge t\}},\quad
\sigma_X(p)=\sup_{u\in S^{N-1}}\|\langle u,X\rangle\|_p.
$$
The count is one-sided. **Theorem 3.3 (PDF p. 14)** states that, writing $X^*(1)\ge\cdots\ge X^*(N)$ for the decreasing rearrangement of $|X_i|$, for $1\le\ell\le N$ and $t\ge C\log(eN/\ell)$,
$$
\mathbb P(X^*(\ell)\ge t)\le\exp\left[-\sigma_X^{-1}\left(\frac{t\sqrt\ell}{C}\right)\right].
$$
Its proof uses both $X$ and $-X$ because $N_X(t)$ counts only coordinates with $X_i\ge t$. **Theorem 3.4 (PDF p. 14)** states that for isotropic log-concave $X\in\mathbb R^N$, $m\le N$, and $t\ge1$,
$$
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\},\ |I|=m}|P_I X|\ge Ct\sqrt m\log\frac{eN}{m}\right)
\le\exp\left[-\sigma_X^{-1}\left(\frac{t\sqrt m\log(eN/m)}{\sqrt{\log(em/m_0)}}\right)\right],
$$
where $C$ is absolute and
$$
m_0=m_0(X,t)=\sup\left\{k\le m:\ k\log\frac{eN}{k}\le\sigma_X^{-1}\left(t\sqrt m\log\frac{eN}{m}\right)\right\}.
$$
The denominator's logarithmic factor and the definition of $m_0$ are part of the printed result; the authors remark they expect the logarithmic loss might be removable but do not prove that.

**Proposition 4.1 (PDF p. 15).** For independent isotropic log-concave $X_i\in\mathbb R^N$ and $Y=\sum_{i=1}^n x_iX_i$,
$$
\sigma_Y(p)\le C(\sqrt p\,|x|+p\|x\|_\infty),\qquad p\ge1.
$$
The proof symmetrizes each one-dimensional projection, compares its tails to symmetric exponentials, and uses the exponential-sum moment bound. Corollary 4.2 converts this into an order-statistic tail; Theorem 4.3 gives two projection-norm regimes according as $\|x\|_\infty$ is above or below $m^{-1/2}$.

## Random matrices and proof chain

For an $n\times N$ matrix $A$ with independent isotropic log-concave rows, define $A_{k,m}=\sup\|A(J,I)\|_{\ell_2^m\to\ell_2^k}$ over row/column sets of sizes $k,m$. **Theorem 5.1 (PDF pp. 19–20)** gives, for $t\ge1$,
$$
\mathbb P(A_{k,m}\ge Ct\lambda_{k,m})\le\exp\left(-\frac{t\lambda_{k,m}}{\sqrt{\log(3m)}}\right),
$$
where
$$
\lambda_{k,m}=\sqrt{\log\log(3m)}\,\sqrt m\,\log\left(\frac{e\max\{N,n\}}m\right)+\sqrt k\,\log\left(\frac{en}k\right).
$$
Both logarithms in the two summands are outside the square roots; this was checked against rendered PDF p. 20, equation (5.3), and the following proof page p. 21. The proof passes from individual coordinate-set projection tails to weighted sums, then uses chaining/net bounds uniformly over column sets and sparse row vectors. The paper's RIP application (Theorem 6.4, PDF p. 40) assumes independent isotropic log-concave rows and its displayed sparsity/dimension condition; it is not a generic norm-moment claim.

## Scope and interface

The paper supplies Euclidean moment, projection, order-statistic and matrix estimates under explicit log-concavity, isotropy and independence conditions. It does not imply a bound for every norm in C153. Its $Z_p$ geometry is not a theorem that every symmetric log-concave measure admits C154's all-Borel-set enlargement by a single multiple of its own $Z_p(\mu)$.
