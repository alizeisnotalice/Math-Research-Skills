Throughout, $V:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ is locally Lipschitz and, when required, satisfies $\int(1+|x|^2+|\nabla V|^2)e^{-V}dx<\infty$; $d\mu_V=e^{-V}dx/\int e^{-V}$. The paper fixes a Euclidean scalar product. The asymmetric cost is $c_V(x,y)=V(y)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)\cdot(y-x)$.

**Proposition 1 (PDF pp. 2–3).** For every absolutely continuous probability measure $\nu$, $W_{c_V}(\mu_V,\nu)\le H(\nu\|\mu_V)$. When $V$ is convex, equality holds iff $\nu$ is a translate of $\mu_V$. The paper also gives a sharpened equality characterization under a positive Cheeger constant.

**Theorem 3 (PDF p. 5).** With $N(t)=\min(t^2,t)$ and a numerical $c>0$, set
$$
\widetilde c_V(x,y)=c_V(x,y)+cN(h(\mu_V)|y-x|).
$$$
For every probability measure $\nu$ with the same barycenter as $\mu_V$, $W_{\widetilde c_V}(\mu_V,\nu)\le H(\nu\|\mu_V)$. It follows that entropy dominates $W_{c_V}+cW_{N(h|y-x|)}$, and in particular the source's weaker scalar form $W_{c_V}+c\min\{h^2W_1^2,hW_1\}$. The norm in the remainder is tied to the chosen scalar product.

**Proposition 4 (PDF p. 6).** If $V\in C^2$ is convex with $D^2V\ge\lambda I$, and $\nu,\mu_V$ have the same mean, then
$$
H(\nu\|\mu_V)-\frac\lambda2W_2^2(\mu_V,\nu)\ge cW_{N(h(\mu_V)|y-x|)}(\mu_V,\nu)
$$
with the further lower bound $\widetilde c\min\{h(\mu_V)^2W_1^2,h(\mu_V)W_1\}$. The paper notes that $h(\mu_V)^2$ may be replaced by $\lambda$ up to a numerical constant.

**Proposition 5 (PDF p. 6).** For $V\in C^2$ convex with $\int e^{-V}<\infty$, and locally Lipschitz $g\in L^2(\mu_V)$ satisfying the vector-centering condition
$$
\int x\left(g(x)-\int g\,d\mu_V\right)d\mu_V(x)=0,
$$
$$
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_V}(g)\le\int [D^2V+c h(\mu_V)^2I]^{-1}\nabla g\cdot\nabla g\,d\mu_V.
$$
The source states that $h(\mu_V)^2$ can be replaced by the spectral-gap parameter $\lambda(\mu_V)$; it also notes a relaxation of convexity for this proposition when the displayed matrix field remains nonnegative.

## Proof chain and scope

The proofs use the Brenier map $T=\nabla\psi$, Monge–Ampère and an entropy decomposition with a trace remainder. A scalar convexity estimate compares that remainder with $N(h|\nabla\theta|)$; imposing equal barycenters removes the translation equality mode. Linearizing the transport inequality gives the strengthened Brascamp–Lieb estimate with the specific vector-centering condition above.

These are transport and variance inequalities, not a concentration inequality asserting $\mu(A+\beta Z_p(\mu))$ for every Borel $A$. They do not imply C154 or an arbitrary-norm moment bound. Keep the asymmetric $c_V$, same-mean constraint, Cheeger constant and Euclidean cost in their stated roles.
