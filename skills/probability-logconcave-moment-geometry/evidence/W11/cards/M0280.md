## Statements

For isotropic log-concave $X\in\mathbb R^n$, **Theorem 1.1** (PDF p. 1, printed p. 3) states that there is an absolute $c>0$ such that, for every $t\ge1$,
$$
\mathbb P\bigl(|X|\ge c t\sqrt n\bigr)\le e^{-t\sqrt n}.
$$

For any log-concave random vector $X\in\mathbb R^n$ and $p\ge1$, **Theorem 1.2** (PDF p. 2, printed p. 4) states
$$
\bigl(\mathbb E|X|^p\bigr)^{1/p}\le C\left(\mathbb E|X|+\sigma_p(X)\right),\qquad
\sigma_p(X)=\sup_{z\in S^{n-1}}\bigl(\mathbb E|\langle z,X\rangle|^p\bigr)^{1/p},
$$
for an absolute $C$. The reverse comparison follows directly from $|\langle z,X\rangle|\le|X|$ and monotonicity of moments. The article notes that the stronger form with coefficient exactly $1$ on $\mathbb E|X|$ was open there.

## Proof chain read

The proof first reduces the general law to the symmetric case by passing to $X-X'$ for an independent copy and paying an absolute constant (PDF p. 3). Lemma 2.1 bounds the minimum directional $L_p$ norm of a projected vector by its strong moment and mean norm. Lemma 2.2 constructs, for a full-dimensional symmetric log-concave vector in $\mathbb R^q$, a norm satisfying $(\mathbb E\|Y\|^q)^{1/q}\le500\mathbb E\|Y\|$ (PDF pp. 3–4). Lemma 2.3 combines Gaussian rotational invariance, concentration of a Gaussian norm, and Gordon's Gaussian minimax inequality to bound the Euclidean $L_p$ moment by an expected minimum over a Gaussian matrix plus $\sigma_p(X)$ (PDF pp. 4–5). For $q=\lceil p\rceil$, condition on the Gaussian matrix, apply the constructed norm to its image of $X$, and average; this gives Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 5). Markov's inequality with $p=t\sqrt n$ and the one-dimensional log-concave growth estimate yield Theorem 1.1 from Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2).

## Transfer limit

The strong norm here is Euclidean. This establishes Paouris' Euclidean tail/moment estimate and is an ingredient in other proofs, but does not imply the all-norm C153 bound or the $Z_p$-body enlargement C154.
