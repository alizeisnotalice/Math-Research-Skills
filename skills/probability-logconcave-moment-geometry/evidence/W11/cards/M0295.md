# M0295 — Chevet-type inequalities for subexponential Weibull variables and estimates for norms of random matrices

For $r\in[1,2]$, let the entries $X_{ij}$ be independent symmetric Weibull random variables with exact tails $\mathbb P(|X_{ij}|\ge t)=e^{-t^r}$ for $t\ge0$, and let $g_{ij}$ be standard Gaussian. For nonempty bounded $S\subset\mathbb R^m$ and $T\subset\mathbb R^n$, **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2)** gives, with universal comparison constants uniform over the stated $r$ range,
$$
\mathbb E\sup_{s\in S,t\in T}\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}s_it_j\asymp
\sup_{s\in S}\|s\|_{r^*}\,\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}\sum_jX_jt_j
+\sup_{t\in T}\|t\|_{r^*}\,\mathbb E\sup_{s\in S}\sum_iX_is_i
+\mathbb E\sup_{s\in S,t\in T}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}s_it_j,
$$
where $r^*=r/(r-1)$ with endpoint conventions and the one-index $X_i$'s are independent symmetric Weibull variables with the same shape. By Gaussian Chevet, the last term is equivalent to the two analogous Euclidean ($\ell_2$) terms. This is an expectation comparison, not a tail statement by itself.

**Corollary 1.3 (PDF p. 3).** If the full matrix vector in $\mathbb R^{mn}$ has an isotropic unconditional log-concave law, the corresponding Chevet upper bound follows by comparison of every norm of an isotropic unconditional log-concave vector with a symmetric exponential vector. The paper explicitly does not claim this comparison for arbitrary isotropic log-concave matrices. **Theorem 1.5 / Corollary 1.6 (PDF pp. 6–7)** bound maximal $\ell_p\to\ell_q$ norms over all $k\times l$ submatrices, respectively for independent centered $\psi_r$ entries (two-sided when entries are iid symmetric Weibull) and isotropic unconditional log-concave matrices (two-sided in the iid symmetric exponential case). Keep those entry-law and unconditionality restrictions.

## Proof chain and limits

Theorem 1.1 uses generic chaining: Gaussian suprema are represented by $\gamma_2$, Weibull suprema by the sum of $\gamma_r$ and $\gamma_2$, and the tensor index set $S\otimes T$ yields the two $r^*$ terms; Gaussian Chevet supplies the Gaussian term and the lower estimate. Corollary 1.3 applies the unconditional-vector norm comparison to those one-index processes. For maximal submatrix norms, the paper writes the relevant index sets as unions of $\ell_{q^*}$ and $\ell_p$ balls, estimates their norms and coordinate order statistics (Lemmas 4.1–4.2), and inserts those estimates into the Chevet bound. The theorem depends on the particular product/tensor structure and entry assumptions; it does not prove C153 for every norm and dependent log-concave vector, or C154's all-set $Z_p$ enlargement.
