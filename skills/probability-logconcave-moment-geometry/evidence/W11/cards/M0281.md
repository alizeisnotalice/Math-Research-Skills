# M0281 — On the mean-width of isotropic convex bodies and their associated $L_p$-centroid bodies

## Main mean-width estimates

For an origin-symmetric isotropic convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$, Theorem 1.1 states
$$
M^*(K)\le C\sqrt n\,\operatorname{Rad}(X_K)\log(1+n)L_K
\le C'\sqrt n\,\log^2(1+n)L_K,
$$
where $M^*$ is half the spherical mean width, $L_K$ the isotropic constant, and $\operatorname{Rad}(X_K)$ the Rademacher projection norm of the associated normed space. The paper emphasizes that removing $L_K$ is tied to the slicing problem.

Theorem 1.2 gives for every isotropic probability measure $\mu$ and $p\ge1$,
$$
M^*(Z_p(\mu))
\le C\,\operatorname{Rad}(X_{Z_p(\mu)})
\max\left\{\frac{p\log(1+p)}{\sqrt n},\sqrt p\right\}.
$$
This extends Paouris' sharp $\sqrt p$ mean-width estimate through $p\le\sqrt n$ and gives dimension/range-dependent control at higher $p$.

## Relevance and limitation

These estimates give geometric information on $Z_p(\mu)$ and are useful inputs to entropy/Sudakov arguments. They do not establish a measure expansion inequality for every Borel set and do not prove the uniform weak/strong moment bound in C153. Preserve the isotropy, mean-width observable, and Rademacher/slicing losses.
