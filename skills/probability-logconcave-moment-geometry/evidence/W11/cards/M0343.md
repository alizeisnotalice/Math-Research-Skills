# M0343 — Entropy and Information jump for log-concave vectors

**Source and read evidence.** Pierre Bizeul, *Comptes Rendus Mathématique* 361 (2023), G2, pp. 487–493, DOI [10.5802/crmath.390](https://doi.org/10.5802/crmath.390). For independent $X_0\sim\mu,X_1\sim\nu$, set $X_\lambda=\sqrt{1-\lambda}X_0+\sqrt\lambda X_1$, $0\le\lambda\le1$. Let $D(\mu\|\gamma)$ be relative entropy to standard Gaussian and $I(\mu\|\gamma)$ relative Fisher information. **Theorem 1 (Quantitative Shannon–Stam, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 489).** If $\mu,\nu$ are log-concave isotropic measures with Poincaré constants $c_0,c_1$, respectively, then
$$
\delta_{E,\lambda}(\mu,\nu):=\operatorname{Ent}_L(X_\lambda)-(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Ent}_L(X_0)-\lambda\operatorname{Ent}_L(X_1)\ge\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{4\max(c_0,c_1)}\bigl(D(\mu\|\gamma)+D(\nu\|\gamma)\bigr).
$$
**Theorem 2 (Quantitative Blachman–Stam, same page).** Under the same assumptions,
$$
\delta_{I,\lambda}:=(1-\lambda)I_L(X_0)+\lambda I_L(X_1)-I_L(X_\lambda)\ge\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{4\max(c_0,c_1)}\bigl(I(\mu\|\gamma)+I(\nu\|\gamma)\bigr).
$$
The normalizations, independence and equal identity covariance assumptions are part of both statements; a finite Poincaré constant is retained in the denominator. The rendered source page confirms the factor $4\max(c_0,c_1)$ is the denominator.

**Proof chain.** For smooth log-concave densities, a projection formula for the Hessian of a marginal gives a matrix-order bound by the conditional mean Hessian; applying it to the weighted-sum projection controls the Hessian of a convolution. Jensen's inequality then yields a convexity deficit for the squared Hilbert–Schmidt Hessian norm. Rewriting relative-to-Gaussian Hessians gives the intermediate functional $M=K+2I$. The Poincaré inequalities for $X_0,X_1$, isotropy and conditioning bound the Hessian difference below by $(I_0+K_0+I_1+K_1)/(2\max(c_0,c_1))$. Along the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup, convolution commutes with evolution, Poincaré constants decrease, $\partial_t\operatorname{Ent}=I$, and $\partial_tI=-2I-2K$. Integrating the information-deficit differential inequality and using $I(X_t)\le e^{-2t}I(X_0)$ proves Theorem 2; integrating it along the semigroup gives Theorem 1. The paper describes the latter as an extension of Ball–Nguyen from identically distributed pairs to arbitrary pairs and adds the information analogue.

**Scope.** These are stability inequalities for entropy and Fisher-information convolution deficits, under isotropy and spectral-gap assumptions. They are not norm-moment, centroid-body, arbitrary-norm C153 or C154 set-enlargement statements.
