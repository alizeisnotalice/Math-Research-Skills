## Exact statement and scope

Conjecture 1.1 (PDF p. 2): for every $n$-dimensional log-concave random vector $X$, every norm on $\mathbb R^n$, and every $1\le p<\infty$, there are absolute $C_1,C_2$ such that

$$
(\mathbb E\|X\|^p)^{1/p}\le C_1\mathbb E\|X\|+C_2\sup_{\|\varphi\|_*\le1}(\mathbb E|\varphi(X)|^p)^{1/p}.
$$

This is C153. It is explicitly a conjecture here, not a theorem. The source allows arbitrary covariance and arbitrary finite-dimensional norm; the desired constants are independent of dimension, norm, law, and $p$.

## Results that are proved

- Proposition 2.1 (p. 2): for any $n$-dimensional random vector (no log-concavity assumption), the inequality holds for $p\ge n$ with $C_1=0,C_2=10$. The proof uses an exponential-size norming family of linear functionals and the high moment to absorb its cardinality. This does not cover the low/intermediate $p<n$ regime.
- Proposition 2.2 (pp. 2–3): if the inequality holds for symmetric log-concave vectors with constants $C_1,C_2$, it holds for all log-concave vectors with constants $4C_1+1,4C_2$. The chain uses an independent copy $X'$, symmetry and log-concavity of $X-X'$, then the centered vector $X-\mathbb EX$; triangle and Minkowski inequalities recover $X$. Thus a proof for the symmetric class would suffice, but the proposition does not establish the conjecture.
- Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 (pp. 3–4): independent-coordinate log-concave vectors satisfy the comparison, by the two-level concentration of product exponential measure and symmetrization.
- Theorem 2.7 (p. 4): Paouris' result gives the comparison for Euclidean norms for arbitrary log-concave $X$. The norm must be Euclidean; applying it after a general linear map does not preserve isotropic covariance in a way that solves C153 for arbitrary norm.
- Theorem 3.1 (p. 4): for isotropic unconditional log-concave $X$, any norm, and $p\ge1$, the proved bound has $C\mathbb E\|E\|+\sigma_{\|\cdot\|,X}(p)$ on the right, where $E$ is an independent variance-one product exponential vector. This is not the target $C\mathbb E\|X\|+C\sigma_X(p)$ without a further comparison.

## Proof mechanism and transfer limits

The product case converts norm deviations into Talagrand two-level concentration; the Euclidean case uses Paouris' concentration/moment theorem. The arbitrary-norm step in C153 needs simultaneous control of a norming family with no dimension loss and while preserving the actual first moment $\mathbb E\|X\|$. Neither the $p\ge n$ net argument, Euclidean Paouris inequality, independent-coordinate argument, nor unconditional estimate with $\mathbb E\|E\|$ supplies that missing interface.

## C154 relationship

The paper's Proposition 2.5 relates a suitable two-sided concentration inequality to weak/strong moment comparison. It does not prove the $Z_p(\mu)$-shaped expansion for every symmetric log-concave measure in C154. Do not replace that body by a Euclidean ball or infer C154 from a KLS/Cheeger estimate without proving the required $Z_p$ inclusion with the right uniform scale.
