## Entropy jump theorem

Let $X\in\mathbb R^d$ be isotropic and log-concave with density $f$, and suppose it satisfies the spectral-gap form of Poincaré inequality: for all smooth mean-zero $s$,
$$
c\int s^2f\le\int|\nabla s|^2f,
$$
for some $c>0$. Let $Y$ be an independent copy of $X$, and let $G$ be standard Gaussian in $\mathbb R^d$. **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3; printed p. 83)** states
$$
\operatorname{Ent}\!\left(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt2}\right)-\operatorname{Ent}(X)
\ge\frac{c}{4(1+c)}\bigl(\operatorname{Ent}(G)-\operatorname{Ent}(X)\bigr).
$$
For isotropic $f$, $c\le1$, so the coefficient may be weakened to $c/8$. Log-concavity is used in the marginal information-production comparison; the authors explicitly say they do not know whether that step holds without log-concavity.

**Theorem 5.1 (PDF p. 13; printed p. 93)** is a geometric consequence. If an isotropic log-concave $X$ has an entropy jump with coefficient $\kappa\in(0,1)$, then its isotropic constant satisfies $L_X=f(0)^{1/d}\le e^{2/\kappa}$. Combined with Theorem 1.1 this gives a bound in terms of the Poincaré constant for that individual law; it does not settle a dimension-uniform statement unless the spectral gap itself is dimension-uniform.

## Proof chain read

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck evolute $X_t=e^{-t}X+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}G$ preserves isotropy and log-concavity. The entropy deficit is the integral of the Fisher-information deficit, while Lemma 2.1 expresses the derivative of Fisher information through $\operatorname{Tr}\int f_t(\operatorname{Hess}\varphi_t)^2$ (PDF pp. 5–8). Lemma 3.1 bounds this Hessian-square quantity under taking a marginal, using the operator Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and convexity of the marginal potential. Applied to the pair $(X_t,Y_t)$ and its normalized sum, this yields Theorem 3.2 and a differential inequality for the information-production gap (PDF pp. 8–11). The Poincaré inequality is applied to centered score-coordinate functions to compare the Hessian trace terms; integration along the OU flow and the entropy/Fisher identity gives the factor $c/[4(1+c)]$ (PDF pp. 11–13). For Theorem 5.1, the proof uses $-\log f(0)\le\operatorname{Ent}(X)\le-\log f(0)+d$, a convolution lower bound on the density at zero in the symmetric case, symmetrization for a general law, and the Shannon–Stam inequality (PDF pp. 13–15).

## Transfer limit

This is an entropy jump under isotropy, log-concavity and a specified Poincaré constant. It does not yield arbitrary-norm weak/strong moment comparison or $Z_p$-body enlargement by itself. Keep the law-dependent spectral-gap assumption explicit.
