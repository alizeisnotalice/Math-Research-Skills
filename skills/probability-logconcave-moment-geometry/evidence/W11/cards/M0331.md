# M0331 — Orlicz centroid bodies

**Source and read evidence.** Erwin Lutwak, Deane Yang and Gaoyong Zhang, *Journal of Differential Geometry* 84 (2010), no. 2, pp. 365–387, DOI [10.4310/jdg/1274707317](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1274707317). The complete 23-page local published PDF (printed pages 365–387) was read through all references; SHA-256 `c65ab37f8ae7733983570e9191ea233ed1400dae077340ce02368f162c7fd7b2`. The main theorem was checked on rendered physical PDF p. 3 (`evidence/W11/notes/visual-audit/M0331-pdf3-batch7.png`) and its symmetrization conclusion on p. 19.

Let $\mathcal C$ be the paper's class of convex $\phi:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$ with $\phi(0)=0$, where $\phi$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$ or strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$. For a star body $K$ containing the origin and $\phi\in\mathcal C$, the Orlicz centroid body is defined by its support function
$$
h_{\Gamma_\phi K}(x)=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\frac1{|K|}\int_K\phi\!\left(\frac{x\cdot y}{\lambda}\right)dy\le1\right\}.
$$
**Main Theorem (PDF p. 3; printed p. 367).** If $K$ is a convex body in $\mathbb R^n$ with the origin in its interior and $\phi\in\mathcal C$, then $|\Gamma_\phi K|/|K|$ is minimized if and only if $K$ is an ellipsoid centered at the origin. The theorem is for convex bodies, including potentially nonsymmetric ones, and the stated class $\mathcal C$ need not be even. The paper's §4 star-body extension is posed as an open problem; do not upgrade it to a theorem from this source.

## Proof mechanism and geometric/probabilistic distinction

Section 1 establishes convex-body and Steiner-symmetrization notation. Section 2 proves that the implicit Luxemburg-type function is a support function, then gives continuity, scaling and $GL(n)$ covariance of $\Gamma_\phi$. In §3, the key inequality is $\Gamma_\phi(S_uK)\subset S_u(\Gamma_\phi K)$; equality conditions force the chord-midpoint structure that characterizes origin-centered ellipsoids. Iterated Steiner symmetrization and volume continuity yield the volume-ratio minimum and equality case. This is a geometric operator on a body with normalized uniform volume measure; it is not the probabilistic moment body $Z_p(\mu)$, so it supplies neither C153's arbitrary-norm moments nor C154's all-Borel-set $Z_p$ dilation.
