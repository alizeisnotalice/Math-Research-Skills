## Definitions and theorem

The graph may be directed and have parallel edges. It is **locally finite** when each vertex has finite in- and out-degree; **strongly connected** when every ordered pair is joined by a directed path; **quasi-transitive** when its automorphism group has finitely many vertex orbits (definitions, physical p.3). A finite directed SAW is forward extendable if it is an initial segment of a singly infinite directed SAW; backward extendable if it is the terminal segment of a singly infinite directed SAW arriving from infinity; doubly extendable if it is a subwalk of a doubly infinite directed SAW. In undirected graphs, the paper regards each undirected edge as two oppositely directed edges.

Let \(\sigma_n(v),\sigma_n^F(v),\sigma_n^B(v),\sigma_n^{FB}(v)\) count ordinary/forward/backward/doubly extendable length-\(n\) SAWs from \(v\); the unrooted counts are suprema over vertices and the corresponding growth constants are defined by nth-root limits when they exist.

**Theorem 1 (physical p.2).** For every infinite, locally finite, strongly connected, quasi-transitive directed graph \(G\):
\[
\mu^F,\ \mu^B,\ \mu^{FB}\text{ exist and }\quad
\mu=\mu^F=\mu^B=\mu^{FB},
\]
and, for each vertex \(v\),
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\sigma_n^F(v)\bigr)^{1/n}=\mu^F.
\]
The pointwise statement is only asserted for the forward-extendable count. The paper explicitly warns that the analogous backward/doubly pointwise statement need not hold: a vertex with only one neighbor joined in both directions has \(\sigma_n^B(v)=\sigma_n^{FB}(v)=0\) for every \(n\ge1\) (p.2). The equality is about exponential rates; it is not equality of finite-\(n\) counts.

## Proof architecture and dependencies

1. **Existence and two comparisons (Lemma 5, physical pp.6–7).** Submultiplicativity gives the growth limits. Encode SAWs as rooted trees. For any locally finite rooted tree, \(\operatorname{br}(T)\le\underline{\operatorname{gr}}(T)\) (Lemma 2, p.4); Furstenberg’s subperiodic-tree theorem identifies growth and branching number when the tree is subperiodic (Theorem 3, p.5). The full SAW tree has growth \(\mu\); the forward tree is obtained by pruning finite bushes, remains 1-subperiodic, and has the same exponential growth, yielding \(\mu=\mu^F\). Applying the same comparison to the reversed directed graph yields \(\mu^B=\mu^{FB}\). Theorem 1(ii)’s rootwise forward limit (p.7) uses a good/bad-vertex pruning argument: bad rooted subtrees have a uniform strict branching-number deficit; finite directed paths from any vertex to a good one and subperiodicity transfer that rate bound.
2. **Unimodular case (physical pp.5, 8–9).** Define the positive automorphism weight \(M\) by stabilizer-orbit ratios. Unimodularity means it is constant on each transitivity class. The mass-transport principle (Theorem 4, p.5), applied to forward/backward SAW counts, gives for a constant \(C(G)\ge1\)
   \[
   C^{-1}\le\frac{\sigma_n^F(G)}{\sigma_n^B(\overleftarrow G)}\le C
   \]
   (Lemma 6, p.8). In the transitive case \(C=1\). Reversal and Lemma 5 identify all exponential rates. The article notes strong connectivity may be weakened to connectivity of the graph obtained by forgetting directions in the unimodular proof (p.9 remark), but Theorem 1 retains the stated strong-connectivity assumption.
3. **Non-unimodular case (physical pp.9–13).** Lemma 7 constructs a doubly-infinite \(\alpha\)-quasi-geodesic with directed edges pointing toward its origin. The proof uses a nonconstant weight along an orbit, bounded weight ratios across edges, concatenated bounded-length directed paths, and loop erasure; the surviving sequence satisfies a linear distance lower bound. For an arbitrary length-\(n\) SAW, count its intersections with the two halves of this quasi-geodesic. If one side has at most \(\delta n\) intersections, decompose into at most \(\delta n+1\) backward-extendable segments and use the convolution bound (5.4), p.13. If both sides have more intersections, the quasi-geodesic separation forces a backward-extendable segment of length at least \(2\alpha\delta n\). Assuming \(\mu^B<\mu\), the count bound in (5.4), with \(\sigma_j^B\le C(\mu^B+\epsilon)^j\), makes both contributions have exponential rate below \(\mu\) for sufficiently small \(\delta,\epsilon>0\), contradiction. Therefore \(\mu=\mu^B\), and Lemma 5 completes the equalities.

The argument imports Furstenberg’s 1967 tree theorem and the mass-transport principle; those are cited dependencies, not proved from first principles here. The non-unimodular proof is graph-theoretic and does not assume a lattice embedding or a probability law.

## Transfer boundary

For a fixed undirected transitive lattice graph, this theorem identifies the exponential growth rate after restricting to forward/backward/doubly extendable walks; it says nothing about the distribution of the walk or convergence of its rescaled curve. It is therefore only auxiliary to C107 and cannot supply the missing tightness, curve-topology, DMP, or SLE-identification steps. The theorem does not cover the random supercritical-percolation cluster in C108 as a deterministic quasi-transitive graph; it neither proves existence of a full quenched nth-root limit nor root-independence of such a limit there.
