## Model and exact conclusion

The paper studies honeycomb-lattice walks in a half-plane with bulk step weight \(x\), boundary-contact fugacity \(y\), and the general O(n) loop formalism; the SAW case is loop fugacity \(n=0\). The bulk critical point is \(x_c=1/\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\), so \(\mu=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\). The surface critical fugacity \(y_c\) separates the desorbed and adsorbed growth regimes.

- **Theorem 2, printed/PDF p. 3:** for honeycomb SAW (the \(n=0\) case), \(y_c(0)=1+\sqrt2\). This is an exact theorem.
- **Conjecture 1, printed/PDF p. 3:** for the O(n) loop model, \(n\in[-2,2]\), the conjectured bulk weight is \(x_c(n)=1/\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2-n}}\), and the Batchelor–Yung surface formula is \(y_c(n)=1+2/\sqrt{2-n}\). The paper does not prove this general-n assertion; its theorem is the SAW case \(n=0\).

## Observable identity and proof dependencies

Section 2 (printed/PDF pp. 3–8) defines the parafermionic observable as a sum over a SAW component and disjoint loops, weighted by \(x^{|\gamma|}y^{c(\gamma)}n^{\ell(\gamma)}e^{-i\sigma W(\gamma)}\). Lemma 3 (the local identity, printed/PDF pp. 4–6) gives the interior-vertex cancellation for \(n=2\cos\theta\), with the two parameter branches stated in the paper. Summing the local relation over the trapezoidal domain yields the boundary identity (11), Proposition 4, printed/PDF pp. 7–8. These identities are model- and lattice-specific; the local identity by itself is not the adsorption theorem.

Section 3 (printed/PDF pp. 8–13) establishes the needed half-plane and strip growth facts: Proposition 5 gives existence and monotonicity/convexity properties of the half-plane growth rate and the critical threshold; Propositions 6–8 control strip growth and show the strip critical fugacities \(y_T\) decrease to \(y_c\). Section 4 specializes to \(n=0\). With \(\alpha=\cos(3\pi/8)\), \(\epsilon=\cos(\pi/4)\), \(\beta(y)=(1+\sqrt2-y)/(\sqrt2 y)\), the global identity (16) is
\[
1=\alpha A_{T,L}(x_c,y)+\epsilon E_{T,L}(x_c,y)+\beta(y)B_{T,L}(x_c,y).
\]
Here \(A,E,B\) count the paper’s arch, boundary-to-side, and bridge families in the finite domain, with weights \(x_c^{|\gamma|}y^{c(\gamma)}\). Setting \(y^*=1+\sqrt2\), so \(\beta(y^*)=0\), positivity and convergence in the strip imply \(y^*\le y_c\) (Section 4.2, printed/PDF p. 14).

For \(0\le y<y_T\), Proposition 9, Eq. (18), printed/PDF p. 14, passes \(L\to\infty\) in (16):
\[
\alpha A_T(x_c,y)+\beta(y)B_T(x_c,y)=1.
\]
Theorem 10, printed/PDF pp. 14–15, proves \(B_T(x_c,1)\to0\) as \(T\to\infty\), without a rate. Section 4.5 factors arches at their last boundary contact and uses this decay in the reverse inequality to obtain \(y_c\le y^*\); hence equality.

Theorem 10’s proof occupies the full appendix (printed/PDF pp. 16–24). The renewal equation gives a probability law on irreducible bridges. If mean bridge height were finite, Proposition 12 transfers this to finite mean width; the two-sided stationary ergodic renewal process (Proposition 15) and symmetry then give positive density of diamond renewal points (Proposition 16). Lemma 17 supplies typical finite prefixes with controlled height and many diamond points. The stick-break map (Eq. (27), p. 23), rotating a selected segment by \(\pi/3\), creates wide bridges; a bound on the multiplicity of preimages contradicts the width/height law of large numbers. This proves infinite mean height, which via the renewal limit yields \(B_T(x_c,1)\to0\).

## Limits and tool use

The theorem concerns the half-plane honeycomb adsorption model at \(n=0\), not bulk SAW scaling limits. The general O(n) formula remains a conjecture in this paper. The SLE-based expectation \(B_T(x_c,1)\asymp T^{-1/4}\) (Remark after Theorem 10, printed/PDF p. 15) is not proved; the theorem establishes only convergence to zero, with no decay rate. Numerical/series estimates for other lattices in the introduction are not rigorous results of this theorem.
