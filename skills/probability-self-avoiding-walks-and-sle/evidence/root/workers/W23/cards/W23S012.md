## Models and assumptions

For a locally finite infinite connected quasi-transitive graph, §2 uses strictly positive H-invariant edge weights φ, bounded above and bounded away from zero by quasi-transitivity. A walk weight is the product of its edge weights and w(Π) is the sum over walks. The weighted connective constant is the n-th-root limit of aggregate n-step SAW weights.

For unbounded support, §3 instead works on a finitely generated virtually (H,F)-indicable group Γ, with a symmetric summable weight φ:Γ→[0,∞), φ(1)=0, whose positive support spans Γ. The induced weighted complete graph need not be locally finite. A generalized edge length ℓ is positive, symmetric, and bounded below by ℓ_inf>0. Weighted SAWs and bridges are counted in length bins [m,m+c), not by edge count; c must be sufficiently large (at least A from (5.3)).

The key extra hypothesis is (3.9): for ε∈[1,2) and C>0,
|h_F(u)−h_F(v)|≤C ℓ(u⁻¹v)^ε whenever φ(u⁻¹v)>0.
It bounds a walk's total height span by Cℓ(π)^ε. The strict exponent ε<2 ensures the distinct-span partition factor remains subexponential in ℓ(π).

## Exact conclusions

**Theorems 2.1 and 2.5 (pp.5–7).** In the locally finite weighted setting, the weighted connective constant exists. With a unimodular graph height function and invariant positive weights, the weighted bridge constant equals it. The paper explicitly omits the detailed proof of Theorem 2.5 and summarizes its dependency in §8, p.25; this card does not claim a new line-by-line proof beyond what the source states.

**Theorem 2.7 (pp.7–8).** For the locally finite setting, under the sup metric on H-invariant edge weights, μ and β are continuous. The bound is
1−d_sup(ψ,φ)/φ_inf ≤ μ(G,ψ)/μ(G,φ) ≤ 1+d_sup(ψ,φ)/φ_inf,
and the same comparison holds for β. The lower estimate is informative when d_sup<φ_inf; the paper does not claim a positive lower bound otherwise.

**Theorem 3.7 (pp.10–11).** Under the unbounded-support group assumptions with ℓ∈Λ, binned weighted connective and bridge constants exist for sufficiently large c and are c-independent. For μ, the general definition uses limsup; it becomes a limit if ℓ-length on the support is bounded.

**Theorem 3.10 (p.12; proof §6, pp.17–22).** Under the extra condition (3.9), μ_{φ,ℓ}=β_{φ,ℓ}; for all sufficiently large bin widths c, the full limit w_φ(σ_{m,c})^(1/m) exists and equals μ. If ν≤φ, then μ_{ν,ℓ}≤μ_{φ,ℓ}. Rendered p.12 confirms the full-limit statement and exponent range.

**Theorem 4.1 (p.13; proof §7, pp.22–25).** On the set of (φ,ℓ) with common C, ε, and μ≤W, μ is continuous for the normalized metric D_sup in (4.1). Proposition 7.4 (pp.24–25) proves convergence under truncating φ only if the pair is continuous at zero: small positive φ(γ) must force ℓ(γ)→∞.

## Proof dependencies and constants

For Theorem 3.10, the authors select a finite generating subset of the support to get a locally finite Cayley subgraph and strong height function. They choose uniformly positive-weight, uniformly bounded-length upward extensions and bounded orbit connectors. The bridge-unwrapping map reorders/reverses pieces and inserts connectors; for at least three pieces it multiplies weight by θ_{i,j}² and shifts length by 2s_{i,j}. The proof pays those factors using θ_min, orbit-connector length r, and bounded multiplicity r+1 (pp.18–21). The span sequence has distinct positive integer terms. Hardy–Ramanujan bounds its partition count; (3.9) puts total span at O(ℓ^ε), so the loss is subexponential because ε<2. The SAW count is then split at a minimum-height vertex and extended by a lower step (pp.21–22).

Constants in Propositions 6.1–6.2 are denoted D and depend on ψ,A,a,θ_min,θ_max,r,s; the same letter D may change value. The source gives no explicit numeric final constant. This dependence matters when comparing different weights or groups.

## Method use and transfer limits

- Provisional skill use: weighted SAW growth, bridge constants, height-controlled unfolding, continuity, and truncation under the stated group and length hypotheses.
- C107: scalar weighted graph counts only; no curve tightness, domain-Markov property, or SLE identification follows.
- C108: not a quenched theorem. The paper's weights are deterministic and group-translation-invariant, or invariant on a quasi-transitive graph. It does not prove an almost-sure limit for counts in a random percolation cluster or independence of the root within that cluster.
- Do not assert a full binned limit for general unbounded weights: §3.7 explicitly leaves this open absent an added condition; bridge equality uses (3.9).
