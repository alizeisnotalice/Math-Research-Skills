## Model and main results

The model is a uniform infinite half-planar quadrangulation with simple boundary (UIHPQS), decorated by a self-avoiding path; the principal theorem glues two independent UIHPQS copies along their positive boundary rays. The theorem is about random planar maps, their graph metric, vertex measure, and a marked interface path. It is not the SAW on a fixed Euclidean lattice.

**Theorem 1.1 (printed and physical p. 7).** Let `Q_zip` be the positive-boundary gluing of two independent UIHPQS maps, with interface path `λ_zip`. For `n∈N`, rescale the graph metric by `(9/8)^{1/4} n^{-1/4}`, put mass `(4n)^{-1} deg(v)` at each vertex, and parameterize the linearly interpolated interface by
`η_zip^n(t)=λ_zip((2^{3/2}/3)n^{1/2}t)`, `t≥0`.
The resulting curve-decorated metric measure spaces converge in law in the local Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov–uniform (GHPU) topology to the metric gluing of two independent Brownian half-planes along positive boundary rays, with the summed pushforward area measure and glued boundary path. This random metric space is identified, **using [GM16, Corollary 1.2]**, as a weight-4 `√(8/3)`-LQG quantum wedge decorated by an independent chordal SLE`8/3`, parameterized by LQG boundary length. The convergence theorem itself is not an independent proof of that SLE/LQG identification.

**Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 (printed/physical pp. 8–9).** The paper proves the analogous local GHPU scaling limits for (i) two UIHPQS maps glued along their full boundaries and (ii) one UIHPQS glued to itself along the two boundary rays. Their quantum-cone/two-sided or whole-plane SLE descriptions likewise invoke [GM16, Corollaries 1.5 and 1.4], respectively. The authors state that the proofs are essentially identical to the detailed proof of Theorem 1.1.

## Proof dependency and mechanism

The paper says its proof can be read without SLE/LQG theory. The nontrivial external inputs it names are the local GHPU framework and UIHPQS-to-Brownian-half-plane convergence of [GM17b, Theorem 1.12], plus the UIHPQS peeling estimates (Section 3, based on cited random-map results). The proof is not the naive assertion that gluing commutes with convergence; the metric gluing must first be controlled.

1. **Peeling (Section 3, pp. 20–27):** establish spatial-Markov peeling rules and estimates when peeling around a vertex or boundary arc of the UIHPQS.
2. **Glued peeling and jumps (Section 4, pp. 27–31):** define a joint exploration of both maps near the glued interface and prove upper-tail controls on its jumps.
3. **Moment estimates (Section 5, pp. 31–44):** Proposition 5.1 gives boundary-cluster moment bounds for every `p∈[1,3/2)`. The restriction is tied to the `k^{-5/2}` peeling/swallowing tail, so do not state a second-moment result beyond the proved range. First-moment peeling-time and martingale bounds feed the geometric controls.
4. **Geodesics (Section 6, pp. 44–62):** Propositions 6.1–6.2 and their multiscale good-scale argument control geodesics in the glued map, including how much of a near-minimizing/geodesic path can remain near the interface. These estimates rule out metric collapse under gluing.
5. **Scaling limit (Section 7, pp. 62–77):** combine tightness/subsequential GHPU limits with maps from the two Brownian half-planes into the limit; establish quotient/interface properties and use Section 6's geodesic controls to prove the quotient map preserves distances, hence identifies the limit with the Brownian-half-plane gluing.
6. **Appendix A (pp. 77–78):** notation index. References are pp. 79–81.

The crucial metric scaling is `n^{-1/4}`, boundary time scaling `n^{1/2}`, and the explicit constants above. These constants belong to this quadrangulation normalization. Do not move them to Euclidean-lattice SAW or a different map encoding without a normalization argument.
