## Exact model and theorem

Let \(c_n\) be the number of n-step nearest-neighbor self-avoiding walks on the hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice \(H\), rooted at a fixed vertex. Concatenation gives \(c_{n+m}\le c_nc_m\), so \(\mu=\lim_n c_n^{1/n}\) exists. Theorem 1 proves

\[
\mu(H)=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2},\qquad x_c=\mu(H)^{-1}=1/\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}.
\]

The proof switches to walks between edge midpoints and assigns a walk \(\gamma\) weight \(x^{\ell(\gamma)}\), where \(\ell\) is the number of visited lattice vertices. The partition function \(Z(x)=\sum_{\gamma:a\to H}x^{\ell(\gamma)}\) has radius of convergence \(1/\mu(H)\).

## Proof dependencies and constants

1. For a finite simply connected domain \(\Omega\) and boundary starting mid-edge \(a\), define
   \[
   F(z)=\sum_{\gamma\subset\Omega:a\to z}e^{-i\sigma W_\gamma(a,z)}x^{\ell(\gamma)}.
   \]
   Lemma 1 states that at \(x=x_c\), \(\sigma=5/8\), every vertex \(v\) with incident mid-edges \(p,q,r\) satisfies \((p-v)F(p)+(q-v)F(q)+(r-v)F(r)=0\).

2. Its proof pairs paths visiting all three incident mid-edges, reversing the almost-complete loop, and groups paths visiting one or two mid-edges into extension triplets. In the paired case, relative to their common segment the two windings are \(-4\pi/3\) and \(+4\pi/3\), so their difference is \(8\pi/3\); the vector-weighted pair contributions then cancel. Triplet contributions cancel because the added step contributes \(x_c\). The turn phase is \(\lambda=e^{-i5\pi/24}\), and the critical identity uses \(x_c^{-1}=2\cos(\pi/8)\). Simple connectivity and boundary placement are used to fix the winding offsets.

3. Summing the local relation over vertices of a finite strip cancels interior mid-edges. Boundary winding and reflection yield
   \[
   1=c_\alpha A^{{x_c}}_{T,L}+B^{{x_c}}_{T,L}+c_\epsilon E^{{x_c}}_{T,L},\quad
   c_\alpha=\cos(3\pi/8),\quad c_\epsilon=\cos(\pi/4).
   \]
   After \(L\to\infty\), the same identity holds with limiting partition functions.

4. To show \(Z(x_c)=\infty\), if some limiting \(E_T^{x_c}>0\), summing the finite-height contributions over \(L\) already diverges. Otherwise \(E_T^{x_c}=0\) for all \(T\), so \(1=c_\alpha A_T^{x_c}+B_T^{x_c}\). Cutting a newly wider left-crossing walk at its first visit to the next strip gives \(A_{T+1}^{x_c}-A_T^{x_c}\le x_c(B_{T+1}^{x_c})^2\). Hence
   \[
   B_T^{x_c}\ge \frac{\min\{B_1^{x_c},(c_\alpha x_c)^{-1}\}}{T},
   \]
   so the sum of bridge weights diverges and therefore \(Z(x_c)=\infty\). This implies \(\mu(H)\ge x_c^{-1}\).

5. The boundary identity gives \(B_T^{x_c}\le1\). For \(x<x_c\), every width-\(T\) bridge has length at least \(T\), so \(B_T^x\le(x/x_c)^T B_T^{x_c}\le(x/x_c)^T\). The canonical Hammersley–Welsh decomposition into strictly ordered bridge widths then gives \(Z(x)\le 2\prod_{T>0}(1+B_T^x)^2<\infty\). Thus \(\mu(H)\le x_c^{-1}\), proving equality.

## Scope and migration gaps

- This is an exact connective-constant theorem on the hexagonal lattice. The proof is lattice-specific; the authors explicitly say the method does not directly apply to \(\mathbb Z^2\).
- The paper's SLE(8/3) discussion is conjectural. Conjecture 1 proposes chordal SAW convergence; Conjecture 2 proposes a normalized observable limit. The local relation supplies only about two-thirds as many equations as edge-observable values, so it alone does not imply a scaling limit. No lattice tightness, domain-Markov proof, or SLE identification is established here.
- Therefore this source supports the critical fugacity and an observable-based route, not C107's weak-convergence conclusion. See also W23S002 for improved counts and its explicit conditional theorem.
