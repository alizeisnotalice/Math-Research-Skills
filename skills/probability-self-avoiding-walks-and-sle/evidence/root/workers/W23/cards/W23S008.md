## Definitions and exact statements

For a graph \(G=(V,E)\), let \(\sigma_n(v)\) be the number of length-\(n\) self-avoiding walks (SAWs) from \(v\), with parallel-edge choices counted as distinct. The paper distinguishes the rooted lower and upper growth rates
\[
\underline\mu(v):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sigma_n(v)^{1/n},\qquad
\overline\mu(v):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sigma_n(v)^{1/n},
\]
from the global connective constant
\[
\mu(G):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sup_{v\in V}\sigma_n(v)\right)^{1/n},
\]
whose limit exists by subadditivity (physical p.3, (2.1)). These rooted lower/upper rates need not be identified with each other on a general graph. For an infinite connected quasi-transitive graph with finite vertex-degrees, Hammersley’s Theorem 3.1 (pp.3–4) states the stronger result \(\lim_n\sigma_n(v)^{1/n}=\mu(G)\) for every \(v\); this full rooted limit is used only under those hypotheses.

**Theorem 1.1 (p.1).** If \(G\) is infinite, connected, \(\Delta\)-regular, vertex-transitive, and simple, with \(\Delta\ge2\), then
\[
\mu(G)\ge \sqrt{\Delta-1}.
\]
The radical covers all of \(\Delta-1\), not just \(\Delta\). The standard upper bound is \(\mu(G)\le\Delta-1\) (p.6, (3.3)). Vertex-transitivity and simplicity are essential hypotheses for the stated lower bound; merely quasi-transitive regular graphs can have \(\mu=1\) (p.7, Figure 4.1).

**Theorem 4.1 (p.6).** For an infinite connected vertex-transitive \(\Delta\)-regular graph, \(\mu\le\Delta-1\); also \(\mu\ge\sqrt{\Delta-1}\) if either (a) \(G\) is simple, or (b) \(G\) is nonsimple and \(\Delta\le4\).

**Theorem 4.2 (pp.7, 13–14).** If \(G\) is infinite, connected, quasi-transitive (parallel edges allowed), and \(\Delta\ge3\), then \(\mu(G)<\Delta-1\) in either case: (a) \(G\) is \(\Delta\)-regular and contains a cycle; or (b) every vertex has degree at most \(\Delta\), and at least one vertex has degree at most \(\Delta-1\). Thus the regular tree is the boundary case: the strict result does not apply to it.

**Theorem 4.3 (p.8).** For an infinite connected \(\Delta\)-regular graph (parallel edges allowed), if a vertex \(v\) satisfies the paper’s condition \(\Pi_v\), then its rooted lower growth rate satisfies \(\underline\mu(v)\ge\sqrt{\Delta-1}\). The theorem’s proof gives a liminf bound; it does not assert a full rooted limit for every graph satisfying only \(\Pi_v\). Condition \(\Pi_v\) is global: for every infinite SAW \(\pi\) from \(v\), and each traversed triple \(ewe'\), red nonextendable outgoing edges at \(w\) must admit an equal-sized family of distinct rerouting edges \(f_j=\langle x_j,y_j\rangle\), where \(y_j\) lies on the earlier prefix \(\pi_w\), \(y_j\ne w\), and a SAW from \(w\) beginning with the corresponding red edge reaches \(x_j\) while avoiding \(\pi_w\) except at \(w\) (pp.7–8). In cubic graphs, \(\Pi_v\) is equivalent to the paper’s local third-edge extension condition: for every relevant infinite SAW and traversed triple, the third incident edge must start an infinite SAW extension. The separate graph-level condition that every vertex lies on some doubly infinite SAW is stated only as a sufficient condition for a cubic graph to satisfy \(\Pi\) (p.8), not as an equivalent reformulation. The bridge graph \(B_\Delta\) satisfies \(\Pi\) and has \(\underline\mu(v)=\mu=\sqrt{\Delta-1}\) (p.8); the paper further realizes every value in \([\sqrt{\Delta-1},\Delta-1]\) among the stated \(\Pi\)-graphs by replacing tree edges with bridge-graph segments (p.9).

## Proof mechanisms and dependencies

- **Lower bound under \(\Pi_v\), Theorem 4.3 (physical pp.9–11).** Count only extendable finite SAWs. For an extendable \(2n\)-step SAW \(\pi\), at each of its first \(2n\) vertices the \(\Delta-2\) off-path edges split into red/blue. The \(\Pi_v\) rerouting witnesses for distinct positions are disjoint: a common witness would splice two red detours and the intervening segment of \(\pi\) into an infinite extension, contradicting redness. Therefore the total number of blue edges/branches is at least \(n(\Delta-2)\). Group branches by their roots. An induction on total branch count shows the number of resulting extendable \(2n\)-step SAWs is at least \((\Delta-1)^n\). Hence the rooted lower growth rate obeys \(\underline\mu(v)=\liminf_n\sigma_n(v)^{1/n}\ge\sqrt{\Delta-1}\); monotonicity of \(\sigma_k\) is used to pass from even to all lengths. This argument uses the extendability/rerouting condition, not only local regularity, and supplies a liminf conclusion rather than a full limit in the general \(\Pi_v\) setting.
- **Theorem 4.1(a), physical p.11.** For a simple vertex-transitive graph, edge connectivity to infinity is \(\lambda_f=\Delta\) (cited Lemma 3.3); infinite Menger then supplies \(\Delta\) edge-disjoint infinite SAWs from each vertex, which imply \(\Pi_v\). The nonsimple cases \(\Delta\le4\) are handled separately by degree/parallel-edge case analysis on pp.11–13.
- **Strict upper bound, Theorem 4.2, physical pp.13–14.** Fix an incident edge \(e\) at \(u\), and count \(n\)-step SAWs avoiding \(e\). Quasi-transitivity plus a cycle (case a), or a deficient-degree vertex (case b), gives a uniform length \(N\) with \(\sigma_N(u,e)\le(\Delta-1)^N-1\). Iterating the last-block count inequality bounds \(\mu\le[(\Delta-1)^N-1]^{1/N}<\Delta-1\). The cycle’s uniform bounded-distance occurrence is where quasi-transitivity enters.
- **General-graph connective constant, physical pp.3–6.** The paper cites Hammersley’s result and gives its own subadditivity/root-independence argument. It proves a multiplicative comparison between counts rooted at adjacent vertices and propagates equality along paths.

## Transfer boundary

This is a graph-combinatorics source for degree-based connective-constant bounds and extension/re-routing counting. It supplies no tightness theorem, curve topology, domain-Markov property, or identification of a planar SAW scaling limit with SLE; use only as auxiliary connective-constant evidence for C107. It does not address random percolation-cluster quenched growth or the existence/root-independence of a full quenched limit in C108. Exact finite tree enumeration in `../math/tree_saw_counts.py` illustrates the equality boundary \(\mu=\Delta-1\) for the regular tree; that finite computation is not the proof of Theorem 4.2.
