## Standing model and definitions

Section 2 (physical pp.3–4) takes graphs to be infinite, connected, locally finite; parallel edges are allowed, and walks with the same vertex sequence but different edge choices count separately. For quasi-transitive graphs, the connective constant \(\mu(G)\) exists, is independent of the root, and obeys \(\sup_v\sigma_n(v)\ge\mu^n\) (Theorem 2.1, cited from [18]). In Section 3 the base graph is explicitly infinite, connected, vertex-transitive, simple and of finite degree \(\Delta\) (physical p.5); the augmented graph may have parallel edges.

## Main results

**Quasi-transitive edge augmentation, Theorem 3.2 (physical p.5).** Let \(G=(V,E)\) be the standing infinite connected vertex-transitive simple graph, and let \(\bar G=(V,\bar E)\) be formed by adding a nonempty set of edges (possibly parallel to existing edges). Assume \(\bar E\setminus E\) is quasi-transitive under a subgroup \(A\) of a transitive group \(\Gamma\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\); assume \(A\) has the finite-coset property (there are finitely many coset translates partitioning \(V\)); and assume \(A\le\operatorname{Aut}(\bar G)\). Then
\[
\mu(G)<\mu(\bar G).
\]
The finite-coset property holds, in particular, when \(A\) is a normal subgroup of \(\Gamma\) acting quasi-transitively on \(G\), or \([\Gamma:A]<\infty\) (Proposition 3.3, p.5). The theorem does not say that any arbitrary finite/local edge modification of a lattice strictly changes its connective constant.

**Normal quotient, Theorem 3.8 (physical pp.9–10).** Let \(\Gamma\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) act transitively, and let \(A\lhd\Gamma\) be nontrivial. The directed orbit quotient \(\vec G=G/A\) has vertices the \(A\)-orbits; the directed edge multiplicity from orbit \(v\) to orbit \(w\) is \(|\partial v\cap w|\). Labeled lifts show \(\vec\mu\le\mu(G)\). Define the type of \(A\) by the minimum distance between distinct vertices in a common orbit: type 1 if it is 1, type 2 if it is 2, and type 3 if it is at least 3. Then \(\vec\mu<\mu(G)\) if (a) the type is 1 or 3; or (b) the type is 2 and at least one of:

1. (i) there is a SAW \(v_0,w,v'\) with \(v_0,v'\) in the same orbit and \(|\partial v_0\cap [w]|\ge2\); or
2. (ii) there is a SAW \(v_0=w_0,w_1,\ldots,w_\ell=v'\) with \(v_0,v'\) in the same orbit, the intermediate orbit classes \([w_i]\) pairwise distinct from each other and from \([v_0]\), and \(v'=\beta v_0\) for some \(\beta\in A\) fixing none of the intermediate vertices \(w_i\).

If \(\mu(G)=1\), the paper invokes the degree-2 classification to identify \(G\cong\mathbb Z\); its quotient has finitely many vertices, hence \(\vec\mu=0\). The theorem is stated for the vertex-transitive/locally finite connected setting, not for arbitrary quasi-transitive graphs; the authors give a counterexample to that relaxation immediately after the statement (p.10).

**Cayley consequences.** Corollary 4.1 (physical p.14) gives strict decrease when a nontrivial relator is added to a finitely presented infinite group under its exact presentation conditions. Corollary 4.3 (physical p.15) gives strict increase when a nonidentity element not already in the generator set is added together with its inverse. These are group-presentation consequences, not lattice-scaling-limit results.

**Algorithmic qualification, Theorem 3.11 (physical p.13).** Under the same quotient assumptions, if a finitely computable sequence \(b_n\le\mu(G)\) converges upward to \(\mu(G)\), there is a terminating algorithm giving \(R(G,A)<1\) with \(\vec\mu/\mu(G)\le R\). The authors say the algorithm is unlikely to be useful in practice because it requires successive SAW enumerations of \(G\) and \(\vec G\). This is not an explicit numerical gap available from the theorem alone.

## Proof chain and proof-detail caution

- **Quotient.** SAWs of the orbit graph lift by fixed edge labels to SAWs in \(G\), so \(\vec\mu\le\mu\). Section 5 (pp.16–26) introduces shortest orbit-return cycles and their length \(\Lambda\). Lemmas 5.3–5.4 (pp.18–20) use a pattern-theorem counting argument to show that SAWs with too few occurrences of the relevant cycle pattern have strictly smaller exponential growth than \(\mu\). For the remaining walks, select a positive-density set of well-separated cycle occurrences; independently choose which occurrences to replace by a cycle copy. Equations (5.32)–(5.36) (pp.24–25) compare the binomial number of choices to the number of resulting lifted SAWs, producing an exponential multiplicity and hence a strict growth gap. The constants are existential and depend on \(\epsilon,m,\Delta,\Lambda\); the result does not supply a universal gap.
- **Proof coverage of Theorem 3.8.** The formal proof on pp.16–25 explicitly assumes either condition (a) or type-2 condition (b)(i) (Assumption 5.2, p.16), and completes those cases. Remark 5.5 (pp.25–26) says it remains to handle type 2 with (b)(ii), then sketches a reduction to Lemma 5.4’s Case 3 using the no-fixed-intermediate-vertex hypothesis. Preserve this proof-detail qualification: the theorem statement includes (b)(ii), but the article’s treatment is a short proof sketch rather than the fully expanded main proof given for the other cases. Reviewer should assess whether that sketch closes the dependency for the intended use.
- **Augmentation.** Section 6 (pp.27–35) starts from a new edge plus a shortest old-graph path, creating a cycle absent in \(G\). Lemma 6.1 gives a strict exponential loss for walks avoiding a designated cycle pattern. Lemmas 6.2–6.3 propagate this through a finite sequence of edge-orbit deletions and turn liminf deficits into a positive-density pattern restriction. Reinsertions of a positive density of edge-orbit cycles create many distinct SAWs in \(\bar G\); equations (6.30)–(6.41) bound reconstruction multiplicity and force a strict increase. The proof first reduces the added edges to one \(A\)-orbit (Assumption 6.4, p.33) and then uses the finite-coset/algebraic hypotheses.

## Transfer boundary

These are exact monotonicity theorems for infinite transitive graphs under global orbit/group assumptions. They are useful as auxiliary connective-constant tools for C107, but do not establish tightness, a discrete domain-Markov property, curve convergence, or SLE identification. They do not apply to the random percolation cluster in C108 merely because one compares local graph environments; the required transitivity/quotient setup is absent. Theorem 3.11 is a conditional algorithmic existence statement, not a practical computed bound.
