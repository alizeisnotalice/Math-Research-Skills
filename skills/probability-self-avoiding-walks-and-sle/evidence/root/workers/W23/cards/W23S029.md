## Model and notation

The paper's ambient class \(\mathcal G\) is infinite, connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graphs without loops or parallel edges (physical pp. 1, 4–5). An \(n\)-step SAW visits no vertex twice; a trail traverses no undirected edge twice and may revisit vertices. Their counts from a root have exponential rates \(\mu(G)\) and \(\mu_T(G)\), with \(\mu\le\mu_T\) (pp. 1–2, 5). The degree is constant by transitivity.

A graph height function \((h,H)\) has \(h:V\to\mathbb Z\), \(h(0)=0\), a quasi-transitive subgroup \(H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) under which height differences are invariant, and at every vertex both a lower- and a higher-height neighbor (Definition 4.1, physical p. 10). “Transitive height function” adds that \(H\) is vertex-transitive; unimodularity is a separate property (pp. 10–12). Bridges are defined relative to \(h\) or \(-h\), and bridgeability means an \(H\)-image of the pattern occurs in a bridge.

## Theorems and proof dependencies

- **Theorem 1.2 (physical/printed p. 2).** If \(G\in\mathcal G\) has a transitive graph height function, degree at least 4, and a cycle, then \(\mu(G)<\mu_T(G)\). The paper explicitly presents this as a partial resolution of Question 1.1, not an iff under these hypotheses alone.
- **Theorem 3.3 (p. 7), hypercubic pattern theorem.** For \(d\ge2\) and a bridgeable SAW pattern \(p\in\Sigma_b\), let \(P=\{p,p^{\rm ref},p^{\rm rev},p^{\rm rr}\}\). If \(\sigma_n(P,\epsilon)\) counts \(n\)-step SAWs from 0 containing at most \(\epsilon n\) copies of each member of \(P\), there exists \(a>0\) such that \(\limsup_n\sigma_n(P,\epsilon)^{1/n}<\mu(\mathbb Z^d)\) for \(0\le\epsilon<a\). The theorem requires a bridgeable pattern; the reflection/reversal variants are part of this lattice formulation.
- **Proposition 3.7 and Theorem 3.3 proof (pp. 8–10).** Bridge counts satisfy the prime-bridge renewal identity \(b_n=q_n+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}q_m b_{n-m}\), so \(B(x)=Q(x)/(1-Q(x))\). At \(x=1/\beta(\mathbb Z^d)\), Abel's lemma gives \(Q(x)=1\). The source states that removing prime bridges containing \(p\) gives \(Q_p(\beta^{-1})<1\), and then uses continuity inside the convergence ball to assert \(Q_p(\beta^{-1}+\eta)<1\) for some \(\eta>0\) (Eq. 3.17, physical p. 9). The displayed continuity statement alone does not justify a right-neighborhood of a boundary point: the convergence radius of this subseries may equal \(\beta^{-1}\). For example, \(R(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}x^n/[2n(n+1)]\) has radius 1 and \(R(1)=1/2\), but diverges for every \(x>1\). This is an exact counterexample to that inference form only, not a graph counterexample or a disproof of the paper's pattern theorem. Theorem 3.3 and the source's claimed growth conclusion remain recorded as stated; the extra boundary/radius input in this proof step is unverified here. The bridge-to-SAW transfer uses a subexponential \(e^{c\sqrt n}\beta(P)^n\) bound (Eq. 3.18), citing Hammersley–Welsh results. The positive-density extension from zero occurrences is referred to [36, Lemma 7.2.5], not proved in full here.
- **Theorem 4.5 (p. 13), general height-function SAW pattern theorem.** For a height function on \(G\) and bridgeable \(p\notin\Pi\), the exponential growth rate of SAWs containing neither \(p\) nor its reversal is strictly below \(\mu(G)\); for some \(a>0\), the same strict gap holds when there are at most \(\epsilon n\) copies of either for \(0\le\epsilon<a\). In the transitive-height case \(\Pi=\varnothing\); in the merely quasi-transitive case the finite exceptional set depends on the chosen connectors and has size at most \(M(M-1)\) for \(M\) \(H\)-orbits (pp. 13, 21–24). The source develops the proof in §5 using bridge growth, half-space decomposition and reversal. At physical p. 24, Eq. 5.23 repeats the step from a boundary inequality to a strict right-neighborhood using continuity inside the convergence ball; as at Eq. 3.17, the radius/boundary input needed for that inference is not verified by the displayed continuity statement alone. Preserve Theorem 4.5 as source-stated, while recording this proof-input limit. Unimodularity is not an assumption of Theorem 4.5.
- **Theorem 4.7 (p. 14), trail analogue.** Under the same height-function setup and a bridgeable trail-pattern \(p\notin\Pi\), the rate of trails avoiding both \(p\) and its reversal is below \(\mu_T\), with a positive low-frequency threshold. The source states that its proof follows mutatis mutandis and omits it in §5.4 (p. 27); joining two trails can itself create a trail-pattern, which the omitted surgery must handle.
- **Theorem 6.1 (p. 27).** For \(d\ge4\) and \(G\in\mathcal G_d\) with a height function, \(\mu<\mu_T\) iff there are a cycle \(C\) and \(v\in C\) whose trail \(t\)-pattern \(p(C,v)\) traverses \(C\) and returns to \(v\), and is \(t\)-bridgeable. The forward strict-gap direction applies Theorem 4.7; conversely \(\mu_T=\beta_T^+\vee\beta_T^-\), so a sufficiently long trail bridge above \(\mu\) is not a SAW, contains a closed subwalk, and yields a cycle-circumnavigation \(t\)-pattern. **Proposition 6.2** (p. 27; proof pp. 28–30) shows that when the height function is transitive and \(G\) is not a tree, such a bridgeable cycle-pattern exists. The construction uses a doubly-infinite trail with height tending to opposite infinities; the final degree-4 case needs two choices of first descending neighbor. Transitivity is used only in that final construction. Question 6.3 (p. 28) asks whether quasi-transitivity suffices; it remains open in the paper.
- **Theorem 7.2 (p. 31), line-graph/kite relation.** Replacing each degree-\(d\) vertex of \(G\) by a kite \(K_d\) gives line graph \(G_K\). For \(d\ge3\), \(\mu(G_K)\ge\mu^-(G_K)\) and \(g_d(1/\mu^-(G_K))=1/\mu(G)\), where \(g_d(\xi)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}\xi^\ell (d-2)!/(d-\ell-1)!\). The coefficient counts simple length-\(\ell\) paths in \(K_d\) joining two fixed distinct vertices. Separately, \(\mu_T(G)=\mu_1(G_K)\) (Eq. 7.1, p. 31), where \(\Sigma_1\) requires traversing exactly one kite edge per visit and permits later returns. Do not identify all SAWs of \(G_K\) with trails of \(G\); the paper explicitly notes extra SAWs when \(d\ge4\) (pp. 4, 30–32).

## Transfer to W23 problems

- **C107:** auxiliary counting-method source only. The pattern theorem is for \(\mathbb Z^d\) or transitive/quasi-transitive graphs with the stated height function and bridgeability; it is not a tightness theorem for hexagonal-lattice uniform SAWs, does not establish a curve-space weak limit, and does not give a domain Markov property or identify SLE\(_{8/3}\).
- **C108:** no transfer asserted. Its random two-dimensional supercritical percolation cluster is not the fixed transitive graph model treated here; neither quenched root-independent growth limits nor the C108 full \(n\)-th-root limit follow.

## Scope and proof-completeness notes

Theorem 1.2 needs degree at least 4, a cycle, and a transitive height function. The sharper iff in Theorem 6.1 retains the \(t\)-bridgeability condition; Proposition 6.2 proves that condition under transitivity, not for arbitrary quasi-transitive height functions. Theorem 4.7's detailed proof is omitted. In §3, the boundary-to-radius step at Eq. 3.17 is not justified by the displayed continuity-alone input; §5 repeats the same unverified input at Eq. 5.23. The exact power-series example above shows only that this inference form needs an additional radius or boundary argument; it is not a counterexample to either graph theorem. The bridge-to-SAW transfer and positive-frequency steps cite [36] and related sources. Those external cited proofs were not independently reread by this reader for this card. No result is transferred to planar scaling limits, weak SAW, random-cluster environments, or SLE.
