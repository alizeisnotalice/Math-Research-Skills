## Model and theorem statements

Throughout, walks are nearest-neighbour SAWs on the hypercubic lattice \(\mathbb Z^d\), with \(d\ge2\). Let \(c_n\) count length-\(n\) walks from the origin and \(\mu_c\) be the connective constant. A bridge has its initial point as the unique minimum of the \(d\)-th coordinate and its endpoint among the maxima (the endpoint need not be the unique maximum); \(b_n\) counts length-\(n\) bridges from 0.

- **Theorem 1.3, printed/PDF p. 3.** For each \(d\ge2\), there is an increasing \(\phi_d:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)\) such that for every pair of integers \(n\ge m>0\),
  \[
  \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathbf1\{\omega:0\to\mathbb Z^{d-1}\times\{m,m+1,\ldots\},\ \omega\text{ is a length-}n\text{ bridge}\}
  \le b_n e^{-\phi_d(m/n)n}. \tag{1.1}
  \]
  The input is the Duminil-Copin–Hammond bridge sub-ballisticity estimate. Its proof gives no usable numerical lower bound on \(\phi_d\).

**Literal endpoint scope flag.** The paper literally states (1.1) for every integer `n>=m>0` with `phi_d(1)>0`. At `d=2,n=m=1`, the only length-one bridge is the upward step, so `b_1=1` and the event count is also 1. The displayed bound would require `1<=exp(-phi_d(1))<1`, a contradiction. More generally, every nonempty bridge has positive endpoint height, so threshold `m=1` includes every bridge at every `n>=1` and also requires finite-input handling. This finite input case is checked by the saved MATLAB enumeration [`../math/p16_literal_endpoint_check.m`](../math/p16_literal_endpoint_check.m), with output [`../math/p16_literal_endpoint_check.json`](../math/p16_literal_endpoint_check.json). We retain the exact statement as printed and flag this literal defect; we do not infer a corrected endpoint, asymptotic-only version, or replacement constants. The paper’s qualitative Theorem 1.2 remains recorded as source-stated, but this card does not claim that its proof input has been repaired.

- **Theorem 1.2, printed/PDF p. 2.** As \(n\to\infty\), \(c_n\le \exp(o(\sqrt n))\mu_c^n\), for every \(d\ge2\). This is a qualitative improvement over the Hammersley–Welsh stretched-exponential correction.
- **Theorem 1.4, printed/PDF p. 3.** If an increasing \(\phi:(0,1]\to[0,\infty)\) satisfies (1.1), define
  \[
  \Phi(\varepsilon)=\inf_{\varepsilon\le\delta\le1}\delta^{-1}\phi(\delta),\qquad
  \psi(\varepsilon)=\sup\{\lambda\ge1:\ \varepsilon\le1-\exp[-\Phi(\lambda^{-1})/(\lambda-1)]\},
  \]
  and
  \[
  \Psi(n)=\inf_{0<\varepsilon<1}\left\{2[1-(1-\varepsilon)^{\psi(\varepsilon)}]^{-1}-(n+1)\log(1-\varepsilon)\right\}.
  \]
  Then for every integer \(n\ge0\), \(c_n\le e^{\Psi(n)-2}\mu_c^{n+1}\). The displayed definition of \(\psi\) is used on \((0,1)\); endpoint values are irrelevant to the infimum.

**Conditional-input limitation for Theorem 1.4.** The `Psi` formula and Theorem 1.4 inequality above are preserved as the paper’s exact conditional statement. An application requires a compatible verified input and explicit treatment of the finite endpoint case above. No fixed-speed asymptotic or prefactor variant is substituted into (1.1), and the exact (Psi) constants are not transferred to such a variant here.

- **Corollary 1.5, printed/PDF p. 4.** If the rate in (1.1) has a power lower bound \(\phi(\varepsilon)\ge C\varepsilon^\nu\) for some \(C>0,\nu>1\), the proof yields \(c_n\le\exp(O(n^{(\nu-1)/(2\nu-1)}))\mu_c^n\). The paper states the corollary with \(\phi(\varepsilon)=C\varepsilon^\nu\); the lower-bound formulation is the weaker condition used in its proof. Values \(\nu\le1\) are ruled out in Remark 2.5. The paper does not prove that its non-quantitative `phi_d` has any such power lower bound. Thus this is an additional source-stated conditional hypothesis, not an unconditional quantitative result. Its use also needs a compatible verified form of (1.1) and explicit finite-size handling; we have not re-derived the exact `Psi` constants for a modified input.

## Proof spine and usable interface

Section 2.1 (printed/PDF pp. 4–6) gives a short Hammersley–Welsh argument from the Madras–Slade susceptibility inequality, Proposition 2.1, \(\chi(z)\le z^{-1}\exp(2B(z)-2)\), where \(B(z)\) is the bridge generating function. Proposition 2.2 supplies the elementary coefficient estimate \(c_n\le \exp(\sqrt{8n}+O(n^{-1/2}))\mu_c^{n+1}\).

Section 2.2 (printed/PDF pp. 6–8) inserts the sub-ballistic bridge input. It splits bridges contributing to the height-truncated bridge partition function at length \(\lambda n\); the long part is penalized by (1.1), and the short part by the factor \((1-\varepsilon)^m\). With the definitions above, Lemma 2.4 obtains \(\xi((1-\varepsilon)z_c)\ge-\psi(\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon)\). Summing this bridge bound controls \(B((1-\varepsilon)z_c)\), and Proposition 2.1 plus the coefficient estimate proves Theorem 1.4. The paper uses positivity of \(\phi\) to infer \(\psi(\varepsilon)\to\infty\) and presents qualitative Theorem 1.2 and the conditional quantitative corollary by this route. We preserve those source-stated conclusions while flagging that the literal endpoint input in (1.1) needs explicit finite-size handling before it can serve as a verified application. Under a separately verified compatible quantitative rate, the paper’s optimization yields the stated corollary exponent; such a rate is not supplied here.

**Transfer limit:** this source controls SAW counts on \(\mathbb Z^d\) and bridge generating functions. It gives no sharp asymptotic for \(c_n\), no numerical value of the 2D critical exponent, and no curve tightness or SLE identification. The intro’s \(\gamma_2=43/32\), conformal-field and SLE discussion is conjectural/physics context, explicitly not a theorem of this paper. The quantitative corollary cannot be applied unless a corresponding quantitative \(\phi\) is independently proved.
