## Model and main theorems

For nearest-neighbor SAW counts rooted at the origin, write \(c_n=|\mathrm{SAW}_n|\) and \(\mu=\lim_n c_n^{1/n}\). The paper proves:

- **Theorem 1.2, square lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\).** For every \(\varepsilon<1/466\), infinitely many \(n\) satisfy
  \[
  c_n\le \exp(n^{1/2-\varepsilon})\mu^n.
  \]
  More strongly, infinitely many \(j\) have this bound for every integer \(n\in[j,j^{4/3}]\). Thus the set of lengths for which the bound holds has **upper asymptotic density one**: along the right endpoints \(N_j=\lfloor j^{4/3}\rfloor\), its density is at least \((N_j-j+1)/N_j\to1\). The theorem does not establish that natural density exists; if it does exist, it must equal one. This is not an all-\(n\) result.
- **Theorem 1.3, honeycomb lattice \(H\).** For every \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/42)\), all sufficiently large \(n\) satisfy
  \[
  c_n(H)\le \exp(n^{1/2-\varepsilon})\mu(H)^n.
  \]
- **Theorem 1.4, conditional.** If Conjecture 1 holds for some \(k\), namely the connective constant of SAWs on the universal cover with the radius-\(k\) singularity neighborhood removed satisfies \(\mu(U_k^\infty)=\mu(H)\), then there exist \(C_0,C>0\) with \(c_n(H)\le C_0n^C\mu(H)^n\) for all \(n\). The conjecture is not proved in this paper. The proof also notes a weaker sufficient hypothesis involving good-polygon growth, but that condition is likewise assumed rather than established.

## Proof architecture and exact interfaces

### Hammersley–Welsh map

For half-space walks, alternating record maxima/minima split the path into branches that become bridges after alternate vertical reflection. Their heights form a strictly decreasing sequence. Concatenating a bridge list is many-to-one, with incoming multiplicity controlled by the number of integer partitions of the total height; the Hardy–Ramanujan partition estimate gives \(\exp(C\sqrt n)\). This yields the classical \(c_n\le\exp(C\sqrt n)\mu^n\) bound. Lemmas 2.1–2.2, physical pp.6–8.

### Wide polygon abundance

For \(p\in\mathrm{SAP}_m\), the wide-polygon set \(\mathrm{WSAP}_m^u\) requires line-width at least \(u\) and height at most \(16u\). The rooted/oriented representative convention is specified in §3.1.

- On \(\mathbb Z^2\), Proposition 3.1 says that for every \(\alpha>29\), infinitely many \(u\) admit an \(m\in[4u+4,4u^2+4]\) with \(|\mathrm{WSAP}_m^u|\ge u^{-\alpha}\mu^m\). Lemma 3.3 bins polygon diameters, keeps a constant fraction of polygons that are at least as wide as high, joins matching-height pairs by plaquette surgery, proves injectivity by undoing the unique plaquette, and uses Cauchy–Schwarz on the height classes. The final subsequence uses divergence of the critical bridge generating series \(\sum_m\mu^{-m}|\mathrm{SAB}_m|=\infty\).
- On \(H\), Proposition 3.2 gives constants \(C,c_0>0\) such that for every \(u\in\mathbb N\) there is \(m\le c_0u^2\) with \(|\mathrm{WSAP}_m^u(H)|\ge C u^{-10}\mu(H)^m\). The proof in §6 first obtains the parafermionic boundary identity in a triangle/strip, then concatenates three crossing walks into an upper-half-plane arc, reflects and pairs arcs into a round polygon, and bounds the number of possible lengths by \(O(u^2)\) and the number of rooted representatives per polygon trace by at most \(16u\). This all-\(u\) abundance is stronger than the square-lattice subsequence bound.

### Madras join and polygon insertion

For the square lattice, Lemma 3.4 requires the inserted polygon's vertical range to lie at least three units inside the bridge's range. The join preserves a bridge, adds \(m+16\) edges, has junction-edge chemical distance \(m+7\), preserves all but at most two branch edges, and at most four bridge/polygon inputs share a specified output and junction. The join commutes for vertically separated polygons. On \(H\), Appendix A changes the surgery to face/hexagon operations: it adds \(m+18\) edges, junction chemical distance is \(m+3\), and the inverse multiplicity constant is \(L(H)=3\) instead of \(L(\mathbb Z^2)=12\).

For walks confined to a strip with many branches, §§5.1–5.4 find a large family of viable near-touch slots, place \(\kappa r\) polygons, and use intersections of adjacent branches in the unfolded output to recover each surgery. The multi-valued map has at least
\[
\exp\{\kappa r[(1/2-\varepsilon)\log n-\log(8\kappa)]\}
\]
outgoing choices and at most \(L^{\kappa r}\) inverse choices. Polygon scarcity contributes the factor \(n^{-\alpha\delta}\) per insertion. The relevant square-lattice parameter conditions are
\[
2\varepsilon\le\delta<1/2-\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon+\alpha\delta<1/2
\]
for half-space walks, and the stronger conditions \(4\varepsilon\le\delta<1/2-\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon+2\alpha\delta<1/2\) to obtain the SAW interval bound. Taking \(\delta=4\varepsilon\) and \(\alpha>29\) close to 29 gives \(\varepsilon<1/[2(1+8\cdot29)]=1/466\). On \(H\), all-\(u\) abundance removes the subsequence-stability strengthening; \(\delta=2\varepsilon\), \(\alpha>10\) close to 10 gives \(\varepsilon<1/[2(1+2\cdot10)]=1/42\).

### Conditional polynomial bound

Section 7 lifts walks to an eight-fold cover, uses the parafermionic observable to compare planar walks with inner lifted walks, constructs outer lifted walks of length \(O(n^2)\) and weight at least \(c n^{-103}\), then breaks resulting polygons into pieces avoiding the deleted neighborhood of the singularity. Under \(\mu(U_k^\infty)=\mu(H)\), good polygons have normalized growth at most \(\mu(H)\); joining with useful round polygons yields a polynomial bound for the two-point function and hence Theorem 1.4. The equality hypothesis is the essential unresolved interface.

## Limits and transfer checks

- Exact \(\varepsilon\) ranges and quantifiers are lattice-dependent. Do not report the square-lattice theorem as all \(n\), or the honeycomb result as a square-lattice result.
- The polygon abundance lower bound must match the same lattice, width, height, and length range as the insertion proof.
- Theorem 1.4 is conditional. It does not prove that the punctured universal cover has the honeycomb connective constant.
- The source discusses conjectural SLE scaling in its introduction/references but proves no SAW scaling limit. Count bounds alone do not establish tightness or identify an SLE law.
