## Model and theorem statements

Let \(\mathbb H\) be the honeycomb lattice in the paper’s fixed Euclidean embedding (edge length \(\sqrt3/3\)); \(\mathbb P_n\) is uniform over all length-\(n\) SAWs from the origin. The critical step weight is \(x_c=1/\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\), so the connective constant is \(\mu=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\).

- **Theorem 1, printed/PDF p. 2.** There exists a constant \(c>0\) such that for every integer \(n\ge1\),
  \[
  \mathbb P_n\!\left[\max_{0\le k\le n}\|\gamma_k\|\ge 10^{11}\,\frac{n}{\log(n+1)}\right]\le e^{-c n^{2/3}}.
  \]
  This is an upper-tail bound at a large explicit displacement threshold; it is not a sharp estimate of typical radius.
- **Theorem 2, printed/PDF p. 3.** With \(\varepsilon=10^{-10}\), the printed statement says “for every \(T\ge1\)” and gives \(B_T\le100T^{-\varepsilon}\). Definition 2.2 (printed/PDF p. 6) defines strips only at integer index \(k\ge0\), so this card records the theorem on its defined domain \(T\in\mathbb Z, T\ge1\); it does not assert a real-\(T\) extension. Definition 2.2 specifies the strip between \(y=0\) and \(y=\sqrt3 k/2\), and \(B_k=\sum_{\gamma\in B(\mathrm{Strip}_k)}x_c^{\ell(\gamma)}\), over bridges from 0 to the top mid-edge boundary, contained in that strip. No rounding/interpolation convention or real-parameter constant adjustment is supplied by this source card. The estimate is for the honeycomb lattice and its critical weight.
- **Theorem 3, printed/PDF p. 16.** The same polynomial bound is first proved for the triangular-walk partition function \(D_T\); Lemma 2.3 gives \(B_T\le D_T\) (indeed with the stronger factor \(\cos(\pi/8)\)). This is the recurrence target in Section 3; here too the strip/triangle width parameters are the integer indices defined in Section 2, and no real-parameter extension is asserted.

The intro’s expected scale \(n^{3/4+o(1)}\), attributed to Flory and derived by Lawler–Schramm–Werner under a conformal-invariance assumption, is prediction/conditional context, not a conclusion proved here.

## Proof spine and dependencies

Section 2.1 (printed/PDF pp. 4–5) defines the parafermionic observable for mid-edge SAWs; at the honeycomb critical weight and spin \(5/8\), the local relation sums over domains to identities between walk partition functions. Definitions 2.2–2.4 (pp. 6–7) introduce strip bridges \(B_T\), triangle walks \(D_T\), and upper-half-plane walks \(G_k\). Lemmas 2.2–2.3 give summability/tail control for \(G_k\), monotonicity, and \(B_T\le \cos(\pi/8)D_T\).

Section 3 proves the polynomial decay. The renewal-time estimate (Lemma 3.1, printed/PDF pp. 8–9) is combined with a geometric construction that splits into cases (a) and (b) (Sections 3.2, pp. 10–15). Its averaged recurrence is Corollary 3.1, Eq. (11), printed/PDF p. 15: it relates \(T^4D_{18T}^5\) to \((\sum_{i=0}^{3T}D_i)^4\sum_{k=T}^{21T}G_k\), with integer floor conventions in the paper. Lemma 3.4 (pp. 16–17) turns that recurrence and the monotone sequence bound into \(D_T\le100T^{-10^{-10}}\); Theorem 2 follows by comparison with \(B_T\).

For Theorem 1, Section 4 (printed/PDF pp. 18–20) decomposes a long SAW into bridges and connecting pieces. Lemma 4.1 bounds the critical weight of bridges reaching height at least order \(n/\log n\) by \(e^{-c n^{2/3}}\). Choosing block scale \(m\asymp n^{1/3}\), the bridge penalty has exponent \((5/3+o(1))n^{2/3}\), while the connecting-piece count is at most \(\exp((1+o(1))\log 4\,n^{2/3})\); the strict margin \(5/3-\log4>0\), checked numerically in the accompanying MATLAB record, absorbs the latter. Hammersley–Welsh unfolding and the lattice rotation/projection step then give Theorem 1.

## Transfer limits and evidence distinction

This is a rigorous quantitative improvement for uniform critical honeycomb SAW, relying on the lattice-specific parafermionic identity and boundary two-point summability. It does not establish the conjectured sharp displacement exponent, a scaling-limit theorem, or convergence to SLE. The theorem’s huge threshold and tiny exponent are part of the proven statement and should not be silently strengthened.
