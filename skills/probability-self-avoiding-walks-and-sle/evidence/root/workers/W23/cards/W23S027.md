## Model and main statements

The general Grigorchuk group \(\Gamma_\omega\) is encoded by \(\omega\in\{0,1,2\}^{\mathbb N_0}\), with recursively defined automorphisms. The paper studies its 4-generator Cayley graph and the three distinct 3-generator Cayley graphs obtained by deleting one of \(b,c,d\), as well as orbital Schreier graphs. For a rooted transitive graph \(G\), \(\mu(G)=\lim_n\sigma_n^{1/n}\), where \(\sigma_n\) counts rooted n-step SAWs.

- **Theorem 1.2 (physical/printed p. 2; literal source statement, with counterexample).** The PDF says every 3-generator Cayley graph of a general \(\Gamma_\omega\) has \(\mu\ge\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), with strictness if \(\omega\in Q\) (sequences containing all three symbols). This universal lower bound is contradicted by the source's allowed constant encoding \(\omega=000\ldots\): the recursions (2.3)–(2.4) give \(d=I\) and \(b=c=(a,b)\); \(a,b\) are distinct nonidentity involutions. Let \(t=ab\). The exact recursion gives sections of \(t^2\) as \(ba=t^{-1}\) and \(ab=t\), so \(t\) has order \(2^m\) on depth \(m\), hence infinite order. Thus \(\langle a,b\rangle\cong D_\infty\). After the paper's simple-edge coalescence, each nominal 3-generator Cayley graph is the doubly-infinite path, whose rooted SAW counts are \(c_n=2\) for \(n\ge1\) and whose connective constant is \(1<\varphi\). This exact infinite-group argument, not finite-level computation, shows the literal theorem scope cannot be used unchanged.
- **Theorem 3.1 (physical pp. 6–7; stated bounds and proof-scope caveat).** The degree-4 case prints \(\tau\le\mu(G_\omega)<\tau'\), with \(\tau\) the reciprocal positive root of \(2\xi^2(1+2\xi+2\xi^2)-1=0\), and \(\tau'\) the reciprocal root of \(B(1+D+D^2+D^3)-1=0\), \(B=2D\), \(D=\xi^2(1+2\xi+2\xi^2)\) (reported about 2.167 and 2.508). For \(G_\omega(\neg\delta)\), \(\delta_0=I\), it prints \(\gamma\le\mu<\gamma'\), where \(\gamma\) is the reciprocal root of \(2\xi^4(1+\xi^2)(1+\xi)^2-1=0\), and \(\gamma'\) uses \(B=2\xi^2(1+\xi^2)\), \(D=2\xi^3\) (about 1.635 and 1.895). For deletion of either remaining \(z\in\{\beta,\sigma\}\), it prints \(\varphi\le\mu<\gamma''\), where \(\gamma''\) uses \(B=\xi^2(1+\xi)^2\), \(D=\xi^2(1+\xi^2)\) (about 1.892), and states strictness of the lower bound if some recursively defined coordinate \(z_k(\omega)=I\), \(k\ge1\). The constant encoding above is a direct counterexample to the literal universal lower bound in the \(\delta=d\) deletion case. The refined generating-function arguments checked on pp. 16, 18–19 yield non-strict \(\le\) at their upper endpoints; the strict refined endpoints are retained only as the source-stated claim, not as independently established by those displayed arguments. A coarser separate strict bound does not by itself upgrade the refined endpoint.

Since both displayed powers are of \(A_1\), \(\pi\) cancels algebraically. ## Proof spine

Section 2 (physical pp. 3–6) develops the recursive automorphism definitions, relations among \(b,c,d\), Cayley and Schreier graph structure, and the kite decomposition after deleting a generator. Section 3 (pp. 6–7) states interval bounds using explicitly constructed SAW subsets and generating functions; positive roots give the numerical endpoints. The source's universal quantifiers include a degenerate constant encoding, so the stated lower bounds are not valid for every printed input. The upper-bound constructions decompose walks into units/kites and sum admissible repeated traversals, but the displayed refined generating-function estimates establish non-strict endpoints only.

Section 4 (pp. 8–13) derives asymptotic label frequencies in the orbital Schreier graphs. Theorem 4.1 (p. 11) uses recursion period \(2^{|w|}\) and computes label frequency \(\pi_\ell=\sum_{a\ge1}2^{-a}\mathbf1(\lambda_{\omega_a}=\ell)\); induction over suffixes proves the finite-word frequency formula. Section 5 (pp. 13–19) proves the three graph cases: lower bounds count walks avoiding repeated kite visits; upper bounds use generating-function constraints on successive deleted-label kites; for the strict lower-bound case, positive frequency of a missing-label unit is used. Equation (5.8) is preserved exactly as printed: \([A_1]^\pi[A_1]^{1-\pi}A_2-1=0\); no replacement of the second \(A_1\) is inferred.
