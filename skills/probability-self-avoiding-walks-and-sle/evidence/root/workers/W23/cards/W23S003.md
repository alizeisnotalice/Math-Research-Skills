## Model, observables, and exact results

Let ω be iid Bernoulli bond percolation on ℤ^d, with edge-open probability (p>p_c(d)). Let (C) be the unique infinite open cluster. For a vertex (x), (Z_N(x,ω)) is the number of (N)-step nearest-neighbor self-avoiding paths from (x) whose every edge is open. Write (s_N) for the number of unrestricted (N)-step SAWs from the origin. Then

\[
\mathbb E_p Z_N(x)=p^N s_N, \qquad \lim_{N\to\infty}(\mathbb E_p Z_N(x))^{1/N}=p\mu_d,
\]

where submultiplicativity gives μ_d= lim (s_N^{1/N}).

**Proposition 1.1 (PDF p.3; proof §2.1, pp.5–7).** For every (x\in C), the quantity
\[
\mu_d(p):=\limsup_{N\to\infty} Z_N(x,ω)^{1/N}
\]
is almost surely independent of (x) and nonrandom; it is at most (p\mu_d). The ratio μ_d(p)/(pμ_d) is nondecreasing in (p\in(p_c,1]).

**Theorem 1.4 (PDF p.4; proof §§3–4, pp.8–19).** In (d=2), for every (p\in(1/2,1)),
\[
\mu_2(p)<p\mu_2.
\]
The ratio μ_2(p)/(pμ_2) is strictly increasing on ((1/2,1]). The theorem concerns the limsup-defined rate. Remark 1.2 says the full limit (\lim_N Z_N(x)^{1/N}) is believed to exist but is only conjectured there. The exponential decay of (Z_N/\mathbb E_p Z_N) follows from the strict gap of limsup growth rates; the paper does not prove that the full nth-root limit exists.

## Proof dependencies and quantitative scale

1. **Root independence and deterministic limsup.** Lemma 2.1 handles any bounded-degree infinite connected graph. For adjacent roots, split a path according to the first visit to the neighboring vertex; convolution bounds compare the two sequences and preserve their limsup nth roots. The rate is unchanged under finite environment modifications, hence is tail measurable; iid zero-one law makes it deterministic. This establishes independence among vertices of the infinite cluster, but only for the limsup rate.
2. **Monotonicity.** Couple (p<p') by common iid uniforms. Conditional on the denser environment, the thinner edge indicators have open probability (p/p'), giving the conditional expectation identity used with Markov and Borel–Cantelli to compare the limsup rates.
3. **Fractional-moment reduction.** With θ=1/2, it suffices to prove a strict exponential fractional-moment bound for (Z_N). Divide ℤ² into edge blocks (I_x) of side (N_0), associate to each path its connected block animal (A), and use ((\sum_i a_i)^\theta\le\sum_i a_i^\theta). The number of size-(m) rooted animals is at most (49^m). This reduces the problem to a per-animal bound; summing these estimates and applying Markov plus Borel–Cantelli yields a strict limsup gap.
4. **Small animals (§3.5).** For a small animal, tilt the open-edge probability inside its blocks to (p'=\lambda p/[1-p(1-\lambda)]), with ((1-\lambda)\sqrt{1-p}\,N_0=1). The density factor has the form shown in (3.32); because there are (2mN_0^2) edges in (m) blocks, its inverse-moment cost is controlled while the tilted path weight falls. The cutoff is (m\le N/[N_0(\log N_0)^{1/4}]).
5. **Large animals (§3.6).** Choose separated blocks and put a quadratic tilt on their enlarged edge neighborhoods. The centered quadratic form (Q_x) uses kernel (1/d(e,e')), where (d(e,e')) is Euclidean distance between edge midpoints. Under the original product environment its mean is zero and variance is bounded uniformly in (p,N_0). Under a path-open conditioning, the path contributes a harmonic sum at least ∼\log N_0, so the tilted mean is at least ((1-p)\sqrt{\log N_0}). With (N_0=\exp(C_2/(1-p)^2)), this is order \(\sqrt{C_2}\); Chebyshev makes the bad-event probability small by choosing the absolute constant (C_2) sufficiently large. This part uses the two-dimensional (1/r)-kernel geometry and a separated-block selection. It is not a dimension-free estimate.
6. **Strict constant.** The result obtained is
\[
\mu_2(p)\le 4^{-1/N_0^2}\,p\mu_2<p\mu_2,
\qquad N_0=\exp\!\left(\frac{C_2}{(1-p)^2}\right),
\]
for a sufficiently large absolute (C_2). Thus one explicit lower bound on the relative gap is (1-4^{-1/N_0^2}); the paper notes this bound is likely very far from optimal as (p\uparrow1). Do not present a numerical evaluation of this tiny bound as evidence for the theorem.

## Scope and migration gaps

- This is a **quenched edge-dilution** theorem on the planar infinite cluster. The annealed rate is (p\mu_2), computed from the unconditioned expectation; quenched and annealed path ensembles are not interchangeable.
- For C108, this source proves root-independent, deterministic **limsup** growth and strict quenched/annealed separation. It does not prove existence of the full (N)-th-root limit asked in C108. It is a direct partial result and a proof template, not a solution of C108.
- The separate volume-exponent proposition in §3.2 assumes (d\nu_d<2) and yields convergence to zero in probability only; it is not the strict exponential theorem. The iid random-site-potential model in §5 has different hypotheses and observable, so do not mix it with the Bernoulli bond result.
- The proof uses two-dimensional coarse graining and detailed model-specific change of measure. Its statement must not be generalized to (d\ge3) from this paper.
