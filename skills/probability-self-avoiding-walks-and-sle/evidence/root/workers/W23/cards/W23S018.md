## Model and notation

The target is nearest-neighbor SAW on `Z^d`, `d≥1`, with connective constant `μ=lim_n c_n^{1/n}`. The source defines memory-τ walks by requiring `ω(i)≠ω(j)` whenever `0<|i-j|≤τ`: τ=0 is ordinary random walk, τ=2 forbids immediate reversals, and τ=4 additionally forbids elementary squares. For an allowed walk class, `C_τ(x,y;β)` is its β-weighted two-point/Green function, `C_A` imposes avoidance of taboo set A, and `\widetilde C_A^z` additionally forbids the next-to-last site of an attached loop from being z. The critical simple-walk parameter is `β_0=(2d)^{-1}`; for τ=2, `β_2=(2d-1)^{-1}`.

## Precisely stated inequality and proof mechanism

There is no numbered main theorem for the central bound; it is derived in “Inequalities first version,” “Inequalities second version,” and the preceding loop-erasure identities (physical pp. 6–11).

**First version (Eq. (2.29), physical p. 9).** For τ=0 or τ=2, and integer `k≥0`, define `Λ_τ(k;β)` as the supremum of the taboo-loop Green function over all k-vertex sets A arising as the range of a k-step SAW starting at a neighbor of the origin (the precise source definition includes the relevant τ-memory walk class). Whenever the corresponding critical Green-function quantities are finite, loop-erasure/restoration gives
\[
\mu\;\ge\;{\mu_\tau\over \Lambda_\tau(k;\mu_\tau^{-1})},
\qquad \mu_0=2d,\quad \mu_2=2d-1.
\]
This is a bound, not a formula for μ. For `k=0, τ=0`, `Λ_0(0;β_c)=C_0(0,0;β_c)` is the **diagonal return/Green function**, not the susceptibility. At `β_c=(2d)^{-1}` it is finite for `d≥3` and diverges for `d≤2`, so the k=0 instance is unusable in d≤2. The full susceptibility `χ_0(β_c)=\sum_x C_0(0,x;β_c)` diverges at criticality in every dimension. The `k=1, τ=0` identity `C_{0,\{e\}}(0,0;β_c)=2-1/C_0(0,0;β_c)` yields a finite taboo return Green function even in d=2 by the source’s critical limiting/potential-kernel argument; this does not make the un-taboo diagonal Green function finite.

**Second version (Eq. (2.39), physical pp. 10–11).** The additional condition that an attached memory-2 loop’s next-to-last site avoids the next backbone vertex yields the improved denominator `\widetilde\Lambda_2(k;β)`. For `τ=2`, `k≥0`, and `d>0`, the source states `μ≥μ_2/\widetilde\Lambda_2(k;μ_2^{-1})` wherever the displayed Green-function quantities are finite. The key evaluation identity (Eq. (2.40), physical p. 11; proved as Eqs. (3.16)–(3.18), physical p. 19) is
\[
\widetilde C_2^{A,e}(0,0;β)=\frac{C_2^A(0,0;β)-β C_2^A(0,e;β)}{1-β^2}.
\]
It depends on the memory-2 walk class and the exact pair of diagonal/off-diagonal taboo Green functions; it is not the ordinary-walk deletion identity `C_A-C_{A\cup\{e\}}`. In §3.3 the authors first subtract loops whose penultimate site is e from `C_2^A(0,0;β)` (3.16), then use repeated inclusion–exclusion to express that excluded-loop sum as `β[C_2^A(0,e;β)-βC_2^A(0,0;β)]/(1-β²)` (3.17), giving (3.18). For `|A|≥1` this identity is usable at the critical point in all dimensions; the d=2, `A=∅` case has infinities in the direct recursion and requires a limiting argument before substitution. The source notes an overcount in the first k backbone sites when simplifying the bound; retain that qualification.

**Optimized bounds (physical pp. 11–15).** Instead of maximizing over a single worst taboo geometry, split incoming backbones into straight and bent types. Let a and b be the corresponding maximal taboo Green functions. Count n-step memory walks with s straight internal vertices using `c_n(s)≤\binom ns d^{n-s}` (with the first-step factor accounted separately), then optimize a split point using Stirling’s formula. This yields the source’s max/unique-crossing lower bound (physical pp. 14–15); it is still only as good as the verified comparison `b≤a` and the finite Green-function inputs. The authors state that the inequality `b≤a` is checked numerically for the applications, so any use relying on that comparison must preserve that evidence class.

### Loop-erasure/restoration derivation

1. A memory walk is mapped injectively to a self-avoiding backbone plus the ordered loops erased at successive last visits. Reversing the procedure restores the walk if each attached loop avoids previous backbone sites (and, for the second version, satisfies the next-to-last-site restriction).
2. Sum over backbones and upper-bound each loop weight by the maximum over admissible taboo sets; relaxing a larger forbidden set to k sites only increases its Green function.
3. The resulting SAW generating function is bounded by a convolution/geometric series in the taboo-loop weight. When that weight is finite and below the walk-class growth threshold, convergence of the generating function at the corresponding β implies a lower bound for μ by the reciprocal critical radius.
4. The memory-τ=2 case is not a copy-paste of τ=0: cutting a loop at its first hit creates dependent pieces. The authors solve a finite linear system in symmetry classes (three equations/unknowns in the displayed one-step example, physical pp. 18–19) and then use inclusion–exclusion. Keep the model’s memory convention and that dependence explicit.

## Green-function computation, expansions, and limitations

- Physical pp. 16–19 reduce finite taboo-set Green functions to the massless free-field lattice propagator and finite inclusion–exclusion recursions. For τ=0, adjoining b to A uses
  `C_{0,A∪{b}}(y,x)=C_{0,A}(y,x)-C_{0,A}(y,b)C_{0,A}(b,x)/C_{0,A}(b,b)`.
  Here the diagonal `C_0(0,0;β_c)` is the simple-random-walk expected number of returns to 0. It is finite in d≥3, infinite in d≤2. This is distinct from the endpoint-summed susceptibility `χ_0(β_c)=\sum_x C_0(0,x;β_c)`, which is infinite for every d at the critical parameter. For τ=2, memory dependence requires the finite symmetry-reduced linear system described above; the second-version identity is separately (2.40)/(3.18), not a direct taboo-set deletion formula.
- Appendix A, physical pp. 27–34, transforms the critical Green function into a one-dimensional Bessel integral and gives rigorous quadrature, truncation-tail, Taylor/asymptotic, and rounding error controls for the reported d≥3 calculations. Numerical tabulations are presented for d=2–6. **The authors explicitly say they did not perform rigorous error analysis for their d=2 values** (physical p. 21); those planar numerical estimates are not certified bounds.
- High-d expansions are expansions of their lower-bound methods, not exact values of μ. In the notation `s=(2d)^{-1}`, the best displayed combined τ=2 taboo lower bound (Eq. (6.24), physical p. 26) is `s^{-1}-1-s-3s^2-18s^3-124s^4+O(s^5)`; it agrees only through the coefficients the source explicitly states. Do not upgrade an asymptotic lower-bound expansion to a rigorous finite-d numerical bound without its error control.
- The source uses some numerical comparisons to select optimized straight/bent taboo sets and to discuss d=2 quality. Those comparisons are not pure theorem hypotheses; their computational status and the missing planar rigorous error estimate must remain visible.
