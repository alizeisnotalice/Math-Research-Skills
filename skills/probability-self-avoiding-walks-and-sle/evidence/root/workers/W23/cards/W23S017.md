## Model and exact quantities

On nearest-neighbor `Z^d`, let `S_N` be the set of N-step SAWs from 0, let `ω_e` be iid Bernoulli(p) bond states, and let `Z_N(ω)` count those `S∈S_N` whose every edge is open. Work in the supercritical regime `p>p_c(d)`; the unique infinite cluster is denoted `C`. For `x∈C`, `Z_{N,x}` is the corresponding count rooted at x. The deterministic connective constant of the undiluted lattice is `μ_d=lim_N |S_N|^(1/N)`.

**Proposition 1.1 (printed p. 3; physical pp. 2–3; quoted from [19]).** For each fixed `p>p_c` and every `x∈C`, `limsup_N Z_{N,x}^{1/N}` is almost surely constant and independent of x. Denote it by `μ_d(p)`. It satisfies `μ_d(p)≤p μ_d`, and `μ_d(p)/(p μ_d)` is nondecreasing in p. The source does not prove that the limsup is a full limit; this proposition is cited, not reproved here.

**Theorem 1.3 (printed p. 4; physical p. 4).** For every `ε>0`, there is a dimension threshold `d_0(ε)` such that for `d≥d_0(ε)`,
\[
p\le {1\over 2d}+{1\over(2d)^2}+{2+3\log 2-\varepsilon\over(2d)^3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu_d(p)<p\mu_d.
\]
The interval relevant to the conclusion is supercritical; using the source’s expansion `p_c(d)=1/(2d)+1/(2d)^2+7/[2(2d)^3]+O(d^{-4})`, the theorem implies `p_c^{(2)}>p_c` for sufficiently large d. The paper explicitly leaves `d_0(ε)` nonquantitative. Its phase-transition statement is only established in sufficiently high dimensions.

## Proof chain and dependencies

1. Set `W_N=Z_N/(p^N |S_N|)`. The strict inequality `μ_d(p)<p μ_d` is equivalent, using the already-established deterministic limsup rate and `|S_N|^(1/N)→μ_d`, to exponential a.s. decay of `W_N`.
2. **Lemma 2.1 (printed/physical p. 5).** Write `d\widetilde P_N/dP=W_N` (with `E_P W_N=1`). The source hypothesis is the size-biased **probability event**
   `\widetilde P_N(W_N≤e^{cN})≤e^{-cN}`,
   not an expectation bound. Indeed, by change of measure,
   `P(W_N∈[e^{-cN/2},e^{cN}])=\widetilde E_N[W_N^{-1}; W_N∈[e^{-cN/2},e^{cN}]]≤e^{cN/2}\widetilde P_N(W_N≤e^{cN})≤e^{-cN/2}`;
   separately, Markov gives `P(W_N≥e^{cN})≤e^{-cN}` because `E_P W_N=1`. Borel–Cantelli therefore yields almost-sure exponential decay of `W_N`, hence `μ_d(p)<pμ_d`. No dimension-uniform numerical c is supplied.
3. **Lemma 2.2 (physical p. 5; proof referred to [2], Lemma 1).** Under `P_N^*`, the environment distribution of `Z_N` equals that obtained by choosing a uniform N-step SAW spine S, forcing its edges open, and leaving all other edges iid Bernoulli(p). This is the finite-size bias/spine identity.
4. **Proposition 2.3 (printed/physical p. 6; proof §§3–5, pp. 6–20).** Given ε>0, for d sufficiently large and `p≥(2d)^{-1}`,
   ` (π_N⊗P)(\widetilde Z_N(ω,S)<2^{(3-ε)N/(2d)^2})≤e^{-c(ε,d)N}`.
   Thus, with high probability under the spine construction, the environment has at least the stated number of open N-step SAWs. This is a lower-tail bound on the path count; it does not say large counts are rare. For the Theorem 1.3 proof, (2.10) and `|S_N|^{1/N}→μ_d` ensure the exponential benchmark `e^{cN}p^N|S_N|` lies below the Proposition 2.3 threshold for a suitable c>0. Hence the event `\widetilde Z_N≤e^{cN}p^N|S_N|` in the size-biased criterion is contained in the exponentially rare low-count event. Together with Lemmas 2.1–2.2 and the cited high-d expansion for `μ_d`, this yields the strict gap. No computable dimension threshold is given.
5. The counting construction in §3 uses three short bridge types over the spine. For a nonoverlapping selected family, (3.3) counts distinct alternate paths by matching the numbers of types (b) and (c), because only these alter path length; type (a) gives a factor `2^{n_a}`. Good-spine controls ensure many candidate sites. Conditional edge openness gives independent Bernoulli bridge events only after the construction has restricted candidate edge sets to be disjoint; local conflicts are removed by selecting nonoverlapping squares, not by treating all candidate bridges as independent.
6. **Proof of Proposition 3.2, §4, physical pp. 10–13.** The path-count lower estimate proceeds through well-separated bridge sites; bridge overlaps are controlled by bad-site counts. Bounded-difference concentration for the underlying iid walk increments/edge variables and combinatorial selection of separated sites give exponentially small exceptional probability.
7. **Proof of Proposition 3.1, §5, physical pp. 14–20.** A uniform N-step lattice SAW has exponentially few bad spine patterns with high probability. The proof compares local turn/return statistics to finite-memory/nonbacktracking walks, uses stopping-time decompositions and exponential estimates, then applies the result to the density of eligible sites. Details are dimension-dependent; constants are not made explicit.

The paper’s heuristic identification of exponential disorder relevance with trajectory localization is expressly heuristic. The theorem concerns counts and does not establish end-to-end-distance or overlap behavior.
