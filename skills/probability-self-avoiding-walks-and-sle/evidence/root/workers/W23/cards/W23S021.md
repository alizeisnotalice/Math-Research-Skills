## Model definitions

The paper's class \(\mathcal G\) consists of infinite, locally finite, connected, transitive, simple graphs. Its edge-isoperimetric constant is \(\phi(G)=\inf_{0<|W|<\infty}|\partial_eW|/(\Delta|W|)\); amenability means \(\phi=0\). A graph height function \((h,H)\) has \(h:V\to\mathbb Z\), \(h(1)=0\), is invariant in differences under a quasi-transitive \(H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and has both a lower- and a higher-height neighbor at every vertex. On a Cayley graph, “strong” additionally requires a finite-index subgroup acting by left multiplication. “Harmonic” means \(h(v)=\deg(v)^{-1}\sum_{u\sim v}h(u)\).

## Results and assumptions

- **Theorem 4.1, physical/PDF p. 7; strengthened as Theorem 9.1, pp. 13–16.** If \(\Gamma\) is infinite, finitely generated, and elementary amenable (the paper's EFG class), every locally finite Cayley graph of \(\Gamma\) has a harmonic strong graph height function. Theorem 9.1 gives a finite-index normal subgroup \(H\triangleleft\Gamma\), \(1<[\Gamma:H]<\infty\), and a height function of form \((h,H)\). The proof is transfinite induction through elementary amenable groups, treating extensions (infinite quotient pulls a height down; finite quotient lifts a nonzero homomorphism on a finite-index subgroup) and directed unions (finite generation places the group in a stage).
- **Theorem 5.1, physical/PDF p. 8.** The Cayley graph of the first Grigorchuk group with generator set \(\{a,b,c\}\) has no graph height function. More strongly, for finite-index \(H\le\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), every H-difference-invariant function is constant on each H-orbit. The proof combines the graph's restricted automorphism group with the fact that the group is torsion.
- **Theorem 6.2, physical/PDF pp. 9–12.** For \(G\in\mathcal G\) with degree \(\Delta\ge3\), let \(P\) be simple-random-walk transition matrix and define \(\lambda\) as the bottom of \(I-P\), i.e. the largest value satisfying \(\langle f,(I-P)f\rangle\ge\lambda\langle f,f\rangle\) for all \(f\in\ell^2(V)\). Then, with \(c=\Delta(\Delta-1)/(\Delta-2)^2\),
  \[
  \mu(G)\ge(\Delta-1)^{(1+c\lambda)/2}.
  \]
  This strictly improves \(\sqrt{\Delta-1}\) iff \(\lambda>0\), equivalently iff \(G\) is nonamenable. Theorem 6.2's sentence calls \(\lambda\) the “spectral bottom of P,” but equation (6.1) defines it for \(I-P\), and the surrounding text says \(\lambda=1-\rho(P)\); this source wording mismatch is preserved here rather than silently redefined.
- **Theorem 7.1, physical/PDF p. 12.** For nonamenable \(G\in\mathcal G\) of degree \(\Delta\ge3\), girth \(g\le\infty\), and the same \(\lambda>0\), an absolute \(C>0\) satisfies
  \[
  \left[\frac1{\Delta-1}+\frac{C\log(1+\lambda^{-2})}{g\Delta}\right]^{-1}\le\mu(G)\le\Delta-1.
  \]
  Equality in the upper bound holds exactly for a tree (\(g=\infty\)). The proof invokes the Benjamini–Nachmias–Peres percolation threshold comparison and \(\mu(G)\ge1/p_c(G)\). The authors explicitly warn that this bound alone gives no continuity at the regular tree because \(\lambda(G)\) is not continuous in the usual graph metric.
- **Theorem 8.1, physical/PDF p. 12; Theorem 8.2, p. 13.** The Cayley graphs of the Higman group and the stated Higman-type group \(\Gamma'\) have no graph height function. Their proofs use absence of group height functions together with stabilizer/automorphism and finite-index-normal-subgroup criteria; these are group-specific examples, not a general classification.

## Proof route for the spectral bound

Start with an extendable \(2n\)-step SAW \(\pi=(v_0,\ldots,v_{2n})\), and augment it with a mid-edge at \(v_0\). The off-path oriented edge set has \(2n(\Delta-2)\) elements. Color an edge red if taking it after the corresponding prefix makes a non-extendable SAW, and blue otherwise. Lemma 6.4 proves
\[
B_\pi\ge \frac{n(1+c\lambda)}{\Delta-2}-\frac{\Delta-1}{2}.
\]
The proof bounds the finite-component/red contributions by an injection into blue edges and controls infinite-component edges with the spectral escape estimate; it then applies the extendable-SAW counting lemma of Grimmett–Holroyd–Peres to obtain at least \(C(\Delta-1)^{n(1+c\lambda)}\) extendable \(2n\)-step walks. Taking exponential growth rates gives Theorem 6.2. The height-function existence/nonexistence proofs are separate from this spectral argument.

## Transfer boundary

The results concern deterministic transitive graph/Cayley graph classes, elementary amenability, graph height functions, and connective constants. They do not give honeycomb SAW curve tightness, convergence, a DMP, or SLE identification. The spectral estimates require transitivity, local finiteness, simplicity, degree constraints, and (for Theorem 7.1) nonamenability; they are not Euclidean-lattice scaling results. Do not transfer the theorem's graph hypotheses to an arbitrary random environment.
