## Exact model and theorem

The model is the uniform law \(P_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\) on rooted, strict, nearest-neighbor n-step self-avoiding walks \(\gamma=(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_n)\) on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), with \(\gamma_0=0\). The norm in the principal theorem is Euclidean. The statement is for the **maximum displacement of the whole path**, not just its endpoint.

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p.1, literal source wording).** For every \(v>0\) there exists \(\epsilon(v,d)>0\) such that, for every \(n\in\mathbb N\),
\[
P_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\!\left(\max_{0\le k\le n}\|\gamma_k\|\ge vn\right)\le e^{-\epsilon n}.
\]
The source prints this prefactor-free inequality for every \(n\), but its literal finite-size range cannot hold: for \(n=1\) and \(v=1/2\), every one-step SAW has maximum displacement 1, so the probability is exactly 1 while \(e^{-\epsilon}<1\) for every \(\epsilon>0\). Preserve the source wording when quoting it, but for applications use the asymptotic form: for each fixed \(v,d\), there are \(\epsilon(v,d)>0\) and \(n_0(v,d)\) such that the bound holds for \(n\ge n_0\). Enlarging a finite constant gives the all-size form \(C(v,d)e^{-\epsilon n}\). This finite-size qualification does not change the sub-ballistic conclusion. The paper’s Corollary 1.2 gives \(n^{-2}\mathbb E_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\|\gamma_n\|^2\to0\). The proof is by contradiction through bridge theory, not by a direct concentration inequality for the non-Markovian walk.

## Proof dependencies and counting mechanism

### Bridge law and renewal decomposition

The bridge is oriented in the second coordinate \(y\): its initial vertex is the unique minimum-height vertex, and its endpoint is a (not necessarily unique) maximum. Proposition 2.1 (PDF pp.6–7) gives
\[
e^{-C\sqrt n}|\mathrm{SAW}_n|\le |\mathrm{SAB}_n|\le|\mathrm{SAW}_n|
\]
and a corresponding generating-function comparison. This is the Hammersley–Welsh unfolding cost; \(C\) is an unspecified constant. Renewal points split a bridge into two bridges; irreducible bridges are exactly those with no interior renewal point, and the decomposition is unique. Kesten’s Lemma 2.2 establishes
\[
\sum_{\gamma\in\mathrm{iSAB}}\mu_c^{-|\gamma|}=1,
\]
so \(P_{\mathrm{iSAB}}(\gamma)=\mu_c^{-|\gamma|}\) is a probability law. Here \(\mu_c\) is the connective constant of the rooted lattice SAW.

The **ballistic assumption** on bridges is that for some \(v>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log P_{\mathrm{SAB}_n}(y(\gamma_n)\ge vn)=0.
\]
It means the ballistic endpoint event is not exponentially unlikely along a subsequence; it does not say its probability tends to one. Under this assumption, Theorem 2.3 (PDF p.8) proves that for some \(\delta>0\) the event \(|R_\gamma|\ge\delta n\) also has only subexponential cost in the limsup sense. Corollary 2.4 then forces \(E_{\mathrm{iSAB}}|\gamma|<\infty\).

### Why the finite mean is impossible

Theorem 2.5 (PDF pp.8–10, proof §4 pp.18–25) proves instead \(E_{\mathrm{iSAB}}|\gamma|=\infty\). Its mechanism strengthens renewal points to **diamond points**, where the past and future lie in opposite quadrants bounded by the two diagonals \(x+y=\mathrm{constant}\), \(y-x=\mathrm{constant}\). Assuming finite mean, the authors concatenate iid irreducible bridges to form a semi-infinite and then a bi-infinite renewal path. A renewal shift is stationary and ergodic; past and future have the reflected independent laws. A law of large numbers and a finite initial string of unit bridges yield positive probability that the origin is a diamond point, hence a positive density of diamond points almost surely.

The stickbreak surgery at two well-separated diamond points rotates the intervening segment by a right angle and inserts two eastward edges. Diamond geometry makes the output self-avoiding. For bridges with \(n\) renewal blocks, typical width at most \(\epsilon n\), and at least \(\delta n/2\) diamond points, there are order \(n^2\) choices of cut points in disjoint index ranges. The surgery adds exactly two edges, giving total bridge weight
\[
S=\left(\frac{\delta n}{10\mu_c}\right)^2P_{\mathrm{iSAB}}^{\otimes\mathbb N}(\mathrm{SAB}_n).
\]
Conversely, each output has at most \((2\nu n)^2\) reconstructions, and the altered bridge has width exceeding \(\epsilon n\). The weighted counting bound (4.10) forces a positive lower bound for the probability of width \(>\epsilon n\), contradicting the law-of-large-numbers width control under the assumed finite mean. This proves Theorem 2.5; together with Corollary 2.4, it rules out the ballistic assumption.

### From the contradiction to Theorem 1.1

Theorem 2.3 is proved through a subsequence argument. For bridges reaching height \(vn\), at least \(vn\) height bands are crossed. Proposition 3.2 (PDF p.11 and proof pp.12–18) successively reduces the number of visits to height bands, at subexponential cost along a suitable subsequence. Its proof divides paths into: already many renewal points; few zigzags, which can be unfolded at subexponential cost; and many zigzags but few renewals, where a multivalued unfolding selects many short zigzags and produces new renewals. The inverse multiplicity is bounded by recording the zig/zag points in the output. The resulting statement is explicitly **subsequential**; do not upgrade it to all n. On physical p.18, the proof of Corollary 2.4 starts by assuming \(E_{\mathrm{iSAB}}|\gamma|=\infty\), truncates each iid block length at \(k\), and applies exponential Markov to show that renewal-rich bridges have probability at most \(e^{-\epsilon n+C\sqrt n}\). This contradicts Theorem 2.3's subexponential lower cost under the ballistic assumption. Therefore the ballistic assumption forces finite mean; it is not finite mean that produces this exponential renewal-point upper tail. Theorem 2.5 proves the opposite, infinite-mean conclusion, ruling out ballisticity.

If Theorem 1.1 failed, axial symmetry and classical Hammersley–Welsh unfolding give the bridge comparison
\[
P_{\mathrm{SAB}_n}(y(\gamma_n)\ge d^{-1/2}vn)\ge \frac{1}{2d}e^{-C\sqrt n}P_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\!\left(\max_{k\le n}\|\gamma_k\|\ge vn\right).
\]
This would produce a ballistic bridge subsequence. Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 would force finite mean, contradicting Theorem 2.5's infinite-mean theorem. Thus the sub-ballistic exponential-tail conclusion follows for all sufficiently large \(n\); the source's stronger prefactor-free all-\(n\) wording is kept above with its finite-size caveat. Corollary 1.3 (PDF pp.3–4) further states that at critical fugacity the finite-domain SAW between smooth boundary points has probability tending to zero of having length at most \(K/\delta\); its proof is a sketch reducing to subcritical Ornstein–Zernike estimates.

## Scope and migration gaps

- This is a rigorous geometric result for uniform rooted lattice SAW on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\). It proves an asymptotic exponential tail for linear displacement (with the source's finite-size all-n caveat above) and the endpoint consequence \(\mathbb E|\gamma_n|^2=o(n^2)\). It does **not** give a power-law exponent \(\nu\), a nontrivial lower displacement bound, tightness of rescaled curves, or any SLE limit.
- The paper discusses a different finite-domain two-endpoint ensemble and other graphs; statements there are separately conditioned or conjectural. Do not treat them as consequences of Theorem 1.1 without the cited additional arguments.
- For C107, the result is useful control of macroscopic movement for lattice SAW. It leaves the curve topology, tightness, discrete domain-Markov/conditioning mechanism, and identification of subsequential limits as SLE(8/3) unproved.
- The proof is dimension-uniform in its statement but uses lattice coordinate bridges and their renewal geometry. A transitive graph, percolation cluster, or random environment needs a fresh bridge construction and proof; none is supplied here for C108.
