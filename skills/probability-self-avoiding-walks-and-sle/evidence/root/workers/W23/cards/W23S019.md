## Models and theorem statements

The paper treats nearest-neighbor SAW, Bernoulli bond percolation, lattice trees and bond lattice animals on `Z^d`, via model-specific lace-expansion functions. The critical two-point function is denoted `G_{p_c}(x)`; for percolation it is the connection probability. The dimension threshold is model-dependent.

**Theorem 1.1 (printed p. 3; physical p. 3).** For nearest-neighbor SAW in `d≥5`, and for percolation/LTLA in sufficiently high dimension,
\[
G_{p_c}(x)=a_d A |x|^{-(d-2)}+O(|x|^{-(d-2+2/d)}),\qquad
a_d={d\,\Gamma(d/2-1)\over 2\pi^{d/2}},
\]
where A is model-dependent and given by (1.44). The paper says its complete SAW proof covers d≥5; its detailed percolation proof is only for large d (say d≥30). It states d≥19 suffices for the theorem, relying on more detailed diagram estimates not reproduced here; do not say this paper itself supplies the complete d=19 proof. For LTLA the paper asserts sufficiently high dimensions without a numeric threshold in this theorem statement.

**Theorem 1.4 (printed p. 8; physical pp. 8–9).** For d≥3 and `Z^d`-symmetric (not necessarily nonnegative) J, assume
\[
\sum_x J(x)=1,\quad \widehat J(0)-\widehat J(k)\ge K_0 {|k|^2\over 2d},\quad
\sum_x |x|^2J(x)=K_1>0,\quad
\sum_x |x|^2|J(x)|\le K_2,
\]
and pointwise `|J(x)|≤K_3 |||x|||^{-(d+2)}`. Then the Green function `C(x)` with Fourier kernel `(1-\widehat J(k))^{-1}` obeys
`C(x)~(a_d/K_1)|x|^{-(d-2)}`. If additionally `\sum |x|^{2+ρ}|J(x)|<K'_2` and `|J(x)|≤K'_3 |||x|||^{-(d+2+ρ)}` for finite `ρ>0`, then the stated error is `O(|||x|||^{-(d-2+(ρ∧2)/d)})`. The author explicitly says this error is nonoptimal; an improved exponent `(ρ∧2)` is only attributed to unpublished work [6]/Lawler, not proved here.

**Corollary 1.6 (printed pp. 9–10; physical pp. 9–10).** If the additional symmetric g satisfies `\sum |g(x)|<∞` and `|g(x)|≤K_4 |||x|||^{-d}`, then `H=C*g` has leading coefficient
\[
a_d\,{\sum_y g(y)\over \sum_y |y|^2J(y)}.
\]
With matching `ρ`-weighted tails on J and g, the error transfers with exponent `(ρ∧2)/d`. For the model theorem, A is exactly (1.44), using the model-specific g,J from (1.20)–(1.22), not a universal constant.

## Proof route and dependencies

1. **Lace representation (Proposition 1.3, physical pp. 6–8).** For p≤pc, `\widehat G_p=\widehat g_p/(1-\widehat J_p)`, with J,g defined separately for SAW, percolation and LTLA through `\widehat\Pi_p`. The convergent expansion supplies diagrammatic bounds, infrared bounds, second-moment control, and critical-point normalization. Applicability depends on the model’s existing lace-expansion convergence theorem.
2. **Critical extension (Appendix A, physical pp. 57–61).** Left-continuity in p of finite- and infinite-volume quantities and dominated convergence extend bounds/representation from p<pc to p=pc. For percolation, continuity of lace coefficients is handled through finite-volume increasing/decreasing event approximants, not monotonicity asserted for each raw coefficient. This is a proof dependency for applying the Gaussian lemma at criticality.
3. **Gaussian lemma proof (Section 2, physical pp. 14–26).** Fourier inversion is represented using a heat-kernel/integral decomposition. Split the integral into large t (small-frequency behavior; quadratic dispersion and K1 determine the leading Gaussian coefficient) and bounded/small t (tail/derivative controls produce the stated error). The source separately gives an example showing the pointwise `|J(x)|=O(|x|^{-(d+2)})` threshold cannot generally be relaxed for d>4. The improved `(ρ∧2)` error in Remark 1.5(iv) is expressly outside the proof in this article.
4. **Diagram estimates (Section 3, physical pp. 27–38).** Control the weighted diagrams and pointwise lace coefficient `Π(x)` from the assumed/model-specific critical two-point decay. SAW, percolation, and LTLA require separate diagrams and dimensional restrictions. Lemmas 1.7–1.8 are bootstrap inputs; percolation at d≥19 needs sharper estimates than the displayed detailed argument.
5. **Weighted Fourier estimates (Section 4, physical pp. 39–56).** Lemma 1.9 transfers weighted spatial summability of diagrams to fractional derivatives/Fourier regularity, with distinct even-integer and noninteger exponent cases. Combining these bounds iteratively gives the weighted estimates needed for J,g, then Corollary 1.6 yields Theorem 1.1.
6. **Convolution appendix (Appendix B, physical pp. 61–65).** Lemma B.1 supplies decay and asymptotic convolution rules. Its proof splits the convolution sum into near-x, near-origin and middle regions; summability of the second factor fixes the leading coefficient. This supports the transfer from C to H and is part of the full proof.

The local proof outline keeps cited external convergence results separate from the estimates proved here. The result does not claim universal dimension thresholds or a model-independent amplitude.
