## Model and results

The Fisher transformation replaces each degree-three vertex by a triangle. Write (F(G)) for the graph obtained by transforming every vertex of a cubic graph, and (mu(G)) for its SAW connective constant. The paper uses connected, infinite, quasi-transitive graphs so the connective constant is well-defined. The SAWs used in the proof may start and end at edge midpoints; their length is the number of vertices visited.

- **Theorem 3.1, physical/PDF pp. 4–5.** For an infinite connected quasi-transitive cubic graph (G_0), set (G_{k+1}=F(G_k)), with connective constants (mu_k). Then
  \[
  \mu_k^{-1}=g(\mu_{k+1}^{-1}),\qquad g(x)=x^2+x^3.
  \]
  The sequence \(\mu_k\) converges monotonically to \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), and for every integer \(k\ge1\),
  \[
  -\left(\frac47\right)^k\le \mu_k^{-1}-\phi^{-1}
  \le \left[\frac{7-\sqrt5}{2}\right]^k.
  \]
  The direction of monotonicity depends on \(\mu_0\): the decreasing limit holds iff \(\mu_0\ge\phi\). **Source-print qualification:** the displayed upper base is \((7-\sqrt5)/2\approx2.381966>1\), so this literal one-sided upper bound does not decay and is not itself a geometric convergence rate. Keep it as printed; do not silently replace it with its reciprocal.
- **Theorem 3.2, physical/PDF pp. 7–8.** For infinite connected quasi-transitive cubic \(G_0\), if \(|\gamma(G_0)|<\infty\) and \(\eta(G_1)\) exists, the exponents \(\gamma,\eta\) of \(G_0,G_1\) agree. The exponent \(\nu\) is also invariant only under the additional common-asymptotic hypothesis (3.6): the mid-edge SAW counts \(\sigma_{n,k}\) obey two-sided bounds \(C_{1,k}L(n)n^{\gamma-1}\mu_k^n\le \sigma_{n,k}\le C_{2,k}L(n)n^{\gamma-1}\mu_k^n\) with one common slowly varying \(L\). Do not state unconditional \(\nu\)-invariance.
- **Theorem 3.3, physical/PDF p. 8.** Let \(G\) be infinite, connected, bipartite, and quasi-transitive as a *coloured* graph; every black vertex has degree three. Transform only black vertices to get \(\widetilde G\). Then \(\mu(G)^{-2}=h(\mu(\widetilde G)^{-1})\) with \(h(x)=x^3+x^4\). Under the corresponding Theorem 3.2 assumptions the critical exponents \(\gamma,\eta,\nu\) agree. For the hexagonal lattice \(H\), using the known \(\mu(H)=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\), this gives the transformed-lattice constant \(\widetilde\mu\approx1.75056\); this number is not itself a scaling-limit statement.

## Proof route

For Theorem 3.1, define SAW partition functions \(Z_0(x),Z_1(x)\) and the restricted \(Z_1^*(x)\) for walks whose endpoints are old-edge midpoints. Inclusion gives \(Z_1^*(x)\le Z_1(x)\). Classifying an arbitrary walk by its number of old-edge-midpoint endpoints and shortening the endpoint when needed gives
\[
Z_1(x)\le [1+2x+2x^2+2x^3]^2 Z_1^*(x)+6|W_0|(1+x+x^2),
\]
so both partition functions have the same convergence radius. Collapsing each Fisher triangle gives the exact midpoint-walk identity \(Z_0(x^2(1+x))=Z_1^*(x)\): each base walk with \(n\) steps has \(2^n\) lifts. Applying the radius criterion \(Z_k(x)<\infty\) for \(x<\mu_k^{-1}\), and infinite for \(x>\mu_k^{-1}\), yields \(\mu_0^{-1}=x^2(1+x)=g(x)\) at \(x=\mu_1^{-1}\).

Theorem 3.1(b) separately asserts monotone convergence to \(\phi\). A geometric contraction can also be derived from the inverse recurrence, without altering the printed theorem: set \(u_k=\mu_k^{-1}\), \(a=\phi^{-1}\), and \(h=g^{-1}\). Then \(u_{k+1}=h(u_k)\). By (1.1), \(u_k\in[1/2,1]\); since \(g(1/2)=3/8<1/2\), for every \(t\in[1/2,1]\) we have \(h(t)>1/2\) and
\[
0<h'(t)=\frac{1}{g'(h(t))}\le \frac{1}{g'(1/2)}=\frac47.
\]
Because \(a\in[1/2,1]\) is fixed by \(h\), the mean-value theorem gives the separate derived estimate
\[
|u_k-a|\le (4/7)^k|u_0-a|.
\]
The local inverse derivative is \(h'(a)=1/g'(a)=2/(7-\sqrt5)\approx0.419821\); this local derivative is not the displayed source upper base. These are deductions from the recurrence and interval (1.1), not quotations from Theorem 3.1(b).

Theorem 3.2 compares midpoint-walk generating functions and asymptotic count growth under its stated exponent hypotheses. Theorem 3.3 uses the coloured bipartite structure and a weighted partition-function identity; its transformed relation is cubic/quartic rather than quadratic/cubic because only one color class is decorated. The complete proofs and the exponent definitions/assumptions were read in §§4–5, physical/PDF pp. 8–13.

## Transfer boundary

These are exact transformations for the stated cubic or coloured-bipartite graph classes. They do not prove a honeycomb SAW curve scaling limit, tightness in a curve topology, a discrete domain Markov property, or SLE identification. The equality of critical exponents is conditional as described above. Treat the hexagonal numerical constant as a connective-constant identity, not as evidence for C107's curve-level limit.
