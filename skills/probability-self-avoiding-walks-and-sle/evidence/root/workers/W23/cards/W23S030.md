## Model and theorem statements

Let \(X\) be continuous-time nearest-neighbour simple random walk on \(\mathbb Z^d\), with rate-\(2d\) Poisson jumps and uniform choice among the \(2d\) neighbours. Its intersection local time is
\[
I(T)=\int_0^T\!\int_0^T\mathbf1_{\{X(S_1)=X(S_2)\}}\,dS_1dS_2=\sum_x(L_T^x)^2.
\]
For \(g>0\),
\[
G_{g,\nu}(a,b)=\int_0^\infty E_a[e^{-gI(T)}\mathbf1_{\{X(T)=b\}}]e^{-\nu T}\,dT,\qquad
\chi(g,\nu)=\sum_bG_{g,\nu}(a,b)=\int_0^\infty c_{g,T}e^{-\nu T}\,dT
\]
(Definitions (1.1)–(1.3), physical pp. 2–3). This is continuous-time **weak** self-avoidance: intersections are penalized, not forbidden.

- **Theorem 1.1 (physical/printed p. 3).** In \(d=4\), for each sufficiently small \(g>0\), there is \(A_g>0\) such that as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\),
\[
\chi(g,\nu_c(g)+\epsilon)\sim A_g\epsilon^{-1}(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{1/4}.
\]
As \(g\downarrow0\), \(A_g=(bg)^{1/4}(1+O(g))\), with \(b=1/(2\pi^2)\) from the bubble asymptotic \(B_{m^2}=8\sum_xC_{m^2}(x)^2\sim b\log m^{-2}\) (Eq. 1.8, p. 3). Here the paper uses \(\gamma=1/4\) specifically for the logarithmic correction, not for the ordinary susceptibility critical exponent (p. 7).
- **Theorem 1.2 (p. 3).** In \(d=4\), with \(a=2C_0(0)\), \(\nu_c(g)=-ag+O(g^2)\) as \(g\downarrow0\).
- **Theorem 4.1 (physical p. 16).** For \(d=4\) and sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), continuous functions \(\nu_{c,0},z_{c,0}\) on \([0,\delta)^2\), differentiable in \(g_0\), and continuous \(c(g_0)=1+O(g_0)\) satisfy \(\widehat\chi(m^2,g_0,\nu_{c,0},z_{c,0})=m^{-2}\). At the same critical parameters, \(\partial_{\nu_0}\widehat\chi\sim -m^{-4}c(\widehat g_0)/(\widehat g_0B_{m^2})^\gamma\) as \((m^2,g_0)\to(0,\widehat g_0)\), for \(m^2,g_0,\widehat g_0\in(0,\delta)\); also \(\nu_{c,0}(m^2,0)=z_{c,0}(m^2,0)=0\), and their \(g_0\)-derivatives are uniformly bounded (Eqs. 4.27–4.29).
- **Lemma A.1 (pp. 57–58).** For every \(d>0\), a critical value \(\nu_c\in(-\infty,0]\) exists with \(\chi(g,\nu)<\infty\iff\nu>\nu_c\). For \(d>2\), \(-2C_0(0)g\le\nu_c\le0\), by subadditivity and Jensen using \(E I(T)\le2TC_0(0)\). For \(d=1,2\), finiteness of \(\nu_c\) uses a separate lower bound restricted to strictly self-avoiding jump trajectories; the printed bounds are \([-2-4g,0]\) for \(d=1\) and \([-4-2g,0]\) for \(d=2\) (p. 58). It does not use the \(d>2\) Green-function estimate in those dimensions.

The authors explicitly state (pp. 3–6) that the susceptibility result yields only a Cesàro-averaged path-count implication by a Tauberian theorem; pointwise \(c_T\) asymptotics, a path scaling limit and SLE\(_{8/3}\) remain conjectural/unproved in the article. In particular, the paper proves no planar scaling-limit theorem.

## Proof chain for the logarithmic correction

1. **Torus approximation and supersymmetric representation (§§2–3, pp. 7–13).** Lemma 2.1 proves monotone finite-torus approximation \(\chi_N\uparrow\chi\) and compact-uniform analytic convergence above \(\nu_c\), using folded-walk coupling and convergence of self-intersection penalties. Differential-form integration turns the finite-volume two-point function into an exact supersymmetric field integral (Proposition 3.1, Eq. 3.10, p. 11); normalization comes from the supersymmetric partition identity.
2. **Renormalized parameters (§4, pp. 13–20).** The parameters \((g,\nu)\) are represented through \((m^2,g_0,\nu_0,z_0)\), with the susceptibility identity and a critical surface. Theorem 4.1 (p. 16) gives the critical-renormalization estimates, including \(\widehat\chi=m^{-2}\) and the derivative asymptotic with the bubble factor to power \(\gamma=1/4\). Lemma 4.3 (p. 19) is the elementary inversion step: if \(u'(t)=(-\log u(t))^{-\gamma}(1+o(1))\), \(u(0)=0\), and \(u(t)>0\), then \(u(t)=t(-\log t)^{-\gamma}(1+o(1))\). Its proof integrates \(u'(-\log u)^\gamma\), changes variables, and uses \(\int_0^u(-\log v)^\gamma\,dv=u(-\log u)^\gamma(1+O(1/|\log u|))\).
3. **RG construction and critical flow (§§5–7, pp. 21–46).** A finite-range covariance decomposition yields progressive integration, with a three-coordinate polynomial \(V=g\tau^2+\nu\tau+z\tau_\Delta\) and an infinite-dimensional polymer/error coordinate \(K\). The quadratic flow has two marginal/centre directions, one relevant direction, and a contracting \(K\) direction. Theorem 6.3 (pp. 33–34) controls a single RG step; Theorem 6.4 (p. 35) transfers the estimates to infinite volume. Proposition 7.1 (pp. 37, 40–46) constructs the global critical flow by a centre-stable-manifold argument and supplies the tuned initial parameters. Theorem 7.2 (pp. 39–40) is explicitly restated from external source [7]; its proof is not reproduced here.
4. **Bubble sum, derivative, and integration (§8, pp. 46–56).** Lemma 8.4 (p. 50) identifies \(\sum_j\beta_j=B_{m^2}\). Lemma 8.5 (pp. 50–51) yields \(\widetilde g_\infty\sim1/B_{m^2}\); Lemma 8.6 (pp. 52–54) controls derivatives along the critical flow and gives the \((\widetilde g_j/g_0)^{1/4}\) factor. The proof of Theorem 4.1 (pp. 54–55) differentiates the susceptibility identity, showing the leading derivative term dominates the \(W,K\) error contributions. Then Eqs. (4.55)–(4.62) (pp. 19–20) yield \(F=1/\chi\) with \(F'\sim c_0(g)/(\widetilde g_0(0)b)^{1/4}(-\log F)^{-1/4}\); Lemma 4.3 gives the stated \(\epsilon^{-1}(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{1/4}\) divergence and amplitude. Theorem 1.2 follows separately from the tuned-flow recurrence (8.59)–(8.66), pp. 55–56.
