## What the paper establishes

This is a model-by-model local algebra paper, not a scaling-limit theorem for SAW curves. The observable is a parafermionic weighted sum over loop configurations with a specified open/connecting path and winding phase. Discrete holomorphicity means that the oriented sum of the observable over the edges of each elementary face vanishes. The calculations pair local configurations that differ only in the face adjacent to the marked path and solve the resulting finite linear systems in the Boltzmann weights.

**Self-dual square-lattice Potts/FK loops (§2, physical pp. 4–7).** For `0≤Q≤4`, Eq. (5) writes `√Q=2 cos γ`, with `0≤γ≤π/2`. The observable (6) sums configurations in which `0,z` lie on the same loop with weight `P(G)e^{-isθ(z)}`. At the isotropic self-dual point, the local holomorphicity condition is `√Q=2 sin(πs/2)` (Eq. (7)). For the anisotropic self-dual model on a rhombically embedded lattice, local cancellations give the two linear equations (9) in the plaquette weights `a,b`; with `λ=e^{iπs}` and `μ=e^{iα(s+1)}`, their determinant is the product in Eq. (10). The nontrivial branch selected in the paper gives `s=1−2γ/π` (Eq. (11)), which satisfies Eq. (7), and the weight ratio (12). Comparing with the Temperley–Lieb/Yang–Baxter parametrization `a(u)=sin u`, `b(u)=sin(γ−u)` yields Eq. (13), a linear relation between `(s+1)α/2` and `π/2−u`. The authors explicitly say this is not the usual `α=πu/γ` relation and explain a tunable turn-angle parameter that repairs the mismatch. Do not suppress this caveat.

**Square-lattice O(n) loops (§3, physical pp. 7–10).** The paper uses the dilute square-lattice loop model with `n=−2 cos(2η)`, `0≤η≤π/2`, and an observable over configurations containing an oriented open path from `0` to `z`. It solves the local holomorphicity constraints in the vertex weights. The `v=0,n=1` six-vertex specialization has a special degeneracy; for generic `v=0,n≠−1`, the only stated solution is the trivial spin `s=−1`, corresponding to dense/ghost loops rather than the physical antiholomorphic continuum candidate discussed in the paper. For `v≠0`, the determinant condition is `cos(4πs)=cos(6η)` (derived from the local system); the nontrivial branch yields the weight parametrization displayed in Eq. (20), which matches the integrable Yang–Baxter weights after a change of variables. These are local lattice identities and integrability comparisons. The discussion of continuum universality and some central-charge consequences uses cited Coulomb-gas/transfer-matrix/Bethe-Ansatz input, not a proof here of convergence of the lattice curve to SLE.

**Two-colour `C_2^(1)` loop model (§4, physical pp. 11–12).** A further local cancellation calculation gives the spin/weight constraint (23) and the Yang–Baxter-compatible weights (24). The short final §5 summary is on p. 12; references occupy pp. 12–13.

## Proof mechanism, constants, and scope

The proof ingredient is exact local enumeration/cancellation: list the face connectivities, write the winding phases and the ratios of the paired Boltzmann weights, then require the coefficient of each external connectivity to cancel. A determinant-zero condition gives candidate spins; substituting the spin into the equations gives the allowed weight ratios. The equations and normalizations are model-specific; there is no universal theorem that discrete holomorphicity alone implies a scaling limit.

The introduction explicitly warns that lattice discrete holomorphicity is not by itself enough to identify SLE: one still needs tightness/continuity and a scaling-limit argument with the needed boundary/Markov structure. This source therefore supports a **candidate local-observable method** for a lattice model only after the parafermion, embedding, and weights have been matched. It does not prove C107's curve tightness, discrete domain-Markov property, weak convergence, or SLE identification. It is not a Busemann, geodesic, first-passage, or growth theorem.
