## Models and source results

The notes cover several different models, which must not be merged:

- Sections 1–5: **strict nearest-neighbor SAW** on \(\mathbb Z^d\), then a separately embedded honeycomb-lattice SAW. A bridge on \(\mathbb Z^d\) uses the first coordinate as height and satisfies \(\omega_1(0)<\omega_1(i)\le\omega_1(n)\) for \(i\ge1\).
- Sections 6–7: finite-volume integral representations, then **continuous-time weakly self-avoiding walk** on \(\mathbb Z^d\), with rate-\(2d\) continuous-time SRW and self-repulsion weight \(\exp(-g\sum_v L_{v,T}^2)\). These are not the strict discrete-time planar SAW model.

### Bridge, polygon, and honeycomb results

**Theorem 2.6 (physical PDF p.13).** For nearest-neighbor strict SAW on \(\mathbb Z^d\), for every \(B>\pi\sqrt{2/3}\) there is \(n_0(B)\), independent of \(d\ge2\), such that
\[
c_n\le b_{n+1}e^{B\sqrt n}\le \mu^{n+1}e^{B\sqrt n},\qquad n\ge n_0(B).
\]
Here \(c_n\) counts rooted n-step SAWs, \(b_n\) rooted bridges, and \(\mu=\lim c_n^{1/n}\). The proof unfolds a half-space walk into bridges with strictly decreasing positive spans. Theorem 2.4 (physical PDF p.12) states that if \(P_D(A)\) counts partitions of \(A\) into strictly decreasing positive integers, then
\[
\log P_D(A)\sim \pi\sqrt{A/3}.
\]
In particular, the one-half-space estimate uses \(h_n\le P_D(n)b_n\). To compare an arbitrary SAW, the proof splits at the last visit to its minimum first-coordinate hyperplane and bounds \(c_n\) by a convolution with two partition factors, \(P_D(n-m)P_D(m+1)\). The two factors each contribute their own partition entropy; summing their exponents at the balanced split gives the final threshold \(B>\pi\sqrt{2/3}\). Thus the \(\sqrt2\) in Theorem 2.6 comes from the two-half-space convolution, not from Theorem 2.4. The threshold is strict; the theorem does not assert it at equality.

Corollaries 2.7–2.8 (PDF pp.14–15) give \(b_n^{1/n}\to\mu\) and divergence of the bridge generating function at critical fugacity. Theorem 2.9 (pp.16–17) injects pairs of bridges with a common endpoint into an endpoint SAW plus two cut indices, then applies Cauchy–Schwarz to obtain
\[
2d\,c_{2n+1}(e_1)\ge \frac{b_n^2}{n(n+1)^2(2n+1)^{d-1}}.
\]
Corollary 2.10 then shows polygon and walk connective constants agree and gives, for some \(C>0\),
\[
\mu^{2n}e^{-C\sqrt n}\le c_{2n+1}(e_1)\le(n+1)\mu^{2n+2}.
\]

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp.17–22).** For honeycomb SAW, with the notes’ midpoint convention, \(\mu_H=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\) and \(x_c=1/\mu_H\). The proof defines the winding-weighted holomorphic observable
\[
F_z(x)=\sum_{\omega:a\to x}e^{-i\sigma W_\omega(a,x)}z^{\ell(\omega)}
\]
in a finite simply connected honeycomb domain. At \((z,\sigma)=(x_c,5/8)\), Lemma 3.4 gives a local three-mid-edge linear relation. Its proof groups paths into pairs (those visiting all three incident mid-edges) and triplets (one-mid-edge paths with their one-step extensions); the phase cancellation uses \(\lambda=e^{-i5\pi/24}\), the pair’s winding offsets \(\mp4\pi/3\), and \(x_c^{-1}=2\cos(\pi/8)=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}\). Summing over a finite strip cancels interior contributions and yields the boundary identity (3.16), with \(c_\alpha=\cos(3\pi/8)\) and \(c_\epsilon=\cos(\pi/4)\). At criticality, either a positive limiting top/bottom contribution forces susceptibility divergence, or it vanishes for each strip and a cut-at-first-crossing inequality gives \(B_T(x_c)\ge c/T\), again forcing divergence. Below criticality, bridge weights decay geometrically in strip width and the bridge-unfolding product gives finite susceptibility. This is lattice-specific.

### Lace expansion and high dimensions

**Theorems 4.1–4.2 (PDF p.26)** are stated for strict nearest-neighbor SAW on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge5\), with constants \(A,D,c,\epsilon>0\): \(c_n=A\mu^n[1+O(n^{-\epsilon})]\), \(\mathbb E_n|\omega(n)|^2=Dn[1+O(n^{-\epsilon})]\), a Brownian scaling limit after division by \(\sqrt{Dn}\), and \(G_{z_c}(x)=c|x|^{-(d-2)}[1+O(|x|^{-\epsilon})]\). These theorems are cited results in the notes, not proved in full there.

Sections 4–5 derive the fixed-length lace recursion by repeated inclusion–exclusion, introduce the lace coefficients \(\pi_m(x)\), and Fourier-transform it to \(\widehat G_z(k)=[1-z|\Omega|\widehat D(k)-\widehat\Pi_z(k)]^{-1}\). The bubble diagram is \(\mathcal B(z)=\sum_xG_z(x)^2=\|\widehat G_z\|_2^2\). The differential inequality \(\frac d{dz}(z\chi(z))\ge\chi(z)^2/\mathcal B(z)\) reduces linear susceptibility divergence to finiteness of \(\mathcal B(z_c)\).

Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 5.4 (PDF pp.36–40) use diagrammatic bounds on lace terms plus a continuity/bootstrap argument comparing the SAW two-point function with simple random walk. The small parameter satisfies \(\beta\le K/(d-4)\) for nearest-neighbor walks, so the theorem applies only in sufficiently high dimension, or \(\beta\le K L^{-d}\) for spread-out walks in any \(d>4\) when the spread parameter \(L\) is sufficiently large. If \(\beta\le\beta_0\), the critical bubble is at most \(1+C\beta\), and hence \(\chi(z)\asymp(1-z/z_c)^{-1}\). Several convergence and diagram estimates are cited or sketched with details deferred to the lace-expansion references; do not use this exposition as a line-by-line substitute for those sources.

### Integral representation and the 4D weak-SAW result

Lemma 6.7 and Theorem 6.11 (PDF pp.47–49) develop the boson/Grassmann integral identity. For a finite set \(\Lambda\), \(a,b\in\Lambda\), and \(C=A^{-1}\) with positive Hermitian part, Theorem 6.11 (physical PDF p.48, Eq. (6.43)) is
\[
\sum_{\omega\in S_{a,b}(\Lambda)} C_\omega
=\int e^{-S_A}\,\bar\varphi_a\varphi_b\prod_{x\in\Lambda\setminus\{a,b\}}(1+\tau_x),
\qquad \tau_x=\varphi_x\bar\varphi_x+\psi_x\bar\psi_x.
\]
Here \(S_{a,b}(\Lambda)\) is the set of SAWs \(a=x_0,\ldots,x_\ell=b\) with distinct intermediate vertices in \(\Lambda\setminus\{a,b\}\), and \(C_\omega=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}C_{x_{j-1},x_j}\). The displayed \(\int\) is the notes' differential-form integral (Definition 6.4): it integrates the bosonic coordinates and extracts the top-degree fermionic/differential-form coefficient; it is not a pointwise scalar identity. For degree-zero factors it agrees with the Gaussian measure of covariance \(C\) (Remark 6.8), while Lemma 6.9 gives \(\int e^{-S_A}F(\tau)=F(0)\). The Gaussian field alone generates a self-avoiding walk with a background of loops; the anticommuting differential forms make the normalized integral of the remaining factors equal to one, eliminating those loops. This is an exact finite-volume identity, not by itself an infinite-volume asymptotic theorem.

**Theorem 7.1 (PDF p.50).** For the continuous-time weakly SAW just defined, in dimension \(d\ge4\), for \(g\ge0\) sufficiently small there is \(c_g>0\) such that
\[
G^{(g)}_{\nu_c(g)}(x)=c_g|x|^{-(d-2)}(1+o(1)),\qquad |x|\to\infty.
\]
The notes state this as a theorem of Brydges–Slade and sketch a rigorous renormalisation-group proof: finite-volume torus approximation (Theorem 7.2), integral representation (Theorem 7.3), finite-range covariance decomposition (Theorem 7.5; \(d>2\), positive mass, sufficiently large dyadic scale factor, fixed derivative order), and progressive integration/polymer recursion. The flow equations and fixed-point step in Theorems 7.7–7.8 are summarized, not derived in full; the version’s bibliography describes one detailed account as “in preparation.” The section also discusses a hierarchical-lattice logarithmic endpoint-distance result; it must not be transferred as a theorem for strict SAW on \(\mathbb Z^4\).

## What this source does not prove for the target problems

- For C107, the notes explicitly label honeycomb SAW-to-SLE(8/3) convergence and the rescaled observable limit as conjectures (Conjectures 1.5 and 3.6; PDF pp.10, 23–25). They outline a possible route via tightness and martingale identification but prove neither. The exact honeycomb connective constant, Hammersley–Welsh counting, and high-dimensional results do not supply lattice-curve tightness, a discrete domain-Markov property, or SLE identification.
- The strict-SAW and weak-SAW statements have distinct path measures. Neither the high-dimensional strict-SAW theorem nor the 4D continuous-time weak-SAW theorem is a 2D strict-SAW scaling-limit result.
- This notes chapter does not prove the quenched random-cluster limit in C108. No environment or cluster SAW is analyzed here.
- Numerical exponents in the introduction, Coulomb-gas/O(n) phase predictions, and SLE scaling claims remain predictions/conjectures as labeled by the notes.
