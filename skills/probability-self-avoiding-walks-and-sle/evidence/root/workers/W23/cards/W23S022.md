## Model and theorem statements

The main tessellation model is the regular hyperbolic tiling \(\mathcal H(d,k)\), with vertex degree \(d\), face degree \(k\), and \((d-2)(k-2)>4\). The source counts SAWs from a root \(o\) as \(c_n\), and SAPs containing \(o\) as \(p_n\), identifying two polygons if they have the same edge set. This convention differs from some SAW books (the paper notes \(p_4=4,p_6=12\) in \(\mathbb Z^2\)). It defines \(\mu_w=\lim c_n^{1/n}\), \(\mu_p=\limsup p_n^{1/n}\), and \(\mu_{p,2}\) for closed non-backtracking walks.

- **Theorem 1, physical/PDF p. 2.** For every regular hyperbolic tessellation, \(\mu_p(\mathcal H(d,k))<\mu_w(\mathcal H(d,k))\).
- **Theorem 2, physical/PDF p. 2.** For fixed integer \(k\ge3\), \(\mu_w(\mathcal H(d,k))=d-1-O(1/d)\) as \(d\to\infty\). For every hyperbolic tessellation, \((k-1)/(k-2)\le\mu_p\le5\).
- **Theorem 3, physical/PDF p. 3.** For uniform \(n\)-step SAW \(\mathbb P_n\) on any \(\mathcal H(d,k)\), there are constants \(\varepsilon,c>0\) such that
  \[
  \mathbb P_n\{d(o,\omega(n))\ge\varepsilon n\}\ge1-e^{-cn}\quad(n\ge0).
  \]
  Except for \(\mathcal H(3,7),\mathcal H(7,3)\), a constant \(M\ge1\) also gives \((\mu_w)^n\le c_n\le M(\mu_w)^n\) for every \(n\ge0\).
- **Theorem 4, physical/PDF p. 3.** On any vertex-transitive graph, the strict connective-constant gap \(\mu_p<\mu_w\) implies the same exponential ballisticity estimate. This is a conditional general-graph theorem; the paper proves the strict gap for the hyperbolic tessellations in Theorem 1.
- **Theorem 6, physical/PDF pp. 9–14.** For a finite plane graph \(G\in\mathcal S(d,k)\) with its boundary SAP \(P\), inner chords \(\operatorname{ch}(P)\), and inner vertex boundary \(\partial^V P\),
  \[
  (k-2)|\operatorname{ch}(P)|+\big((d-2)(k-2)-3\big)|\partial^V P|\le |P|-k.
  \]
  Here \(\mathcal S(d,k)\) has a SAP boundary, every bounded face has a simple boundary of degree \(k\), and every internal vertex has degree \(d\). The proof decomposes general polygons through the no-chord/connected-boundary cases and counts faces, incidences, and internal edges using Euler's formula.
- **Theorem 7, physical/PDF pp. 14–16.** Put \(r=((k-2)(d-2)-3)/(k-2)>0\). For every \(\mathcal H(d,k)\),
  \[
  \mu_p\le \min_{p\in[0,1]}\Big[(1-p)^{1/(k-2)}p\big(1-(1-p)^r\big)\Big]^{-1}.
  \]
  The proof uses a mixed site/bond percolation event for a SAP, the isoperimetric bound, and the fact that disjoint occurring SAP interiors force an expected-count bound. It supplies an explicit general upper bound, not an exact value.
- **Theorem 8, physical/PDF p. 17.** For \(\mathcal H(3,k)\) (hence hyperbolic \(k\ge7\)),
  \[
  \mu_p\le N_k:=\frac{k-4}{(k-5)^{(k-5)/(k-4)}}.
  \]
  This is a separate low-degree argument: a dual-graph/interface “unzipping” construction and a count of interface walks bound polygon growth. Do not apply its formula outside \(d=3\).
- **Theorem 9, physical/PDF pp. 27–29.** \(\mu_{p,2}<\mu_w\) for every hyperbolic tessellation except \(\mathcal H(7,3)\) and \(\mathcal H(3,7)\). The proof combines previously established cases with finite recursive type-counting matrices for the remaining tessellations.

## Proof spine and checked finite calculations

For Theorem 4, let \(X\) be a geodesic from \(o\) to the SAW endpoint, with length \(r=d(o,y)<\varepsilon n\). The closed traversal of the SAW and the reversed geodesic is decomposed into “almost” edge-disjoint subwalks \(W_j\) and SAPs \(P_i\), not edge-disjoint SAPs. The geodesic subwalks \(S_i\) used in the construction are pairwise edge-disjoint. Each edge outside \(X\) belongs to at most one polygon; an edge of \(X\) is in at most one \(S_i\) and at most one residual subgraph \(P_i\setminus S_i\), so it is counted in at most two polygons. The source then obtains \(M\le\varepsilon n\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^M|P_i|\le(1+\varepsilon)n\). Counting the possible polygon collections using \(p_m\le C\rho^m\), for \(\rho\in(\mu_p,\mu_w)\), gives an exponential penalty for sub-ballistic walks; choosing \(\varepsilon\) small proves \(\mathbb P_n(d(o,\omega(n))<\varepsilon n)\le e^{-cn}\). Theorem 3 follows from Theorems 1 and 4.

The upper bound proof for Theorem 1 is not just the asymptotic expansion in Theorem 2: it uses the polygon bounds (Theorems 7–8) and compares them against lower bounds for SAWs. For the difficult tessellations, the author partitions walks by layer and recent local type, writes a finite recursive matrix, and uses its Perron growth rate to lower-bound \(\mu_w\). Appendix A (pp. 33–35) is needed: Lemma A.1 inductively proves that every layer \(G_n\) is a SAP containing all prior layers inside, with separate arguments for \(d>3\) and \(d=3\). The layer property is an input to the recursive count proof, not a cosmetic appendix.

For \(\mathcal H(7,3)\), the proof's p. 22 matrix is
\[
A_{7,3}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0&2\\2&2&2&0\\2&1&0&1\\2&2&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and for \(\mathcal H(4,5)\), p. 23 uses
\[
A_{4,5}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&2\\1&0&1\\2&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
MATLAB recomputation gives dominant eigenvalues 5.139119427254 and 2.866198262509, with eigenpair residuals \(4.260\times10^{-15}\) and \(2.637\times10^{-15}\). The paper uses these finite matrix values as numerical comparisons against its polygon upper bounds; they are not independent proof of the universal theorem. ## Transfer boundary

The exact growth, layer, and polygon statements are for hyperbolic tessellations; Theorem 4 alone is for arbitrary vertex-transitive graphs and requires the strict gap \(\mu_p<\mu_w\). Neither scope proves the Euclidean/honeycomb C107 curve scaling limit or C108 random-percolation-cluster full exponential-growth limit. In particular, hyperbolic ballisticity, finite-layer embeddings, and matrix comparisons do not transfer to honeycomb SLE or to a random cluster without additional model-specific proof.
