## Model and theorem statements

For \(d\ge2\), \(\mathrm{SAW}_n\) is the set of nearest-neighbour self-avoiding paths of length \(n\) on \(\mathbb Z^d\) from 0 and \(\mathbb P_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\) is uniform. The event is the maximum Euclidean displacement of any vertex along the path, not just the endpoint.

- **Theorem 1.2 (physical/printed p. 2).** For every \(v>0\) and integer \(n\ge n_0(v)\),
  \[
  \mathbb P_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\!\left(\max_{0\le k\le n}\|\gamma_k\|\ge vn\right)\le e^{-n/T(v)},
  \quad T(v)=\exp^{\circ O(\log(1/v))}(1),
  \quad n_0(v)=4C_{HW}^2T(v)^2+2T(v)\log(1+2d).
  \]
  Here \(C_{HW}\) is the constant of Proposition 2.1. Corollary 1.3 (same page) gives constants \(c>0,n_0\) such that for \(n\ge n_0\),
  \(\mathbb P(\max_k\|\gamma_k\|\ge n/\exp(c\log^*n))\le e^{-n^{2/3}}\).
- **Theorem 1.4 (physical/printed p. 3; source-scope issue).** The PDF literally states: “There exists \(C>0\) such that for every \(T\ge1\),”
  \[
  \frac1T\sum_{k=1}^{T}b_k\mu_c^{-k}\le\frac{C}{\log(\log T)}.
  \]
  The left side is positive, while at \(T=2\) the displayed denominator is negative and at \(T=1\) it is undefined. The rendered page confirms this is what the source prints. Do not use the literal range as a valid bound for those values; the paper provides no correction in the read artifact. Any application must separately establish an appropriate large-integer range and constants. This caveat does not license silently changing the source theorem.
- **Theorem 1.5 (physical/printed p. 4).** Under the irreducible-bridge law \(\mathbb P_{iSAB}(\gamma)=\mu_c^{-|\gamma|}\), the theorem states for every \(v\ge1\),
  \(\mathbb E_{iSAB}[|\gamma|^{\le h(v)}]>v\), with \(h(v)=\exp(\exp(O(v)))\). This is a truncated expectation statement; the proof is in §5.

## Proof spine

Section 2 (physical pp. 5–8) fixes the hypercubic SAW and bridge/renewal notation. Proposition 2.1 gives the Hammersley–Welsh comparison \(e^{-C_{HW}\sqrt n}c_n\le b_n\le c_n\); Lemmas 2.3–2.5 establish Kesten’s irreducible-bridge probability law, the logarithmic tail \(\mathbb P_{iSAB}(|\gamma|\ge k)\le C/\log k\), and the bridge/Cesàro identity used later.

Section 3 (pp. 8–11) recasts the qualitative Duminil-Copin–Hammond proof in terms of the Dialectica interpretation. It identifies the quantitative inputs needed to instantiate the proof; this logic discussion is not an independent geometric estimate.

Section 4 (pp. 11–20) proves the displacement bound. §4.1 controls the number of renewal points by a binomial large-deviation estimate. §4.2 unfolds finite bridges using zigzags; repeated unfolding trades large height for many renewal points, whose probability is small. §4.3 chooses the tower-scale parameters \(T(v),n_0(v)\), then uses Hammersley–Welsh to transfer the bridge event bound to uniform SAW. The constants and iterated-exponential scale arise from composing the quantitative components, not from a sharp scaling exponent.

Section 5 (pp. 21–31) proves Theorem 1.5. Diamond-point lower bounds (Lemmas 5.1–5.7) feed a finite truncated expectation argument (Theorem 5.8, p. 27); a modified stickbreak construction (Lemma 5.9 and Theorem 5.10, pp. 29–30) contradicts the assumed upper bound and proves the double-exponential cutoff. Theorem 1.4 is then obtained at the end of p. 31 from Wald’s identity and the bridge decomposition, subject to the literal range issue recorded above.

## Transfer limits and evidence

This provides rigorous bridge-counting and quantitative sub-ballisticity tools for uniform nearest-neighbour SAW on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\). It does not prove a planar curve-space tightness theorem, convergence, a discrete domain-Markov property, or identification with SLE. It also does not prove a sharp displacement exponent.
