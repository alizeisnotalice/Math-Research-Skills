# Surface immersion increments and stabilized scalar comparison maps

For *Smooth isometric immersions of closed surfaces into Euclidean four-space* (SHA `1f0bf283be86c80bc4c41547dac66f49bdfe73768373554058b53c8681d6ba3a`, physical pp. 8, 10, 15--20), Proposition 3.1 assumes a closed surface, an immersion with nonzero second form, preferred normal, disk coordinate, and `Hess_gamma(F)x(partial_y,partial_y)>0`. It adds `a^2 dx^2` while preserving later transverse-curve data. Proposition 4.1 gives `P(G+U)-P(G)-delta^2H=O_tau(delta eta^q+delta^3/tau)`; Proposition 5.1 turns a high-order approximate metric into an exact immersion under (5.1)--(5.3).

For *Positive scalar curvature forces rational inessentiality* (SHA `dae319c1cc2582086b82ff65f2c210a972975ad321b340cff4c9d5211dcb14dc`, physical pp. 18, 27, 31), retain the compact comparison map `G=eta(||V||_{p_R}/T_0)(V-y_0)/R` and the one-pass bounds `|ell_i^+-ell_i|<pi/(2w)`, `|d ell_i^+|<=L/4`, and `Scal(g^+)>=Scal(g)-C_0(n+1)(2+log q)L^2w^2`. Both are conditional source components, not generic immersion or positive-scalar-curvature solvers.
