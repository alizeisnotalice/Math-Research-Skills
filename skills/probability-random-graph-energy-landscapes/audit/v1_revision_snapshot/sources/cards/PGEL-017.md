## Model and scope

The nonlinear stability/count results concern the undirected, positive, equal-coupling nearest-neighbor **single-cycle** model. In power-flow form the edge flow is $P_{i,i+1}=K\sin\Delta_{i,i+1}$ and the injections are balanced, $\sum_iP_i=0$. Angle differences are principal values modulo $2\pi$, and solutions are defined modulo a common phase shift. The stability matrix is the symmetric linearization with one rotational zero mode. The paper's stability/count convention is nonpositive spectrum (a linear PSD test for $-M$); this does not assert strict decay on the phase quotient, and the matrix test alone is not a nonlinear-stability theorem. In finite-capacity statements, the injection vector is held fixed as $K$ varies and the feasible interval is nonempty, in particular $K\ge(P^*_{\max}-P^*_{\min})/2$.

The graph-wide flow result in Theorem III.6 is more general: on any connected graph, two flows satisfying the same Kirchhoff balance differ by a circulation in the cycle space. The state-count and stability conclusions in §IV are **not** thereby generalized to arbitrary graphs.

## Main results and proof chains

1. **Theorem III.6 (PDF p. 7; proof pp. 7–8): cycle-space parametrization.** For a connected graph with incidence matrix $A$, two balanced flow vectors $I'$ and $I''$ have difference $I^\circ=I'-I''\in\ker A$. Choose a spanning tree; each non-tree edge gives its fundamental cycle. Subtract the corresponding cycle flows to eliminate all non-tree coordinates; the remaining circulation is supported on a tree and must vanish by leaf elimination. Thus $\ker A$ is generated by loop flows. On a tree, the flow is unique. This is a statement about conservation-law solutions; it does not imply every such flow is realizable by a stable Kuramoto state.

2. **Theorem IV.1 (PDF pp. 10–12): infinite-coupling limit on a cycle.** For a sequence of stable cycle solutions with finite fixed injections as $K\to\infty$, the limiting gaps lie in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ and are all equal to $2\pi q/n$, where $q\in\mathbb Z$ is the winding number. The proof writes $P_{i,i+1}=P^*_{i,i+1}+K\epsilon=K\sin\Delta_{i,i+1}$; all sines converge to the common $\epsilon$, so each limiting gap is on the low or high arcsine branch and all cosines have equal absolute value. If all branches are high, the stability matrix has a positive mode; if both branches occur, the relevant $2\times2$ principal minor of $-M'$ is $-1$, so $-M'$ is not positive semidefinite. Only the all-low branch is stable, and the limiting winding identity forces equal gaps. This is a limit statement; at finite $K$, fixed injections generally make the gaps unequal.

3. **Theorem IV.3 (PDF p. 13; proof pp. 13–21) and Corollary IV.4 (p. 13).** For the fixed one-cycle power-flow instance, the number $N(K)$ of stable solutions is nondecreasing in coupling $K$. The proof parameterizes each winding constraint as a level curve $A_j(K,\epsilon)=2\pi q$. Lemmas IV.5–IV.7 constrain critical points and level-curve topology; Lemma IV.8 identifies an additional zero eigenvalue by $\sum_i c_i^{-1}=0$; Lemma IV.9 shows this degeneracy occurs at most once on the relevant level curve; Lemma IV.10 supplies the cut necessary condition and excludes two gaps with negative cosine. These steps give the continuation/stability-change argument and monotonicity. Corollary IV.4 **prints**
   $$N_\infty:=\lim_{K\to\infty}N(K)=2\lfloor n/4\rfloor+1,$$
   and states that this value is an upper bound for the finite-$K$ stable-solution count, independently of $K$ and the fixed balanced injections. Keep the source's formal limiting-configuration count separate from the literal limit of finite-$K$ counts at the endpoint case: if $4\mid n$, the twists $q=\pm n/4$ have every edge cosine zero and hence $M=0$, so they pass the source's nonpositive-spectrum test but are not strict quotient-stable. The independent fixed-injection audit for $n=4$, $P=(1,-1,0,0)$, finds $N(K)=1$ for every $K>1$, while the formal zero-injection/infinite-coupling PSD twists are $q=-1,0,1$ (count $3$). Thus the universal literal identity between $\lim N(K)$ and the formal count is quarantined pending reconciliation; this audit does not refute Theorem IV.3 monotonicity, the finite upper bound $N(K)\le 2\lfloor n/4\rfloor+1$, or the existence of the formal limiting configurations. See `../math/PGEL-017_corIV4_endpoint_reconciliation.md`; that note is a derived boundary audit, not a source proof or a replacement theorem.

4. **Finite-$K$ large-gap boundary (PDF pp. 12–13, 19–21, 23).** Lemma IV.10 states the necessary stability condition
   $$\sum_{i\in S,\,j\notin S}\cos\Delta_{ij}\ge0$$
   for every nonempty node set $S$. On a cycle, two gaps with negative cosine would define a cut violating this condition; hence at most one gap can lie outside $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$. The paper further identifies the only possible exceptional line as the most-loaded one and shows its stable solution continues from an all-low-branch solution at a larger $K$. These are cycle-specific conclusions.

5. **Appendix A (PDF p. 24):** an induction verifies the Thomas-algorithm/LU pivot identity $\mu_{i-1}\beta_i=-\mu_i$ for the tridiagonal stability submatrix, using the recurrence for elementary symmetric products in $c_i=\cos\Delta_i$.

## Limits and safe reuse

- Reuse Theorem III.6 for incidence-kernel/cycle-space parametrization of flows on a connected graph.
- Reuse the §IV count, branch, and continuation results only for the stated positive symmetric single-cycle model with fixed balanced injections; they do not establish a count theorem for multicycle graphs, random graphs, signed edges, or oriented/nonreciprocal dynamics.
- The asymptotic count is an upper bound for finite $K$, not a lower bound. Finite $K$ can include one large-gap stable state, so do not impose the all-gaps-$\pi/2$ condition on every stable finite-$K$ state.
- Exact finite-instance SymPy checks are saved in `../math/verification_results.json`. At $n=8$, the PSD winding set is $\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$, but the $q=\pm2$ matrices are identically zero and are not strict on the phase quotient; $q=-1,0,1$ have exactly the rotational zero mode and are strict on the quotient. These finite linear-algebra checks say nothing about nonlinear stability. They illustrate the spectrum, not the general proof.
