## Main results

Let δ=δ(n). **Theorem 1 (subcritical, PDF p. 3)** assumes δ>0 and δ²n→∞, with β=1−δ. It gives cutoff at (1/2)(n/δ)log(δ²n), window n/δ, and spectral gap (1+o(1))δ/n.

**Theorem 2 (critical window, PDF p. 4)** assumes δ=O(n⁻¹/²) and β=1±δ. Mixing is order n^(3/2), there is no cutoff, and the gap is order n⁻³/².

**Theorem 3 (supercritical, PDF p. 4)** as printed assumes δ>0 and δ²n→∞, with β=1+δ. It gives no cutoff and mixing-time order
t_exp(n)=(n/δ)exp[(n/2)∫₀^ζ log((1+g(x))/(1−g(x)))dx],
where g(x)=(tanh(βx)−x)/(1−x tanh(βx)) and ζ is its unique positive root. It also gives gap order 1/t_exp(n). The simplification (n/δ)exp((3/4+o(1))δ²n) is stated for the special case δ→0; retain that extra condition.

## Proof map

Permutation invariance makes normalized magnetization S_t=n⁻¹Σ_iX_t(i) a birth-and-death chain. Section 3.1 (PDF pp. 8–10) gives its transition kernel. Sections 3.2–3.4 (pp. 10–14) connect magnetization to full mixing and establish one-dimensional contraction and monotone coupling. Section 3.5 (pp. 14–16) proves the spectral-gap reduction using an increasing eigenfunction and a Dirichlet-form comparison.

- **Subcritical, §4 (PDF pp. 16–29):** Theorem 4.1 studies magnetization hitting near zero. Drift, moment/variance recurrences and monotone coupling give the cutoff estimates. Theorem 4.6 lifts magnetization mixing to configurations by a two-coordinate-chain argument. The gap proof combines path coupling and a Dirichlet test function with a fourth-moment estimate.
- **Critical, §5 (PDF pp. 29–35):** Hitting/coalescence gives upper bounds; third-moment drift and the n^(1/4)-rescaled limiting magnetization law, with quartic potential, give lower bounds. Birth-and-death estimates give the gap order; bounded mixing-time times gap rules out cutoff.
- **Supercritical, §6 (PDF pp. 35–42):** The birth-and-death structure reduces the barrier crossing to effective resistance/commute time. Lemma 6.1 (p. 36) gives the hitting-time representation. Resistance across [0,ζ] and stationary conductance yield the exponential integral; hitting-time bounds give mixing and gap scales. References: pp. 42–43.

## Scope and transfer limits

- The reduction depends on complete-graph symmetry; it does not prove the same phase diagram for arbitrary or inhomogeneous graphs.
- High-temperature and critical regimes have separate hypotheses and conclusions.
- The low-temperature integral and its 3/4 simplification have different stated conditions.
- Censored low-temperature behavior is a companion result, proved separately in W04S018.
