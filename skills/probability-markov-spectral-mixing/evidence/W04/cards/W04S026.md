# Source card — Aldous–Cruz–Feng, “Markov chains and mappings of distributions on compact spaces II: Numerics and Conjectures” (2024)

**Identity and reading evidence.** The 29-page arXiv v1 author manuscript (`arXiv:2403.18153v1`, 26 March 2024) is an informal numerical/conjecture companion, explicitly “not intended for formal publication.” Only the relevant PDF pages were read: pp.1–3 (definition of `π_{j,k}`, iteration and fixed points), 14–15 (finite-space Conjecture 5), and 21–22 (interval questions and partial density result). The source remains `partial_read`; no theorem is attributed to it from a simulation or conjecture. Rendered pp.15 and 22 were visually checked.

## Exact A121 statement in the source

**Conjecture 5 (PDF pp.14–15).** For a finite metric space with distinct distances, the authors conjecture that for almost every initial distribution, with respect to the natural uniform/Lebesgue measure on the probability simplex, repeated application of `π_{j,k}` converges to a point mass `δ_s` or an equal two-point law `δ_{s_1,s_2}`. The preceding pages show finite examples with unstable fixed points; those numerical trajectories are illustrations, not proof of the a.e. assertion. Page 15 explicitly identifies the reference simplex measure.

The mapping `π_{j,k}` is well defined by Theorem 1 of the companion paper W04S025. On a finite space, once the distance-order rule is fixed, each kernel entry is a finite sum of degree-`k` monomials in the masses of `θ`. Solving `πK_θ=π` is the inner stationary-law problem; using the finite linear system to express `π(θ)` algebraically/rationally on the open simplex is a derived finite-state reduction, not a theorem of this numerical manuscript.

## Exact A122 interval boundary

**§7.3 (PDF pp.21–22).** The authors ask whether any `(j,k)` admits a full-support invariant law on `[0,1]`; they suspect no. They also ask whether a non-omnipresent law can arise as a limit from a full-support initial law. They explicitly say the answer to the second would imply the first. Theorem 9 on p.22 restates W04S025 Theorem 4 for `k=2` and adds a density-only nonexistence condition for some parameters. This does not exclude singular full-support measures for general `(j,k)`. The paper itself notes a uniform four-point invariant law for `π_{3,4}` on a four-point subset of `[0,1]`, so broader fixed-point claims need their exact support qualification.

## Use boundary

Useful as the primary source of the A121 conjecture and the A122 question wording, but not as proof evidence for either conjecture. For W04 it is at most a cross-tool lead: the subject is nonlinear dynamics of probability measures, not Ising heat-bath spectral gaps, GKS/FKG, or generic Glauber mixing.
