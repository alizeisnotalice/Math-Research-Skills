## Comparison statements and proof mechanism

Throughout §§1–2 the chains are finite, irreducible and reversible on a common finite state space. The paper uses the Dirichlet form E_P(f,f)=1/2 Σ_x,y π(x)P(x,y)(f(x)−f(y))² and the plus form for I+P, which controls eigenvalues near −1. The minimax characterization in §2A (PDF p. 4) turns form comparisons into eigenvalue comparisons; when stationary inner products differ, the source's stationary-measure domination hypothesis is also needed.

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 4–5; printed pp. 698–699).** For each directed transition (x,y) of a comparison chain P′, choose a path γ_xy using transitions of P. Its congestion is
A=max_(z,w):P(z,w)>0 [π(z)P(z,w)]⁻¹ Σ_{(x,y):(z,w)∈γ_xy} |γ_xy|π′(x)P′(x,y).
Then E_{P′}≤A E_P. The proof telescopes each endpoint difference into edge increments, applies Cauchy–Schwarz with path length, then reorders the sum by comparison-edge load on each P edge. Theorem 2.2 is the companion plus-form comparison: odd-length paths and the corresponding multiplicities are needed to control the bottom of the spectrum; the Dirichlet-form bound alone does not control negative eigenvalues.

Section 2C's Theorem 2.3 (PDF pp. 10–12) lets a flow split each comparison transition over multiple paths. The same path-length-weighted load is averaged over that flow. The proof repeats the telescoping/Cauchy–Schwarz argument. A flow is useful when a deterministic routing is unbalanced, but its maximum congestion still must be explicitly bounded.

## Exclusion-process applications

For a connected base graph G₀=(X₀,E₀), |X₀|=n, and 1≤r<n, §3 defines a chain on r-subsets. It chooses a particle proportional to its base-graph degree, chooses one of its neighbors uniformly, and moves it if empty, otherwise holding. Its stationary weight is proportional to Σ_{a∈A}d(a). The quantity d_r in (3.5) is the maximum mean degree over r-sets. Δ₀ and Δ₀* in (3.1) are base-graph path congestion quantities, with the starred version used for odd-path/plus-form comparison.

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 13–15; printed pp. 707–709).** The eigenvalues β_i(r) satisfy
β_i(r)≤1−k_i(n−k_i+1)/(r d_r Δ₀),
where k_i=j when C(n,j−1)≤i<C(n,j), 0≤j≤min(r,n−r). In particular β₁(r)≤1−n/(r d_r Δ₀), and β_min(r)≥−1+(n−r−1)/(d_r Δ₀*). These displays were checked visually on PDF p. 14. The proof compares with Bernoulli–Laplace diffusion, whose spectrum is explicit, and routes each comparison exchange through a carefully chosen base-graph path, resolving occupied sites by legal moves. The positive-form and odd-path/plus-form comparisons then give the two sides of the spectrum. For a segment with loops at the endpoints, Remark 2 gives β₁(r)≤1−4/(r n²).

**Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 23–24; printed pp. 717–718).** If β₁⁰ is the second eigenvalue of the one-particle walk and d₀,d₀′ are the maximum and minimum base-graph degrees, then
β₁(r)≥1−d₀(1−β₁⁰)/(d₀′r).
The proof lifts a one-particle eigenfunction φ₀ to φ(A)=Σ_{x∈A}φ₀(x), applies the variational characterization, counts transitions along each base edge, and uses the finite-population variance formula for sampling without replacement. The source says the degree comparison is crude for irregular graphs; it notes a refinement using minimum mean degree, but d_r from the upper-bound theorem cannot simply be substituted in this proof.

Sections 5–6 apply the bounds to cycles, cubes, grids, transposition walks, stars, complete bipartite graphs and related examples; each example's graph, stationary law and routing constants must be retained. The final section gives spectral-to-total-variation bounds and open questions, noting that gap-only TV estimates can be far from sharp.

## Scope and transfer limits

- The comparison theorems need finite reversible chains on a common state space, explicit transition support and proved path/flow congestion. Stationary-measure conditions matter when converting form inequalities into eigenvalue comparisons.
- Negative-spectrum conclusions require the odd-path/plus-form result.
- Exclusion results use the specific degree-proportional particle-selection rule in (3.2); they do not immediately transfer to other rules or nonreversible chains.
- Routing quality and graph geometry are part of the input; simple routings and degree substitutions can be loose.
