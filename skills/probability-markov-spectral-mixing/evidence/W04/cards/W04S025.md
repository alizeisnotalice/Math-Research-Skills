## Relevant definitions and exact result

Given a compact metric space `(S,d)`, a probability law `θ`, and `1<=j<=k`, the kernel `K_{θ,j,k}` at `s` draws `k` iid `θ` points and moves to the `j`th nearest, breaking equal-distance ties uniformly. The mapping `π_{j,k}(θ)` is the unique stationary law of this inner chain.

**Theorem 1 (PDF p.5; proof pp.5–6).** For every compact metric `S`, `θ`, and `j,k`, the chain has a unique stationary law and, from any starting point, its variation distance `D(t)` satisfies

`D(2t) <= (1 - 1/k^(k-1))^t` for integer `t>=1`.

It also gives `θ(A)^k <= π_{j,k}(θ)(A) <= k θ(A)` for measurable `A`, so `π` and `θ` have the same null sets/support. Proof: a common-sample coupling uses the `k` nearest-choice cells induced by one sample batch; an independent batch falls cellwise into matching cells with probability at least `Σ_i θ(B_i)^k >= k^{-(k-1)}`. This gives geometric coupling and TV convergence. The measure bounds follow from “all k samples in A” and “at least one sample in A.”

## A122's proven subcase

**Theorem 4 (PDF p.10; proof pp.10–12).** On `[0,1]`, the only `π_{2,2}`-invariant distributions are point masses `δ_s` and equal two-point laws `δ_{s_1,s_2}`. Theorem 3 (PDF pp.8–9) shows the invariant distributions for `π_{1,2}` and `π_{2,2}` coincide. For Theorem 4, the authors reduce by scaling to a law whose support contains 0 and 1. Lemma 5 derives the distribution-function inequality `F(x)<=F(x)^2+2F(x)P(Y<=(x+Y_2)/2)` and, after division by `F(x)` and `x↓0`, forces `P(Y<=Y_2/2)>=1/2`; symmetry then makes the event `Y/2<Y_2<2Y` null, contradicted by conditioning on an interval `[y,3y/2]`. Lemma 6 uses the endpoint masses `θ_0,θ_1`, inequality (17), and the equality conditions in (18)–(19) to force support `{0,1}` and weights `1/2,1/2`.

This directly resolves only the `k=2` slice of A122. It does not address all `j,k`.

## Source-level direction mismatch and proof repair

The printed proof of Lemma 5 (PDF p.10) has an internal direction mismatch. It defines `X` to be the farther of `Y_1,Y_2`, so on the cross event `Y_1≤x<Y_2`, the condition for `X≤x` is `Y≥(Y_1+Y_2)/2`; this is the source's printed `A_2`. But the next claimed inclusion uses `Y≤(x+Y_2)/2`, which does not follow from `A_2` as printed (the exact rational counterpoint `x=1/5, Y_1=1/10, Y_2=9/10, Y=4/5` is recorded in `evidence/shared/reviews/R02/batch5-S025-direction-check.json`). This is a proof-direction typo, not evidence that Theorem 4 is false.

A valid repair follows from the paper's already-proved Theorem 3 (PDF pp.8–9): every `π_{2,2}`-invariant law is also `π_{1,2}`-invariant. Transfer the same `θ` to the nearest-sample kernel and redefine `X` as the nearer of `Y_1,Y_2`. Then for `Y_1≤x<Y_2`, the cross event contributing to `{X≤x}` is `Y≤(Y_1+Y_2)/2`, and hence

`A_2 ⊆ {Y_1≤x, Y≤(x+Y_2)/2}`,

because `Y_1≤x`. The symmetric event gives the same bound. Thus independence yields exactly the stated inequality

`F(x)≤F(x)^2+2F(x)P(Y≤(x+Y_2)/2)`.

Dividing by `F(x)>0` and taking `x↓0` gives `P(Y≤Y_2/2)≥1/2`; symmetry gives `P(Y_2≤Y/2)≥1/2`. Since `θ({0})=0`, these disjoint events force `P(Y/2<Y_2<2Y)=0`, contradicted by conditioning iid samples on an interval `[y,3y/2]`. This establishes Lemma 5 using the nearest-kernel reformulation. The repair is explicitly separate from the source's printed farthest-sample argument; do not silently attribute the corrected direction to the original proof.

## Mapping boundary

This paper is a rigorous source for the **inner order-statistic Markov chain** and its fixed-parameter mixing, and a one-parameter partial answer to A122. It is not a spectral-gap comparison theorem and does not prove convergence of iterates of `θ -> π_{j,k}(θ)`. In particular, the TV coupling estimate holds with `θ` fixed; it supplies no contraction or almost-everywhere convergence statement for the nonlinear iteration. See `reading_coverage.json#W04S025`.
