# Source card — Kargin (2011), “Relaxation Time Is Monotone in Temperature in the Mean-Field Ising Model”

**Reading:** complete six-page arXiv v1; title, author, version and journal record checked against arXiv and the DOI record. arXiv lists v1 only (posted 2011-03-02); journal reference: *Statistics & Probability Letters* 81(8), 1094–1097 (2011), DOI 10.1016/j.spl.2011.03.001.

**Model and theorem as printed (PDF pp.1–3).** Random-site discrete-time heat-bath dynamics on the complete graph with a common pair coupling `J` and common field `H`. Theorem 1 (p.3) states that the second eigenvalue `λ₂` is nondecreasing in `J`, hence relaxation time is nondecreasing along this one-parameter homogeneous family. This theorem statement is broader in `H` than the proof step audited below.

**Audited proof chain (pp.3–6).** Lemma 3 (p.3) gives the simple-eigenvalue derivative formula using a normalized reversible eigenvector. Permutation symmetry reduces the relevant eigenproblem to the magnetization birth–death chain (p.4); Lemma 5 (p.5) identifies the second eigenvalue with that reduced chain and its eigenvector, and Lemma 6 gives uniqueness of an increasing eigenvector up to scaling. The derivative matrix is tridiagonal (p.6). The sign argument uses monotonicity of the eigenvector and a sign pattern around the midpoint.

**Proof boundary at nonzero field.** The symmetry step on PDF p.5 asserts that `g_k=−f_{n−k}` is another increasing eigenvector, and then infers `f_k=−f_{n−k}` and the midpoint sign pattern. Reflection `k↦n−k` is not a symmetry of the magnetization transition matrix when `H≠0`. The finite check recorded by R02 also confirms that this identity fails for `n=3,J=0.4,H=0.7` (discrepancy `0.4029118514`). At `H=0`, the reflection step is valid, and the remaining p.5–6 derivative argument establishes the homogeneous zero-field case.

**Evidence-pack conclusion.** We use Kargin only for the audited homogeneous complete-graph, zero-field (`H=0`) case. We record that the source as written states a uniform-field theorem, but the present proof audit does not certify its `H≠0` case. This is a proof-scope limitation, not a counterexample to the theorem. No corrected version or independent proof for `H≠0` was located in the checked version record. Even at `H=0`, this varies the common coupling, not one selected edge on an arbitrary graph.
