# Source card — Holley (1974), “Remarks on the FKG Inequalities”

**Reading:** complete six-page paper; proof pp.1–5.

**Theorem 6 (PDF pp.3–4).** For strictly positive measures `μ₁,μ₂` on a finite distributive lattice, the cross-condition
`μ₁(x∨y) μ₂(x∧y) ≥ μ₁(x) μ₂(y)` for all `x,y`
implies a coupling `(X₁,X₂)` with `X₁≥X₂` almost surely; hence expectations of every increasing function are ordered. The proof embeds the lattice in a finite subset lattice, uses unit-step paths and a coupled single-coordinate generator whose increase/decrease rates are chosen from the two measures, and shows the cross-condition preserves the order relation. Finite-state ergodicity and the long-time limit give the desired coupling.

**Corollaries 11–12 (PDF pp.4–5).** Corollary 11 states the increasing-function comparison. Corollary 12 obtains FKG by tilting a log-supermodular measure by an increasing function and applying the comparison with the original measure; the resulting covariance is nonnegative.

**Boundary.** Holley compares stationary measures under an explicit two-measure condition. It does not compare transition kernels, update schedules, eigenvalues or relaxation times. The order direction and cross-condition must be stated whenever it is used.
