## Form and constants

The symmetric form is `D(f,f)=½∫J(dx,dy)(f(x)−f(y))²+∫K(dx)f(x)²`, with symmetric σ-finite jump measure `J`, killing measure `K`, and the stated regularity/domain assumptions in the paper. `λ₀=inf_{||f||₂=1}D(f,f)`; when `K=0`, `λ₁=inf_{π(f)=0,||f||₂=1}D(f,f)`. The isoperimetric constants `h`, `k`, and `k′` use the paper’s different denominators: respectively one-sided mass (including killing), `π(A)π(Aᶜ)`, and `min{π(A),π(Aᶜ)}`. They are not interchangeable without the source’s stated restrictions.

For nonnegative symmetric scale `r(x,y)` and `s(x)`, define `J^(α)=1_{r>0}J/r^α` and `K^(α)=1_{s>0}K/s^α`, `α∈{0,1/2,1}`. The general-form theorem assumes the operator norm condition `||J^(1)(·,E)+K^(1)||_{L¹_+(π)→R_+}≤1`; unbounded rates are not covered without this scaling/domain condition.

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p.3).**

`λ₀ ≥ h^(1/2)²/(2−λ₀^(1)) ≥ h^(1/2)²/[1+√(1−h^(1)²)]`.

**Theorem 1.2 (PDF p.3, with `K=0`).**

`λ₁ ≥ [k^(1/2)/(√2+√(2−λ₁^(1)))]²`, and `λ₁ ≥ k′^(1/2)²/[1+√(1−k′^(1)²)]`.

If `||J(·,E)+K||≤M`, choosing the scale `r=s=M` yields `λ₀≥M(1−√(1−h²/M²))`; with `K=0`, `λ₁≥M(1−√(1−k′²/M²))`. The source compares these to the cruder `h²/(2M)` and `k′²/(2M)` bounds. Do not drop the boundedness/scaling assumption in applying the sharper expressions.

## Proof mechanisms and applications

Theorem 1.1 adds a cemetery state to encode killing, applies Cauchy–Schwarz to `f²(y)−f²(x)`, and relates the energy of `f²` to the scaled form; a square-root estimate for `λ₀^(1)` gives the second displayed bound. Theorem 1.2 shifts a centered test function by a constant and optimizes the shift, then uses a median split and the level-set crossing estimate to turn the isoperimetric profile into a centered Poincaré bound. Theorem 1.3 proves a local-to-global criterion using a region `B` with `π(B)>1/2`, a local Cheeger bound, and a Lyapunov-type drift condition `−sup_{Bᶜ}Ωφ>0`, together with bounded jump oscillation and finite local mass. Theorem 1.4 combines local Neumann/Dirichlet spectral bounds with a cutoff and optimizes the interface parameter. Theorem 1.5 obtains a complementary upper bound via an exponential test/Lyapunov function and gradient control.

Section 4 applies these criteria to Markov chains and birth–death examples. For `a_i=b_i=i^γ`, the paper finds a positive spectral gap iff `γ≥2`; it notes the basic `k>0` criterion can fail to detect a gap and uses the refined `k^(1/2)` criterion. This is a general symmetric-form paper, not an assertion that every graph Ising model satisfies a uniform gap bound. Every application must verify form domain, symmetry, scaling, killing, and which Cheeger denominator is being used.
