## Dirichlet-form comparisons

For finite chains `M,M′` on the same state space with stationary laws `π,π′`, write `E_M(f,f)=½∑_{x,y}π(x)P(x,y)(f(x)−f(y))²` and `F_M(f,f)=½∑πP(f(x)+f(y))²`. A flow routes each directed transition demand `π′(x)P′(x,y)` over paths, with an edge used at most twice per route; edge congestion includes path length and the amount of routed flow divided by `π(z)P(z,w)`.

**Theorem 1 (PDF p.5; printed p.93).** Any such flow gives `E_{M′}(f,f)≤A(f)E_M(f,f)`. The telescoping path sum and Cauchy–Schwarz bound each target difference by route length times the sum of squared source-edge increments, then summing target demand yields congestion `A(f)`.

**Theorem 2 (PDF p.5; printed p.93).** If the comparison flow uses odd-length paths, then `F_{M′}(f,f)≤A(f)F_M(f,f)`. This controls the upper side of the negative spectrum as well as the Poincaré form. Ordinary paths alone do not imply it. Theorem 1 assumes both chains are ergodic on the same state space; Theorem 2 has the same chain assumptions plus an odd flow.

For reversible finite ergodic `M`, if eigenvalues are `1=β₀>…>β_{N−1}>−1`, then `λ₁=1−β₁`, `λ_{N−1}=1+β_{N−1}`, and `β_max=max(β₁,|β_{N−1}|)`. Theorem 5 (PDF p.6; printed p.94) gives the lower bound `τ(M,ε)≥[β_max/(1−β_max)]log(1/(2ε))`; Theorem 7 (PDF p.8; printed p.96) gives `τ_x(M,ε)≤[1/(1−β_max)]log(1/(επ(x)))`. Theorem 8 (PDF p.8; printed p.96) combines them under reversible ergodic chains with the same stationary law and an odd flow to obtain the displayed comparison upper bound `τ_x(M,ε)≤A(f)[τ(M′,δ)/log(1/(2δ))+1]log(1/(επ(x)))`, `0<δ<1/2` (specialized in the paper at `δ=1/(2e)`). Theorem 10 (PDF p.10; printed p.98) allows an ordinary flow only under the additional condition `β_max(M)=β₁(M)`, which removes a negative-eigenvalue obstruction.

## Periodicity example and nonreversible scope

Example 11 exhibits a two-state chain switching with probability `1−δ` and holding with probability `δ`: its mixing time grows like `⌊1/(2δ)⌋`, while a comparison reset chain mixes in one step and ordinary flow congestion stays small. This is why an ordinary-flow comparison cannot control near-periodic behavior through `F_M`; odd flows, a lazy chain, or an independent lower-endpoint spectral bound are required. Observation 14 notes lazy dynamics `(I+P)/2` introduces the corresponding factor-two normalization.

The nonreversible section (PDF pp.12–21) distinguishes the Poincaré constant `λ₁` from discrete-time mixing. Theorem 17 (PDF p.13; printed p.101) lower-bounds conductance using mixing time; Theorem 19 (PDF p.16; printed p.104) gives `λ₁≥Φ²/8` through additive reversiblization `P̂=(P+P*)/2`, which preserves the Dirichlet form and conductance. This does **not** imply a discrete-time upper mixing bound for the original nonreversible `P` from `λ₁` alone; the directed 3-cycle illustrates the failure. Their continuous-time upper bound, Theorem 22 (PDF p.18; printed p.106), and the discrete-time `R(M)M` reversible-gap bound, Theorem 23 (PDF p.20; printed p.108), have additional structure and clock conventions. Lemma 27 (PDF p.22; printed p.110) constructs a flow with `A(f′)≤8κ(f)B(f)` from the state congestion and edge overlap parameters it defines; its hypotheses and routing are part of that lemma.

Use the survey to select comparison tools, then verify every stationary measure, route parity, congestion definition, reversibility assumption, and clock on the target chain. The paper supplies no per-edge Ising gap monotonicity result.
