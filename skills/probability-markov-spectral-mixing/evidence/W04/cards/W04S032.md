## Main finite-spin weak-dependence theorem

For a finite product spin system `μ` on `Y=X^I`, §4 defines an interdependence matrix `J` by `J_ii=0` and

`d_TV( μ(·|x_{−i}), μ(·|y_{−i}) ) ≤ J_ij` when `x` and `y` differ only at site `j`.

For hard constraints the theorem also requires a uniform lower bound on every supported one-site marginal after every partial conditioning: `β̃(μ)≥α₁`. For full-support measures this reduces to `I(μ)=min_i min_y μ(y_i|y_{−i})`; Corollary 2.3 is stated on physical p.5 and its proof is on physical p.16. If `‖J‖_{2→2}≤1−α₂`, Theorem 4.1 (physical p. 11) gives entropy tensorization, an mLSI with constant

`ρ₀ = 2|I| α₁^(−1) α₂^(−2)`,

and an ordinary LSI for the coordinate-local-variance difference operator with

`σ² = log(α₁^(−1)) / (log(2) α₁ α₂²)`.

The entropy proof is: weak dependence yields conditional-entropy tensorization (4.3); applying `Ent_ν(e^f)≤Cov_ν(f,e^f)` to each conditional term and identifying the Glauber Dirichlet form gives the mLSI. For the LSI, a finite one-coordinate lower-probability bound controls conditional entropy by conditional variance, then tensorization sums the coordinate variances. The source attributes the first tensorization step to Marton's result and Götze–Sambale–Sinulis; those external proofs are dependencies, not re-proved here.

Theorem 2.2 (physical p. 5) applies the same conditions to the random-site Glauber kernel. For a sequence with constants `α₁,α₂` uniform in system size, it obtains entropy decay `Ent_μ(P_t f)≤Ent_μ(f) exp(−α₁α₂² t/(2|I|))` and `t_mix=O(|I| log |I|)`. The bound on the least state mass follows by multiplying the conditional-probability lower bound over sites. The article's kernel is `P` with generator `P−I` (one uniformly selected site per discrete update / total-update-rate-one Poisson clock); rescaling to rate one per site multiplies the continuous-time gap by `|I|`.

## Model-specific hypotheses and proof chain

Theorem 2.1 (physical pp. 4–5) gives sufficient conditions for four model sequences: ERGM when `½ Φ′_|β|(1)<1`; vertex-weighted ERGM when `sup_{x∈(0,1)}|φ′_β(x)|<1`; random proper colorings when maximum degree is uniformly bounded and `k≥2Δ+1`; and hard-core measures when maximum degree is bounded and fugacity `λ<1/(Δ−1)`. These are sufficient regimes, not sharp thresholds.

Propositions 4.2–4.5 (physical pp. 12–16) verify the criteria. For the ERGM, one-site conditional probabilities are bounded below because the energy change is `O(1)`, and the influence row sum is at most `½Φ′_|β|(1)`. For colorings, `J_vw=(Δ+1)^(−1)1_{v∼w}` gives `‖J‖≤Δ/(Δ+1)`; the lower conditional-marginal bound uses the Jerrum coloring-count ratio. For hard-core, `J_vw=λ/(1+λ)1_{v∼w}` so `‖J‖≤Δλ/(1+λ)<1`; the proof separately bounds the conditionals, first with `λ/2^(Δ+1)≤μ(σ_v=1)≤λ/(1+λ)` and then for arbitrary partial pinning by reduction to a smaller graph (physical pp. 15–16).

For an Ising Gibbs law, this is only a conditional auxiliary route: one must construct a valid influence matrix from the actual heat-bath conditionals, prove its operator norm is strictly below one, and prove the required lower conditional-probability bound uniformly over the graph sequence. This article does not state a blanket arbitrary-graph Ising theorem, and it does not prove edgewise monotonicity of the Ising spectral gap.

## Concentration results and transfer limits

Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 4.1 give an LSI under the same weak-dependence conditions. Theorem 3.6 (physical p. 10) converts bounded higher-order difference norms into multilevel tails; Theorem 3.7 gives `L^p` bounds for centered multilinear-type polynomials, with a zero-diagonal tensor. Sections 3.1–3.2 apply these tools to triangle counts in ERGMs and subgraph counts in Erdős–Rényi graphs. These concentration applications are auxiliary to W04's spectral-gap question.

Use this source only after checking both the influence norm and the all-partial-conditionals lower bound, the model and boundary conditions, and the update clock. Theorems 2.1–2.2 give mixing and entropy estimates for a fixed family of chains; they do not compare the gaps at two different edge-coupling vectors. FKG/GKS equilibrium inequalities likewise do not follow from this LSI result and are not proved in the paper.
