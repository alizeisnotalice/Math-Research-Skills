**Model and uniqueness parameter.** The main setting is an antiferromagnetic two-spin system on an n-vertex graph of maximum degree Δ≥3, with edge parameters 0≤β≤γ, γ>0, external field/fugacity λ>0 and βγ<1. The Gibbs weight is the two-spin edge-activity model from §3.2. For integer d≥1 the tree recursion is F_d(x)=λ((βx+1)/(x+γ))^d; its unique positive fixed point is x̂_d. The parameters are d-unique with gap δ if |F′_d(x̂_d)|≤1−δ, and up-to-Δ unique if this holds for every integer 1≤d<Δ. This is a tree uniqueness criterion used to control arbitrary finite graphs; the graph itself need not be a tree or have a girth lower bound.

**Main conclusions.** Theorem 1.1 (physical p. 4) states that every such antiferromagnetic two-spin model that is up-to-Δ unique with gap δ has Glauber spectral gap at least 1/(C(δ)n), with C(δ) depending only on δ and not on Δ or the model parameters; the paper gives C(δ)=(1/δ)^{O(1/δ)}. Theorem 1.3 (p. 4) specializes to hard-core fugacity λ≤(1−δ)λ_c(Δ), λ_c(Δ)=(Δ−1)^(Δ−1)/(Δ−2)^Δ: gap ≥1/(C(δ)n) and t_mix(ε)≤C(δ)n(n log Δ+log(1/ε)). Theorem 1.4 (p. 4) treats the Ising model with edge activity β in [(Δ−2+δ)/(Δ−δ),(Δ−δ)/(Δ−2+δ)] and external field λ>0, giving the same gap order and t_mix(ε)≤C(δ)n(n+log(1/ε)); here C(δ)=exp(O(1/δ)). Keep the model-specific parameterization and the closed interval exactly as stated.

**Spectral-independence boosting.** Definition 1.5 bounds the spectral radius of the absolute influence matrix under every feasible pinning; Definition 1.8 requires this bound to persist under every positive local-field reweighting μ(φ), φ∈(0,1]^V. Theorem 1.9 (physical p. 6) states: if μ is completely η-spectrally independent, then for every θ∈(0,1),

    λ_gap^GD(μ) ≥ (θ/2)^(2η+7) λ_min-gap^GD(μ^(θ)).

The formula checks on physical PDF pp.6–7 and p.9 show that **Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 1.9 use the same factor** `(θ/2)^(2η+7)`; p.9's proof reaches that same power. Lemma 2.4 directly gives

`λ_gap^GD(μ) ≥ λ_gap^FD(μ,θ) · λ_min-gap^GD(μ^(θ))`.

Thus Theorem 1.9 follows by composing the Lemma 2.3 field-chain bound with Lemma 2.4. There is no separate `θ²/(2η+7)` bound in Lemma 2.3 and no stronger-fraction-versus-weaker-theorem distinction. The adaptive field dynamics selects each site into an update block with probability 1 for a − spin and θ for a + spin, then resamples the block from the θ-biased law conditional on its complement. Uniform-block spectral independence (Theorem 2.5) and the k-transformation limit (Lemmas 2.7–2.8) prove the field-chain bound.

For uniqueness-to-spectral-independence, §§8 and Appendix B use a potential function for the tree recursion. Theorem 8.8 (physical p. 41) says an (α,c)-potential with contraction and boundedness conditions yields spectral independence 2c/α for the local-field two-spin law. The proof passes root-to-vertex influence to a self-avoiding-walk tree and sums a geometric decay; Appendix B proves the SAW influence identity, the weighted tree influence bound, and the symmetrization lemma used for the uniqueness contraction. For the up-to-Δ regime, the paper obtains complete 144/δ-spectral independence in the favorable spin direction and then compares to an easier θ-biased model.

**Transfer limits.** These are spectral-gap and mixing results for the specified Glauber chain, not direct worst-case spatial-mixing bounds. Preserve complete (not merely unpinned) spectral independence for Theorem 1.9, the d<Δ quantifier in up-to-Δ uniqueness, the local-field positivity, and each theorem’s model/parameter restrictions. The field dynamics is an auxiliary chain in the proof; its rapid mixing alone is not the claimed original Glauber-chain conclusion.
