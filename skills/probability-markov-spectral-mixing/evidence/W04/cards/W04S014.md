## Theorems and clock conventions

For a finite spin system, `R` is a nonnegative dependency matrix whose entries upper-bound the source's conditional update influences; its orientation is fixed by the paper and must be checked before constructing it. Let `J_n` be the matrix of all ones and `‖·‖` any matrix norm.

**Lemma 1 (PDF p.2; proof p.18).** If `‖R‖≤μ<1`, random-update Glauber dynamics has the asymptotic mixing bound

`τ̂_r(ε) ≲ n(1−μ)⁻¹ log((1−μ)⁻¹ J_n/ε)`.

Time is in individual random-site updates.

**Lemma 2 (PDF p.3; proof p.20).** Under the same condition `‖R‖≤μ<1`, systematic-scan Glauber dynamics has

`τ̂_s(ε) ≲ (1−μ)⁻¹ log((1−μ)⁻¹ J_n/ε)`.

Here time is counted in complete scans; multiply by `n` to convert to individual site updates. Lemma 2 uses the norm of `R` itself.

**Lemma 22 (PDF p.18; proof pp.18–19).** For the scan matrix/product `R⃗=R₁R₂⋯R_n`, the separate sufficient condition `‖R⃗‖≤μ<1` gives systematic-scan mixing time at most `(1−μ)⁻¹ log(Cn/ε)`; for an operator norm the paper gives `(1−μ)⁻¹ log(J_n/ε)`. Lemmas 23 and Corollary 24 relate this product to `R`; Corollaries 25–27 then recover Dobrushin and weighted criteria. Keep the scan-product hypothesis attached to Lemma 22: it is not Lemma 2's hypothesis.

**Lemma 3 (PDF p.3; improved scan proof §§3.3, PDF pp.24–25).** If `R` is symmetric with zero diagonal and `λ=‖R‖₂=λ(R)<1`, the scan-count bound improves to

`τ̂_s(ε) ≲ (1−λ/2)(1−λ)⁻¹ log((1−λ)⁻¹ n/ε)`.

The symmetry and zero-diagonal assumptions are essential to this special estimate. These lemmas are sufficient mixing criteria, not necessary conditions.

## Proof mechanisms and scope

Section 3.1 (PDF pp.14–19) uses path coupling to contract weighted Hamming disagreements and perturbation to obtain positive weights. The scan-product result in Lemma 22 propagates coordinate disagreement through one full ordered scan, multiplying the coordinate-update matrices; Lemma 23 and Corollary 24 compare that product to `R`. Section 3.2 (pp.20–23) translates the criteria to coordinate oscillations and Dobrushin inequalities. Section 3.3 (pp.24–25) scales the upper triangle for symmetric zero-diagonal `R`, bounds the scan product by induction, and obtains the improved factor. Section 4 (pp.26–36) applies matrix criteria to colorings of sparse graph classes under the stated hereditary graph-class and density hypotheses.

Before applying a bound, check that every entry of `R` dominates the source's conditional total-variation influence, with the same update rule and orientation; verify the precise matrix norm condition and use the correct clock. A failed norm test gives no lower bound on mixing time. The coloring results and matrix criteria do not establish edgewise monotonicity of Ising spectral gaps.
