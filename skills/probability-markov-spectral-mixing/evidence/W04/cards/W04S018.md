## Model and main results

The source uses Curie–Weiss heat-bath dynamics with stationary weight proportional to exp[(β/n)Σ_{i<j}σ_iσ_j]. Censoring reflects a proposed negative-magnetization state to its spin-flipped configuration; the censored chain is supported on nonnegative magnetization and differs from the original chain.

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 4).** Let δ>0 satisfy δ²n→∞ arbitrarily slowly with n and set β=1+δ. The censored chain has cutoff point
t_n=(1/2+[2(ζ²β/δ−1)]⁻¹)(n/δ)log(δ²n),
with window order n/δ. For the all-plus initial state, the cutoff constant is only [2(ζ²β/δ−1)]⁻¹; order and window are unchanged. Here ζ is the unique positive solution tanh(βζ)=ζ. **Theorem 2** gives censored spectral-gap order δ/n under the same regime. Section 7 describes proof-estimate extensions to fixed positive δ; keep that separate from Theorem 1's printed near-critical condition.

## Proof map

Section 3.1 (PDF pp. 9–10) derives original and censored magnetization kernels; §3.2 (pp. 10–11) gives monotone coupling. The censored magnetization law is the reflected absolute-value version of the original scalar chain. This scalar monotonicity does not imply a full-configuration monotone coupling; the paper gives a counterexample.

Section 4 (PDF pp. 11–37) proves magnetization cutoff through four regions: zero to n⁻¹/⁴ (§4.1, pp. 12–20); n⁻¹/⁴ to √δ (§4.2, pp. 20–22); √δ to ζ (§4.3, pp. 22–29); and near one to ζ (§4.4, pp. 29–32). Drift and fluctuations are controlled with moment recurrences, variance bounds, FKG association, Taylor estimates for tanh and geometric threshold schedules. Lemmas 4.17–4.20 (pp. 34–37) concentrate near ζ and couple two magnetization chains. Section 4.5 (pp. 33–37) combines the phase estimates.

Section 5 (PDF pp. 38–43) lifts scalar cutoff to the full censored chain. After burn-in, two-coordinate-chain estimates control the initially positive and negative spin sets separately; the proof needs a joint coupling and concentration of spin averages, not only scalar magnetization distance.

Section 6 (PDF pp. 44–51) proves the spectral-gap theorem. The censored magnetization lower bound uses edge conductances and bottleneck ratio Φ*=Θ(δ/n); the upper bound uses a Dirichlet test based on S−ζ, fourth-moment control and a lower variance bound. For the full chain, functions constant on magnetization slices inherit the scalar gap; zero-slice-mean functions contract under within-slice Hamming coupling at rate 1−c/n, separating remaining modes at order 1/n.

Section 7 (PDF pp. 52–54) records fixed-δ modifications to intermediate thresholds, drift estimates and the two-coordinate good set when ζ is close to one. Section 8 (p. 54) gives conjectures for transitive graphs, not theorems. References are on pp. 54–55.

## Scope and transfer limits

- The result is for the complete graph and this specific reflection rule, not arbitrary censoring or geometries.
- Censored and original dynamics differ in stationary support and mixing behavior.
- Preserve the printed near-critical condition separately from §7's fixed-δ extension.
- Magnetization coupling alone does not prove full-chain mixing; the two-coordinate estimates are required.
- Section 8 statements are conjectures.
