# Source card — Jain–Pham–Vuong (2021 preprint; 2022 journal version)

**Reading:** complete 19-page arXiv v1, PDF pp.1–19; theorem and proof sections 2–3, including Appendix A, checked. The journal version is *Information Processing Letters* 177 (2022), article 106268, DOI 10.1016/j.ipl.2022.106268. The arXiv v1 title includes “coupling with the stationary distribution”; the final journal title is “Spectral independence, coupling, and the spectral gap of the Glauber dynamics.”

**Model and gap convention.** A q-spin Gibbs system with nonnegative symmetric interaction entries and positive fields; random-site discrete heat-bath Glauber dynamics. The theorem uses the absolute spectral gap `1−max(|λ₂|,|λ_min|)`.

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p.5).** If maximum degree is `Δ`, the system is `(C,η)` spectrally independent, and `50⌈2C⌉Δ≤n`, then the spectral gap is bounded below by
`c₁.₁ (1−η)^(1+2C) / (25Δ⌈2C⌉)^(5⌈2C⌉) · 1/n`,
with the paper's universal constant `c₁.₁` and parameter conventions. This is a sufficient lower bound; it is not parameter monotonicity.

**Proof chain (PDF §§2–3, pp.8–17; Appendix A, pp.14–17).** Definitions 2.2–2.4 introduce influence matrices and pin-conditioned spectral independence. The authors identify Glauber dynamics with an `n↔n−1` down-up walk and invoke Theorem 2.5 to pass from that walk's gap. Fact 3.2 equates approximate tensorization with a gap lower bound of order `1/(Cn)`. Proposition 3.3 derives ℓ-uniform block variance factorization from local variance contraction plus a martingale decomposition; Appendix A spells this out through martingale increments and the `α_i` recurrence. Corollary 3.4 gives conditional approximate tensorization. Fact 3.5 bounds component sizes of a random ℓ-subset, Fact 3.6 factors variance across product components, and Proposition 3.7 transfers block factorization to single-site factorization using the graph component tail. Combining these estimates proves Theorem 1.1.

**Applications and boundary.** The paper treats colorings, hard-core models and matchings (PDF pp.6–7, 13–14). Do not transfer these hypotheses automatically to a two-spin Ising edge derivative. The theorem provides a gap lower bound for each system meeting its assumptions, not an ordering between systems with different interactions.
