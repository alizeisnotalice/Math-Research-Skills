# Source card — Aldous (1982), “Some Inequalities for Reversible Markov Chains”

**Reading evidence.** Entire 13-page local PDF read (physical pp.1–13; printed pp.564–576). The source's opening model explicitly takes `X_t` to be an ergodic finite-state **continuous-time** Markov chain with transition-rate matrix `Q` and transition semigroup `P_t=(p_{ij}(t))`; all time parameters below are measured on that CT clock. The spectral representation uses decay factors `exp(−t/β_r)`. Formula-heavy physical p.3 (printed p.566) containing that representation and Proposition 8, plus pages for Theorems 5–6 and Lemma 38, were rendered and visually checked. The source is the 1982 *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* article, not merely a secondary citation.

## Definitions and theorem statements

The paper compares four time parameters for this finite irreducible continuous-time chain with stationary law `π` and rate matrix `Q`. Let `A_t=max_i ||P_t(i,·)−π||_TV` for its continuous-time transition semigroup and `τ₁=min{t:A_t≤(2e)⁻¹}`. Let `α_i` be the infimum of `E_i T` over stopping times `T` in this continuous-time filtration for which `X_T∼π`, and `τ₂=max_i α_i`. Define `τ₃=max_{i,k}∑_j π_j |E_i H_j−E_k H_j|`, and `τ₄=max_{i,A}π(A)E_i H_A`, where `H_j,H_A` are continuous-time hitting times and the remaining conventions are as in the paper. These are not discrete-time step counts.

**Theorem 5 (printed pp.565–566; PDF pp.2–3).** For a finite irreducible reversible continuous-time chain, all four `τ_r` are comparable up to universal numerical constants: there are absolute `C_rs` with `τ_r≤C_rs τ_s` for `r,s∈{1,2,3,4}`. The theorem is a comparison, not equality or an exact constant identity.

**Theorem 6 (printed pp.566–567; PDF pp.3–4).** For a general finite irreducible continuous-time chain (without reversibility), absolute constants `K₁,K₂,K₃` give `τ₂≤K₁τ₁(1+log(1/π_min))`, `τ₄≤τ₃≤K₂τ₂`, and `τ₂≤K₃τ₄(1+log(1/π_min))`. The `log(1/π_min)` losses are part of the stated general-chain result; do not promote Theorem 5’s constant-factor equivalence to nonreversible chains.

**Proposition 8 (printed p.566; PDF p.3).** For the source's finite-state reversible continuous-time chain, `β` is the relaxation time, the reciprocal of the smallest nonzero decay rate, so the slow spectral mode decays as `exp(−t/β)`. The proposition states

`β ≤ τ₁ ≤ β[1 + ½ log(1/π_min)]`.

Thus the relaxation time is a lower bound on the paper's worst-start TV threshold, with the stated minimum-stationary-mass logarithm in the upper bound. Do not read `β` as the inverse relaxation time.

## Proof chain and caveats

The argument first establishes submultiplicativity `A_{s+t}≤2A_sA_t` in continuous time, hence `A_t≤exp(1−t/τ₁)`. Reversibility and Cauchy–Schwarz turn an `L²(π)` decay estimate into a two-time kernel lower bound; an exact-stationarity stopping construction then gives `τ₂≤Cτ₁`. Strong-stationary-time and coupling estimates yield `τ₃≤Cτ₂` and `τ₄≤τ₃`. For the converse comparison, a canonical stopping-time construction is bounded using a quota/crossing argument to obtain `E T≤C τ₄(1−log π_min)`; the reversible spectral smoothing lemma (Lemma 38, printed pp.573–574; PDF pp.10–11) supplies `τ₁≤Cτ₄`. The paper’s constants are universal and not optimized. The general-chain route incurs logarithmic dependence on `π_min`.

Examples 45–50 (printed pp.574–575; PDF pp.11–12) illustrate which logarithmic factors cannot generally be removed and why hitting-time parameters alone do not imply a reversible-style mixing equivalence. The source's continuous-time generator/clock, stopping-time convention and finite irreducibility matter. A discrete-time chain needs a separately specified Poissonization or other clock conversion, including its periodicity implications; these inequalities do not apply to arbitrary discrete-time step counts as written. None is a claim about Ising interaction monotonicity.
