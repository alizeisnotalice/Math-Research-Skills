# Source card — Lubetzky & Sly (2012), “Critical Ising on the Square Lattice Mixes in Polynomial Time”

**Reading evidence.** Independent full-text read of physical PDF pp. 1–26 (complete manuscript). PDF SHA-256: `8a8fc37fe3059e350e8700ac7da0d587eec33b2820160e69f9fb448229ffb5ce`. Rendered and inspected pp. 2, 12, 20 and 22. Full section map: `reading_coverage.json#sources.W04S020`.

**Model and theorem.** Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2) concerns the zero-field ferromagnetic Ising model on any finite square-lattice box Λ⊂Z² of side length n, at β_c=½log(1+√2), with any fixed boundary condition τ. For the continuous-time single-site Glauber dynamics, the inverse spectral gap is at most n^C for an absolute C, independent of Λ and τ. The paper allows its stated finite-range, translation-invariant local update rates satisfying detailed balance and uniform positivity/boundedness; heat-bath and Metropolis are examples. Corollary 2.1 converts this to polynomial total-variation mixing. Theorem 3.1 extends the upper bound to rectangles in terms of their shorter side. This is a polynomial upper bound; the exponent is not optimized.

**Static and dynamic proof mechanism.** Theorem 3.2 (physical p. 12; rendered) is the key spatial-mixing estimate. For Λ=[1,r]×[1,r′], r′/r>α>0, and α≤ρ<r′/r, let the boundary conditions ξ,η differ only on the bottom side. Their induced spin laws on the upper subrectangle Λ_T=[1,r]×[ρr,r′] differ in total variation by at most exp(−δρ), with δ depending only on α. The proof combines overlapping-block dynamics with critical FK-Ising crossing estimates: at q=2 criticality, RSW bounds crossing probabilities uniformly under arbitrary boundary wiring; reveal the FK cluster from the differing boundary and use FKG plus the domain Markov property to couple the remaining configurations. The Edwards–Sokal coupling then makes spins agree outside the revealed cluster. Recursively comparing block gaps gives the polynomial gap bound.

Theorem 3.7 imports the needed RSW crossing estimate for critical FK-Ising; it is not proved from scratch here. The proof’s constants can be expressed through fully wired crossing probabilities (remark on p. 19). The FK parameter is p=1−e^(−2β_c)=√2/(1+√2). The authors use the q=2 random-cluster representation, FKG, and Domain Markov conditioning; each is part of the critical planar input, not a generic feature of Glauber chains.

**Lower bound and boundary variants.** Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 19–20) says that for every fixed boundary condition on an n×n square, gap^−1≥c n^(7/4), with absolute c>0; the same bound holds for rectangles whose shorter side has length n. The test function is magnetization in a central square. Its Dirichlet form is O(n²), while, with probability bounded below uniformly in the boundary, the central square is disconnected from the boundary in the dual FK picture; Onsager critical correlations then give variance Ω(n^(15/4)), producing the n^(7/4) ratio. Theorem 4.2 treats free and periodic boundary conditions by adapting the spatial-mixing/block proof. Section 4.3 transfers the result to the critical antiferromagnet on the bipartite square lattice by a checkerboard spin flip.

**Use and limits.** This is the critical 2D box result with arbitrary fixed boundary, not a theorem for higher-dimensional lattices or arbitrary planar graphs. The RSW/FK critical input is specific to two dimensions. The polynomial exponent C is unspecified in the main statement; it must not be replaced by the n^(7/4) lower bound, which is in the opposite direction and comes from Theorem 4.1.
