## Chapter 3 §§3.6.1–3.6.4: form, variational principle, and comparison

**§3.6.1, Dirichlet formalism (PDF pp.87–89).** For a reversible chain, the discrete-time form is `E(g,g)=½ Σ_i Σ_{j≠i} π_i p_ij (g(j)−g(i))²`; in continuous time replace `p_ij` by the off-diagonal rates `q_ij`. For a weighted graph this is the conductance form. Lemma 3.24 gives, for a finite continuous-time chain with arbitrary initial law `ρ(t)` and density `f_i(t)=ρ_i(t)/π_i`,

`d/dt ||ρ(t)−π||²_{2,π} = −2 E(f(t),f(t))`.

**Theorem 3.25 (PDF pp.89–91).** For finite reversible chains, with `π(g)=0`, the relaxation time has the Rayleigh–Ritz characterization `τ₂=sup ||g||²_{2,π}/E(g,g)`; equivalently the usual spectral gap is `inf E(g,g)/Var_π(g)`. The proof conjugates the reversible transition operator to a symmetric matrix and applies the second-eigenvalue variational formula. This is a relaxation-gap statement, not an absolute-gap or aperiodicity bound for discrete-time worst-case mixing.

**Lemma 3.26 (PDF pp.91–92).** In continuous time, `||ρ(t)−π||_{2,π} ≤ exp(−t/τ₂)||ρ(0)−π||_{2,π}`. The proof differentiates the squared norm using Lemma 3.24, applies the extremal characterization to `f−1`, and integrates the differential inequality. Corollaries 3.27–3.28 (PDF pp.92–93) illustrate variational comparison under state-space collapse and increased conductances.

**Lemma 3.29 (direct comparison; statement PDF p.93, proof concludes p.94).** For random walks on a common weighted graph, let `(w_e,w_i)` and `(w_e^*,w_i^*)` be edge and vertex weights and `τ₂,τ₂^*` the corresponding relaxation times. With loops excluded from the edge extrema, the source **prints**

`min_e(w_e/w_e^*) / max_i(w_i/w_i^*) ≤ τ₂/τ₂^* ≤ max_i(w_i/w_i^*) / min_e(w_e/w_e^*)`.

The displayed proof first establishes, for `g` of `π*`-mean zero and `π`-mean `b`,

`||g||²_{2,π*}/E*(g,g) ≤ (||g−b||²_{2,π}/E(g−b,g−b)) · [max_i(w_i*/w_i) / min_e(w_e*/w_e)]`.

Taking `g` to attain `τ₂*` gives `τ₂*≤τ₂[max_i(w_i*/w_i)/min_e(w_e*/w_e)]`. Rewriting this inequality gives the **proof-derived lower bound**

`min_i(w_i/w_i*) / max_e(w_e/w_e*) ≤ τ₂/τ₂*`.

This is not the source's printed lower bound above: the printed numerator/denominator use edge and vertex extrema in the opposite places. Reversing the roles of starred and unstarred weights gives the upper bound printed in the lemma. An exact two-state lazy-chain check demonstrates that the printed lower side is false as written: give each state loop weight `2`, and give the single connecting edge weight `1` in the first graph and `1/2` in the starred graph. Then the transition matrices have relaxation times `τ₂=3/2` and `τ₂*=5/2`, so `τ₂/τ₂*=3/5`; the printed lower expression is `5/3`, whereas the proof-derived lower expression and printed upper expression both equal `3/5`. The exact calculation is in `evidence/W04/numerical/s002_lemma329_lower_audit.json`. This is a formula-level source discrepancy, not a path-congestion lemma; use only the valid proof-derived lower side and the upper side under the stated weight comparison. The examples immediately following on p.94 explain why explicit ratios matter: joining two `n`-cliques by one edge gives `τ₂∼n²/2`, while an `n`-edge matching between them gives `τ₂*∼n/2`, despite comparable total edge weights (Example 3.30); attaching `k=o(n^{1/2})` leaves to a clique keeps `τ₂` bounded, while adding all pair edges among those new vertices gives `τ₂*∼k` as `k→∞` (Example 3.31). These examples motivate the following common-stationary-law comparison; they are not path constructions.

**Lemma 3.32 (direct comparison; PDF p.94).** For transition matrices `P,P*` with the same stationary distribution `π`, if `p_ij ≥ δ p*_ij` for every `j≠i` and `δ>0`, then `τ₂(P)≤δ⁻¹τ₂(P*)`. This conclusion follows directly from the Dirichlet forms: the common-`π` variance is unchanged, while the off-diagonal domination gives `E_P(f,f)≥δE_{P*}(f,f)`. The following source remark says the hypothesis can be rewritten `P=δP*+(1−δ)Q` with a possibly reducible reversible transition matrix `Q`. For `0<δ<1`, that mixture interpretation requires every entry of `Q` to be nonnegative, equivalent to full-entry domination `p_ij≥δp*_ij` for all `i,j`; off-diagonal domination alone does not ensure it. At `δ=1`, the decomposition formula is undefined; the endpoint mixture identity would separately require `P=P*`. For example, with `P` all entries `1/2`, `P*` having diagonal `9/10` and off-diagonal `1/10`, and `δ=4/5`, both chains are reversible with uniform `π` and the hypothesis holds, but the proposed `Q` has diagonal `−11/10` and off-diagonal `21/10`. The exact check is `evidence/W04/numerical/s002_lemma332_mixture_audit.json`. This caveat concerns the remark's mixture explanation, not the valid Dirichlet comparison in the lemma; neither is a routing/path theorem.

## Chapter 4 §4.4: relaxation, correlations, variance, and paths

**Definitions and Lemma 4.23 (PDF pp.131–133).** The book defines `τ₂=(1−λ₂)⁻¹` in discrete time and `τ₂=λ₂⁻¹` in continuous time. With `π_* = min_i π_i`, the continuous-time statement is

`τ₂ ≤ τ₁ ≤ τ₂ [1 + ½ log(1/π_*)]`.

The separate discrete-time bound concerns `τ₁^(2)` and includes the factor `4e/(e−1)` multiplying the same bracket. Keep the clock attached to these parameters; no periodic-chain conversion is supplied by the continuous-time formula.

**Lemmas 4.24–4.25 (PDF pp.132–133).** These compare `τ₂` to `τ₀` and the commute-time parameter `τ_*`; the latter uses the minimum stationary mass. Their proofs again use the variational characterization and are not Ising parameter comparisons.

**Lemma 4.28 and Proposition 4.29 (PDF pp.134–137).** For a stationary reversible chain, maximal correlation is `exp(−t/τ₂)` in continuous time and `β^t` in discrete time, where `β=max(λ₂,−λ_n)`. For a centered `g`, the stationary integrated process `S_t=∫₀ᵗg(X_s)ds` has `t⁻¹Var_π(S_t) ↑ σ² ≤ 2τ₂||g||²_{2,π}` and `A(t/τ₂)tσ² ≤ Var_π(S_t) ≤ tσ²`, with `A(u)=1−u⁻¹(1−e^{−u})`. The proof expands the covariance in the reversible spectrum and integrates the exponential terms. The proposition concerns a stationary trajectory; it does not say a nonstationary run is already at equilibrium.

**Algorithmic discussion and Corollary 4.31 (PDF pp.137–139).** The text warns that burn-in and averaging are distinct costs. Its estimate has order `τ₁+ε⁻²τ₂` steps, implemented by a continuized burn-in followed by a stationary-length average; this is a fixed-chain sampling bound.

**Theorem 4.32 (distinguished paths; PDF pp.139–141).** For discrete-time random walk on a weighted graph, choose a path `γ_xy` for every ordered pair of vertices, and let `r(γ)=Σ_{e∈γ}1/w_e`; the source's `w` is the total graph weight. Its two bounds are

`τ₂ ≤ w · max_e Σ_{x,y} π_xπ_y r(γ_xy) 1(e∈γ_xy)`,

`τ₂ ≤ w · max_e [w_e⁻¹ Σ_{x,y} π_xπ_y |γ_xy| 1(e∈γ_xy)]`.

The proof writes variance as `½Σ_{x,y}π_xπ_y(g(y)−g(x))²`, telescopes the increments along each chosen path, applies Cauchy–Schwarz with resistance/length weights, and invokes the variational characterization. The source notes that the argument extends to continuous time by setting `w_ij=π_iq_ij`; every use still needs an explicit path family and the source's graph/conductance convention. **Path routing appears here**, not in Lemma 3.29 or Lemma 3.32.

## Transfer limits

These sections supply fixed-chain variational, comparison, and mixing tools. Lemma 3.29 needs controlled edge/vertex weight ratios; Lemma 3.32 needs a common stationary law and entrywise transition domination; Theorem 4.32 needs an explicit path family and its congestion calculation. None alone gives a derivative sign or edgewise monotonicity for Ising spectral gaps. Preserve reversibility, conductances, stationary measures, and update-clock normalization when transferring a bound.
