**Model and exact inequality.** Let ν₀ on {±1}ⁿ have density proportional to exp(½⟨x,Jx⟩+⟨h,x⟩) relative to uniform measure, with J symmetric positive semidefinite and 0≼J≺Id. The external field h is arbitrary. The dynamics is the natural continuous-time heat-bath Glauber chain with each site updated at rate one, and Dirichlet form Eν₀(φ,φ)=Σᵢ Eν₀[(Eν₀[φ(X)|X_{~i}]−φ(X))²]. Theorem 1 (physical p. 4) proves

    (1−||J||op) Varν₀(φ) ≤ Eν₀(φ,φ)

for every real test function. Thus the spectral gap is at least 1−||J||op; the estimate is dimension-free and nonvacuous only when the stated operator-norm condition holds.

**Proof architecture.** The proof uses stochastic localization, not a direct Dobrushin estimate on the original model. It evolves a path ν_t=F_tν₀ with random quadratic interaction J_t and field q_t, defines the barycenter a_t and adjusted barycenter V_t, and selects a smooth PSD matrix C(V_t,H_{J_t}) supported on the positive eigenspace of J_t. Lemma 2 (p. 4) supplies Im(C)⊆H, trace at least dim(H)−1, Lipschitz dependence, and ||C(v,H)v||≤δ. The evolution dJ_t/dt=−C_t² and dF_t(x)=F_t(x)⟨C_t(x−a_t),dW_t⟩ drives the interaction toward rank one. The martingale variance is controlled by the δ-small vector condition; the Dirichlet form is a supermartingale. At the terminal rank-one law, Theorem 6 / the cited L² Dobrushin influence criterion closes the estimate. The proof then sends δ→0. Existence/uniqueness details for this process are in §2.0.1 (pp. 6–7); the rank-one estimate and supermartingale steps are §§2.1–2.2 (pp. 7–10).

Theorem 11 (physical p. 10) gives the continuous-time mixing estimate: TV distance is at most ε once

    t ≥ ((1+2||J||op)n + 2||h||₁ + log(1/ε))/(1−||J||op).

The minimum atom estimate used there is min_x ν₀(x)≥2^(−n)exp(−2n||J||op−2||h||₁). Discrete-time updates cost an additional Poissonization factor n, as specified below Theorem 11. Section 4’s Theorem 12 is a structural mixture decomposition stated with a proof sketch; it is not the proof of Theorem 1 and should not be upgraded to a fully established decomposition beyond what is written.

**Important Dirichlet-form boundary.** Appendix A (physical p. 13) constructs an SK example showing that the Glauber Dirichlet form above can differ from the usual squared discrete-gradient form by an exp(Θ(β√n)) factor under the paper’s scaling. The local transition rates matter: a bound for the wrong Dirichlet form cannot be transferred without a rate comparison. The theorem is for PSD quadratic interactions with ||J||op<1; it is not a result for arbitrary Ising coupling matrices on arbitrary graphs, nor does the random-matrix example alter those hypotheses.
