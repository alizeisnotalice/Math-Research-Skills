# Source card — Peres–Winkler (2013), “Can Extra Updates Delay Mixing?”

**Reading:** complete 12-page manuscript; PDF pp.1–12. Bibliographic record checked: *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 323(3), 1007–1016 (2013), DOI 10.1007/s00220-013-1776-0; arXiv:1112.0603v1 (2011).

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp.4, 7).** In a finite monotone spin system with totally ordered spins, full-support stationary law `π`, and monotone single-site conditional update kernels, start from the top configuration (or a law whose likelihood ratio `μ₀/π` is increasing). For a specified update schedule, deleting any subsequence of updates produces a law that stochastically dominates the fully updated law and is at least as far from `π` in total variation. The result extends to randomized schedules and subsequences.

**Proof chain.** Lemma 2.1 shows the update preserves an increasing likelihood ratio. Lemma 2.2 is the one-dimensional Chebyshev association inequality. Lemma 2.3 establishes that the starting law dominates its own update; Lemma 2.4 converts a stochastic-order comparison into the claimed TV comparison using an upper set. Theorem 2.5 iterates the one-step censoring argument; Theorem 1.1 follows by the schedule construction on p.7. The hypotheses are monotone conditional kernels and the extremal/increasing-likelihood-ratio start.

**Applications (PDF pp.8–10).** The paper compares systematic and alternating updates (factor at most 2), random and alternating updates (logarithmic in n), a hanging subgraph with one boundary vertex, and block versus single-site dynamics.

**Boundary.** This compares censored schedules for one fixed stationary model. It is used inside mixing arguments such as Ding–Peres, not as an interaction-parameter monotonicity or gap theorem.
