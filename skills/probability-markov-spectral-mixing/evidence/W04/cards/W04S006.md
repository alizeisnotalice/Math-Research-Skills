# Source card — Fortuin–Kasteleyn–Ginibre (1971)

**Reading:** full 15-page paper; Proposition 1 and proof pp.3–5, Ising application pp.10–11.

**Proposition 1 (PDF p.3; printed p.91).** For a finite distributive lattice `Γ`, a strictly positive measure satisfying `μ(x∧y)μ(x∨y)≥μ(x)μ(y)` has nonnegative covariance for any two increasing functions (and likewise for two decreasing functions). The proof first reduces a measure with restricted support to a sublattice and then proceeds by induction on lattice length. Choosing an atom partitions the lattice into the atom's upper interval, its complement lower portion, and their shared structure; the covariance is decomposed in (2.4)–(2.5). The induction hypotheses and the ratio comparison (2.9)–(2.12), including an increasing-function/decreasing-ratio covariance estimate, show each remaining contribution is nonnegative.

**Ising application (PDF pp.10–11).** For pairwise ferromagnetic Ising weights on `{−1,+1}^V`, the product order is a distributive lattice and nonnegative pair couplings give log-supermodularity. Arbitrary one-site fields multiply by a product of coordinate factors and cancel in the meet/join comparison. Therefore increasing spin observables are positively correlated for arbitrary fields in this pairwise model.

**Boundary.** FKG is an equilibrium association inequality. It is not a finite-time semigroup comparison, a GKS derivative identity for the gap, or a spectral-gap monotonicity theorem.
