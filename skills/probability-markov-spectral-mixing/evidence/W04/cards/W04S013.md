## Model and main statements

The state space is a feasible subset Ω⊆Q^V on an n-vertex graph of maximum degree at most Δ. Each discrete step selects a site uniformly and applies an ergodic, local, reversible update depending only on that site and its neighbors. Hard constraints are allowed. The source defines mixing time at TV threshold 1/(2e). Nonredundancy means each site has at least two spin values that occur in some feasible configuration; it rules out padding by inert sites.

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 4).** For fixed integer Δ≥2, every n-vertex graph of maximum degree at most Δ and every nonredundant Glauber dynamics in this class have discrete-time mixing time Ω(n log n), with implied constant depending only on Δ. The source names heat-bath Ising, hard-core and coloring chains among applications, subject to ergodicity and boundary-condition qualifications.

**Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 4).** For any integer-valued 2≤Δ(n)<n, there are graphs of maximum degree Δ(n) and associated dynamics with mixing time O(n log n/log Δ(n)); thus the bounded-degree hypothesis cannot be omitted.

An independent rate-one Poisson clock at every site gives the continuous-time chain. Proposition 2.1 and Corollary 2.2 (PDF pp. 9–10) compare clocks; in the source's convention τ_mix ≥ n τ_mix^C/6. Therefore continuous-time Ω(log n) yields discrete-time Ω(n log n).

## Proof ingredients and dependency chain

1. **Disagreement percolation, §3.1 (PDF pp. 10–12).** A shared-clock coupling shows a discrepancy reaches a distant set only along a time-ordered update chain. Lemma 3.1 bounds propagation over graph distance d by a term with scale (e t Δ/d)^d, with endpoint-set cardinality factors. Lemmas 3.3–3.4 give frozen-spin versions. The displayed inequalities were checked against rendered originals.
2. **Complete monotonicity, §3.2 (PDF pp. 12–14).** Lemma 3.6 says that if a finite continuous-time chain is reversible with stationary law π and starts from π conditioned on Ψ, then P(X_t∈Ψ) is a nonnegative mixture Σ_i α_i e^(−λ_i t). The reversible spectral expansion makes each coefficient a square. Lemma 3.5 applies this to a site's spin set Q_v: with μ=π(X(v)∈Q_v), and initial law conditioned on X₀(v)∈Q_v,
   P(X_t(v)∈Q_v) ≥ μ +(1−μ)e^(−t/(1−μ)).
   The proof conditions the exponential mixture on its nonstationary part and uses Jensen; the initial hazard is bounded by the rate-one clock at v.
3. **Main lower bound, §4 (PDF pp. 15–19).** Theorem 4.1 gives continuous-time Ω(log n) for fixed maximum degree. In the unconstrained case, the proof chooses separated centers, freezes a distant exterior and compares stationary-conditioned initial distributions with center spins respectively inside or outside specified proper spin sets. Disagreement percolation limits communication; complete monotonicity preserves a detectable bias, and a concentration/second-moment argument over centers yields TV separation. Lemma 4.2 extends this to hard constraints by constructing a frozen region and two feasible configurations that remain distinguishable for time T. Corollary 2.2 converts back to discrete time.

Section 5 (PDF pp. 19–23) gives boundary cases: a three-spin construction simulates reversible random walks and yields the degree/mixing tradeoff; nonuniform site selection needs each site's probability at most O(1/n) for the generalized lower bound; block updates require bounded block size, graph degree, local reversible rules and bounded continuous-time update rates. References are on pp. 23–24.

## Scope and transfer limits

- The lower bound needs bounded degree, locality, reversibility, ergodicity, nonredundancy and a sufficiently uniform update schedule; it is not a mixing upper bound.
- Hard constraints require the feasible-state and frozen-region argument in §4.2.
- The theorem does not imply Ω(n log n) for unbounded degree or arbitrary schedules; §5 constructs counterexamples.
- Preserve the source's fixed TV threshold and clock conversion when changing time conventions.
