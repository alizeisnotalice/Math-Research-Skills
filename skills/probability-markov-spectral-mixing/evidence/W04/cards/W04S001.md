## Chapter 15: Ising heat-bath chain and proof mechanisms

The chapter uses the zero-field ferromagnetic Ising law

`πβ(σ) ∝ exp(β Σ_{ {v,w}∈E } σ(v)σ(w))`, `σ∈{−1,+1}^V`,

and the **discrete-time random-site heat-bath clock**: choose one vertex uniformly from `V` each step and resample its spin from its conditional Gibbs law. Given neighboring-spin sum `S(σ,v)`, the probability of `+1` is `(1+tanh(βS(σ,v)))/2`. No external field or fixed-boundary version is asserted by these statements.

**Theorem 15.1 (PDF pp.231–233; print pp.215–217).** For a graph with `n` vertices and maximum degree `Δ`, put `c(β)=1−Δ tanh β`. If `Δ tanh β<1`, then `t_rel≤n/c(β)`, `d(t)≤n(1−c(β)/n)^t`, and `t_mix(ε)≤n(log n+log(1/ε))/c(β)`. If every vertex has even degree, the second part improves this with `c_e(β)=1−(Δ/2)tanh(2β)` under `(Δ/2)tanh(2β)<1`. The proof is path coupling in Hamming distance: couple the selected site and its uniform resampling variable; a one-site discrepancy disappears when that site is updated and can spread only to a neighbor, with probability controlled by the heat-bath influence. Lemma 15.3 proves the influence envelope `sup_x[tanh(β(x+1))−tanh(β(x−1))]=2 tanh β`; when all degrees are even the relevant neighbor sum is odd, giving the sharper `tanh(2β)` value and the improved contraction.

**Proposition 15.8 (PDF pp.237–238; print pp.221–222).** Let `G=(V,E)` have maximum degree `Δ`, and let `G̃=(V,Ẽ)` be obtained by deleting `r=|E\Ẽ|` edges, at the same inverse temperature and on the same vertex set. For the zero-field Ising random-site Glauber gaps `γ` on `G` and `γ̃` on `G̃`, the displayed comparison is

`1/γ ≤ exp(2β(Δ+2r))/γ̃`, equivalently `γ̃ ≤ exp(2β(Δ+2r)) γ`.

The proof compares Gibbs point masses after deleting the `r` interactions, compares one-site transition probabilities using the degree bound, and transfers the resulting Dirichlet-form inequality. It is an exponentially loose graph-deletion comparison, not an edgewise monotonicity theorem for arbitrary coupling vectors.

**Theorem 15.10 (PDF pp.239–240; print pp.223–224; source-level statement/proof discrepancy).** For the Ising block dynamics with blocks `{V_i}_{i=1}^b` covering `V`, `M=max_i |V_i|`, and `M_* = max_v |{i:v∈V_i}|`, the source's **printed statement** is

`γ_B ≤ M² M_* (4 exp(2βΔ(M+1))) γ`,

with `Δ` the maximum graph degree. The displayed proof uses the comparison theorem by routing each block move through at most `M` single-site flips. Equation (15.22) bounds the Gibbs-weight ratio for configurations differing on `r` sites by `exp(2βΔr)`; (15.23) bounds the block/single-site transition ratio; and (15.24) then counts at most `(2^M)^2=4^M` block-state pairs for each edge. The proof displays

`R ≤ 2(n/b) M exp(2βΔ(M+1)) M_* 4^M`,

and uses block coverage to infer `n/b≤M`, yielding from those displayed lines only

`R ≤ 2 M² M_* 4^M exp(2βΔ(M+1))`.

The final printed proof line instead asserts `R ≤ M² M_* (4 exp(2βΔ(M+1)))`, which does not follow algebraically from the preceding displayed bound for general `M`. This is a source-level proof-display discrepancy; it does not by itself refute the theorem. Keep the theorem's printed statement and the displayed congestion arithmetic separate.

### Independently derived alternate proof (accepted by R02; not the author's printed proof)

For a finite graph `G=(V,E)` with `n=|V|≥1`, maximum degree `Δ`, and the **homogeneous zero-field ferromagnetic Ising law**

`π_β(σ) ∝ exp(β Σ_{ {u,v}∈E } σ_uσ_v)`, `σ∈{−1,+1}^V`, `0≤β<∞`,

let the single-site chain choose a site uniformly from `V` and resample its spin from `π_β` conditional on the other spins. Let the block chain choose uniformly from `b≥1` blocks `B_1,…,B_b⊆V` whose union is `V`, and resample the whole block from that same conditional law. Compare their **discrete-time per-update** gaps `γ` and `γ_B` under this common stationary law and these exact clocks. Put `M=max_j|B_j|` and `M_* = max_v #{j:v∈B_j}`. Then the separately derived bound is

`γ_B ≤ M M_* exp(2βΔM) γ`.

For a block `B` of size `m`, condition on its outside spins and let `μ` be the conditional Ising law on `{−1,+1}^m`, `ν` uniform on that cube, `a=min_x μ(x)/ν(x)`, and `A=max_x μ(x)/ν(x)`. Finite `β` gives `a>0`. The variational formula for variance, evaluated at the `ν`-mean, gives `Var_μ f≤A Var_ν f`. For a cube edge `{x,y}` differing at site `i`, the conditional heat-bath Dirichlet coefficient under `μ` is `μ(x)μ(y)/(μ(x)+μ(y))`; since both masses are at least `a2^{-m}`, monotonicity of `uv/(u+v)` makes this coefficient at least `a` times the uniform-cube coefficient. Thus

`Var_μ f ≤ (A/a) Σ_{i∈B} E_μ Var_i(f | outside B)`.

The last step uses product-measure variance tensorization: in the uniform-cube Walsh expansion, `Var_ν f` sums squared coefficients on nonempty coordinate sets, while `Σ_i E_ν Var_i f` weights each by its size. Only edges touching `B` change its conditional log density; there are at most `Δm` such edges and each product changes by at most `2`, so `A/a≤exp(2βΔm)`. Averaging over outside spins and summing over blocks, with each site covered at most `M_*` times, yields

`E_block(f,f) ≤ (n/b) M_* exp(2βΔM) E_single(f,f) ≤ M M_* exp(2βΔM) E_single(f,f)`,

because `n≤bM`. Evaluate the block Rayleigh quotient at a minimizer of the single-site Rayleigh quotient to obtain the displayed gap inequality. Empty blocks contribute zero. Since `M≥1` and `β≥0`, this coefficient is no larger than the printed source coefficient `4M²M_*exp(2βΔ(M+1))`; this independent argument therefore proves the printed comparison under the listed hypotheses without repairing the author's displayed congestion arithmetic. The argument does **not** cover nonzero or unbounded fields, hard constraints, different stationary laws, nonuniform block/site clocks, or a continuous-time normalization change.

**Theorem 15.13 (PDF pp.242–244; print pp.226–228).** For the zero-field Ising model on an `n×n` square, `t_rel=(1−λ_*)^{-1} ≥ exp(ψ(β)n)` with `ψ(β)>0` whenever `β>(1/2)log α`, where `α` is the square-lattice connective constant. The proof uses a topological cut: configurations with plus crossings in both directions form `S_+`; its complement and the corresponding minus-crossing event force a fault line. For a fixed self-avoiding fault-line path of length `ℓ` with at most `k` defects, flipping one side changes the Gibbs weight by a factor yielding probability at most `exp(2β(2k−ℓ))`; counting such paths by `(n+1)α_ℓ` makes the fault-line event exponentially unlikely above the stated threshold. Every configuration in the one-flip boundary of `S_+` has a fault line with at most three defects, while `π(S_+)→1/2`; the conductance bound therefore gives the exponential relaxation-time lower bound. This is a low-temperature square-lattice result, not a general graph theorem.

It records Nacu’s cycle result and poses a stronger fixed-time distance question. This is an open-problem statement, not a theorem.
