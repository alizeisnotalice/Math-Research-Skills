# Source card — Lebowitz–Monroe (1972), “Inequalities for Higher Order Ising Spins and for Continuum Fluids”

**Reading:** complete 12-page paper; PDF pp.1–12, including the continuum section.

**Model and theorem (PDF pp.3–5).** Spins take values `p,p−2,…,−p` and the Hamiltonian includes pair coefficients `J(i,j)`, higher-order coefficients `γ(i,j)` and one-body terms (equation (2.1), p.3). Theorem 1 (pp.4–5) gives positive correlation of increasing observables in coordinatewise order under `J(i,j) ≥ (2p−2)² |γ(i,j)|`. The proof verifies the lattice condition for the Gibbs weight: one-body terms cancel from the meet/join comparison, while the coefficient bound controls the worst unordered pair of spin values; FKG Proposition 1 then gives association.

**GKS versus FKG.** The introduction and §2 distinguish GKS hypotheses (nonnegative interaction coefficients, with the stated positive-field variant) from the displayed sufficient FKG coefficient condition. The theorem allows negative higher-order `γ` when dominated by the pair coefficient, so the two inequalities are not interchangeable. Sections 3–4 give spin-one examples; the paper identifies a model for which neither quoted inequality applies. The continuum extension (pp.8–11) passes the lattice inequalities to a limit under its piecewise-continuity and stability assumptions.

**Boundary.** These are static correlation results for the stated multi-spin models, not dynamic gap results. For the classical pair Ising model use the simpler pairwise FKG condition; do not import a higher-spin or GKS claim outside its own coefficient and field assumptions.
