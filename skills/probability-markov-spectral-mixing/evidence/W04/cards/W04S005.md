# Source card — Shirai (2010), “A remark on monotonicity for the Glauber dynamics on finite graphs”

**Reading:** complete six-page paper. Model is zero-field ferromagnetic Ising with random-site discrete heat-bath updates.

**Reduction (PDF p.3).** On the path/cycle setting, the update action on the span of single-spin functions is represented by a killed nearest-neighbor kernel `Q`; random-site laziness gives `Q_lazy=(n−1)I/n+Q/n`. Starting all plus, `E[σ_a(t)]` is the survival probability of the associated killed walk from `a` through `t` steps. This makes a finite-time observable depend on all transition probabilities and their time composition.

**Theorem 1 (printed p.34; PDF p.3).** If a finite graph contains a path of length 3, there is a site, an edge, a time and a positive ferromagnetic coupling vector at which the all-plus fixed-time expectation decreases locally in that edge coupling. The proof sets couplings outside a four-vertex path to zero, then invokes joint continuity of the finite-chain expectation and its parameter derivative to transfer strict negativity to sufficiently small positive couplings on the remaining graph.

**Explicit path calculation (PDF pp.4–5).** For P3 with `x_i=2J_i`, take `(x₁,x₂,x₃)=(2s,s,s)`. At `t=3` the non-lazy survival derivative is `e^(−2s)/2+O(e^(−3s))`; expressing the lazy chain as a binomial mixture of non-lazy step counts yields Corollary 5's derivative `−e^(−2s)/128+O(e^(−3s))` for large s. The paper also gives the finite exact parameter example; W04's separate symbolic full-chain check at `(28,14,14)` gives derivative about `−5.400994688×10⁻¹⁵`.

**Boundary.** This is a counterexample to fixed-time expectation monotonicity, not to spectral-gap monotonicity. The derivative in this calculation belongs to a transient observable; it is not a gap derivative and does not contradict Nacu's cycle gap result.
