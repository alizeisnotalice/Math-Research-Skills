# Source card — Lubetzky & Sly (2013), “Cutoff for the Ising Model on the Lattice”

**Reading evidence.** Independent full-text read of physical PDF pp. 1–34 (complete article manuscript). PDF SHA-256: `2c72ec3ebe84c0e38eef3815ab6c50899d0b937934aab3ff6ee8cf56687782ac`. Rendered and inspected pp. 2, 10, 12 and 27. Section coverage is recorded at `reading_coverage.json#sources.W04S021`.

**Main results.** Time is continuous and every vertex updates at rate one; n is the torus side length (the state space has n^d spins). Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 2–3) proves that the zero-field ferromagnetic 2D Ising model on (Z/nZ)² at 0≤β<β_c has total-variation cutoff at λ_∞^−1 log n with window O(log log n). Here λ_∞ is the infinite-volume continuous-time Glauber spectral gap. Theorem 2 (p. 3) extends this to any dimension d≥1 and field h whenever the infinite-lattice Ising model has strong spatial mixing (SSM): cutoff occurs at (d/(2λ_∞))log n with O(log log n) window. Theorem 4 (p. 4) also gives |λ(n)−λ_∞|≤n^(−1/2+o(1)) under SSM. The theorem is for periodic tori; do not silently replace this with arbitrary finite-volume boundary conditions.

**SSM-to-cutoff proof chain.** Section 2 states the heat-bath / Metropolis-compatible Glauber framework, spectral gap and log-Sobolev conventions, and SSM: boundary perturbations affect a projected interior set by at most c₁ exp(−c₂ dist), uniformly over boundary conditions. Theorem 2.3 obtains a uniform log-Sobolev constant on periodic tori under SSM. Theorem 3.1 (physical pp. 11–12) reduces global L¹ distance to the L² distance of a projection on a box of side 2log³n inside a smaller torus of side 3log³n. Its upper implication bounds TV by a product-type expression in m_t² plus 3n^(−9d); its lower implication shows TV→1 when the number of separated boxes times m_t² diverges. Sections 3.1–3.3 implement this reduction: finite propagation controls dependence over short time; sparse sets decompose into small well-separated components; barrier dynamics and a log-Sobolev decay bound couple distant pieces; a product χ² estimate proves the upper direction, while independent blocks and a likelihood-ratio/CLT argument prove the lower direction.

Section 4 first derives cutoff existence from the L¹–L² reduction, then locates it using spectral gaps on smaller tori. Theorem 4.2 (physical p. 27; rendered) uses r≈3log³n and t*=d/(2λ(r))log n, with explicit lower/upper offsets of 15d/(α_s*λ*)log log n and 25d/(α_s*λ*)log log n. Lemma 4.3 (p. 27) gives convergence of λ(r) to an intermediate limit at rate r^(−1/2+o(1)); the remaining argument identifies that limit with λ∞ using finite-support test functions and SSM. The exact offsets are proof-level bounds, while the theorem’s window remains O(log log n).

**Extensions and limitations.** Section 5 explains extension to monotone systems and, on even tori, antimonotone systems via a bipartite order (including antiferromagnetic Ising and hard-core examples). The arbitrary-boundary Theorems 7–8 in the introduction are cited from the companion paper, not proved here. High-temperature or nonzero-field 2D SSM supplies examples of the theorem’s hypothesis; the result does not cover the 2D critical point, where SSM fails. The source’s rate-one-per-site clock and torus geometry are part of the cutoff location.
