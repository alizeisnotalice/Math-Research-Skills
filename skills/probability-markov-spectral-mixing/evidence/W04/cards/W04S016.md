# Source card — Ding–Peres, “Mixing time for the Ising model: a uniform lower bound for all graphs”

**Version and reading:** arXiv:0909.5162v2, last revised 2013-09-25; the added appendix contains the simpler `n log n/2` lower-bound proof. The journal article appeared in *Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré, Probabilités et Statistiques* 47(4), 1020–1028 (2011), DOI 10.1214/10-AIHP402. Complete 12-page arXiv v2 read, including appendix. Model: zero-field ferromagnetic Ising on any n-vertex graph with nonnegative edge interactions, random-site discrete heat-bath updates.

**Theorem 1 (PDF p.3).** From all plus, the mixing time has uniform lower bound `(1/4+o(1)) n log n`, with the infimum over graphs and nonnegative interactions. The theorem is a mixing-time lower bound, not an edgewise gap comparison.

**Main proof chain (PDF pp.3–10).** Lemma 2.1 uses an increasing second eigenfunction and Peres–Winkler censoring to lower-bound mixing by the inverse gap when that gap is small. Otherwise test the variational formula with total magnetization `S=Σ_v σ_v`; its Dirichlet energy is at most 2, so the assumed gap lower bound forces `Varπ(S)≤2n log n`. FKG gives nonnegative pair covariances. A random subset `F` of size `⌊√n/log n⌋` then has controlled pair covariances. Accelerate updates on blocks `{v}∪Fᶜ`, `v∈F`; Peres–Winkler censoring compares the original chain to the block-updated chain. Projecting to F produces a coupon-collector estimate for the plus-start mean magnetization. GHS concavity gives Corollary 2.6, bounding conditional expectation by sums of single-conditioning covariances. Together with FKG this yields a monotone one-step coupling contraction `1−1/(2|F|)` (Lemma 2.8). Lemma 2.9's martingale/variance recursion bounds `Var(S_t)≤16|F|`; Chebyshev separation at `t₀=½|F|log|F|−20|F|` gives the theorem's scale after translating block updates back to random-site updates.

**Appendix (PDF pp.11–12).** The revised proof sharpens the constant to a lower bound of the form `n log n/2−3n log log n`, using a gap split, coupon collection for the accelerated updated set, and a stationary variance estimate.

**Boundary.** This uses the gap as an input to mixing lower bounds and then proves those bounds. It does not establish that the gap is monotone in a selected interaction.
