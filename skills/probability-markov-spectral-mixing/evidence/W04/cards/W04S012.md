# Source card — Bubley–Dyer (1997), “Path Coupling: A Technique for Proving Rapid Mixing in Markov Chains”

**Reading:** complete nine-page paper, PDF pp.1–9. The original Theorem 1 and its product-space restriction were checked in the rendered PDF page 3 (printed p.225); the card separates that theorem from a general path-metric extension.

## Original result: Theorem 1 (General Path Coupling)

**Model (PDF pp.2–3).** The state space must be the full product `Ω=C^V`. At each step choose a coordinate `j∈V` according to a fixed distribution `J`, then update only coordinate `j` using the conditional transition law `κ_{X,j}`. Adjacent configurations differ in exactly one coordinate, and the path metric used in the theorem is Hamming distance `H`.

**Hypothesis and conclusion (PDF p.3).** Define
`β = max_{X,Y∈Ω, i∈V, Y=X_{i→c}≠X} {1−J(i)+Σ_{j∈V} J(j) dTV(κ_{X,j},κ_{Y,j})}`.
If `β<1`, then after `t≥⌈ln(n/ε)/ln(β⁻¹)⌉` steps the chain is within `ε` in total variation of stationarity (with `n=|V|` coordinates, as in the paper). This is not a theorem for an arbitrary state graph or arbitrary chosen metric.

**Proof mechanism (PDF p.3).** For any two product configurations, connect them by a Hamming path of length `H(X,Y)`. Couple the updated coordinate distributions maximally along consecutive path states. The displayed `β` bounds the expected Hamming distance after one step by `βH(X,Y)`. Iterating gives `E H(X_t,Y_t)≤β^t n`; since Hamming distance is integer-valued, disagreement probability is at most this expectation. The coupling lemma and logarithms give the stated mixing bound. The paper's separate `β≤1` remark on p.3 adds a change-event lower-bound condition `α` and a gambler's-ruin estimate; it is not part of the strict-contraction theorem.

## Generalization used in this skill (derived, not Theorem 1)

For a finite connected state graph with path metric `d`, suppose every adjacent pair has a coupling of its one-step transition laws with `E d(X',Y')≤ρd(x,y)`, `ρ<1`, and suppose the adjacent-pair couplings can be glued along shortest paths. Write `D=diam(Ω,d)<∞` and `δ=min_{x≠y}d(x,y)>0`. Path coupling gives `E d(X_t,Y_t)≤ρ^t d(X_0,Y_0)`. Since `1{X_t≠Y_t}≤d(X_t,Y_t)/δ`, the coupling lemma gives

`dTV(P^t(x,·),π) ≤ (D/δ)ρ^t`.

If the path metric is generated by unit-length edges, then `δ=1`, yielding `Dρ^t`. The earlier shorthand `Dρ^t` therefore needs a unit-separation normalization; it is false for an arbitrarily rescaled metric. This is a separately proved abstraction, not Bubley–Dyer Theorem 1.

**Other source result (Theorem 2, PDF pp.3–5).** The Metropolis Path Coupling theorem separately uses the minimum transition distance `H'`, not Hamming distance; its strict-contraction and nonexpansion/change-event cases have their own transition and acceptance hypotheses. Do not mix those conditions with Theorem 1.

**Applications (PDF pp.5–8).** Graph colourings (`k≥2Δ+1`), extended Potts/hypergraph systems, TWICE-SAT, sink-free/source-free orientations, and fixed-size independent sets illustrate coupling design for fixed chains. None compares two different Ising interaction vectors or proves edgewise gap monotonicity.
