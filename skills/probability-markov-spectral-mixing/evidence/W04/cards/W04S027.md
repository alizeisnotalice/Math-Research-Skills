## Model and exact results

Throughout §§1–3, `X` is finite and `P` is irreducible and reversible with respect to a full-support probability `π`; `Q(x,y)=π(x)P(x,y)=Q(y,x)`. Eigenvalues are ordered `1=β₀>β₁≥…≥β_{m−1}≥−1`. The Dirichlet form is `E(φ,φ)=½∑_{x,y}Q(x,y)(φ(y)−φ(x))²`; `1−β₁=inf E(φ,φ)/Var_π(φ)`.

**Proposition 1 (statement: printed p.37 / PDF p.3; proof: printed p.38 / PDF p.4).** Choose one path `γ_xy` for every ordered pair of distinct states, with no edge repeated within any single path. Set `|γ_xy|_Q=∑_{e∈γ_xy}Q(e)^{-1}` and

`κ(Γ)=max_e ∑_{γ_xy∋e}|γ_xy|_Q π(x)π(y)`.

Then `β₁≤1−1/κ`. The proof expands `Var_π φ=½∑π(x)π(y)(φ(x)−φ(y))²`, telescopes increments along `γ_xy`, applies weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, interchanges the edge/path sums, and bounds the resulting edge load by `κ E(φ,φ)`.

**Corollary 1 (printed p.39; PDF p.5).** For simple random walk on a connected simple graph, `π(x)=d(x)/(2|E|)`, `Q(e)=1/(2|E|)`. For any chosen paths, with maximum degree `d_*`, maximum path length `γ_*`, and maximum edge path count `b=max_e #{γ_xy:e∈γ_xy}`, `β₁≤1−2|E|/(d_*² γ_* b)`. Choosing geodesics makes `γ_*` the graph diameter. This is a one-sided Poincaré/eigenvalue bound; bottleneck overlap can make it weak.

**Proposition 2 and Corollary 2 (printed pp.40–41; PDF pp.6–7).** If the irreducible reversible chain is aperiodic, choose for each state `x` an odd-length return path `σ_x`; define `|σ_x|_Q` as above and `ι=max_e ∑_{σ_x∋e}|σ_x|_Qπ(x)`. Then `β_min≥−1+2/ι`. For simple random walk on a connected non-bipartite graph, `β_min≥−1+2/(d_* σ_* b_*)`, where `σ_*` is maximum odd-return-path length and `b_*` maximum number of chosen return paths containing an edge. The proof writes `φ(x)` as an alternating half-sum of endpoint sums `φ(e⁺)+φ(e⁻)` around an odd closed path, applies Cauchy–Schwarz, and uses `½∑Q(x,y)(φ(x)+φ(y))²=E_π[φ²]+⟨φ,Pφ⟩_π`. Combining upper and lower endpoint estimates controls `β_* = max(β₁,|β_min|)` and hence the absolute spectral gap `1−β_*`. The odd-path hypothesis is substantive: bipartite period-2 chains have eigenvalue −1 and no positive absolute gap.

**Proposition 3 (printed p.42; PDF p.8).** For finite irreducible reversible `P`, `β_* = max(β₁,|β_min|)`, all states `x`, integer `n`, and continuous-time Poissonization at total jump rate one, `P_t=e^{t(P−I)}`:

`4 ||P^n(x,·)−π||_TV² ≤ ((1−π(x))/π(x)) β_*^{2n}`,

`4 ||P_t(x,·)−π||_TV² ≤ ((1−π(x))/π(x)) exp[−2(1−β₁)t]`.

The discrete-time bound must retain the negative endpoint eigenvalue; Poissonization removes the parity obstruction. Do not substitute per-edge/per-site rate-one time without rescaling `t`.

**Proposition 6 (statement: printed p.52 / PDF p.18; §3C proof: printed pp.56–58 / PDF pp.22–24).** Proposition 6 is printed as `1−2h≤β₁≤1−h²` for `h=min_{π(S)≤1/2} Q(S,Sᶜ)/π(S)` under the stated finite reversible ergodic-kernel assumptions; no laziness or nonnegative-spectrum hypothesis is stated. The lower half is the two-level test-function bound. The printed upper half is not supported by the source proof: §3C equation (3.4) (printed p.56; PDF p.22) states `h²/2≤λ₁≤2h`, and the derivation on physical pp.22–24 (equations (3.5)–(3.7)) establishes only `λ₁≥h²/2`, hence `β₁≤1−h²/2`. An exact four-state check also contradicts the printed `β₁≤1−h²` upper half: take the path conductances `1/10,2,10` and add self-loop conductance `1/1000` times each original weighted degree, then normalize `q` so `π(x)P(x,y)=q(x,y)`. The resulting chain is reversible, irreducible and aperiodic; exhaustive cut enumeration gives `h=10000/11011`, while `β₁=(21+500√70)/21021 > 1−h²`. The exact matrix, all cuts, and symbolic comparison are in `evidence/W04/numerical/s027_cheeger_printed_bound_audit.json`. This is recorded as an apparent source-side misstatement, pending independent review; W04 uses only the proof-supported weaker `β₁≤1−h²/2`.

**Propositions 7–9 (printed pp.53–60; PDF pp.19–26).** Proposition 7 defines canonical-path congestion `η=max_e Q(e)^{-1}∑_{γ_xy∋e}π(x)π(y)` and proves `β₁≤1−1/(8η²)`: every cut of mass at most 1/2 is crossed by total path weight `π(S)π(Sᶜ)≥π(S)/2`, which implies `h≥1/(2η)`, then the proof-supported weaker Cheeger estimate applies. Proposition 8 gives a random-geodesic bound for distance-transitive graphs: `β₁≤1−1/(8d²)≤1−1/(8γ_*²)`, with `d` the expected distance from a fixed vertex to a uniform vertex and `γ_*` the diameter. Proposition 9 gives `β₁≤1−1/(6n⁷)` for the stated Broder matching chain when each vertex of the bipartite `n+n` graph has degree at least `n/2`; the source invokes Jerrum–Sinclair canonical paths and bounds the load/length, rather than proving their path-load lemma from scratch.

## Proof diagnostics, examples, and transfer boundary

Section 2 examples check tightness and failure modes: odd cycles have the right gap order but a factor-about-two canonical-path constant; the cube path bound is order `d⁻²` versus exact gap `2/d`; binary trees expose path bottlenecks; the Bernoulli–Laplace and symmetric-triangulation examples show when averaging over random paths improves a deterministic construction. Section 3 examples compare Cheeger and Poincaré bounds and show neither universally dominates the other. These are examples, not general optimality results.

The permanent result is specific to the Broder chain on perfect and near-perfect matchings with the stated dense bipartite graph condition. It is not a generic Ising/Glauber theorem. To transfer a path comparison to a new chain, re-derive `π`, `Q`, path congestion and, for worst-case discrete-time TV, the bottom eigenvalue/periodicity control using that chain’s actual update clock. This source does not prove that any Gibbs spectral gap is monotone in its edge couplings.
