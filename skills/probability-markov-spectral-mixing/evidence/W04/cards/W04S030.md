# Source card — Wübker (2009), “L²-spectral gaps for time discrete reversible Markov chains”

**Reading evidence.** Complete 14-page arXiv:0908.0897v1 preprint read (PDF pp.1–14); Theorem 2 and the explicit estimate (13) on PDF p.5 were rendered and visually checked. The paper is treated as a preprint; its body’s anomalous “October 26, 2018” line is recorded as an internal date inconsistency and not used to relabel the arXiv v1.

## Model, constants, and theorem

The paper considers a positive recurrent time-homogeneous Markov chain with stationary law `π`, Markov operator `P` on `L²(π)`, and reversibility when required. The relevant gap is geometric contraction on the centered subspace. For measurable `A` with `0<π(A)<1`, define

`k(A)=∫_A P(x,Aᶜ)π(dx)/(π(A)π(Aᶜ))`, `k=inf_A k(A)`, `K=sup_A k(A)`;

`k₂` is the corresponding normalized two-step flow constant for `P²`. The operator’s upper-edge gap `r₁` and bottom-edge gap `r_{−1}` track spectrum near `+1` and `−1` separately.

Write `L²_0(π)={f∈L²(π):π(f)=0}`. The paper's spectral-gap definition is for the restriction `P₀=P|_{L²_0(π)}`: the stationary eigenfunction is removed. Thus every spectral enclosure below means `σ(P₀)`, not the spectrum of the full Markov operator (which contains eigenvalue `1`).

**Theorem 2 (PDF p.5).** For a reversible chain, the following are equivalent: (i) an `L²` spectral gap on `L²_0(π)`; (ii) `0<k≤K<2`; (iii) `k₂>0`. More quantitatively, with the Lawler–Sokal constant `κ>1`,

`σ(P₀) ⊂ [−√(1−(κ/8)k₂²), min{√(1−(κ/8)k₂²), 1−(κ/8)k²}]`.

The paper also gives (13), a variational lower bound on `k₂` as a supremum over positive parameters `δ,ε₁,ε₂,ε`. Its three terms are `k²δ/16`, `(k/4)(ε₁ε₂(1−δ)−δ)`, and `ε[k((2−ε)(1−ε₁)(1−ε₂)(1−δ)/((1−ε)K)−1/(1−ε))−ε/(1−ε)]`; the estimate is useful as a positivity certificate when a parameter choice makes all three terms positive. The parameters and `0<k≤K<2` hypotheses must be retained.

## Proof chain and transfer boundary

Proposition 1 applies the Lawler–Sokal inequality on the centered space to `P₀²`, giving `σ(P₀²)⊂[0,1−κ k₂²/8]`; spectral mapping then controls both spectral endpoints of `P₀`. Lemma 4 proves `k=0` iff every `k_n=0`; Lemma 5 shows `K=2` forces `k₂=0` by taking sets with one-step conductance tending to two and splitting the two-step flow. Lemma 6 proves (13) by partitioning sets according to one-step escape ranges, with sets `A_{k/4}`, `C`, and `B_{ε₁}`; reversibility converts the three cases into the three lower bounds displayed above. Optimizing yields `k₂>0` from `0<k≤K<2`; the reverse implication follows from Lemmas 4–5 and Proposition 1.

The point of `K<2` is to rule out near-bipartite/periodic behavior at the `−1` endpoint. A one-sided Cheeger constant `k>0` controls only the upper edge and is insufficient for an arbitrary discrete-time reversible chain. A genuinely lazy kernel or heat-bath average may have nonnegative spectrum, but that fact must be established for the actual update operator; continuous-time and discrete-time normalizations are not interchangeable. The theorem is not an Ising interaction-monotonicity statement.
