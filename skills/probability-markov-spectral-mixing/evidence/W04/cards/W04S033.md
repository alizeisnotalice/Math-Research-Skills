## Marton coupling and mixing-time bounded differences

Section 2 defines a Marton coupling for a sequential vector by coupling each pair of futures conditional on changing one current coordinate, and defines an upper-triangular mixing matrix `Γ`. Theorem 2.9 (physical pp. 8–9) states that if changing coordinate `i` changes `f` by at most `c_i`, then

`log E exp(λ(f−Ef)) ≤ λ² ‖Γ C(c)‖² / 8`,

and hence `P(|f−Ef|≥t)≤2 exp(−2t²/‖Γ C(c)‖²)`. For a uniformly ergodic Markov chain, Proposition 2.4 partitions the trajectory into blocks of size at most `τ(ε)` and constructs a coupling with geometrically decaying later-block mismatch. Corollary 2.11 (physical p. 9) gives

`P(|f(X)−Ef(X)|≥t) ≤ 2 exp(−2t²/(‖c‖² τ_min))`,

where `τ_min=inf_{0≤ε<1} τ(ε)((2−ε)/(1−ε))²`; in the time-homogeneous case `τ_min≤9 t_mix` under the paper's threshold conventions. The proof combines the block coupling with a martingale-difference argument and conditional Hoeffding's lemma. This is a concentration inequality for functions of a trajectory, not a coupling proof of the Ising gap's monotonicity.

## Spectral gap, mixing, and additive-functional bounds

Section 3.1 (physical pp. 12–15) defines, for a stationary chain, the reversible gap `γ=1−sup(λ≠1)λ`, the absolute gap `γ*=1−sup(λ≠1)|λ|`, and for nonreversible chains the pseudo spectral gap `γ_ps=max_{k≥1}γ((P*)^kP^k)/k`. Proposition 3.3 relates reversible mixing and `γ*`; Proposition 3.4 gives `γ_ps≥(1−ε)/τ(ε)` and, in particular, `γ_ps≥1/(2t_mix)`, with finite-state `π_min` bounds in the reverse direction. A positive Poincaré gap need not control a negative eigenvalue near `−1`; the source's alternating two-state example shows why general sums use `γ*`.

For stationary reversible chains with `γ>0`, Theorem 3.5 (physical pp. 15–16) gives `Var(Σ_i f(X_i))≤2n Var_π(f)/γ`, an `O(Var/γ²)` error from `n σ_as²`, and for varying `f_i` an upper bound with `2/γ*`. For stationary nonreversible chains with `γ_ps>0`, Theorem 3.7 (physical p. 16) gives the corresponding constants `4/γ_ps` and `16/γ_ps²`.

Theorem 3.9 (physical p. 16) assumes a stationary reversible chain, `f∈L²(π)`, `|f−E_π f|≤C`, and either finite state space or even `n`. It proves

`P(|S−ES|≥t) ≤ 2 exp(−t²/[2n(σ_as²+0.8V_f)+10tC/γ])`,

and also `≤2 exp(−t²γ/[4nV_f+10tC])`. Theorem 3.11 (physical pp. 17–18) gives for a stationary nonreversible chain with `γ_ps>0`

`P(|S−ES|≥t) ≤ 2 exp(−t²γ_ps/[8(n+1/γ_ps)V_f+20tC])`;

its unequal-function extension (3.26) includes the explicit factor `M/k_ps`. Theorem 3.9's even-`n`/finite-state clause and the theorem locators are those of the audited v5 file.

## Proof structure, correction boundary, and W04 use

Sections 5.1–5.2 (physical pp. 25–39) prove the coupling result by a block partition, maximal/Goldstein couplings and martingale increments; the spectral results use the self-adjoint reversible operator, perturbation of its leading eigenvalue and asymptotic variance, and for nonreversible chains powers of `P` and `P*` with a residue-class decomposition. Propositions 3.15–3.17 extend stationary tail bounds through initial-law `χ²` factors, burn-in TV distance and truncation for unbounded summands. Several referenced ingredients (Fill, Lezaud, Kato) remain external dependencies explicitly cited in the proof.

The v5 manuscript itself says on physical p. 3 that the general-state Bernstein proofs in earlier versions and the published 2015 article used an incomplete Lezaud argument, and that v5 supplies a correction. Use this audited v5 when citing those general-state Bernstein proofs; do not silently attribute its corrected proof to the different 32-page journal pagination. For W04's finite spin systems, the finite-state clause in Theorem 3.9 avoids the even-`n` restriction.

This paper concerns concentration, variance, and mixing for a fixed chain (and gives discrete-time spectral/mixing comparisons). It does not order two chains when one Ising edge coupling changes. Its discrete-time statements do not automatically transfer to per-site-rate-one continuous time; state the clock or prove a Poissonization/time-change step. It supplies an auxiliary interface to A040 only after the exact finite heat-bath kernel and its gap/mixing parameters are established.
