# Source card — Martinelli, Sinclair & Weitz (2004), “Glauber Dynamics on Trees: Boundary Conditions and Mixing Time”

**Reading evidence.** Independent full-text read of physical PDF pp. 1–32 (complete 32-page manuscript). PDF SHA-256: `90ff84338ac44547a04f113b689fa2fc9abc44482e03067251b733058a14fdbc`. Formula pages rendered and inspected: pp. 4, 10, 14, 17, 19, 24. See `reading_coverage.json#sources.W04S019` for section-by-section coverage.

**Model and statement.** The paper studies the Ising Gibbs law on a finite complete b-ary tree (b≥2), with inverse temperature β, uniform external field h, and a fixed boundary condition τ; the dynamics is single-site heat-bath Glauber dynamics. Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 4) gives a uniform-in-size spectral-gap lower bound Ω(1) in either of two regimes: (i) arbitrary fixed boundary and either β<β₁ (all h) or |h|>h_c(β) (all β); or (ii) the all-plus boundary, for every β,h. Here β₁ is the tree spin-glass threshold, distinct from the ordinary uniqueness threshold β₀. Theorem 1.2 (p. 5) gives a uniform logarithmic-Sobolev lower bound in the same regimes, hence O(log n) mixing in the paper’s strong sense. These are finite-tree dynamic statements with boundary conditions kept explicit.

**Proof chain and constants.** Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 (pp. 9–10) reduce gap and log-Sobolev lower bounds to variance mixing (VM) and entropy mixing (EM). In particular, VM(ℓ,(1−δ)/(2(ℓ+1−δ))) implies Var(f)≤(3/δ)Dℓ(f), and EM(ℓ,[(1−δ)p_min/(ℓ+1−δ)]²) implies Ent(f)≤(2/δ)Eℓ(f); the proof telescopes over tree levels and charges each site’s block contribution at most ℓ times. Theorem 4.3 (pp. 13–16) bounds VM(ℓ,(γκb)^ℓ), where κ measures root influence through a descendant subtree and γ bounds propagation of one boundary-spin change. Thus γκb<1 gives exponential VM and a constant gap. The coupling proof separates inward propagation (κb) from the outward response γ; Proposition 4.5 gives the one-spin variation in terms of the message ratio R as Kβ(R)=1/(e^(−2β)R+1)−1/(e^(2β)R+1).

For arbitrary boundaries, high-temperature/strong-field exponential spatial mixing gives the first regime directly; in the intermediate regime κ≤γ≤Kβ(1), so γ²b<1 precisely below β₁. For plus boundary, after spin-flip symmetry the proof reduces to h≥−h_c(β). The message recursion is J(a)=e^(−2βh)[Fβ(a)]^b, with Fβ(a)=(a+e^(−2β))/(e^(−2β)a+1); at its least fixed point a₀≤1, Lemma 4.6 gives Kβ(a₀)=J′(a₀)/b≤1/b, yielding γκb<1. The entropy route in §5 uses concentration of the block-to-root conditional densities and the same coupling quantities. Theorem 5.7 (p. 22) is a separate general-tree observation: for arbitrary nearest-neighbor tree spin systems a constant gap gives a log-Sobolev constant at least order 1/log n, sufficient for O((log n)²) mixing, but it is weaker than the Ising-specific uniform bound of Theorem 1.2.

**Use and transfer limits.** Use this source to separate boundary-sensitive tree mixing from lattice critical slowdown and to justify tree recursion/coupling arguments under the theorem’s stated regimes. The plus-boundary result does not imply arbitrary-boundary rapid mixing at low temperature. Sections 6 and 7 record hard-core, coloring, Potts and converse statements with model-specific hypotheses; some are explicitly imported from companion work, so they should not be presented as proofs of general-graph results here. The proof uses the b-ary tree and its recursive message structure; no arbitrary-graph extension follows from the displayed contraction alone.
