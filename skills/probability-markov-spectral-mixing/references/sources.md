# Source map and reading record

The historic W04 ledger's `review_status` field and source cards are immutable historical metadata; they do not encode the current R01 scoped decisions. R01 has now matched all 30 exact R02-baseline works to accepted high-scoped uses and accepted seven additional current works (37 distinct works total). The 37 exact interfaces, row conditions, review records and PDF hashes are in the accepted-use register（本机附件：`references/accepted_scope_register.md`）. Byte-exact source/read/review evidence is mapped in `../evidence/source_evidence_manifest.json`; the historic W04 source record and selected cards remain under `../evidence/W04/`. A 30/30 source match is not blanket approval of every theorem or old card sentence. Reading-unit descriptions below record historical coverage, not current admitted use; the accepted register is authoritative for theorem scope.

## Direct A040 sources

- **W04S001 — Levin–Peres–Wilmer, _Markov Chains and Mixing Times_, 2e (2017).** Full book acquired; complete Chapter 15 read (PDF pp.231–247), §§12.1–12.2 (pp.177–181; §12.2 ends in the upper part of p.181, where §12.3 starts below), and Chapter 26 Question 2. Additional selected pages 181–185 include material from §12.3 and the opening of §12.4. Chapter 15 includes Theorem 15.1's zero-field random-site high-temperature path-coupling criterion; Proposition 15.8's exponential edge-deletion comparison; Theorem 15.13's square-lattice fault-line bottleneck. For Theorem 15.10, the p.239 statement prints a factor `4`, while the p.240 displayed congestion proof counts `4^M` pairs and its intermediate bound does not yield the final printed factor for general `M`; keep that author-proof discrepancy explicit. R02 independently accepted a separate library-derived conditional-variance proof of `γ_B≤M M_*e^{2βΔM}γ` for finite zero-field homogeneous Ising, `0≤β<∞`, uniform site/block heat-bath with covering blocks, common stationary law and the same discrete-time per-update clocks. This alternate proof is not attributed to LPW; its assumptions and proof are linked in the source card and `../evidence/shared/catalogue/integration_math_checks/lpw_block_comparison_repair.md`. Chapter 26 Question 2 (print pp.359–360 / PDF pp.375–376) states the open arbitrary-graph zero-field question. This is not a whole-book read. Current high-scoped use is only §§12.1–12.2; the other read units are historic reading coverage, not active high-scoped routes. [Author textbook PDF](https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/markovmixing.pdf).
- **W04S003 — Nacu (2003), “Glauber Dynamics on the Cycle is Monotone.”** Full text, 10 pp.; random-site heat-bath, zero field, arbitrary positive edge couplings on cycles; proof chain through Perron reduction, ratio bounds and derivative equations (8)–(12); `τ₂=[n(1−μ₂)]⁻¹`. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305056), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-003-0279-x).
- **W04S004 — Kargin (2011), “Relaxation Time Is Monotone in Temperature in the Mean-Field Ising Model.”** Full arXiv v1, six pp.; source states a uniform-field theorem for equal couplings on the complete graph. The audited proof uses `f_k=−f_{n−k}` (PDF p.5), which is justified here only at `H=0`; use the homogeneous zero-field subcase only. This is a proof-scope limitation, not a counterexample to the stated theorem. [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0327), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2011.03.001).
- **W04S005 — Shirai (2010), “A remark on monotonicity for the Glauber dynamics on finite graphs.”** Full text, six pp.; negative fixed-time single-spin expectation derivative on some length-3-path example. ## General spectral-gap and mixing methods

- **W04S002 — Aldous & Fill, _Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs_.** Complete selected units in this unfinished monograph: Chapter 3 §§3.6.1–3.6.4 (physical PDF pp.87–94) and Chapter 4 §4.4 (pp.131–141). The source prints a lower side in Lemma 3.29 with the edge/vertex extrema reversed; the proof instead gives `min_i(w_i/w_i*)/max_e(w_e/w_e*)≤τ₂/τ₂*`, while reversing the weights gives the printed upper side. An exact two-state check refutes the printed lower expression; see the source card and `../evidence/W04/numerical/s002_lemma329_lower_audit.json`. At p.94 Examples 3.30–3.31 illustrate sensitivity to graph changes, followed by Lemma 3.32's same-stationary-law off-diagonal comparison. Its conclusion follows from Dirichlet-form domination for `δ>0`; the source's subsequent mixture remark needs `0<δ<1` and full-entry domination to ensure a nonnegative residual transition kernel. At `δ=1`, the mixture formula is undefined and the endpoint identity separately requires `P=P*`. The stated off-diagonal hypothesis alone does not suffice. See `../evidence/W04/numerical/s002_lemma332_mixture_audit.json`. Neither direct comparison uses path routing; routing appears in Theorem 4.32. Section 4.4 starts on p.131. Current high-scoped use is only the named Chapter 3 §§3.6.1–3.6.4 interfaces; Chapter 4 §4.4 is historical reading coverage, not an admitted route. [Author PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.pdf).
- **W04S027 — Diaconis & Stroock (1991), _Geometric Bounds for Eigenvalues of Markov Chains_.** Complete 27-physical-page scan read (including archive front sheet); Proposition 1 statement is physical p.3/printed p.37 and proof p.4/printed p.38; Propositions 2–3 give odd-return-path and total-variation bounds. Proposition 6 is printed on physical p.18/printed p.52. Its proof-supported weak Cheeger bound `h²/2≤λ₁≤2h` is equation (3.4), physical p.22/printed p.56, derived on pp.22–24; the stronger printed upper half `β₁≤1−h²` is contradicted by an exact four-state reversible-chain example. See the audit artifact and use only the weak bound pending review. Propositions 7–9 include canonical paths, distance-transitive graphs and the dense-graph permanent chain. The odd-path/bottom-spectrum condition is essential for discrete-time absolute convergence. [Project Euclid DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoap/1177005980), [searchable full-text mirror](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/courses/Spring06/sta395/DiacStro1991.pdf).
- **W04S028 — Aldous (1982), _Some Inequalities for Reversible Markov Chains_.** L01 accepts only Proposition 8 (physical p.3 / printed p.566): for the source’s finite irreducible reversible continuous-time chain, `β` is the relaxation time and `β≤τ₁≤β[1+½log(1/π_min)]` at the stated TV threshold. The exact rate clock is required; this does not approve Theorems 5–6 or the full four-parameter comparison for this stage. [Wiley DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-25.3.564), [PDF](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/ConvergeRates/Aldous1982.pdf).
- **W04S029 — Chen & Wang (2000), _Cheeger’s Inequalities for General Symmetric Forms and Existence Criteria for Spectral Gap_.** L01 accepts only Theorem 1.2 inequality (1.11) (physical p.3; proof pp.6–8): `gap≥M(1−√(1−(k′/M)²))≥(k′)²/(2M)` using the source’s minimum-mass cut normalization and verified maximum exit rate. The admitted chain is finite, irreducible, reversible, conservative and has no killing. No product-mass substitution or unbounded-rate/domain extension is admitted. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9804150), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1019160118).
- **W04S030 — Wübker (2009), _L²-spectral gaps for time discrete reversible Markov chains_.** Complete 14-page arXiv v1 preprint read; for the L01 use, retain only Corollary 1 (physical p.7): `k₂>0` iff the centered fixed-kernel `P|L²₀(π)` has a two-sided L² spectral gap, with `k₂(A)=∫_A P²(x,Aᶜ)dπ/[π(A)π(Aᶜ)]` and the infimum over all `0<π(A)<1`. Eq. (4) prints `k` using the strict-half domain `0<π(A)<1/2`, whereas Eq. (6) defines `k_n` over all nontrivial cuts; do not identify these on finite atomic spaces without a stated half-mass convention. The Eq. (13) unrestricted-positive optimizer is excluded as a quantitative certificate by R01’s exact finite counterexample. No parameter derivative is supported. Publication status was not verified. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.0897).
- **W04S031 — Dyer, Goldberg, Jerrum & Martin (2006), _Markov chain comparison_.** L01 accepts only Theorems 1–2 (physical p.5 / printed p.93): ordinary routed flow controls the Dirichlet form `E`; an odd flow also controls the plus form `F` and hence the lower spectral endpoint. Keep the exact route-demand/load hypotheses and common stationary law where required. No unused Example 11 constants or other theorem routes are approved here. [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410331), [Project Euclid DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/154957806000000041).
- **W04S011 — Diaconis & Saloff-Coste (1993), “Comparison Theorems for Reversible Markov Chains.”** Complete 36-page read. Theorems 2.1–2.3 (PDF pp.4–12) compare Dirichlet and plus forms using path congestion; stationary-measure domination and odd paths are required where stated. [Author PDF](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~lsc/papers/comp-rev.pdf).
- **W04S012 — Bubley & Dyer (1997).** Full 9-page paper; original Theorem 1 assumes `Ω=C^V` and Hamming distance (PDF p.3 / print p.225); the general path-metric statement in its card is labeled as a separate derivation. Theorem 2 uses minimum transition distance and Metropolis hypotheses. [PDF](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/Teaching/Markov_Chain_Mixing/Papers/Pathcouple.pdf).
- **W04S013 — Hayes & Sinclair (2007), “A General Lower Bound for Mixing of Single-Site Dynamics on Graphs.”** Complete 24-page read; Theorem 1.1 proves discrete-time Ω(n log n) mixing for nonredundant local reversible dynamics at bounded degree, with Δ-dependent constant; clock conversion is explicit. - **W04S014 — Dyer, Goldberg & Jerrum (2009), “Matrix Norms and Rapid Mixing for Spin Systems.”** Complete 38-page read. ## Geometry-specific Ising mixing sources

- **W04S017 — Ding, Lubetzky & Peres (2009), mean-field mixing-time evolution.** Complete 43-page read; Curie–Weiss cutoff and gap orders are separated across subcritical, critical-window and supercritical regimes; retain the additional `δ→0` condition for the printed near-critical supercritical simplification. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1906), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-009-0781-9).
- **W04S018 — Ding, Lubetzky & Peres (2009), censored mean-field chain.** Complete 55-page read; Theorems 1–2 give censored-chain cutoff and gap order `δ/n` under `δ²n→∞`, with the all-plus cutoff constant distinguished from the worst-case one. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.0633), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-009-9859-1).
- **W04S019 — Martinelli, Sinclair & Weitz (2004).** Complete 32-page read; plus-boundary b-ary-tree Ising has a uniform gap in the stated regimes, while arbitrary-boundary low-temperature claims are not implied. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307336), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-004-1147-y).
- **W04S020 — Lubetzky & Sly (2012), critical square lattice.** Complete 26-page read; zero-field critical square-lattice boxes have polynomial inverse gap upper bounds for arbitrary fixed boundary and `n^(7/4)` inverse-gap lower bounds; FK/RSW input is dimension-specific. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1613), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-012-1460-9).
- **W04S021 — Lubetzky & Sly (2013), lattice cutoff.** Complete 34-page read; continuous-time rate-one-per-vertex cutoff on periodic tori under strong spatial mixing, with location depending on `λ∞`; do not transfer to arbitrary boundary conditions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4320).

## Cross-tool formal120 leads (not core spectral-gap coverage)

- **W04S025 — Aldous & Feng (2024), _Markov chains and mappings of distributions on compact spaces_.** Complete 15-page read. Theorem 1 gives a unique stationary law and fixed-parameter total-variation coupling bound for the nearest-order-statistic kernel; Theorem 4 excludes non-omnipresent interval fixed laws only for `k=2`. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01348), [ALEA version of record](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v21/21-53.pdf). This is auxiliary to A121/A122's inner kernel or `k=2` boundary, not a spectral-gap comparison.
- **W04S026 — Aldous, Cruz & Feng (2024), _… II: Numerics and Conjectures_.** Targeted partial read of PDF pp.1–3, 14–15, 21–22. It is the primary source for A121 Conjecture 5 and A122 §7.3 questions; its numerical trajectories are not proofs. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18153).

A121 asks for almost-everywhere convergence of the nonlinear map `θ↦π_{j,k}(θ)` on a finite-space simplex. A122 asks for all-parameter nonexistence of full-support fixed measures on `[0,1]`. Both primarily concern nonlinear distribution-map dynamics and invariant measures. W04 maps only the stationary-kernel interface and the proved `k=2` subcase; route the core questions to a separate distribution-map/fixed-point tool. These two records are not counted toward the core 30-source target for this skill.

## Further finite-spin and fixed-chain sources

- **W04S032 — Sambale–Sinulis (2020), _Logarithmic Sobolev Inequalities for Finite Spin Systems and Applications_.** L01 accepts Theorem 4.1(1)–(2), Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 under the full-support conditional-mass and interdependence-norm hypotheses: the stated entropy tensorization, modified log-Sobolev constant and total-rate-one entropy decay. Ordinary LSI, hard-constraint support changes and higher-order concentration are outside the accepted row. The uniform `O(n log n)` consequence additionally needs uniform-in-size parameters. Hard-constraint support and unrelated concentration claims are excluded. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07765), [Bernoulli DOI](https://doi.org/10.3150/19-BEJ1172).
- **W04S033 — Paulin (2015), _Concentration Inequalities for Markov Chains by Marton Couplings and Spectral Methods_.** L01 accepts only Theorem 3.5, equations (3.14) and (3.16) (physical pp.15–16 / proof pp.29–30): stationary finite reversible-kernel variance bounds for fixed and varying centered observables, with `γ>0` for the fixed observable and `γ_abs>0` for varying observables. The chain is started at stationarity and discrete observation times are retained. Bernstein/Marton and other concentration results are not included in this row. [arXiv v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.2015), [EJP DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v20-4039).
