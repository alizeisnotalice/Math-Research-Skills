## General spectral-gap and mixing methods

- **W04S002 — Aldous & Fill, _Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs_.** Complete selected units in this unfinished monograph: Chapter 3 §§3.6.1–3.6.4 (physical PDF pp.87–94) and Chapter 4 §4.4 (pp.131–141). The source prints a lower side in Lemma 3.29 with the edge/vertex extrema reversed; the proof instead gives `min_i(w_i/w_i*)/max_e(w_e/w_e*)≤τ₂/τ₂*`, while reversing the weights gives the printed upper side. An exact two-state check refutes the printed lower expression; see the source card and `evidence/W04/numerical/s002_lemma329_lower_audit.json`. At p.94 Examples 3.30–3.31 illustrate sensitivity to graph changes, followed by Lemma 3.32's same-stationary-law off-diagonal comparison. Its conclusion follows from Dirichlet-form domination for `δ>0`; the source's subsequent mixture remark needs `0<δ<1` and full-entry domination to ensure a nonnegative residual transition kernel. At `δ=1`, the mixture formula is undefined and the endpoint identity separately requires `P=P*`. The stated off-diagonal hypothesis alone does not suffice. See `evidence/W04/numerical/s002_lemma332_mixture_audit.json`. Neither direct comparison uses path routing; routing appears in Theorem 4.32. Section 4.4 starts on p.131. [Author PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.pdf).
- **W04S027 — Diaconis & Stroock (1991), _Geometric Bounds for Eigenvalues of Markov Chains_.** Complete 27-physical-page scan read (including archive front sheet); Proposition 1 statement is physical p.3/printed p.37 and proof p.4/printed p.38; Propositions 2–3 give odd-return-path and total-variation bounds. Proposition 6 is printed on physical p.18/printed p.52. Its proof-supported weak Cheeger bound `h²/2≤λ₁≤2h` is equation (3.4), physical p.22/printed p.56, derived on pp.22–24; the stronger printed upper half `β₁≤1−h²` is contradicted by an exact four-state reversible-chain example. See the audit artifact and use only the weak bound pending review. Propositions 7–9 include canonical paths, distance-transitive graphs and the dense-graph permanent chain. The odd-path/bottom-spectrum condition is essential for discrete-time absolute convergence. [Project Euclid DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoap/1177005980), [searchable full-text mirror](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/courses/Spring06/sta395/DiacStro1991.pdf).
- **W04S028 — Aldous (1982), _Some Inequalities for Reversible Markov Chains_.** Complete 13-page read of the source's finite-state continuous-time model with transition-rate matrix `Q` and semigroup `P_t`; Theorems 5–6 compare mixing, stationarity stopping times and hitting-time parameters, with reversibility in the constant-factor result and logarithmic `π_min` losses in the general-chain result. Proposition 8 (printed p.566 / PDF p.3) uses `β` as relaxation time, with `exp(−t/β)` decay and `β≤τ₁≤β[1+½log(1/π_min)]`. These are continuous-time quantities; a discrete-time conversion requires a separate clock/periodicity argument. [Wiley DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-25.3.564), [full-text PDF](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/ConvergeRates/Aldous1982.pdf).
- **W04S029 — Chen & Wang (2000), _Cheeger’s Inequalities for General Symmetric Forms and Existence Criteria for Spectral Gap_.** Complete arXiv v1 read; Theorems 1.1–1.5 give scaled-form Cheeger bounds and local/global spectral-gap criteria, with domain, operator-norm and killing assumptions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9804150), [Annals of Probability DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1019160118).
- **W04S030 — Wübker (2009), _L²-spectral gaps for time discrete reversible Markov chains_.** Complete 14-page arXiv v1 preprint read; Theorem 2 equates a centered-space discrete-time L² gap (`P|L²_0(π)`) with `0<k≤K<2` and `k₂>0`; its spectral enclosure excludes the stationary eigenfunction. The `K<2` condition excludes near-periodicity. Publication status was not verified. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.0897).
- **W04S031 — Dyer, Goldberg, Jerrum & Martin (2006), _Markov chain comparison_.** Complete 24-page arXiv v2 read; Theorems 1–2 (PDF p.5/printed p.93) compare `E` and odd-flow `F`; Theorem 8 (PDF p.8/printed p.96) needs an odd flow for general reversible chains. Nonreversible conductance bounds do not alone yield discrete-time upper mixing for the original chain. Other theorem locators are recorded in the full source card. [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410331), [Project Euclid DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/154957806000000041).
- **W04S011 — Diaconis & Saloff-Coste (1993), “Comparison Theorems for Reversible Markov Chains.”** Complete 36-page read. Theorems 2.1–2.3 (PDF pp.4–12) compare Dirichlet and plus forms using path congestion; stationary-measure domination and odd paths are required where stated. [Author PDF](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~lsc/papers/comp-rev.pdf).
- **W04S012 — Bubley & Dyer (1997).** Full 9-page paper; original Theorem 1 assumes `Ω=C^V` and Hamming distance (PDF p.3 / print p.225); the general path-metric statement in its card is labeled as a separate derivation. Theorem 2 uses minimum transition distance and Metropolis hypotheses. [PDF](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/Teaching/Markov_Chain_Mixing/Papers/Pathcouple.pdf).

- **W04S014 — Dyer, Goldberg & Jerrum (2009), “Matrix Norms and Rapid Mixing for Spin Systems.”** Complete 38-page read. ## Geometry-specific Ising mixing sources

- **W04S017 — Ding, Lubetzky & Peres (2009), mean-field mixing-time evolution.** Complete 43-page read; Curie–Weiss cutoff and gap orders are separated across subcritical, critical-window and supercritical regimes; retain the additional `δ→0` condition for the printed near-critical supercritical simplification. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1906), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-009-0781-9).
- **W04S018 — Ding, Lubetzky & Peres (2009), censored mean-field chain.** Complete 55-page read; Theorems 1–2 give censored-chain cutoff and gap order `δ/n` under `δ²n→∞`, with the all-plus cutoff constant distinguished from the worst-case one. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.0633), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-009-9859-1).
- **W04S019 — Martinelli, Sinclair & Weitz (2004).** Complete 32-page read; plus-boundary b-ary-tree Ising has a uniform gap in the stated regimes, while arbitrary-boundary low-temperature claims are not implied. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307336), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-004-1147-y).
- **W04S020 — Lubetzky & Sly (2012), critical square lattice.** Complete 26-page read; zero-field critical square-lattice boxes have polynomial inverse gap upper bounds for arbitrary fixed boundary and `n^(7/4)` inverse-gap lower bounds; FK/RSW input is dimension-specific. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1613), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-012-1460-9).
- **W04S021 — Lubetzky & Sly (2013), lattice cutoff.** Complete 34-page read; continuous-time rate-one-per-vertex cutoff on periodic tori under strong spatial mixing, with location depending on `λ∞`; do not transfer to arbitrary boundary conditions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4320).

## Cross-tool formal120 leads (not core spectral-gap coverage)

- **W04S025 — Aldous & Feng (2024), _Markov chains and mappings of distributions on compact spaces_.** Complete 15-page read. Theorem 1 gives a unique stationary law and fixed-parameter total-variation coupling bound for the nearest-order-statistic kernel; Theorem 4 excludes non-omnipresent interval fixed laws only for `k=2`. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01348), [ALEA version of record](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v21/21-53.pdf). This is auxiliary to A121/A122's inner kernel or `k=2` boundary, not a spectral-gap comparison.
- **W04S026 — Aldous, Cruz & Feng (2024), _… II: Numerics and Conjectures_.** Targeted partial read of PDF pp.1–3, 14–15, 21–22. It is the primary source for A121 Conjecture 5 and A122 §7.3 questions; its numerical trajectories are not proofs. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18153).

A121 asks for almost-everywhere convergence of the nonlinear map `θ↦π_{j,k}(θ)` on a finite-space simplex. A122 asks for all-parameter nonexistence of full-support fixed measures on `[0,1]`. Both primarily concern nonlinear distribution-map dynamics and invariant measures. W04 maps only the stationary-kernel interface and the proved `k=2` subcase; route the core questions to a separate distribution-map/fixed-point tool. These two records are not counted toward the core 30-source target for this skill.

## Further finite-spin and fixed-chain sources

- **W04S032 — Sambale–Sinulis (2020), _Logarithmic Sobolev Inequalities for Finite Spin Systems and Applications_.** Complete 23-page arXiv v2 read (journal version: *Bernoulli* 26(3), 1863–1890; DOI 10.3150/19-BEJ1172). Corollary 2.3 is stated on physical p.5 and proved on p.16. Theorem 2.2 gives entropy decay and `O(|I| log |I|)` mixing only if the all-partial-conditionals lower bound and interdependence-matrix norm bound both hold; Theorem 4.1 gives mLSI/LSI. It is not a blanket Ising theorem. The source's random-site kernel has generator `P−I`; per-site rate-one rescales the gap by `|I|`. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07765), [Bernoulli DOI](https://doi.org/10.3150/19-BEJ1172).
- **W04S033 — Paulin (2015), _Concentration Inequalities for Markov Chains by Marton Couplings and Spectral Methods_.** Complete 42-page arXiv v5 author-manuscript read, distinct from the 32-page journal pagination. Theorem 2.9/Corollary 2.11 give Marton-coupling concentration for trajectories under mixing-time bounds; Theorems 3.5/3.7 and 3.9/3.11 treat fixed-chain variance and concentration using reversible, absolute or pseudo spectral gaps. The v5 author correction is needed for the general-state Bernstein proof; these discrete-time results do not compare edge-varying Ising chains. [arXiv v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.2015), [EJP DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v20-4039).
