# Tube and maximal transfer

Start by fixing the dimension, tube/cap scale, direction data, extension normalization, weights, and maximal-norm convention.  Preserve every epsilon loss as a named ledger item; a bound with a different norm or scale convention is not interchangeable.

## Three-dimensional tube-to-maximal route

Use this route only for a finite tube family with declared direction/separation data, sample weights, and the source's `L^3` maximal-norm convention.  Apply the finite sampling/maximal reduction with its explicit epsilon loss.  It does not prove the geometric incidence hypotheses, extend to four dimensions, or supply a Kakeya-model certificate from tube counts alone.

## Curved extension and level-set route

Use this route only for a positively curved surface packet array with matching cap/tube extension normalization and an available Kakeya-maximal or elliptic-capacity package.  The allowed transfer is the source's positive tube comparison followed by the stated level-set/layer-cake step, or its declared elliptic packet propagation.  Retain the packet, Furstenberg, continuation, and atomic-array premises; without them no extension or restriction estimate follows.

Read the relevant tube-maximal or diagonal-extension reference before applying a route.  Their three-dimensional normalization and their external geometric inputs must be checked in the target problem.
