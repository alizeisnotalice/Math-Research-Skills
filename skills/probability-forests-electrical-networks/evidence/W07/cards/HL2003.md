## Model and exact criterion

Let (T) be a spherically symmetric rooted tree, with finite level sets (T_n), and suppose simple random walk is transient, equivalently (sum_n |T_n|^{-1}<infty). Define

\[
L_n=\sum_{m>n}|T_m|^{-1},\qquad
S(T)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{1}{|T_n|^2 L_n L_{n-1}}.
\]

Theorem 1.4 says that if (S(T)=\infty), the WSF is change-intolerant; if (S(T)<\infty), it is insertion-tolerant; and if in addition (T) has bounded degree, it is essentially deletion-tolerant. This is a criterion for the stated radial tree class. Theorem 3.3 extends the criterion to spherically symmetric transient networks with radial conductances, using the corresponding energy/flow series (2.4); the default unit-conductance case recovers the displayed tree criterion.

## Density martingale and second moment

The reduction in Lemma 2.1 (PDF pp. 4–6) compares the harmonic-ray/percolation law with the percolation law after a finite modification. In §3, let (mathrm{Ray}_o) be the harmonic measure on rays from the root, and let (mathrm{Perc}_o) be the law of the percolation subtree. The two quantities in the Radon–Nikodym martingale are:

- (I(x)=\mathrm{Ray}_o\{\xi:x\in\xi\}), the **deterministic harmonic mass** of rays passing through (x). On a spherically symmetric tree this is the equally-splitting unit flow, (I(x)=|T_{|x|}|^{-1}).
- (h(x)=\mathrm{Perc}_o\{\omega:x\in\omega\}), the percolation inclusion probability at (x).

For a subtree (t), the level-(n) density is
\[
W_n(t)=\sum_{x\in T_n}\frac{I(x)}{h(x)}\,\mathbf 1_{\{x\in t\}}.
\]
Thus the indicator belongs to the random subtree coordinate; it is **not** (I(x)). The second-moment identity (3.4), derived on physical PDF p. 9, contains the summands (I(u)^2(1/h(u)-1/h(\hat u))) over non-root (u) through level (n). Spherical symmetry makes (I) the equal-splitting flow, so boundedness of this second moment in (n) is equivalent to convergence of the energy series (2.4). The proof then uses the martingale convergence/density criterion: convergence gives absolute continuity and the tolerance conclusions; divergence gives the singular/change-intolerant alternative.

## Scope and proof boundary

The implication uses the rooted spherical structure and transience, with bounded degree needed for the essential deletion conclusion. It does not establish the same series test on arbitrary trees or graphs. In the network extension, conductance enters both the harmonic flow and the energy; do not substitute an unweighted level-size profile without verifying the corresponding flow-energy identity.
