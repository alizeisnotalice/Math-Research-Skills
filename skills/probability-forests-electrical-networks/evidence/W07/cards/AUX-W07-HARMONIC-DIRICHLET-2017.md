## Model, spaces, and theorem

A network is a connected locally finite graph with positive conductances; a weighted planar map is such a network together with a planar-map structure. For an oriented-edge set, the Dirichlet energy is (\mathcal E(\phi)=\frac12\sum_{e\in E^{\to}}c(e)(\phi(e^-)-\phi(e^+))^2\). (D(M)) is the finite-energy space, (D_0(M)) the closure of finitely supported functions, and (HD(M)) its harmonic subspace. For a transient network the Royden decomposition is (D(M)=D_0(M)\oplus HD(M)); this is a direct sum, not generally orthogonal in the full anchored Hilbert norm. In a domain (D\subset\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}), (D(D)), (D_0(D)), (HD(D)) are the analogous finite-energy/Sobolev spaces.

**Theorem 1.5 (physical p. 8; general isomorphism).** Assume (M) is a transient weighted polyhedral planar map, has bounded codegrees and bounded local geometry (bounded degrees and conductances bounded above and away from zero), and admits a double circle packing ((P,P^\dagger)) in a domain (D\subset\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}). Let (z(v)) be the center of the primal circle at (v). Then:

1. For each (h\in HD(M)), there is a unique (H\in HD(D)) with (h-H\circ z\in D_0(M)); call it (\mathrm{Cont}[h]).
2. For each (H\in HD(D)), there is a unique (h\in HD(M)) with (h-H\circ z\in D_0(M)); call it (\mathrm{Disc}[H]).

Both maps are bounded linear operators and inverses. Theorem 1.1 (pp. 2–3) is the special case of a bounded-degree, simply connected, simple planar triangulation circle packed in the unit disk: for bounded harmonic Dirichlet inputs, the corresponding (h) and (H\circ z) are asymptotically equal, in addition to the HD-space isomorphism.

**Boundary and equality scope.** Under Theorem 1.5 assumptions, the difference (h-H\circ z\in D_0(M)) is equivalent to quasi-asymptotic equality, i.e. every level set {(|h-H\circ z|\ge\varepsilon)} has finite capacity (Proposition 2.1, pp. 10–11). For bounded (h,H), ordinary asymptotic equality is equivalent to this capacity condition only when (D) is uniformly transient (Proposition 2.11, pp. 20–24); the unit disk qualifies. Do not apply the asymptotic version to unbounded finite-energy functions or to arbitrary non-uniformly-transient domains.

## Proof mechanisms and quantitative estimates

1. **Capacity detects (D_0) (Proposition 2.1, pp. 10–11).** Capacity is given by Dirichlet's variational principle. If (\phi\in D_0(M)), the truncation (\varepsilon^{-1}|\phi|) is an admissible test and (\mathrm{Cap}(|\phi|\ge\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal E(\phi)<\infty). Conversely, if every level set has finite capacity, choose (\psi_\varepsilon\in D_0) dominating 1 there. Royden decomposition plus the random-walk boundary-limit representation gives (\psi_\varepsilon(X_n)\to0) a.s.; hence \(\limsup_n|\phi(X_n)|\le\varepsilon\), and letting ε decrease to zero forces the harmonic Royden component of φ to vanish. The domain version uses open-neighborhood level sets.

2. **Packing geometry and extension/restriction maps (pp. 11–18).** The Ring Lemma bounds ratios of neighboring primal/dual radii from degree/codegree bounds. The resulting straight-line embedding is “good”: adjacent lengths comparable and face angles uniformly away from 0 and π. The Sausage Lemma supplies a uniform δ₁ so separated edge sausages have disjoint convex hulls.

   - $(R[\Phi](v))$ is the average of Φ over the small concentric disk (P_{\delta_0}(v)). If Φ is harmonic, the mean-value property gives $(R[\Phi](v)=\Phi(z(v)))$.
   - (A[\phi]) is the piecewise-affine extension on the triangulation augmented by dual-face centers; the value at a face-center is the average of φ at its incident vertices.
   - Lemma 2.6 (pp. 13–16) proves both operators bounded and energy controlled, with constants depending only on degree/codegree and conductance bounds: (\mathcal E(R\Phi)\lesssim\mathcal E(\Phi)), (\mathcal E(A\phi)\lesssim\mathcal E(\phi)). For (A), flatten each local triangle and compare its affine gradient to endpoint/face differences; Cauchy–Schwarz charges each edge only boundedly many times. For (R), join independently uniform samples from neighboring disks, apply Jensen/Cauchy–Schwarz along the random segment, then use the sausage geometry to show its sampled-point law has density (O(r(v)^{-2})) on the local convex hull, with bounded overlap.
   - Lemma 2.8 (pp. 16–17) gives (\delta^4\mathrm{Cap}(A)\lesssim\mathrm{Cap}(P_\delta(A))\lesssim\mathrm{Cap}(A)), (0<\delta\le1/2). The lower loss is δ⁻⁴ from averaging (R); upper bound extends a truncated discrete capacity test through (A).
   - Lemma 2.9 (pp. 17–18) bounds local interpolation error by δ times neighboring oscillation and uses the disk Harnack/energy estimate for harmonic functions. Lemma 2.10 (pp. 18–20) proves (\phi-R[A\phi]\in D_0(M)), (A\phi\in D_0(D)\Leftrightarrow\phi\in D_0(M)), and (R\Phi\in D_0(M)\Leftrightarrow\Phi\in D_0(D)). The “only if” directions use capacity comparison and compact exhaustion, not pointwise equality alone.

3. **Royden projections finish the isomorphism (pp. 19–20).** The operators are explicitly (\mathrm{Disc}[H]=(R[H])_{HD}) and (\mathrm{Cont}[h]=(A[h])_{HD}), with boundedness inherited from Lemma 2.6 and bounded Royden projections. Lemma 2.10 shows (h-R\mathrm{Cont}[h]\in D_0(M)); uniqueness follows because if both harmonic candidates differ by a (D_0) term then Lemma 2.10 transfers that difference into (D_0(D)), whose intersection with (HD(D)) is zero. The reverse composition identities follow by projecting the (D_0) residuals. Random-walk/Brownian terminal limits in (2.11)–(2.12) are representations via the classical Royden/Ancona–Lyons–Peres and Doob results; they are not new estimates proved here.

4. **Upgrade to pointwise asymptotic equality (pp. 20–24).** Uniform transience gives a positive lower capacity for small metric disks at every scale/location. The planar elliptic Harnack inequality (2.14)–(2.16), together with the graph Harnack inequality (2.17), propagates any non-vanishing discrepancy at a vertex to a whole nearby ball. Infinitely many such discrepancies give infinitely many disjoint sets with uniformly positive capacity; Lemma 2.13 shows their union has infinite capacity, contradicting quasi-asymptotic equality. No explicit universal numeric constant is claimed: constants depend on the bounded geometry data and the uniform-transience lower bound.

## Consequences and use limits

- Corollary 1.2 (p. 3) identifies harmonic Dirichlet functions on the packed triangulation with Douglas-integrable boundary data φ on ∂D, with (h(v)=E_v[φ(lim z(X_n))]). The classical Douglas formula on p. 3 is D(φ)=E_disk(H), where H is the continuous harmonic extension of φ to the unit disk. This is not an equality with the discrete graph energy E_graph(h): Theorem 1.1 gives a bounded linear isomorphism between the discrete and disk harmonic-Dirichlet spaces, and p. 3 explicitly says such an isomorphism need not be an isometry; it yields comparability of the corresponding energy norms, not equality. Corollary 1.3 (pp. 3–4) gives a.e. convergence along exponentially tangential approach regions, using Nagel–Rudin–Shapiro. Corollary 1.4 (p. 4) says (BHD(T)) is dense in all bounded harmonic functions (BH(T)) for pointwise convergence.
- The paper's boundary representation relies on circle packing and the referenced Poisson-boundary results. It does not imply every bounded harmonic function is Dirichlet; the text explicitly contrasts Douglas-integrable boundary traces with arbitrary bounded Borel traces.
- **Use** the two maps (A,R) plus Royden projections when transporting finite-energy harmonic boundary data between a bounded-geometry planar map and its double-packed domain. If only finite-capacity exceptional sets are controlled, state quasi-asymptotic equality. Upgrade to ordinary asymptotic equality only for bounded functions and a uniformly transient domain. No conclusion follows for unbounded-degree/codegree maps, conductances without uniform two-sided bounds, or a general graph without the planar packing hypotheses.
