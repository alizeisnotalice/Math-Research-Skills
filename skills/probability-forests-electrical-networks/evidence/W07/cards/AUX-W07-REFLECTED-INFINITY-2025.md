## Model and main theorem

The base object is a countably infinite connected graph (G=(V_G,E_G,c)) with positive conductances on unoriented edges and finite weighted degree π(x)=∑_{y~x}c(x,y)<∞ at every vertex. Ordinary conductance random walk is assumed transient for the nontrivial construction. Local finiteness is not needed for Theorem 1.6, but is assumed for the spanning-forest applications and several end/path statements. A continuous-time walk with rate function (w:V_G\to(0,\infty)) waits Exp((w(x))) at (x), then takes a conductance-weighted step.

**Theorem 1.6 (physical p. 5).** There is (w_*:V_G\to(0,\infty)) such that for every (w\ge w_*) pointwise and every start (z), there is a unique-in-law process (X:[0,\infty)\to V_G\cup\{\infty\}) with: (i) for each deterministic (t\ge0), a.s. (X_t\in V_G), and (X) is locally constant around (t); (ii) right continuity at every time with a finite-vertex state; (iii) first holding time at (z) is Exp((w(z))), independent of the first neighbor and followed by the ordinary conductance step; (iv) Markov property; (v) a.s. returns to (z) at arbitrarily large times; and (vi) for every nonempty finite (A\subset V_G), every φ on (A), and its unique finite-energy minimizing harmonic extension (h^\phi),

\[
h^\phi(z)=\mathbb E_z[\phi(X_{\tau_A})],\qquad \tau_A=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t\in A\}.
\]

Thus ∞ is a zero-duration accumulation/boundary state in the path description; the walk repeatedly escapes through infinitely many vertices in finite time and reflects back with a boundary distribution depending on where it approaches infinity. The holding-rate choice (w\ge w_*) changes elapsed time but not the order in which vertices are visited. If ordinary random walk is recurrent, the theorem has the trivial realization by the ordinary continuous-time walk for any positive rate function; transience is where the reflection construction is substantive.

## Proof chain and exact dependencies

1. **Energy minimizer and harmonic measure (Proposition 1.3, p. 4; proof pp. 13–15).** With energy (\mathcal E(f)=\sum_{\{x,y\}}c(x,y)(f(y)-f(x))^2), gradients of functions vanishing on a finite nonempty (A) form a closed subspace of the Hilbert space of antisymmetric square-summable edge flows. Lemma 2.1 (pp. 13–14) identifies its orthogonal complement as the closed span of cycles modulo (A). Project the gradient of the zero-off-(A) extension of boundary data onto the zero-boundary gradient subspace; subtract the projection to obtain the unique minimum-energy extension. Variations at one vertex give discrete harmonicity off (A), and strict convexity gives uniqueness. Linearity plus the maximum principle (Lemma 2.3, p. 15) imply that the pointwise values (h^x_A(z)) are nonnegative and sum to one, so (hm^z_A(x)=h^x_A(z)) is a probability measure (Lemma 2.4, p. 15). This energy-minimizing harmonic measure is the return law used at the approximating boundary.

2. **Finite-exhaustion chains and consistency (Section 3.1, pp. 16–19).** Take increasing finite connected (G_n\uparrow G). On (B_1G_n), the chain follows ordinary random walk while in (G_n); from (B_1G_n\setminus G_n), it jumps into (G_n) according to (hm^x_{G_n}). Lemma 3.2 (pp. 18–19) proves that hitting a finite (A\subset G_n) has the minimizing-harmonic-measure law: the difference of the candidate hitting expectation and the minimizer is harmonic both on (G_n\setminus A) and at the boundary states, hence its extrema on finite (G_n) are on (A) and vanish. Lemma 3.3 (p. 19) then shows consistency: after the finer chain is observed only at visits to (G_m), its transition kernel is exactly the (G_m) chain, by the strong Markov property and Lemma 3.2. Lemma 3.4 (pp. 20–21) couples all exhaustions so their ordered visits agree on common levels.

3. **Countable ordered path and clock (Sections 3.2–3.3, pp. 20–24).** The compatible discrete paths are indexed by a random totally ordered set Ξ of equivalence classes of pairs (exhaustion level, step), rather than by ordinary integer time; this allows arbitrarily many vertices between consecutive finite-level visits. Conditional on the path, assign independent (T_\xi\sim\mathrm{Exp}(w(Y_\xi))). Lemma 3.5 (pp. 21–22; rendered p. 22) constructs shell thresholds: for each finite shell (G_n\setminus G_{n-1}), choose (C_n) so that if all rates there are at least (C_n), the time (S_n) spent in that shell before the first return to the start is at most (2^{-n}) with probability at least (1-2^{-n}). Borel–Cantelli makes the sum of shell times finite a.s.; finite-level recurrence gives infinite total time over the infinite path. This yields finite accumulation times at ∞ and arbitrarily many reflected excursions. Theorem 1.6 states the stronger pointwise lower bound (w\ge w_*); the lemma proves its summability conclusion even if the shell lower bound fails on finitely many levels.

4. **Construction, path properties and uniqueness (pp. 23–28).** Lemma 3.6 gives vanishing tail time outside (G_n) before any fixed ordered index. Put (X=Y_\xi) on the half-open intervals between accumulated holding-time sums and set (X=\infty) on the gaps. Lemma 3.7 (p. 23) proves each fixed deterministic time has a finite state: first almost every time lies in an ordinary holding interval, then shift by an independent uniform variable and use mutual absolute continuity of exponential and exponential-plus-uniform times. Lemma 3.8 proves local eventual agreement of finite-exhaustion processes on compact subsets of a holding interval. The existence proof verifies properties (i)–(vi); property (vi) follows from Lemma 3.2. For uniqueness, Lemma 3.10 (p. 25) upgrades the Markov property to stopping times by dyadic ceiling approximation and right continuity. Any candidate process, sampled on entry/exit from (G_n), has precisely the same finite-state chain and exponential holding times as the approximant (p. 26); its time lag is bounded by (\int_0^t1_{\{X_s\notin G_n\}}ds\to0) by a.e. finite-state paths and dominated convergence. Thus every candidate is the same limit in law.

5. **Continuity / ends / Dirichlet-form context (pp. 26–30).** Lemmas 3.11–3.14 (pp. 26–28) establish finite visits to each finite set on bounded time intervals, no jumps over a finite vertex cutset, existence of an immediately preceding adjacent vertex at finite-state times when (G) is locally finite, and finitely many jumps on a compact interval avoiding ∞. Section 3.6 (pp. 28–30) identifies the process with the active reflected Dirichlet form (maximal Silverstein extension) for speed measure (m(x)=\pi(x)/w(x)); this is contextual, not used in the main construction. Proposition 5.3 (p. 34) assigns each ∞-time a unique graph end when (G) is locally finite, using nested components after deleting finite sets and the no-cutset-skipping lemma.

## Forest, Green kernel and embedding results

- **Aldous–Broder / free spanning forest.** For locally finite (G), the first-entrance parent of each (x\ne z) along (X) defines a spanning forest (Definition 1.14, p. 8). Theorem 1.15 (p. 8; proof pp. 30–32) says this has the conductance-weighted free spanning forest (c-FSF) law. Proof mechanism: finite-exhaustion Aldous–Broder trees; Proposition 2.5 gives pointwise convergence of finite-domain harmonic extensions, hence convergence of finite-chain harmonic-measure transition kernels on each finite 1-neighborhood; irreducibility makes the first (N) common-level visits couple with high probability; take total variation limits on finite edge sets. The paper's (c\equiv1) case is FUSF.
- **Wilson variant with an explicit restriction.** Definition 1.13 stops loop erasure of an (X)-segment if its infinite loop erasure never reaches the endpoint; the resulting increasing Wilson forest is acyclic and spanning. Lemma 1.16 and Theorem 1.17 (pp. 9–10; proof pp. 32–33) establish agreement with FSF only after both random forests are set to ∅ off their connectivity events. The untruncated Wilson forest is not asserted to equal FSF; it may have finite components. Extending loop erasure so it can reflect at ∞ is only suggested as a possible future route.
- **Green function and GFF.** (N_A(y)) counts connected visit intervals at (y) strictly before first hit of (A), not elapsed occupation time or raw number of jumps (p. 10). (G_A(x,y)=\mathbb E_xN_A(y)); Lemma 5.6 (pp. 36–37) proves finiteness, harmonicity off (A\cup\{y\}), and the source identity \(\sum_{x\sim y}c(y,x)(G_A(x,y)-G_A(y,y))=-\pi(y)\) for (y\notin A\). Lemma 5.7 (pp. 36–38) obtains it as a finite-domain Green-kernel limit via minimizing harmonic functions. Lemma 5.8 (p. 38) gives detailed balance (G_A(x,y)/\pi(y)=G_A(y,x)/\pi(x)) and positive semidefiniteness of the resulting kernel. Proposition 1.20 (p. 11; proof p. 38) is the FSF edge formula \(\mathbb P(\{x,y\}\in T_{FSF})=c(x,y)G_{\{y\}}(x,x)/\pi(x)\), proved by a geometric-return count and the first-step conductance probability. Definition 1.21 uses covariance \(\mathrm{Cov}(\Psi(x),\Psi(y))=G_A(x,y)/\pi(y)\).
- **Tutte embedding (pp. 11–12, 34–35).** For a planar map with finite vertex and face degrees, distinguished external face, interior mark (x), and boundary mark (y), map the distinct external-boundary vertices in counterclockwise last-visit order to the unit circle by cumulative energy-minimizing harmonic measure viewed from (x); extend real and imaginary boundary coordinates as minimizing harmonic functions. This is defined even for infinitely many ends/accumulation points. Lemma 5.4 gives pointwise convergence from finite submaps; Lemma 5.5 proves convexity of each non-external face image and disjoint interiors by finite Tutte approximation.

## Conjectural section and transfer limits

Section 6 (pp. 38–48) contains background plus **conjectures, not proved theorems**. For a specified iteratively glued Boltzmann-map/ring model, Conjecture 6.7 predicts a joint Tutte-embedding scaling limit to supercritical LQG/CLE4: boundary length, loops, rescaled graph metric, reflected walk to Brownian motion, and free-boundary-at-∞ GFF. Conjecture 6.8 predicts each graph end has singleton image. In this paper, c denotes the Liouville central charge: footnote 1 (physical p. 3) and Eq. (6.1) (physical p. 39) give c=1+6Q²; the matter-field central charge is 26−c=25−6Q² and is a different parameter. The phase-transition conjectures therefore use the positive-Q thresholds √(3/2), √(5/2), and √(91/24), corresponding under this paper's convention to Liouville c thresholds 10, 16, and 95/4, respectively: Conjecture 6.10 predicts reflected-walk range connected for Q>√(3/2) (c>10), versus positive-probability disconnection for Q<√(3/2) (c<10); Conjecture 6.12 predicts FUSF connected a.s. for Q>√(5/2) (c>16), and infinitely many components a.s. on the infinite-map event for Q<√(5/2) (c<16); Conjecture 6.15 predicts no infinite critical-percolation cluster for Q>√(91/24) (c>95/4), and with positive probability an infinite boundary cluster with uncountably many ends for Q<√(91/24) (c<95/4). Critical equalities are left open. These rely on conjectured scaling-limit correspondences and a Hausdorff-dimension heuristic for singular points; the authors explicitly state the relevant dimension-disjointness claim is not exactly proved (p. 46 footnote 10).

**Use and non-transfer.** Use Theorem 1.6 for a canonical time-changed reflected walk on transient weighted countable networks with pointwise finite conductance and its finite-set hitting law; use Theorem 1.15 for local-finite FSF generation and Proposition 1.20 for its Green-kernel edge probabilities. The Wilson conclusion is connectivity-event-truncated. Do not treat ∞ as a single Poissonian interlacement state: the return law retains the boundary-at-infinity information through energy-minimizing harmonic measure (Remark 1.9, p. 6). No LQG convergence or phase-transition claim in Section 6 is established by the paper. No global theorem is inferred from the local finite-graph approximations without the exhaustion/total-variation limit argument.
