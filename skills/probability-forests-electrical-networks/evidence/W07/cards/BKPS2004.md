# BKPS2004 — geometry of the uniform spanning forest

**Source:** Itai Benjamini, Harry Kesten, Yuval Peres, and Oded Schramm, *Geometry of the Uniform Spanning Forest: Transitions in Dimensions 4, 8, 12, …*, Annals of Mathematics 160(2) (2004), 465–491, DOI 10.4007/annals.2004.160.465. Full reading of the 28-page local PDF, arXiv:math/0107140v3 (13 Feb. 2003), PDF pp. 1–28.

## Main result and proof mechanism

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2; proof pp. 22–24).** For the USF on ℤ^d,

  \[
  \max_{x,y\in\mathbb Z^d}N(x,y)=\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{4}\right\rfloor\quad\text{a.s.},
  \]

  where (N(x,y)) is the minimum number of edges outside the USF on a path from (x) to (y). On the event that two USF components differ, there are infinitely many disjoint paths between them using at most this many non-USF edges. Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2) further gives asymptotic distance 1 between distinct components in dimensions 5–8 and infinite separation in dimensions (d\ge9); for (d\le4) the USF is connected.
- **Stochastic dimension (Definition 2.2, PDF p. 4; Theorem 2.4, pp. 4–10).** A random relation has dimension (d-\alpha) when its one-pair probability is comparable to (\langle xz\rangle^{-\alpha}) and it satisfies the two-pair correlation bound

  \[
  \mathbb P(xRz, yRw)\le C\bigl(\langle xz\rangle^{-\alpha}\langle yw\rangle^{-\alpha}+\langle xzyw\rangle^{-\alpha}\bigr).
  \]

  For independent relations (L,R), composition has stochastic dimension ​\(\min(\dim_S L+\dim_S R,d)\). The proof obtains a lower bound for composition by counting intermediate vertices in dyadic shells and a second-moment estimate, then bounds the two-pair probability by expanding the four terms and summing the power-law kernels.
- **Tail upgrade (Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.4, PDF pp. 12–13).** Ordinary composition need not preserve left-tail triviality, so the proof uses a restricted composition requiring the intermediate point to be at least as far from both endpoints as the endpoints are from each other. This relation preserves the needed tail triviality and retains the dimension-addition rule. Once total dimension reaches (d), positive connection probability is upgraded to probability one by the zero-one lemma for the joint left and remote tails.
- **USF component relation (Theorem 4.2, PDF pp. 14–16).** For (d\ge5), the relation (xUy) iff (x,y) lie in the same USF component has stochastic dimension 4. Wilson’s algorithm reduces the one-point estimate to intersection of independent random walks; Green-function bounds give (\mathbb P(xUy)\asymp\langle xy\rangle^{4-d}). A second-moment estimate supplies the lower bound; separate-intersection and four-point same-component estimates supply the correlation inequality.
- **Finite-set bound (Theorem 4.3, PDF p. 17).** For finite (W\subset\mathbb Z^d), (\mathbb P[W\text{ lies in one USF component}]\lesssim\langle W\rangle^{4-d}), with constant depending on (d) and ​\(|W|\). The proof inducts on ​\(|W|\), chooses a pair whose oriented paths meet farthest from their common root, then uses Wilson’s algorithm and a three-point Green-function convolution.
- **Tail triviality (Theorem 4.5, PDF pp. 18–21).** The USF component relation has trivial left, right, and remote tails. Remote-tail triviality follows from one-endedness of WSF components and the tail triviality of the forest; left-tail triviality couples the path from a fixed vertex to the far wired forest with a path conditioned on a finite cylinder event and uses Liouville/Harnack control.

- **Stochastic-domination coupling (Theorem 4.1, PDF pp. 14–15).** Let \(F,F_0,\ldots,F_m\) be independent WSFs on \(G\). Put \(C_0=C(v_0,F)\); inductively, \(C_j\) is the union of \(F\)-components contained in or adjacent to \(C_{j-1}\). Put \(Q_0=C(v_0,F_0)\); inductively, \(Q_j\) is the union of \(F_j\)-components intersecting \(Q_{j-1}\). Theorem 4.1 states \(C_m\) stochastically dominates \(Q_m\), i.e. there is a coupling with \(C_m\supseteq Q_m\) a.s. The proof first works in a finite wired ball: collapse its complement to a root, delete root-incident edges from independent USTs, and induct on \(j\). Conditional on \(C_j^*=H\), the restriction of the first UST to the complement-side graph \(H^c\) is a UST there; negative association of UST edge indicators lets it dominate the independent next UST restricted to \(H^c\). This extends the coupling so \(C_{j+1}^*\supseteq Q_{j+1}^*\). Taking the wired limit gives the theorem.

- **Theorem 1.1 upper-bound chain (PDF pp. 22–23).** Set \(m=\lfloor(d-1)/4\rfloor\). The stochastic-dimension composition and tail results (Corollary 3.4, Theorems 4.2 and 4.5) first show that the composition of \(m+1\) independent USF component relations connects every pair a.s.; call these independent copies \(F_0,\ldots,F_m\). Now take an independent target WSF \(F\). Theorem 4.1 is essential: its coupling embeds the resulting \(Q_m\) expansion from \(F_0,\ldots,F_m\) inside the successive adjacent-component expansion \(C_m(F)\) of this fixed target forest. At each expansion step, ambient adjacency can be bridged by one edge outside \(F\); within each \(F\)-component use its tree path. Thus a path between any target vertices can be chosen with at most \(m\) edges outside \(F\), proving \(\max_{x,y}N_F(x,y)\le m\) a.s. The target \(F\) is an additional independent sample in the domination argument; the independent-copy union alone does not prove a bound relative to a fixed target forest. The proof of the outside-\(B_r\) refinement additionally conditions on a finite \(D\supset B_r\), uses the wired-USF law on \(G\setminus D\), and reapplies the same domination/composition argument there. A separate union bound in Proposition 5.1 gives the matching lower bound when \(d>4\). This is a statement about **uniform** spanning forests.

## Boundary: minimal forests

The paper’s Conjecture 6.7 (PDF p. 25) proposes stochastic dimension 8 for minimal-spanning-forest components in (d\ge8); it is explicitly a conjecture, not a theorem. The USF dimension transition cannot be transferred to MSF without a separate argument.
