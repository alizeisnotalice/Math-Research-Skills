# BLPS2001 — uniform spanning forests

**Source/version.** Itai Benjamini, Russell Lyons, Yuval Peres, and Oded Schramm, “Uniform Spanning Forests,” *Annals of Probability* 29(1) (2001), 1–65, DOI 10.1214/aop/1008956321. Read the complete 72-page local author PDF, version dated 27 Jan. 2009, including its endnotes and references. The local PDF’s own pagination is cited below; it differs from the journal’s 65-page pagination. Two author-version errata are noted in its front matter: “finite” is corrected to “bounded” in the number-of-sides hypotheses for Theorems 12.4/14.2, and a transfer-current coefficient statement is corrected by a footnote. Keep those qualifications when citing those results.

## Model and conventions (§2; PDF pp. 5–6)

A network is a connected graph with at least two vertices and positive conductances on undirected edges. Loops and parallel edges are allowed; loops do not belong to forests. Oriented edges occur in reverse pairs for flow calculations, but FSF/WSF are undirected spanning-forest laws. The standing network assumption is finite total incident conductance at every vertex,

\[
C_v=\sum_{e\ni v} C(e)<\infty.
\]

Uniform boundedness of \(C_v\) is an additional hypothesis only when stated. This does **not** assume the underlying graph is globally locally finite: a vertex may have infinitely many incident edges whose conductances sum finitely. Automorphisms in the invariant statements preserve the conductance network.

## Electrical projections and free/wired equality (§§4–7; PDF pp. 9–15, 22–25)

**Theorem 4.1 (PDF p. 10; proof pp. 10–11)** is the finite-network single-edge identity: for a finite connected network \((G,C)\), the signed spanning-tree path probability and the signed network-random-walk edge current both equal the unit electrical current \(I^e=P_{\star}\chi^e\); in particular, \(\mathbb P[e\in T]=I^e(e)\). It is not the multi-edge determinant theorem. The latter is the unnumbered **Transfer Current Theorem**, equations (4.4)–(4.5), stated on PDF p. 12 and proved on pp. 12–13. For distinct unoriented edges \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\) in a finite connected weighted network, it gives

\[
\mathbb P[e_1,\ldots,e_k\in T]
=\det[Y(e_i,e_j)]_{i,j=1}^k
=\det[(P_{\star}\widehat\chi^{e_i},\widehat\chi^{e_j})_R]_{i,j=1}^k,
\]

where each edge is oriented to form the transfer-current matrix and \(\widehat\chi^e=\sqrt{C(e)}\chi^e\). The proof first handles a cycle among the selected edges by linear dependence, then writes the determinant as a Gram determinant; Proposition 4.2 (contracting already included edges) and the single-edge current identity identify each Gram–Schmidt factor with the successive conditional inclusion probability. Reversing an edge orientation changes matching row and column signs, so the determinant is unchanged.

For an infinite network, the Hilbert space is \(\ell^2_-(E)\) of antisymmetric edge flows with resistance-energy inner product \((\theta,\eta)_R=\sum_{e\in E_{1/2}}R(e)\theta(e)\eta(e)\). Let \(\star\) be the closed span of conductance-weighted vertex stars and \(\Diamond\) the closed span of cycle flows. The free current is \(I_F^e=P_{\Diamond^\perp}\chi^e\), whereas the wired current is \(I_W^e=P_{\star}\chi^e\); thus the free projection is onto the orthogonal complement of the cycle-flow space, and the wired projection is onto the star-flow space. Propositions 7.1–7.2 (PDF p. 23) identify these as limits along free and wired finite exhaustions, respectively. In DPP terms, FSF has kernel \(P_{\Diamond^\perp}\) and WSF has kernel \(P_{\star}\). **Theorem 7.3 (PDF p. 24)** gives the equivalences

\[
\mathrm{FSF}=\mathrm{WSF}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I_F(e)=I_W(e)\text{ for every edge }e
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell^2_-(E)=\mathcal F\oplus\Diamond
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad HD(G)\cong\mathbb R.
\]

Here \(\mathcal F=\star\) and \(\Diamond\) are the paper’s closed star-flow and cycle-flow spaces, and \(HD(G)\) is the space of harmonic Dirichlet functions. For infinite networks the two spaces need not span all of \(\ell^2_-(E)\); the missing orthogonal part is the gradient space of finite-energy harmonic Dirichlet functions. The proof combines the edge marginals from Theorem 4.1 and the free/wired current limits with this orthogonal decomposition. No transitivity or unimodularity is required for this equality criterion.

**Theorem 7.8 (PDF p. 25)** is the infinite transfer-current theorem: for distinct edges \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\), FSF and WSF inclusion probabilities are respectively determinants of their free and wired transfer-current matrices. It follows by passing the finite theorem to the limits using the projection convergence in Propositions 7.1–7.2. The statement is about inclusion of a fixed finite edge set, not a BK inequality for random disjoint witnesses.

## Ergodicity, singularity, and orbit quantifiers (§8; PDF pp. 26–31)

Theorem 7.8 implies mixing of cylinder events whose edge sets recede from one another (Corollary 8.1). **Corollary 8.2 (PDF p. 26)** says: for an infinite network, if \(\operatorname{Aut}(G,C)\) has an infinite vertex orbit, FSF and WSF are each ergodic; if the two measures are distinct, they are mutually singular. Its proof uses the fact that the hypothesis forces **every** vertex orbit to be infinite, so translates of any finite edge cylinder can be moved arbitrarily far away; an invariant event is then independent of itself by mixing. Distinct ergodic invariant probability measures are singular.

The implication “one infinite orbit \(\Rightarrow\) every orbit infinite” is valid under this paper’s standing network hypotheses, even though the graph need not be locally finite. If there were a finite vertex orbit \(O\), choose any vertex \(v\), a finite path from \(v\) to \(O\), and let \(\varepsilon>0\) be the minimum conductance on that path. The subgraph retaining edges with conductance at least \(\varepsilon\) has finite degree at each vertex because its degree is at most \(C_x/\varepsilon\), and it is invariant under network automorphisms. The path remains in this subgraph. Hence the orbit of \(v\) lies in the finite-radius neighborhood of the finite set \(O\) in a locally finite subgraph, and is finite. Thus a finite orbit would force every orbit finite, contradicting existence of an infinite orbit. Do not claim that balls in the original weighted graph are finite.

**Theorem 8.3 (PDF pp. 26–27)** proves tail triviality of both FSF and WSF on every network, without the orbit condition. Corollary 8.2’s ergodicity/singularity route is therefore one sufficient route to singularity, not the only stated zero-one property. The authors explicitly ask whether distinct FSF/WSF are singular without the orbit hypothesis (Question 15.11); this paper does not resolve the unrestricted version.

## Component count and ends (§§9–10; PDF pp. 32–45)

Corollary 9.6 (PDF p. 34) gives the transitive-graph volume-growth dichotomy: if \(|B(o,n)|=O(n^4)\), the WSF is a single tree a.s.; if \(|B(o,n)|/n^4\to\infty\), it has infinitely many trees a.s. Its proof uses random-walk intersection estimates and the component-count results of Theorems 9.2 and 9.4. Do not read it as a general FSF statement or as a comparison between different Cayley generating sets.

**Theorem 10.1 (PDF p. 35)**: for a network with a transitive unimodular automorphism group, each WSF tree is a.s. one-ended unless the network is roughly isometric to \(\mathbb Z\), in which case it is a.s. two-ended. The proof combines the at-most-two-ends lemma with a trunk argument using transience, Wilson’s method rooted at infinity, and MTP. **Theorem 10.4 (PDF pp. 36–38)**: under the same transitive-unimodular-network hypothesis, if the WSF is disconnected with positive probability, then a.s. every tree has one end. Its proof assumes a two-ended trunk, uses MTP to show the root bush has finite conditional expected size, and then uses the ergodic shift along oriented WSF edges and a further mass-transport contradiction. These are WSF results, not WMSF conclusions.

## Nonamenable-network augmentation (§13; PDF pp. 55–62)

**Theorem 13.7 (PDF p. 59)** assumes edge conductances bounded above and below, bounded vertex degrees, and simple-random-walk spectral radius \(\rho<1\). For every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), WSF union an independent Bernoulli(\(\varepsilon\)) edge set has a.s. infinitely many components, each with infinitely many ends. In the proof, before deriving the path bound, the authors add self-loops if needed so all weighted vertex conductances \(C_v=\sum_{e\ni v}c(e)\) are equal. This may change the simple-walk spectral radius, but it remains below 1. For the simple random walk on this equalized network, reversibility with constant vertex measure and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(\mathbb P_v[X(n)=u]\le \mathbb P_v[X(2n)=v]^{1/2}\le \rho^n\) (equation (13.6)); here \(\rho\) is the spectral radius of the loop-adjusted walk, not necessarily that of the original walk. The proof then bounds expected simple paths in the Bernoulli-augmented forest and uses a cut/path counting argument for the component and end conclusion. The boundedness and spectral-radius assumptions are part of the theorem. This does not prove an invariant augmentation result for FSF.
