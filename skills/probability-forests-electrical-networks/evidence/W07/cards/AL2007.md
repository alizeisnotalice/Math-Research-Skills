# AL2007 — processes on unimodular random networks

**Source/version.** David Aldous and Russell Lyons, *Processes on Unimodular Random Networks*, *Electronic Journal of Probability* 12 (2007), Paper 54, 1454–1508, DOI 10.1214/EJP.v12-463. Read the complete 66-page local author manuscript dated 28 Dec. 2005, including all sections and references. The manuscript’s PDF pagination is used below; it is not the journal pagination.

## MTP and involution invariance (§2; PDF pp. 7–14)

For a random rooted connected locally finite network \((G,\rho)\), unimodularity means that every nonnegative Borel isomorphism-invariant transport \(f(G,x,y)\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb E\sum_x f(G,\rho,x)=\mathbb E\sum_x f(G,x,\rho).
\]

Proposition 2.2 shows it suffices to verify involution invariance for adjacent ordered vertex pairs: split a transport across shortest paths, apply the adjacent identity successively, and telescope. Nonnegative transports may have infinite expectations; signed transports require the corresponding integrability. The paper treats networks with edge marks at both endpoints and connected locally finite underlying graphs; orientation is bookkeeping, not a directed-graph hypothesis.

## Fixed-graph root law and the normalization condition (§3; PDF pp. 14–18)

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 14–16)**: Let \(G\) be a fixed connected locally finite graph, \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and \((o_i)\) a complete orbit section. A probability root law on \(G\) induces a unimodular rooted law iff \(\Gamma\) is unimodular and

\[
c=\sum_i |\operatorname{Stab}(o_i)|^{-1}<\infty.
\]

Here \(|\operatorname{Stab}(o_i)|\) denotes Haar measure of the compact-open stabilizer, not necessarily a finite group cardinality. Under these conditions the unique root law is

\[
\mu([G,x])=c^{-1}|\operatorname{Stab}(x)|^{-1}.
\]

The finite normalizer is necessary, not a cosmetic normalization. Forward proof: define the inverse-stabilizer law and use the stabilizer-weighted MTP; unimodularity permits swapping the two orbit indices. Converse: the rooted MTP forces positive root mass on each orbit and the orbit-pair identity (3.5), which implies unimodularity and inverse-stabilizer proportionality; total mass one forces \(c<\infty\). Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 17) then relates invariant unrooted network laws on a fixed connected unimodular graph satisfying this same finite-normalizer condition to unimodular rooted laws.

## Selected forest application across the full paper (§7; PDF pp. 37–40)

**Theorem 7.4 (PDF p. 40)**: if \(\mu\) is an extremal unimodular probability measure on infinite networks and \(\mathbb P_{p_c(\mu)}\)-a.s. there is no infinite percolation cluster, then WMSF(\(\mu\))-a.s. every tree has exactly one end. This is a random-network theorem; extremality, unimodularity, infinitude of the sampled networks, and absence of an infinite critical cluster are all hypotheses. Proof chain: Proposition 7.3 and Theorem 6.2 give at most two ends; assume a two-ended WMSF trunk. MTP makes the two trunk-direction limsups of edge labels equal. If their common value is \(p>p_c(\mu)\), the root lies with positive probability in an infinite \(p\)-cluster; invasion fills that cluster and, via Theorem 6.7, a nested infinite \(p'\)-cluster for \(p_c<p'<p\). The finite-symmetric-difference relation for invasion trees then forces a trunk label at least \(p'\), contradicting the definition of \(p\). Thus \(p=p_c\), which contradicts the no-infinite-critical-cluster assumption. Extremality is used for the percolation threshold/zero-one setup; MTP is used for the equal trunk limsups.

Other section-level results read include trace and stochastic comparison (§4), random walks and extremality (§5), percolation (§6), amenability/nonamenability (§8), examples (§9), and finite approximation (§10). Proposition 8.3 relates edge-isoperimetric constant and expected degree; Proposition 8.6 gives an invariant coupling under its amenability assumptions. The paper explicitly leaves a general invariant-coupling question open in §2. These do not provide arbitrary FSF/WSF invariant couplings or resolve the W07 target questions without matching hypotheses.
