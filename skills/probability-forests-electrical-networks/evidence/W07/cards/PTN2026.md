# PTN2026 — *Probability on Trees and Networks*

**Evidence level: partial reading.** The corrected paperback PDF (version 19 Aug 2026) is 716 PDF pages. The theorem-level pages below were checked in the local file; the relevant chapters have not yet been read end-to-end. PDF pages differ from printed pages.

## Chapter 8: mass transport and unimodularity

- **Theorem 8.7, PDF p. 296.** For a group action on a connected locally finite graph, the orbit-weighted mass balance uses stabilizer/orbit weights. The unweighted equality is justified only for a unimodular action. Do not apply the unweighted MTP to a merely transitive graph.
- **Theorem 8.10 and Corollary 8.11, PDF pp. 298–300.** A group action admits positive orbit weights μ_x, unique up to a common factor, satisfying the pairwise orbit-balance identity. For a quasi-transitive unimodular action, normalize the inverse orbit weights so that the root distribution over orbit representatives sums to one; this gives the weighted MTP and the usual normalized random root. The proof is double-counting by orbit pairs and then summing the weighted identities.
- **Proposition 8.18 and Theorem 8.19, PDF pp. 304–305.** For an invariant forest on a quasi-transitive unimodular graph, the mass transport that counts outgoing rays gives expected forest degree 2 on one-ended components and strictly more than 2 if a component has at least three ends with positive probability. Theorem 8.19 identifies this with positive probability that a component has p_c < 1 and with conditional expected degree greater than 2. The hypotheses include unimodularity and an invariant-percolation forest.

## Chapter 9: infinite electrical networks

- **Proposition 9.3, PDF p. 331; Theorem 9.5, PDF p. 334.** In the resistance-energy space ℓ²(E,r), the free unit current is the projection \(P_{\Diamond^\perp}\chi^e\) and the wired unit current is \(P_{\star}\chi^e\). They agree on every edge iff the cycle-flow and star-flow spaces span ℓ²(E,r), equivalently iff every finite-energy harmonic function is constant; a missing orthogonal direction is a nonconstant harmonic Dirichlet function.
- **Theorem 9.7, PDF pp. 335–336.** The exact separator hypothesis is expressed through the quantities R_D(W_n) defined in the theorem. Under its disjoint-separator and divergent reciprocal-resistance condition, currents are unique. The proof assumes two distinct currents, obtains a nonconstant finite-energy harmonic difference, and bounds its energy below across the disjoint separators. Do not substitute an unrelated resistance notion without checking the definition of R_D(W_n).
- **Planar duality, Section 9.4, PDF pp. 338–340.** The dual conductance is the primal resistance and free/wired boundary conditions interchange, under the stated plane-embedding and dual-network hypotheses.

## Chapter 10: uniform spanning forests

- **Corollary 10.5, PDF p. 365.** On every infinite network, FSF stochastically dominates WSF, and an exhaustion/weak-limit argument gives a monotone coupling. The authors explicitly caution that the construction need not be automorphism-invariant; it does not prove the invariant-coupling strengthening asked in Question 10.6.
- **Proposition 10.14 and Theorem 10.15, PDF p. 369.** FSF=WSF iff free and wired currents agree on every edge, iff the cycle/star subspaces span, iff all finite-energy harmonic functions are constant. In the flow-space convention above, FSF uses \(P_{\Diamond^\perp}\) and WSF uses \(P_{\star}\). The transfer-current theorem states for distinct edges e₁,…,eₖ that the probability all belong to FSF (respectively WSF) is the determinant of the corresponding free (respectively wired) transfer-current matrix. The proof cites the finite transfer-current theorem and the free/wired current limits.
- **Corollary 10.19, PDF p. 374.** For an action in which **every vertex has an infinite orbit**, FSF and WSF are mixing and ergodic; if they are distinct, they are mutually singular. Tail triviality gives mixing, and an infinite orbit upgrades mixing to ergodicity. The book notes it does not know whether the singularity conclusion holds without this every-vertex hypothesis.
- **Theorem 10.30, PDF p. 377.** On ℤ^d, the USF is a single tree iff d ≤ 4, and has infinitely many components iff d ≥ 5. This is a uniform-forest theorem; it does not settle the minimal-spanning-forest threshold.
- **Theorem 10.45, PDF p. 392.** On a connected bounded-degree nonamenable graph, the WSF has infinitely many trees, each one-ended a.s. The WMSF one-ended theorem is separate and appears in Chapter 11 with additional hypotheses.

## Chapter 11: minimal spanning forests

- **Theorem 11.11, PDF p. 417.** On a unimodular transitive graph, WMSF has expected degree 2 and every tree has at most two ends.
- **Theorem 11.12, PDF pp. 417–418.** Add θ(p_c) = 0; then every WMSF tree is one-ended. The proof uses the invasion-tree labels, an orientation toward the invasion end, the MTP, and a mass-transport contradiction from a two-ended trunk with a last edge above p_c + ε.
- **Theorem 11.15, PDF pp. 418–419.** If the graph is quasi-transitive and unimodular and WMSF≠FMSF, then a.s. every FMSF tree has infinitely many ends and contains infinitely many WMSF trees. The proof uses Theorem 11.12, finite-label-change control, MTP, and the no-isolated-ends result. Unimodularity is part of the theorem, not an optional technicality.
- **Theorem 11.23, PDF p. 422.** On any locally finite connected graph and for every ε∈(0,1), an independent Bernoulli(ε) edge set united with FMSF is connected a.s. This does not establish connectivity of FSF plus an invariant augmentation.

## Chapter 4: finite UST inequality boundary

- **Conjecture 4.10, PDF p. 142.** For a finite weighted UST, the BK inequality for increasing events and disjoint witnesses is conjectural. The book notes the arbitrary-event extension fails. Negative association for determinantal measures concerns increasing events measurable on disjoint fixed edge sets and does not settle the random-witness BK conjecture.

## Scope and unresolved transfer

Always identify the law first: finite weighted UST, FSF, WSF, FMSF, and WMSF have different constructions and conclusions. Electrical uniqueness, equality FSF=WSF, and mutual singularity are distinct claims. Uniform-forest theorems do not settle minimal-forest connectivity. The listed pages support only the statements and hypotheses recorded here; complete reading of Chapters 8–11 remains outstanding.
