## Model and conventions (§§1–3; local physical PDF pp. 3–15; printed article pp. 2–14)

The main infinite-volume setting is a connected, locally finite graph on which \(\mathbb Z^d\) acts with finite fundamental domain. Add self-loops to regularize every vertex to common degree \(D\) and make simple random walk aperiodic; loops are never in a spanning tree. Finite-graph statements concern uniform spanning trees (unweighted); conductance-weighted UST is not the theorem’s stated model. Infinite-graph laws are uniform essential spanning forests, obtained as limits using the cited Pemantle construction. For the infinite periodic graph, edge orientation is a sign convention in the current matrix; the determinant is invariant because reversing one edge flips its row and column.

Section 2 diagonalizes the finite-quotient adjacency operator by Fourier characters and eigenvectors of the Hermitian matrix \(Q(\alpha)\). Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 construct bounded solutions to \((I-A)g=f\) when the Fourier integrals are integrable; for recurrent dimensions the source is a zero-sum dipole rather than a point mass. Section 3 proves Harnack-type limits for hitting distributions on expanding boundaries and their finite contractions (Lemma 3.1); bounded harmonic functions on finite contractions are constant (Corollary 3.2), yielding uniqueness up to constants for the bounded Poisson solution (Corollary 3.3). These facts are needed to pass the finite transfer-current argument to the infinite periodic graph.

## Finite and periodic transfer-current determinants (§4; local physical PDF pp. 16–20; printed article pp. 15–19)

**Theorem 1.1 (local physical PDF p. 4; printed article p. 3)**: for a finite connected \(D\)-regular graph (with self-loops allowed as in the paper’s setup), orient edges arbitrarily. For \(e=\overrightarrow{xy}\), define \(H(e,f)\) as the expected signed number of traversals of \(f\) by simple random walk started at \(x\) and stopped when it hits \(y\). For distinct edges \(e_1,\dots,e_k\), let \(M(i,j)=H(e_i,e_j)\). If \(T\) is the uniform spanning tree, then

\[
\mathbb P(e_1,\ldots,e_k\in T)=\det M.
\]

**Theorem 4.2 (statement and proof outline: local physical PDF p. 16, printed article p. 15; induction proof: local physical pp. 17–18, printed pp. 16–17)** extends the determinant to a connected, locally finite periodic graph with a finite \(\mathbb Z^d\)-fundamental domain, regularized by self-loops to common degree \(D\) with aperiodic simple random walk. For its uniform essential spanning forest, the inclusion probability of distinct edges is \(\det M\), where \(M(i,j)=D^{-1}g(e_i,e_j)\), and \(g(e_i,e_j)=g^{\delta_x-\delta_y}(z)-g^{\delta_x-\delta_y}(w)\) for \(e_i=x\vec y\), \(e_j=z\vec w\). Theorem 4.1 on physical p. 16 identifies the constructed Green-function solution \(g\) with the classical Green function \(H\); Corollary 4.4 on physical p. 19 (printed article p. 18) gives mixed inclusion/exclusion minors. For the proof, Lemma 4.3 (physical p. 17) identifies the inclusion probability after contracting previous edges with a bounded harmonic voltage difference, using the finite-contraction hitting-probability limit from Corollary 3.4. Induction then adjusts the unit-current potential by a linear combination of the earlier edge potentials to cancel their currents; the potential descends through the contraction, Lemma 4.3 gives the conditional inclusion probability, and a determinant ratio (Schur complement) completes the product. The singular-minor case is handled by a cycle among the earlier edges, forcing both probability and determinant to vanish. The article’s printed page 19 (local physical p. 20) also explains how to extend transfer impedances to finite connected nonregular graphs by adding self-loops; these loop additions do not change the electrical transfer impedance.

## Other full-text results (§§5–7; local physical PDF pp. 21–40; printed article pp. 20–39)

Section 5 computes the square-lattice Green function/current matrix explicitly (physical pp. 21–22) and studies high-degree finite UST limits. **Theorem 5.1 (statement: physical p. 23; factorial-moment proof: pp. 24–25; printed pp. 22–24)** sets \(r_k=\deg_*(v_k)\), \(A_k\) equal to the neighbors of \(v_k\), and \(D_k=\deg_T(v_k)-1\). Assume the transfer-impedance approximation (11) holds on \(A_k\) with error \(\varepsilon_k\to0\), and \(\sum_{w\in A_k}\deg_*(w)^{-1}\to1+\lambda\) for \(\lambda>0\); then \(D_k\Rightarrow\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\). The proof sums transfer-current determinants over ordered distinct incident-edge lists and uses Lemma 5.2 to obtain the limiting factorial moments. The section first gives a graph counterexample to Aldous’s earlier weaker degree hypothesis; do not simplify Theorem 5.1 to a degree-growth condition alone.

**Theorem 5.3 (statement: physical p. 26 / printed article p. 25; proof: physical pp. 27–28 / printed pp. 26–27)** assumes a sequence of finite graphs whose minimum and maximum non-loop degrees are both \((1+o(1))k\), and a uniform transfer-impedance approximation \(M(e,f)=k^{-1}(|e\cap f|+o(1))\). It proves convergence of the rooted uniform essential-spanning-forest component to the sin-tree: a singly infinite spine with independent critical Poisson(1) Galton–Watson bushes. The proof uses generalized moments \(N(T;t)\), transfer-current determinants and Lemma 5.2, then invokes the exponential-growth uniqueness criterion proved as Theorem 8.8. The stated consequence about two disjoint long paths is a bound for a rooted local neighborhood under this sequence of graph hypotheses; it is not a result about a global maximum path over the whole sample forest.

**Theorem 6.1 (statement: local physical PDF p. 29, printed article p. 28; proof: physical pp. 31–33, printed pp. 30–32)** identifies the uniform essential-spanning-forest law on the periodic graph as the unique translation-invariant measure with maximal Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy, and gives

\[
H_{\rm top}=\frac1k\int_{\mathbb T^d}\log\!\bigl(D^k\chi_{Q(\alpha)}(1)\bigr)\,d\alpha,
\]

where \(k\) is the number of vertex types in a fundamental domain. The proof compares arbitrary boundary-condition forests to translation-invariant spanning trees, uses the entropy-change bound when only \(O(n^{d-1})\) edges are altered, then diagonalizes the finite torus Laplacian and passes its log determinant to a Fourier integral. Explicit examples include \(\mathbb Z^2\).

Section 7 (physical pp. 33–40) constructs the Temperleyan bipartite graph from a nice planar graph, its dual, and the primal edges. Here “nice” requires finite degree at every vertex and finitely many boundary edges for every face, including the exterior face. Lemma 7.1 gives the finite tree/dual-tree and infinite one-ended/essential-forest correspondences. **Theorem 7.2 (statement: local physical p. 36, printed p. 35; proof: physical pp. 36–37, printed pp. 35–36)** says that for the stated \(\mathbb Z^2\)-periodic planar setting the induced domino-tiling measure is the unique shift-invariant measure of maximal entropy; its entropy per vertex is \(kH(\nu)/(2e)\). Corollary 7.3 gives the square-lattice case. This is a planar tree–dimer correspondence, not a general electrical-network theorem.

## Appendix and proof scope (§8; local physical PDF pp. 41–48; printed article pp. 40–47; references physical pp. 49–50)

The complete appendix proves the Green-function claims used above (**Theorem 8.1**, physical p. 41: convergence, symmetry, harmonic excess, boundedness/dipole boundedness), supplies local central-limit and Harnack proofs (**Theorems 8.2–8.3**, physical p. 43, and the derivation of Lemma 3.1 on pp. 44–45), and establishes tree-valued tightness/moment criteria (Propositions 8.4–8.7, physical pp. 45–46). **Theorem 8.8 (statement: physical p. 46; proof: pp. 46–48; printed pp. 45–47)** says that for a random locally finite rooted tree of height at most \(r\), if \(\mathbb E N(U;t)\le e^{c|t|}\), then the values \(\mathbb E N(U;t)\) over finite rooted test trees of height at most \(r\) uniquely determine its law. Its proof inducts on height, randomly orders child subtrees, and uses the factorial-moment uniqueness lemma (Lemma 8.9, physical pp. 46–47), whose exponential bound yields an entire Fourier series isolating each integer value. The references and end matter on physical pp. 49–50 (printed pp. 48–49) were read.

## W07 use and limits

For A141 this paper supplies the finite **unweighted** UST transfer-impedance determinant; the general positive-contraction and weighted-kernel formulation is covered by Lyons’s DPP paper and PTN. Neither this determinant nor negative association proves the BK disjoint-occurrence inequality. The high-dimensional local-tree and domino-entropy results are separate applications and are not used as a proof of A141.
