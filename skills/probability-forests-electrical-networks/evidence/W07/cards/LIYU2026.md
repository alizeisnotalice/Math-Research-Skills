# LIYU2026 — swap-invariant zero-one law for FSF and WSF

**Source:** Xinyi Li and Yao Yu, arXiv:2610.00938v1, 1 Oct 2026; local PDF `evidence/fulltexts/LIYU2026.pdf`. Full seven-page preprint read. This is a preprint, not a journal version in the source snapshot.

## Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 2–5)

Let (G=(V,E,c)) be connected, locally finite, infinite with (c(e)>0) on every edge. Under either its free spanning forest measure FSF or wired spanning forest measure WSF, every measurable event (A) unchanged under every admissible one-edge swap has probability 0 or 1. A swap removes one present edge and adds one absent edge, with both resulting configurations still spanning forests.

The theorem assumes neither transitivity nor unimodularity. It does not say that every update-stable event is swap-invariant; the authors explicitly leave Hutchcroft’s broader update-stability conjecture open.

## Proof chain

1. Identify FSF as a projection determinantal probability measure on the cycle-flow orthogonal complement (Theorem 2.3, citing the transfer-current theorem).
2. For an edge (e) with (0<\mathbb P(e\in F)<1), condition on (e\in F) by contracting (e), and condition on (e\notin F) by deleting it.
3. The cycle-flow subspaces for contracted and deleted networks differ by codimension one: the missing linear constraint is the potential difference across the endpoints of (e). The projection-DPP coupling theorem then couples the conditional forests to differ by one deletion and one insertion (Lemma 2.5). The WSF argument uses the analogous star-space formulation; if deletion disconnects the graph, take independent WSFs on the resulting components.
4. Swap invariance makes the conditional probabilities of (A) equal. Repeat after finitely many edge states have been revealed; edges of conditional probability 0 or 1 add no information.
5. The edge-revelation martingale (\mathbb E[1_A\mid\mathcal F_n]) is constant at every step and converges to (1_A), forcing (1_A\in\{0,1\}) a.s.

## Corollaries 3.2–3.3 (PDF pp. 5–6)

- The number of components of FSF or WSF is a.s. constant. One swap preserves the number because the common forest has either finitely many components, in which case both added edges reduce the count by one, or infinitely many, in which case both still have infinitely many.
- The total excessive-end statistic (\xi(F)=\sum_{T\in\mathrm{Comp}(F)}(\mathrm{ends}(T)-1)), with infinite cardinalities recorded as (\infty), is a.s. constant. The proof separates the case where deleting one edge leaves a finite component (adding a finite tree leaves ends unchanged) from the case where the swap joins/splits infinite trees (the excess count changes in the same way for both forests).
- Consequently, the event that every tree is one-ended has probability zero or one for FSF and WSF.

## Exact boundary for A025

This zero-one theorem does **not** prove that distinct FSF and WSF measures are mutually singular on every network. A025 requires a measurable separating event whose probability is 1 under one measure and 0 under the other. The theorem only controls swap-invariant events under each measure separately; it does not construct such an event or show the two measures assign different probabilities to one. The abstract and proof do not claim to resolve A025.
