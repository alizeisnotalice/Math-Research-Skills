# M0205 — Values of Brownian intersection exponents II: Plane exponents

**Reading record:** Entire 37-page preprint (PDF pp. 1–37), read. Identity verified on PDF p. 1: Lawler–Schramm–Werner, arXiv:math/0003156v3, 13 December 2000; manuscript dated 5 December 2000. The local preprint title page does not carry journal metadata; the DOI in the source ledger is the journal locator. Review pending.

## Exact exponent statements

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3): for $n\ge2$, the **time** exponent for $n$ independent planar Brownian motions to remain pairwise disjoint up to time $t$ is
\[
\zeta_n=\frac{4n^2-1}{24}.
\]
Thus two-path nonintersection decays as $t^{-5/8+o(1)}$, and simple random walk has the same exponent; Theorem 1.1 and the cited invariance/sharp-estimate work yield the two-sided $k^{-5/8}$ bound in the paper (PDF pp. 3–4).

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 4) proves for λ≥2
\[
\zeta(2,\lambda)=\frac{(5+\sqrt{24\lambda+1})^2-4}{96}.
\]
Corollary 1.3 (PDF p. 4) gives planar Brownian cut-point dimension $3/4$, using Lawler’s relation $2-2\zeta_2$. The theorem’s range $\lambda\ge2$ is essential: it does not by itself evaluate the disconnection limit $\eta_2=\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}2\zeta(2,\lambda)$.

The frontier conclusion is explicitly conditional on an additional analyticity theorem cited as forthcoming: Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 4) states $\lambda\mapsto\zeta(2,\lambda)$ is real analytic on $(0,\infty)$; combining this with Theorem 1.2 by analytic continuation yields Corollary 1.5, frontier dimension $4/3$ a.s. Likewise Corollary 1.6 gives pioneer-point dimension $7/4$ using analyticity of $\zeta(1,\lambda)$ from that companion result (PDF p. 5). These are frontier/pioneer dimensions, not dimensions of all Brownian trace points or of arbitrary Jordan arcs.

## Proof chain and interface

The proof uses radial/chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_6$, Brownian excursion measures, and $\pi$-extremal distance. Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.3 (PDF pp. 8–16) identify the SLE annulus derivative/extremal-distance decay. Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 17–21) identifies radial and chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_6$ up to the first disconnection and a random increasing time change. Lemma 4.3 (PDF pp. 21–22) obtains the small-quadrilateral crossing probability from Cardy’s formula. Section 5 characterizes Brownian exponents by excursion/extremal-distance integrals (PDF pp. 22–30). Section 6 computes $\xi(1,1)=5/4$ and $\xi(1,1,1,\lambda)$ by evaluating a common event involving one $\mathrm{SLE}_6$ trace and one or two Brownian excursions in two orders (PDF pp. 31–34); cascade relations and the analytic continuation then give the stated exponent/dimension results.

The paper cautions that its direct $\zeta(2,\lambda)$ calculation is only for $\lambda\ge2$ (PDF p. 4). The frontier $4/3$ deduction therefore depends on the analyticity result, not merely on substituting $\lambda=0$ into Theorem 1.2. For the Jordan-arc infimum B061, neither the frontier theorem nor the cut-point theorem supplies a lower bound for every arc in the trace.
