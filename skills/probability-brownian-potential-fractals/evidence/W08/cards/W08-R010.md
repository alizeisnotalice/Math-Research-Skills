## Setup and reusable lemmas

A co-adapted Brownian coupling is represented by predictable matrix processes $J,K$ with $J^\top J+K^\top K=I$, using independent driving Brownian motions; the reflected coordinates satisfy the Skorokhod equations in the given convex domain. A coupling is shy if its separation stays bounded below by some positive constant with positive probability.

- **Lemma 4 (PDF p. 4).** If a continuous function $\Psi$ on the compact set of separated pairs, stopped at first exit from the $\varepsilon$-separated region, gives a supermartingale after adding elapsed time for every co-adapted coupling, then every such coupling reaches $\varepsilon$-separation in finite time almost surely.
- **Lemma 5 (PDF p. 5).** A sufficient test is that $Z=\Phi(X,Y)$ has quadratic-variation density bounded below by a positive constant and drift bounded above by a finite constant until $\varepsilon$-separation; an exponential transform $c(1-e^{-\lambda\Phi})$ yields the supermartingale. The precise bounds are on the compact separated-pair region.
- **Lemma 6 (PDF pp. 5–6).** The matrix representation above covers arbitrary co-adapted Brownian pairs through predictable spectral decomposition/pseudoinverse construction.

## Main results

- **Theorem 7 (PDF pp. 6–8).** If $D\subset\mathbb R^n$ is bounded and convex and its boundary contains no line segment, reflected Brownian motion in $D$ has no co-adapted shy coupling. The proof chooses a pole $p\notin D$ and a small $\delta>0$ in
  \[
  V_{p,\delta}(x,y)=\tfrac12\|x-y\|^2+\tfrac\delta2(\|x-p\|^2-\|y-p\|^2).
  \]
  For each fixed positive separation $\varepsilon$, the boundary local-time contribution is nonpositive under the no-segment geometry and the quadratic variation is uniformly nondegenerate on the compact region of pairs separated by at least $\varepsilon$. Lemma 5 gives finite $\varepsilon$-coupling.
- **Theorem 8 (PDF pp. 8–13).** Every bounded convex planar domain, including one whose boundary has line segments, has no co-adapted shy reflected-Brownian coupling. The proof takes finitely many pole potentials, forms a symmetric minimum after regularization, and uses Tanaka’s formula to retain the semimartingale drift and volatility bounds. A large-circle family of poles handles parallel flat pieces; the active-pole geometry and a small-$\delta$ boundary estimate yield the same finite-$\varepsilon$ conclusion.

## Limits

Theorem 7 is all dimensions but excludes boundary line segments; Theorem 8 removes that restriction only in dimension two. Both concern **co-adapted** reflected couplings. They do not rule out arbitrary non-co-adapted couplings and do not establish a spectral-gap tail bound for acute triangles. The paper’s simply connected/nonconvex extensions are questions or conjectures, not theorems. This is a potential-function method reference, not a direct A020 construction. Review pending.
