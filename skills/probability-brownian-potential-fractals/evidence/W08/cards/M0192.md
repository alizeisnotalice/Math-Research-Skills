## Statements and exact scope

Let $X$ be standard planar Brownian motion started at 0. A cut point at time $t$ means $X([0,t))\cap X((t,1])=\varnothing$; the theorem also requires that the point $X(t)$ has no other preimage in $[0,1]$.

- **Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 2).** For every $\epsilon>0$, with strictly positive probability there is such a cut point at $t\in(0,1)$, with the two path portions additionally lying in the angular sectors printed in the theorem: the past has argument in $[0,\pi]$, and the future in $[\pi-\epsilon,2\pi]$, measured relative to $X(t)$.
- **Theorem 2.2 (PDF p. 2).** Almost surely, for every $\epsilon>0$, there is a cut point time $t\in(0,\epsilon)$. The result is about existence arbitrarily close to the starting time, not the Hausdorff dimension of the cut-point set.
- **Theorem 2.3 (PDF p. 2).** Almost surely, for each $\epsilon>0$, there are $s\in(1/2-\epsilon,1/2)$ and $t\in(1/2,1/2+\epsilon)$ whose open middle subpath is disjoint from the path outside $(s,t)$; the endpoints are singly visited as quantified there. Corollary 2.1 extends the existence statements to three-dimensional Brownian motion (PDF p. 2).

## Proof mechanism

The proof is constructive and uses potential theory rather than an exponent-to-dimension theorem (outline, PDF pp. 3–4; detailed proofs, §§3–4, PDF pp. 4–22).

1. For Brownian motion conditioned by a half-plane $h$-process to exit at a designated boundary point, Lemmas 4.1–4.4 build a random family of small boundary-near balls missed by the path with uniformly positive conditional probability. Boundary Harnack bounds the chance of hitting each ball; geometric spacing makes the cumulative hit probability summable.
2. After an initial path segment, missed balls create room for a later segment to make large downward excursions without hitting the past. Lemmas 4.5–4.7 control the expected vertical displacement before the conditioned process exits the complement.
3. Theorem 2.1 uses a sequence of approximate cut points and a renewal-type construction; as the gap parameter tends to zero, a subsequence converges to a true cut point with the angular separation.
4. Theorem 2.2 is not obtained by applying the germ zero–one law (the relevant event is not in the germ sigma-field). Scaling and strong Markov arguments across annuli give repeated positive-probability opportunities, hence almost-sure occurrence arbitrarily near 0 (PDF p. 3 and §4).

## Limits for B061

A cut point is a topological separation point of the Brownian trace. This paper proves existence, not a Hausdorff dimension for cut points and not a lower or upper bound for the infimum over Jordan arcs contained in the trace. A cut-point theorem alone does not show that either side is a Jordan arc of a specified dimension. Keep it separate from the frontier dimension and from Burdzy’s labyrinth-dimension supremum.
