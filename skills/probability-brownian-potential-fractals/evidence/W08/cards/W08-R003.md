# W08-R003 — Synchronous couplings of reflected Brownian motions in smooth domains

**Reading record:** Full arXiv PDF, 68 pages (PDF pp. 1–68), read. Identity verified from PDF p. 1: Burdzy–Chen–Jones, arXiv:math/0501486v1, 27 January 2005. Review pending.

## Exact setup and conclusions

Let $D\subset\mathbb R^2$ be bounded, connected, and $C^4$-smooth, not necessarily simply connected. In addition to $C^4$, the paper assumes: each inward normal direction occurs at at most $N_1<\infty$ boundary points; curvature vanishes at only finitely many points; at each zero-curvature point the local boundary graph has a nonzero cubic coefficient (PDF p. 1). For one standard planar Brownian motion $B$, the two coordinates solve
\[
X_t=x_0+B_t+\int_0^t n(X_s)\,dL_s^X,\qquad
Y_t=y_0+B_t+\int_0^t n(Y_s)\,dL_s^Y,
\]
with inward unit normal and boundary local times. This is synchronous coupling; each coordinate is a reflected Brownian motion, and the pair is strong Markov (PDF pp. 1–2).

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2): if $D$ has at most one hole, then $d(X_t,Y_t)\to0$ a.s. for every initial pair in $D\times D$.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 2–3) defines
\[
\Lambda(D)=\int_{\partial D}\nu(x)\,dx+
\int_{\partial D}\int_{\partial D}|\log\cos\alpha(x,y)|\,\omega_x(dy)\,dx,
\]
where $\nu$ is the signed curvature in the paper’s inward-normal local graph convention, $\alpha$ is the angle between tangent lines in $[0,\pi/2]$, and $\omega_x$ is the boundary measure defined from the Martin kernel with pole at $x$, normalized by $\pi d(x,y)^2\omega_x(dy)/dy\to1$ as $y\to x$. If $\Lambda(D)>0$, then for every initial pair
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\log d(X_t,Y_t)=-\frac{\Lambda(D)}{2|D|}\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
The first integral is $2\pi(1-h)$ by Gauss–Bonnet, where $h$ is the number of holes; the second is nonnegative.

## Proof chain and open interface

The 2005 paper’s main technical proof is in §§3–4 (PDF pp. 7–66). It separates the logarithmic distance change into a local curvature term and contractions at large excursions; excursion theory and ergodicity then yield the strong-law rate in Theorem 1.2. Theorem 1.1 follows because with at most one hole the Gauss–Bonnet term is nonnegative and the double integral is positive (proof on PDF p. 4).

Problem 2.1 (PDF p. 4) asks whether a bounded domain can have positive-probability $\limsup_{t\to\infty}d(X_t,Y_t)>0$, and whether some bounded domain has $\Lambda(D)<0$. Conjecture 2.2 says $\Lambda(D)<0$ should imply nonconvergence a.s.; both the existence and conjectural implication are unresolved in this paper. The exterior disk and ellipse calculations give $\Lambda=0$ (Propositions 2.3–2.4, PDF pp. 5–8); the exterior of a line segment would be a negative candidate but is excluded because its boundary is nonsmooth.

## Migration limits

- This is **synchronous** coupling. It does not settle A020, which concerns mirror/other couplings in an acute triangle.
- Its smooth-domain result is bounded $C^4$, with the listed curvature/normal-fiber assumptions. A generic smooth domain is not automatically covered.
- It proves convergence for at most one hole; for multiple holes it gives a conditional exponent only when Λ(D)>0. It does not prove B062’s sought nonconvergence example.
- Exterior-of-disk examples are unbounded and have Λ=0; they do not directly instantiate B062’s bounded connected domain.
