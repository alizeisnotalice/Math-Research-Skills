## Definitions and theorem scopes

- **Markov coupling and shy coupling (§2, PDF p. 2).** Unless stated otherwise, the paper's couplings satisfy both: (i) $X$, $Y$, and $(X,Y)$ are Markov, and $X,Y$ have identical transition probabilities; (ii) for every $x,y,s$, the conditional law of the future path $(X_t)_{t\ge s}$ given $(X_s,Y_s)=(x,y)$ equals its conditional law given $X_s=x$. The paper notes that this definition differs from similar notions and explicitly leaves whether results hold under other definitions as an open question. A coupling is shy when, from some distinct starting pair, the probability that the coordinates remain a positive distance apart for all times is positive.
- **Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 4–7), graph Brownian motion.** On the paper’s metric graph (finite vertex degrees and edge lengths bounded below by $r_0>0$; degree-two vertices can be suppressed), if every vertex has degree at least three, the authors construct a shy Markov coupling. The construction uses independent motions at large separation and a correlated distance process in the intermediate regime, with a skew-Brownian prescription at vertices that prevents the distance from falling below the chosen positive threshold. This is a graph result, not a reflected planar-domain theorem.
- **Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 21–23), bounded convex domains.** For reflected Brownian motion in a bounded convex domain, a reflected coupling with the source’s strictly increasing separation quadratic-variation lower control cannot be shy; the second assertion also rules out shy couplings under the stated vanishing normalized quadratic variation condition when the domain is strictly convex. Keep these as the theorem’s separate alternatives and retain its co-adapted/reflected-coupling scope.
- **Theorem 4.3 (PDF pp. 24–32), planar result in the paper's default class.** For a bounded convex planar domain with $C^1$ boundary containing no line segment, there is no shy coupling of the reflected Brownian motions among the paper's §2 Markov couplings satisfying (i)–(ii). The theorem is not stated for arbitrary couplings and should not be described as a coupling-class strengthening of a co-adapted theorem.

## Proof routes and limits

For the graph result the distance process is designed to have a positive lower barrier while retaining the required marginals; the vertex excursions are synchronized by labels and a skew-Brownian boundary prescription. For the convex-domain obstruction, Itô’s formula for a negative power of separation combines with convex normal terms and a martingale law of the iterated logarithm; strict convexity plus local-time/ergodic control handles the second alternative. Theorem 4.3 instead uses deterministic pursuit geometry: a Brownian path has positive small-ball probability of following a finite sequence of uniformly useful pursuit moves, and the strictly convex $C^1$ boundary geometry makes the distance decrease until any prescribed positive separation is reached.

The source’s open problems distinguish multiply connected planar domains and domains with flat boundary segments. Do not apply Theorem 4.3 to a polygon or to an arbitrary smooth domain with holes; do not identify the graph coupling with mirror coupling in A020. The theorem concerns shy-ness, not a sharp exponential meeting-time tail or the B061 path infimum. Review pending.
