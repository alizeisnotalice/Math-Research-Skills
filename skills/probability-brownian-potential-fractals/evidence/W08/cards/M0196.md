## Results

The paper studies the limit as the number of planar Brownian paths in the packets tends to infinity; it does not give the exact finite-packet exponents.

- **Proposition 1 (PDF p. 2).** If $\eta_n$ is the disconnection exponent for $n$ independent planar Brownian paths, then
  $$
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\eta_n}{n}=\frac12.
  $$
- **Proposition 2 (PDF p. 2).** For fixed $k$ and positive $a_1,\ldots,a_k$, the $k$-packet non-intersection exponents satisfy
  $$
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\xi_k(\lfloor na_1\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor na_k\rfloor)}{n}
  =\frac12\left(\sum_{j=1}^k\sqrt{a_j}\right)^2.
  $$
  The source defines these exponents by radial exit probabilities for packets of independent Brownian paths; each exponent is logarithmic in the radius (PDF pp. 2, 7).

## Proof structure

1. For Proposition 1, scaling and subadditivity first identify the disconnection exponent as a supremum of finite-scale rates. A finite net of paths approximates any path in a thin sausage. Beurling’s estimate controls the probability of avoiding each net path; then the path-count limit and a fixed-half-line lower bound give the matching asymptotic $1/2$ (§2, PDF pp. 3–6).
2. For Proposition 2, subadditivity in radius and packet counts reduces real weights to integer weights (§3.1–3.2, PDF pp. 7–8). To obtain the **upper bound on the exponent**, §3.3 constructs disjoint angular cones with apertures proportional to $\sqrt{a_j}/\sum_i\sqrt{a_i}$; Brownian cone-exit estimates give a lower bound on the probability of the corresponding non-intersection event, hence an upper bound on its logarithmic decay exponent (PDF pp. 8–13). For the **lower bound on the exponent**, the proof partitions the annulus into colored regions and uses extremal-distance/area estimates to upper-bound excursion probabilities; Cauchy–Schwarz optimizes the allocation, while spider and coloring lemmas control the geometry and passage to large packet counts (§§3.3–3.7, PDF pp. 8–20). Section 4 states remarks and open conjectures; references are on PDF p. 22.

## Scope limits

These are large-packet asymptotics for exponents, not exact values at a fixed number of paths and not a dimension theorem for Brownian frontier or trace arcs. In particular, they provide no estimate of B061’s infimum over Jordan arcs in the Brownian trace. Review pending.
