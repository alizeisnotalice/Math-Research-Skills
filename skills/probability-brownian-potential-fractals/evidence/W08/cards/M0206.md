## Definition and theorem

For integer $k\ge1$, let $X_1,\ldots,X_k$ be independent planar Brownian motions from 0, and let $Y,Y_1,\ldots$ be independent planar Brownian motions from 1. Given the union $X[0,t]=\bigcup_{j=1}^kX_j[0,t]$, set
$$
\Xi_t=\mathbb P\{Y[0,t]\cap X[0,t]=\varnothing\mid X[0,t]\}.
$$
The paper defines $\xi(k,\lambda)=-2\lim_{t\to\infty}\log\mathbb E[\Xi_t^\lambda]/\log t$ for $\lambda>0$ (PDF pp. 1–2).

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3).** For each integer $k\ge1$, the map $\lambda\mapsto\xi(k,\lambda)$ is real analytic on $(0,\infty)$.
- **Corollary 1.2 (PDF p. 3).** For every integer $k\ge1$ and $\lambda\ge0$,
  $$
  \xi(k,\lambda)=\frac{\bigl(\sqrt{24k+1}+\sqrt{24\lambda+1}-2\bigr)^2-4}{48}.
  $$
  For positive $\lambda$, analytic continuation extends the previously proved formula from $\lambda\ge1$. At $\lambda=0$, continuity is supplied by the cited earlier result [8], not by analyticity at zero. In particular, $\eta_2=\xi(2,0)=2/3$.
- **Corollary 1.3 (PDF p. 3).** The outer frontier of planar Brownian motion has Hausdorff dimension $4/3$ almost surely, using the cited identity $\dim_H F=2-\eta_2$ from [6]. This corollary is a frontier statement.

## Proof chain and analytic hypotheses

The proof reduces to $k=2$, since the other integer $k$ cases are said to be essentially the same (PDF p. 4). It constructs a Banach space of functions on pairs of paths to the unit circle whose dependence on the path near 0 decays exponentially (§2, PDF pp. 4–7). The operator $T_\lambda$ adjoins Brownian extensions and weights the event by the conditional avoidance probability to power $\lambda$. Its iterates encode the scale-by-scale avoidance probabilities. Proposition 2.1 identifies $e^{-\xi(2,\lambda)}$ as an isolated simple leading eigenvalue, with the rest of the spectrum strictly inside its modulus (PDF p. 6).

The proof then verifies the two ingredients for analytic perturbation:

1. **Operator analyticity (§4, PDF pp. 10–15).** An $h$-process in the complementary domain and a coupling with rapidly forgetting path segments control perturbations in $\lambda$. Expanding the weight in powers of $\lambda$ gives a locally convergent operator-valued power series in the Banach norm.
2. **Spectral isolation (§§3, 5; PDF pp. 7–9, 16–22).** Subadditivity and two-sided nonintersection estimates identify the exponential growth rate. A weighted Brownian-path coupling yields convergence of normalized iterates to a positive eigenfunction and bounds the operator on the complementary subspace, producing the simple isolated eigenvalue.
3. Analytic perturbation of an isolated simple eigenvalue gives analyticity of $e^{-\xi(2,\lambda)}$, hence of $\xi(2,\lambda)$ locally at each $\lambda>0$ (PDF pp. 4, 22–23).

## Exact interface with M0205 and limits

M0205, Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 5), uses $\zeta(2,\lambda)$ for the time-decay exponent of packet nonintersection, whereas this paper’s conditional-probability definition has $\xi(2,\lambda)$ equal to twice that exponent. Thus $\xi(2,\lambda)=2\zeta(2,\lambda)$; their analyticity assertions are the same theorem in the two normalizations. M0205 Theorem 1.2 gives the exponent formula initially for $\lambda\ge2$, and M0206’s analyticity plus continuity at zero completes the value $\eta_2=2/3$. M0206’s dimension corollary concerns the frontier, not the family of all Jordan arcs in a Brownian trace; it does not resolve B061. Review pending.
