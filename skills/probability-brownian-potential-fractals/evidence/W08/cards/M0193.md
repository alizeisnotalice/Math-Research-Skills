## Theorems and model

For $n\in\{2,3\}$ and integers $k,m\ge1$, take independent $n$-dimensional Brownian motions $X^1,\ldots,X^k$ started at 0 and $Y^1,\ldots,Y^m$ started at the same unit vector. Stop each path at its first exit from the radius-$r$ ball. Define $p_r$ as the probability that the two packets have no cross-intersections up to these exits. Define $q_r$ analogously for the corresponding simple random walks on $\mathbb Z^n$, with the source’s starting positions and exit convention (PDF pp. 1–2).

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2).** The limits
  $$
  \xi(n,k,m)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{-\log p_r}{\log r}
  =\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{-\log q_r}{\log r}
  $$
  exist and are equal.
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2).** For the corresponding fixed-time events $a_r,b_r$, the limits
  $$
  \lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{-\log a_r}{\log r}
  =\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{-\log b_r}{\log r}
  =\frac{\xi(n,k,m)}{2}
  $$
  exist. Here $a_r,b_r$ refer to Brownian motion and random-walk packet nonintersection over the source’s fixed time horizon $r$, not the exit-radius stopping scheme.

## Proof chain

The paper develops a detailed proof for the representative case $n=3,k=m=1$, with the other packet counts and $n=2$ handled by the analogous argument and additional notation (PDF p. 2 and §3).

1. Subadditivity under Brownian scaling gives existence of logarithmic exponents for exit-ball probabilities.
2. The proof of the Brownian/random-walk equality brackets liminf and limsup exponents. It separates initial and terminal pieces of paths, uses boundary Harnack estimates to control exit distributions, and transfers finite-scale separated events by the invariance principle.
3. A finite number of separated opportunities on geometric annuli, plus a non-isolated-intersection lemma, yields the reverse exponent inequality and closes the limit comparison (§3, PDF pp. 7–18).
4. The fixed-time conclusion follows from the exit-time comparison and scaling estimates stated in the proof of Theorem 1.2.

## Scope and non-transfer

This is an existence and invariance-principle result; it does not compute the numerical value of $\xi(n,k,m)$, prove a Hausdorff-dimension formula, or concern a Jordan arc in the planar Brownian trace. The equality of Brownian and random-walk exponents is for the stated dimensions, packet counts, initial configuration, and stopping/time conventions. Review pending.
