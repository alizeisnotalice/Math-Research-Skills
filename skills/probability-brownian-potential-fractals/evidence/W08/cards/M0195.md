# M0195 — Hausdorff Dimension of Cut Points for Brownian Motion

**Reading record:** Entire 20-page EJP PDF read (PDF pp. 1–20; printed pp. 1–20). Title, author, and journal coordinates verified on PDF p. 1. Independent review: pending.

## Definitions and exact theorem

For Brownian motion \(B\) in \(\mathbb R^d\), \(d=2,3\), a time \(t\in[0,1]\) is a cut time of \(B[0,1]\) if \(B[0,t)\cap B(t,1]=\varnothing\); \(B(t)\) is then a cut point. Define \(\zeta_d\) by the non-intersection exponent for two independent Brownian motions started at distinct points on the unit sphere:
\[
\mathbb P(B^1[0,T_n^1]\cap B^2[0,T_n^2]=\varnothing)=n^{-2\zeta_d+o(1)}.
\]
Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3) states \(\dim_H L=1-\zeta_d\) almost surely, where \(L\) is the cut-time set. Consequently \(\dim_H B(L)=2(1-\zeta_d)\) a.s. by Kaufman’s simultaneous dimension-doubling theorem (PDF p. 4). In three dimensions, every continuous curve joining the Brownian endpoints inside the path range contains the cut-point set, so the paper’s defined endpoint-connecting percolation dimension is at least \(2(1-\zeta_3)>1\) (Corollary 1.2, PDF pp. 3–4; uses \(\zeta_3<1/2\)).

## Proof chain and assumptions

1. Establish sharp up-to-constant non-intersection estimates \(a_n\asymp n^{-2\zeta}\) for two paths stopped at radius \(n\), and \(b_n\asymp n^{-\zeta}\) for the fixed-time event of non-intersection on the relevant time windows (PDF pp. 4, 9–18). The exact value of \(\zeta\) is not needed.
2. For upper dimension, cover cut times by dyadic intervals whose two Brownian subpaths do not intersect. The first-moment estimate gives \(\mathbb E K_n\le C2^{n(1-\zeta)}\); Markov plus Borel–Cantelli yields \(\dim_H L\le1-\zeta\) a.s. (PDF pp. 5–6).
3. For the lower bound, define a random measure supported on dyadic intervals whose past/future path segments avoid one another. A second-moment estimate (Lemma 2.3, PDF p. 6; proved with the two-point bound in §4, pp. 18–20) gives bounded expected \((1-\zeta-\varepsilon)\)-energy. A positive-probability Frostman/energy argument yields the lower bound; scale/zero–one arguments upgrade it to a.s. in the stated dimensions (PDF pp. 6–9).
4. The exact cut-point dimension follows by applying simultaneous Brownian dimension doubling to the random cut-time set (Kaufman theorem cited; M0224, Thm. 9.28 gives the full checked book statement).

All exponents are asymptotic or up-to-constant probability estimates; this paper does not compute a universal Hausdorff measure constant.

## Migration limits for B061

- This article’s **percolation dimension** is defined using continuous curves that connect the two prescribed endpoints \(0\) and \(B(1)\), not the infimum over all Jordan arcs in the trace containing any two distinct points.
- The paper proves the cut-time/cut-point formulas in dimensions 2 and 3, but the strict percolation-dimension lower bound it explicitly derives is for \(d=3\). It does not solve the planar Jordan-arc infimum.
- A cut-point lower bound for every endpoint-connecting curve in 3D cannot be transferred to arbitrary arcs in a planar trace.
