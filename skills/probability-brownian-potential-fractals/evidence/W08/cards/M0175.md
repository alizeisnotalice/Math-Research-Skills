## Statement and definitions

The paper's Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2) states that for planar Brownian motion on a fixed nontrivial compact time interval, the frontier (the boundary of the filled hull / outer boundary of the path) has Hausdorff dimension \(4/3\) almost surely. The same theorem announcement gives dimensions of other distinguished subsets (cut points \(3/4\), pioneer points \(7/4\)); those are not the full Brownian trace dimension or the Jordan-arc infimum.

The dimension identity is presented through the two-sided disconnection exponent: \(\dim_H(\mathrm{frontier})=2-\xi(2,0)\), and the exact exponent value yields \(4/3\) (PDF pp. 2–4). The paper identifies the supporting intersection-exponent results and gives an outline; the technical probability estimates and complete proofs are in the cited papers, especially the sharp non-intersection exponent work. Do not confuse the pioneer-point subset dimension \(7/4\) with the full Brownian trace dimension, which is 2, or the frontier dimension, which is \(4/3\).

## Proof architecture (as given in this article)

1. Define Brownian intersection exponents by the decay rate of non-intersection probabilities under simultaneous dilation (PDF §§2–3). The exponent normalization must be retained: the frontier uses the two-sided disconnection exponent, not a generic Brownian intersection exponent.
2. Establish the exact disconnection exponent from the intersection-exponent formulae, giving \(\xi(2,0)=2/3\) (PDF §§3–4 and cited detailed papers).
3. Relate the frontier Hausdorff dimension to this exponent using scale-dependent frontier-time events, one- and two-point estimates, and a Hausdorff/Frostman argument. The full estimates are only sketched here; consult M0172 and the detailed references before reproducing the proof.

No universal Hausdorff measure constant is claimed. The value is an almost-sure dimension statement.

## Critical distinction for B061

The frontier is the outer boundary of the filled Brownian range. B061 asks for the infimum of Hausdorff dimensions over every Jordan arc contained in the unfilled Brownian trace. Those sets and quantifiers differ. The frontier theorem cannot be substituted for a theorem about that infimum, even though both are subsets/geometry associated with the same Brownian path. Burdzy's labyrinth-dimension theorem concerns a **supremum** over Jordan arcs and is also a different question.

The trace of SLE\(_6\) has dimension \(1+6/8=7/4\) by Beffara’s chordal SLE theorem (M0179); \(4/3\) here is the Brownian frontier dimension. Neither value identifies the Brownian-trace Jordan-arc infimum. Keep each object and optimization operator explicit.

## Migration limits

- This paper is an announcement / proof outline. Use its theorem for the frontier value, and cite the detailed exponent and dimension arguments separately when a proof is needed.
- It is not a theorem that the Brownian trace has Hausdorff dimension 4/3; the trace itself has dimension 2. The SLE\(_6\) trace dimension \(7/4\) is a separate theorem about a different random curve.
- It does not provide an upper or lower bound for B061's Jordan-arc infimum without additional arguments.
