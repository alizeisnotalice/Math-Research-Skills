## Model, theorem and proof route

The base diffusion is $dX_t=b(X_t)dt+\sigma dB_t$ on $\mathbb R^d$, with constant invertible $\sigma$, locally Lipschitz $b$, and the assumed non-explosion of the solution. The norm is $\|z\|=(z\cdot Gz)^{1/2}$, for positive definite $G$; the standard and intrinsic choices are $G=I$ and $G=(\sigma\sigma^\top)^{-1}$. In equations (2)–(3) (PDF p. 3), before the meeting time $T$ the reflected process solves $dY_t=b(Y_t)dt+\sigma(I-2e_te_t^\top)dB_t$, where
\[
e_t=\frac{\sigma^{-1}(X_t-Y_t)}{|\sigma^{-1}(X_t-Y_t)|},\qquad T=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t=Y_t\},
\]
and $Y_t=X_t$ for $t\ge T$. Thus the reflection is applied to the driving Brownian noise along the transformed separation direction; for general $\sigma$ it is not reflection directly across the hyperplane orthogonal to $X_t-Y_t$. The matrix $I-2e_te_t^\top$ is orthogonal, so Lévy's characterization gives the reflected stochastic integral a Brownian marginal, and hence $Y$ retains the diffusion law.

For r>0, the paper defines
κ(r)=inf{−2 |σ^−1(x−y)|² (x−y)·G(b(x)−b(y))/||x−y||⁴ : ||x−y||=r}.
Assumption (7), PDF p. 5, is that κ is continuous on (0,∞), lim inf as r→∞ κ(r)>0, and ∫₀¹ r κ(r)^− dr<∞. The paper defines R₀ as the first radius beyond which κ≥0, and R₁≥R₀ by the exact source definition R₁=inf{R≥R₀: κ(r)R(R−R₀)≥8 for every r≥R}. Here R is the candidate threshold in the infimum, while r ranges over all r≥R. These are finite under (7).

  For the concave cost f specified in equations (10)–(11), the reflected pair satisfies t↦e^{ct} E[f(||X_t−Y_t||)] decreasing. Thus the induced Kantorovich distance contracts at rate c. The proof begins with Itô's formula for the separation process (PDF pp. 14–15), bounds its drift using κ, chooses f to make the drift at most −cf, and applies the supermartingale/optional-stopping argument in §4 (PDF pp. 14–16).
- **Remark 3 (PDF p. 6).** The assertions of Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 also hold for normal reflection on smooth bounded convex domains, using the same curvature condition with the infimum restricted to $x,y\in D$; the paper's argument is that boundary local-time terms decrease the distance for convex domains. This extension explicitly chooses $G=I_d$. If the sets over which the infima in (8) or (9) are taken are empty, the respective constants $R_0$ or $R_1$ are set equal to $\operatorname{diam}(D)$. It does not establish the reflected-domain claim for a general anisotropic metric $G$ and does not cover a nonsmooth polygonal triangle.
- **Product diffusion applications (PDF §§3.1, 6–7, pp. 11–14 and 21–25).** Componentwise approximate reflection with a cutoff produces the stated marginal Brownian motions and product-space contraction estimates. It is not an exact mirror coupling theorem for reflected Brownian motion in an acute triangle.

## Migration limits

Use this as a general auxiliary mechanism for reflection coupling and contraction under its diffusion and curvature hypotheses. It does not prove A020's spectral tail rate, and it does not transfer directly to nonsmooth triangular reflection or to a question quantified over all couplings. Keep this distinct from mirror coupling in W08-R004 and synchronous coupling in B062.
