# W08-R005 — The dimension of the Brownian frontier is greater than 1

**Identity/version.** Christopher J. Bishop, Peter W. Jones, Robin Pemantle and Yuval Peres, arXiv:math/9508222v1, submitted 2 Aug 1995. The arXiv PDF says the work appeared in modified form in *Journal of Functional Analysis* 143 (1997), 309–336, DOI 10.1006/jfan.1996.2928. ## Exact conclusions

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 2–3).** For planar Brownian motion over the fixed interval in the theorem, its frontier has Hausdorff dimension strictly larger than 1 almost surely. The theorem also gives a local version: for each open set $V$ meeting the frontier, the frontier portion in $V$ has dimension at least $1+\varepsilon$, with the positive improvement supplied by the proof. Read the time interval and locality quantifiers as printed; this is not a claim about every Jordan subarc of the trace.
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 4–5).** A random compact connected set $K$ satisfying the paper’s uniform conditional tile event—given the configuration outside the tile interior, with probability $>c_0$ it either $\eta_0$-surrounds the homothetic Gosper tile or misses its core—has frontier dimension at least $1+\varepsilon(c_0)$, locally at every frontier point. This is a sufficient random-set criterion, not a conclusion for arbitrary connected random sets.

## Proof mechanism

1. §§1–2 (PDF pp. 2–12) set up a family of self-similar Gosper tiles, Whitney squares and a scale tree. A tile is declared successful using the conditional surround-or-miss event; geometric separation makes the selected descendants into a nested random construction.
2. §§3–5 (PDF pp. 13–23) use Jones’ travelling-salesman $\beta^2$-sum to force a uniform expected offspring surplus. A filtration/branching argument then produces a boundary set of dimension greater than 1. The comparison from the tree boundary to the frontier is through the paper’s distance geometry, not through an identification of frontier points with trace arcs.
3. §§6–7 (PDF pp. 23–30) verify the tile condition for Brownian motion by adding independent exponential killing, conditioning on the outside, splitting according to visits to the tile core and using strong Markov plus Harnack estimates. Scaling and the paper’s zero–one/locality arguments give the stated Brownian frontier conclusion. The last section states a flatness result but expressly omits its proof and points elsewhere; do not cite that omitted proof as established here.

The quantitative output is an existence of a positive dimension increment; no sharp value of $\varepsilon$ is asserted in the card. The proof concerns the **frontier**, a boundary of the filled range. It neither proves nor implies a lower bound for
\[
\inf\{\dim_H J:J\subset B[0,1]\text{ is a Jordan arc with at least two points}\},
\]
and it is not the opposite labyrinth-dimension supremum either. Keep the B061 Jordan-arc family distinct from connected-set infima and from the frontier. Review pending.
