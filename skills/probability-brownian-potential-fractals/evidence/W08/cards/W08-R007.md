# W08-R007 — Loop-Erasure of Plane Brownian Motion

**Reading record:** Entire 10-page arXiv PDF read (PDF pp. 1–10; source manuscript pagination pp. 1–10). Title and author verified on p. 1. Independent review: pending.

## Definitions and theorem

The paper defines loop-erasure of a continuous curve by an increasing time map whose retained initial segment meets the corresponding terminal segment of the original curve only at the current retained point (p. 1). Any such loop-erasure is a simple curve with the same endpoints and contained in the original curve; existence and uniqueness are not automatic for general continuous paths (p. 1).

Theorem 2.1 (p. 2): Let \(D\subsetneq\mathbb C\) be a simply connected domain with \(0\in D\), and let \(B_C\) be standard complex Brownian motion started at 0, with independent real and imaginary standard Brownian coordinates (each variance \(t\)); stop at \(\tau_D=\inf\{t:B_C(t)\notin D\}\), which is a.s. finite for a proper simply connected plane domain. Almost surely the stopped path has a loop-erasure \((\gamma,w)\); \(\gamma\) is the reversal of radial \(\mathrm{SLE}_2\) in \(D\), growing from a random prime end toward 0, and the starting prime end has harmonic measure as seen from 0. This proves existence only. The author explicitly leaves uniqueness up to equivalence and a deterministic erasure algorithm open (pp. 2, 9).

## Proof chain and conditions

- Conformal invariance reduces the proof to the unit disk (p. 2). Conditioning the exit location to be a boundary point \(\rho\) uses the positive Poisson-kernel martingale \(P_\rho(B_C(t))\); the Doob/Girsanov transform has drift \(2\partial_zP_\rho/P_\rho\) (pp. 2–3, with the paper’s convention \(2\partial_z=\partial_x+i\partial_y\)). A conformal map to the right half-plane and time change identify the real coordinate as a completed BES(3) started from 1 and the imaginary coordinate as an independent Brownian motion; this gives convergence to \(\rho\) at the exit time (pp. 3–4).
- It suffices by rotational symmetry to couple the conditioned Brownian path for \(\rho=1\) to radial SLE\(_2\) from 1 to 0 (Theorem 2.2, p. 4). The SLE driving function is \(\sqrt2 B_t\), and its radial Loewner maps satisfy (3.1) (p. 4).
- For independent candidate paths, define the two-time function \(M(t_1,t_2)=Q_{t_2}(B_C^1(t_1))/Q_0(B_C^1(t_1))\), where \(Q_{t_2}\) is the normalized Poisson kernel transported by the radial Loewner map. The SLE local martingale property and harmonicity/Itô calculation make \(M\) a local martingale in each time separately (pp. 4–6; equation (4.1)). On stopping at disjoint nested Jordan curves, modulus distortion bounds \(|\log M|\); changing measure by this stopped density preserves both marginals and yields the desired commutation coupling on that localization (pp. 6–7).
- Extend over a countable exhaustion by rational polygonal separator pairs and a subsequential weak limit of the bounded density couplings (p. 8). Under the limit coupling, for each rational SLE time the first Brownian hitting point on the SLE trace is its tip; continuity and right-continuity extend this to every time. Reversing the SLE curve and using the hitting-time map gives the required loop-erasure (p. 8).

The key conclusion is containment of a simple curve in the Brownian range. The proof does **not** give its Hausdorff dimension. To deduce a numerical B061 upper bound from SLE dimension results, the radial-SLE dimension transfer must be explicitly justified; M0179 is written for chordal SLE in \(\mathbb H\), so do not cite it as if it directly states a radial result.

## Migration limits

The theorem is for planar Brownian motion stopped on exiting a simply connected domain with interior start. It neither treats reflected Brownian motion in a triangle nor supplies the reflected-coupling tail rate in A020. The h-transform in this proof is a conditioning device for a killed planar Brownian path and must not be conflated with reflection coupling of two Neumann diffusions.
