# W08-R002 — Mirror couplings and Neumann eigenfunctions

**Reading record:** Full arXiv PDF, 37 pages (PDF pp. 1–37), read. Identity verified from PDF p. 1: Atar–Burdzy, arXiv:math/0607400v1, posted 18 July 2006 (manuscript dated 6 July 2006). Review pending.

## What the paper proves

Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 7, 26–27) proves simplicity of the second Neumann eigenvalue for a bounded, strictly convex planar domain with $C^2$ boundary satisfying all five geometric assumptions in §2. In the paper's notation these are (PDF pp. 5–7):

1. **Assumption 2.1:** chords $[P_3,Q_3]$ and $[P_4,Q_4]$ exist with $(P_3,Q_3)=(P_4,Q_4)=\alpha$ and $P_1<P_3<P_4<P_6$. For admissible $[P,Q]$, if $P_1<P<P_3$ and $(P,Q)\ge(P)$, there is no active right boundary point; if $Q_4<Q<Q_6$ and $(P,Q)\ge-(Q)$, there is no active left boundary point. The primed analogue also holds.
2. **Assumption 2.2:** for a uniform $\nu>0$, whenever $P\in\partial_{\downarrow}D$, $Q\in\partial_{\uparrow}D$, $(P,Q)\in[\alpha-\nu,\alpha]$, and $P_3<P<P_4$, there is neither a lower-left nor an upper-right hinge; again impose the primed analogue.
3. **Assumption 2.3:** every chord in each designated admissible family has at least one hinge of the required lower-left/upper-right type and no hinge of the forbidden opposite type; impose the primed analogues.
4. **Assumption 2.4:** the reflected right-boundary and left-boundary curves have exactly one nontangential intersection, and both tangent lines there meet the prescribed ray from the chord endpoints; impose the primed analogues.
5. **Assumption 2.5:** every chord from one designated family intersects in $D$ every chord in the corresponding opposite primed family, and conversely for the other pair of families.

The named arcs/families, their ordering, and hinge terms are defined in §2; these concise descriptions are not replacements for checking the full definitions there. They are substantive geometric hypotheses: the theorem is not asserted for every smooth convex domain or every triangle.

The coupling in §3 is the **mirror coupling**: when both particles are interior, their Brownian increments are reflections of one another across a moving mirror line; boundary local-time pushes move that line. It is neither synchronous (same noise) nor the efficient coupling of A020. Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 17–25) constructs an invariant Lyapunov set for the mirror state under the assumptions and preserves the induced partial order. In §4, the authors use that order plus a uniform non-coalescence estimate conditional on not having met to obtain a second Neumann eigenfunction monotone in the order and show the eigenspace is one-dimensional.

Proposition 4.1 (PDF p. 28) formulates a sufficient invariant-separation property for a coupling to force a sign pattern of the second eigenfunction; the proposition does not require that coupling to be mirror, but this paper does not construct arbitrary couplings with the required property. The mirror coupling is the concrete construction supplying it in the main theorem.

## Proof interface and limits

- Theorem 2.1 is a spectral simplicity statement under the exact §2 geometric package, not an efficient coalescence-tail theorem.
- The reflected Brownian marginals and their reflection convention are set out in §3 (PDF pp. 8–10); the proof tracks boundary local times and mirror motion. The geometric Lyapunov set is invariant a.s.; it does not imply finite-time coalescence.
- The article does not prove the A020 acute-triangle tail bound, nor impossibility for all Markovian or non-Markovian couplings. Any use in A020 needs a separate verification that its domain and the requested coupling satisfy this article’s hypotheses.
- Use Theorem 2.1 only after checking all five assumptions. “Strictly convex (C^2)” alone is insufficient.
