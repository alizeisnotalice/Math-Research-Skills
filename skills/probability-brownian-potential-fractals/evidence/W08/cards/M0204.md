# M0204 — Values of Brownian intersection exponents I: Half-plane exponents

**Reading record:** Entire 44-page preprint (PDF pp. 1–44), read. PDF p. 1 identifies Lawler–Schramm–Werner, arXiv:math/9911084v2, 23 October 2000; the manuscript itself is dated 2000. Review pending.

## Main theorem and exact setting

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 4) concerns $p\ge2$ independent planar Brownian motions started at distinct points in the upper half-plane. It gives the time exponent $\widetilde\zeta_p=p(2p+1)/6$ for the event that all paths are pairwise disjoint up to time $t$ and remain in the half-plane; in particular for $p=2$ the half-plane-constrained two-path time exponent is $5/3$. The corresponding unconstrained two-path time exponent is $5/8$, stated as a prediction in the introduction and derived in the later plane-exponent papers, not as the main theorem here.

The proof chain is: chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_6$ locality/restriction (Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.4, PDF pp. 8–10); a generalized Cardy formula for $\mathrm{SLE}_6$, including derivative moments; the annulus/rectangle crossing exponent $u(\lambda)=(6\lambda+1+\sqrt{24\lambda+1})/6$ (Theorem 3.1, PDF pp. 26–29); then Brownian excursion measures, extremal distance and cascade relations transfer the SLE exponent to half-plane Brownian intersection exponents (Theorem 4.1, PDF pp. 37–44). The paper explicitly derives a class of exponents with first $k-1$ weights in $\{0,1/3,1,2,10/3,5,7\}$, with a remark that a subsequent paper extends the formula to all nonnegative weights (PDF p. 37).

## Proof conditions and limits

The radial/chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_6$ comparison used here is only up to first disconnection and up to an increasing random time change (Theorem 4.1 of M0205 gives a related formulation; do not claim full-curve equality). Locality is special to $\kappa=6$. The Brownian transfer uses conformal invariance of excursion measure and restriction, plus the exact extremal-distance geometry; it is not a generic transfer for arbitrary random curves.

This paper computes **half-plane** exponents. It does not itself prove the planar frontier dimension 4/3 or B061’s infimum over planar Jordan arcs. Its introduction points to subsequent papers for full-plane exponents and the frontier result (PDF p. 5).
