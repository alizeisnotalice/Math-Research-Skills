## Dimension tools (Chapter 4)

- **Mass distribution principle (Theorem 4.19, PDF p. 115):** If a finite nonzero Borel measure \(\mu\) on a metric set \(E\) satisfies \(\mu(V)\le C\,\operatorname{diam}(V)^\alpha\) for all closed \(V\) of sufficiently small diameter, then \(\mathcal H^\alpha(E)\ge \mu(E)/C>0\), hence \(\dim_H E\ge\alpha\). The argument closes an arbitrary cover and uses countable subadditivity.
- **Energy method (Theorem 4.27, PDF p. 119):** For a mass distribution, \(I_\alpha(\mu)=\iint |x-y|^{-\alpha}\,d\mu(x)d\mu(y)<\infty\) implies \(\mathcal H^\alpha(E)=\infty\), hence \(\dim_H E\ge\alpha\). The proof bounds Hausdorff content by the truncated energy integral over \(|x-y|<\varepsilon\); finiteness forces that truncated integral to vanish as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
- **Frostman/capacity (Theorems 4.30–4.32, PDF pp. 121–124):** For closed \(A\subset\mathbb R^d\), positive \(\mathcal H^\alpha(A)\) gives a probability measure with a Frostman bound; and \(\dim_H A=\sup\{\alpha:\operatorname{Cap}_\alpha(A)>0\}\). Closedness and Euclidean setting are part of the stated theorem. The proof uses dyadic tree flows / max-flow min-cut and dyadic annuli for the finite-energy direction.
- **Brownian image dimension (Theorem 4.29, PDF pp. 120–121):** For standard \(d\)-dimensional Brownian motion on \([0,1]\), \(\dim_H B[0,1]=2\) a.s. for \(d\ge2\); the lower bound uses occupation measure and \(\mathbb E I_\alpha(\mu)=c\int_0^1\int_0^1|t-s|^{-\alpha/2}dsdt<\infty\) for \(0<\alpha<2\) (the Gaussian negative moment also requires \(\alpha<d\)). The upper bound is from Brownian Hölder regularity. This is the dimension of the full range, not a theorem about dimensions of Jordan subarcs.

## Potential theory (Chapter 8)

- **Dirichlet boundary regularity (Theorem 8.5, PDF pp. 235–236):** For bounded open \(U\) and continuous boundary data, the Brownian exit expectation is harmonic in \(U\); it attains the data continuously at regular boundary points. If every boundary point is regular, it is the unique continuous harmonic solution. The Poincaré cone condition is sufficient (Theorem 8.3), while Wiener’s test supplies a sharp criterion in \(d\ge3\).
- **Last-exit/equilibrium measure (Theorem 8.8, PDF pp. 238–239):** For transient Brownian motion (unkilled in \(d\ge3\), or killed as specified in Definition 3.28), the last exit from compact \(\Lambda\) admits a finite equilibrium measure \(\nu\) with \(\mathbb P_x(B(\gamma)\in A,0<\gamma<T)=\int_A G(x,y)\,d\nu(y)\). It is a last-exit representation; \(\gamma\) is generally not a stopping time.
- **Polarity and hitting/capacity (Theorems 8.20, 8.24, PDF pp. 244–248):** A closed set is Brownian-polar exactly when it has zero capacity for the radial potential \(f(r)=\log(1/r)\) in \(d=2\) or \(f(r)=r^{2-d}\) in \(d\ge3\). For transient Brownian motion stopped as in the theorem and a closed target \(A\), \(\tfrac12\operatorname{Cap}_M(A)\le \mathbb P_0(\tau_A<T)\le\operatorname{Cap}_M(A)\), with Martin kernel \(M(x,y)=G(x,y)/G(0,y)\). The upper estimate uses the first-hit distribution and the lower estimate a second moment / Paley–Zygmund argument; constants are exactly those displayed, not an unspecified equivalence.
- **Wiener test (Theorem 8.37, PDF pp. 258–260):** In \(d\ge3\), \(x\) is regular for closed \(\Lambda\) iff \(\sum_{k\ge1}2^{k(d-2)}\operatorname{Cap}_{d-2}(\Lambda\cap\{2^{-k-1}\le|y-x|<2^{-k}\})=\infty\). The proof compares shell hitting probabilities to Martin capacity and applies a Borel–Cantelli argument for the convergent case.

## Intersections and conformal geometry (Chapter 9 and Appendix 11)

- **Independent Brownian intersections (Theorem 9.8, PDF pp. 271–273):** For integers \(d\ge2,p\ge2\) with \(p(d-2)<d\), the intersection of the ranges of \(p\) independent \(d\)-dimensional Brownian motions has a.s. Hausdorff dimension \(d-p(d-2)\). The proof uses percolation limit sets as stochastic codimension test sets, capacity/polarity, and a zero–one law. This is an intersection of independent ranges, not a loop-erased arc statement.
- **Simultaneous dimension doubling (Theorem 9.28, PDF pp. 289–292):** For Brownian motion in dimension \(d\ge2\), a.s. \(\dim_H B(A)=2\dim_H A\) for every \(A\subset[0,\infty)\) simultaneously. The proof first establishes a pathwise uniform bound on repeated visits to all dyadic cubes, then uses a deterministic covering argument; it is stronger than the fixed-set expectation argument in Theorem 4.33.
- **Radial/chordal SLE bridge (Appendix 11, PDF pp. 347–349):** The beginning of radial SLE in the disc and the conformal image of the beginning of chordal SLE in \(\mathbb H\), at the same \(\kappa\), are mutually absolutely continuous up to the stated initial stopping segment (the chapter states this citing LSW01b). Thus local dimension assertions can transfer on those segments. To transfer a theorem about a complete trace or a Brownian loop-erasure, also justify the passage from local segments to the whole compact curve; this appendix is a partial-proof overview, not that proof.
- The appendix’s SLE(6) computation illustrates an exponent-to-frontier/cut-point dimension pipeline (Theorems 11.9–11.10, PDF pp. 351–354). It treats Brownian hull boundaries and cut points; these dimensions do not determine the infimum over all Jordan arcs in the Brownian trace.

## Conditions and migration limits

1. Specify whether the object is a Brownian range, outer frontier, cut-point set, independent-path intersection, or loop-erased Jordan arc before quoting a dimension.
2. For energy arguments, verify the kernel, ambient dimension, finite measure, support, and the strict exponent integrability range. A positive dimension claim from finite energy is a lower bound at the energy exponent; equality needs a separate upper bound.
3. For potential-theoretic hitting comparisons, use the actual Martin kernel at a reference point and the killed/transient setup. The translation-invariant radial potential alone cannot generally estimate a hitting probability from a fixed start uniformly over translated targets.
4. The textbook’s Brownian path results and SLE overview provide auxiliary facts, not a proof of B061’s Jordan-arc infimum. Do not identify connected path subsets with Jordan arcs absent a separate theorem.
