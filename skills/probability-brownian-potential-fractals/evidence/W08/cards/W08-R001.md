# W08-R001 — Labyrinth dimension of Brownian trace

**Reading record:** Entire 35-page PDF read (PDF pp. 1–35). Author/title verified on p. 1; references on p. 35. Independent review: pending.

## Exact claim

For standard planar Brownian motion \(X\), almost surely the labyrinth dimension of \(X([0,1])\) is 2 (Theorem 1, PDF p. 1). Burdzy defines labyrinth dimension as
\[
\sup\{\dim_H J:J\subset K,\ J\text{ is a Jordan arc}\}.
\]
The stronger Theorem 1′ (PDF p. 2) says: for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a Jordan arc \(\Gamma\subset X([0,1])\) such that the exact Brownian gauge measure of \(X([0,1])\setminus\Gamma\) is below \(\varepsilon\). The gauge is \(\phi(r)=r^2\log(1/r)\log\log\log(1/r)\) for sufficiently small \(r\), the exact Hausdorff gauge of the planar Brownian trace cited to Taylor and Le Gall.

## Proof mechanism

The proof constructs a Jordan arc by a sequence of topological surgeries on a Brownian path, changing only sets of small exact-gauge measure. It relies on:

1. Two-dimensional Brownian motion has no local double cut points (Lemma 2.2, PDF pp. 21–22); the paper explicitly corrects earlier Burdzy cut-point claims/proofs on PDF p. 2.
2. Exact Hausdorff gauge measure of the trace and a strictly larger gauge for double points (PDF pp. 1–2, 15–17) allow self-intersections to be covered with arbitrarily small trace-gauge cost.
3. Reconnecting pieces inside simply connected neighborhoods preserves a continuous path while eliminating repeated crossings. A countable dense family of small neighborhoods and diagonal limiting order construct an injective continuous parametrization; the limiting Jordan arc retains all but arbitrarily small gauge mass (PDF pp. 10–21, with topological lemmas pp. 22–34).

The result is a supremum / existence statement. It does not assert that every Jordan arc has dimension 2, or that the **infimum** of dimensions of arcs in the trace is 2 or 1.

## Relevance and migration limits for B061

- B061 asks for the infimum over Jordan arcs in the Brownian trace. This theorem gives the supremum, equal to the ambient trace dimension 2. It therefore does not settle the infimum.
- Theorem 1′ is stronger than a dimension statement in one direction: it gives arcs capturing all but arbitrarily small mass for the exact trace gauge. It still gives no lower bound on the dimension of every arc.
- The proof is specifically planar standard Brownian motion on \([0,1]\); do not carry the argument to other domains, dimensions, or other path laws without checking the gauge and no-double-cut-point inputs.
