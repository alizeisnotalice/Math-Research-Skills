## Setup and results

Sections 2–4 consider conformally invariant measures on oriented bridges between boundary arcs, with the crossing exponent and restriction/conformal-invariance properties specified there. The strongest conclusions use the paper’s **complete conformal invariance (CCI)** hypothesis; retain that hypothesis rather than using conformal invariance in an informal sense.

- **Brownian excursion example, Lemma 2 (PDF p. 9).** The hull measure associated to Brownian excursions is a CCI measure with crossing exponent 1. Section 2.5 proves that unions of independent bridges give further CCI measures and adds their exponents (PDF pp. 12–13).
- **Theorem 6 (PDF pp. 17–18).** For the stated CCI measure and Brownian-excursion setup, the generalized intersection exponents exist and agree with the corresponding Brownian half-plane generalized exponents. The proof integrates a common two-bridge event in the two orders; complete conformal invariance identifies one rate, while the Brownian exponent definition identifies the other. A strict-concavity lemma supplies the needed logarithmic asymptotic comparison where direct order exchange alone is insufficient (PDF pp. 16–18).
- **Theorem 7 (PDF pp. 18–19).** For $p\ge2$ CCI measures, the ordered intersection exponent exists and is given recursively by the crossing exponent of the first measure against the generalized exponent of the remaining measures. The proof is induction in $p$, using complete conformal invariance and the cascade relation. Symmetry yields the unordered exponent for pairwise disjoint bridges (PDF p. 19).
- **Theorem 12 (PDF pp. 27–28).** The analogous annular intersection exponent exists for $p\ge2$ CCI measures and is expressed through the corresponding disk/half-plane exponent and the CCI crossing exponents. This is a separate annular result, not a full-trace law.

## Proof and application boundaries

The proof chain is: define bridge crossing exponents and CCI in §2; establish Brownian excursion as an example; define generalized intersection exponents in §3; identify them with Brownian exponents by the integration-order/cascade argument; then repeat the comparison for annuli in §4. Pages 28–37 (§5) explicitly label the discussion of self-avoiding walks and percolation as nonrigorous conjectures/predictions. The paper does not prove that either model has a scaling limit satisfying CCI, and its exponent results cannot be transferred to such models without that missing input. These are bridge-intersection exponents, not Hausdorff dimensions of the Brownian trace or Jordan-arc infima. Review pending.
