## Restriction law and Brownian excursion

A chordal restriction hull is a closed connected unbounded set $K\subset\overline{\mathbb H}$ with $K\cap\mathbb R=\{0\}$ and $\mathbb C\setminus K$ connected. For a simply connected test domain $\mathbb H\setminus A$ with $A$ bounded away from 0 and compatible with the chordal setup, let $\Phi_A:\mathbb H\setminus A\to\mathbb H$ be normalized to fix 0 and infinity. The restriction formula is
\[
\mathbb P[K\cap A=\varnothing]=\Phi_A'(0)^\alpha.
\]
The allowed test sets/maps and normalization are part of the definition; this is not a formula for arbitrary closed obstacles.

- **Proposition 3.3 (PDF pp. 10–14).** Scale invariance and conformal covariance imply the power formula for some exponent $\alpha$; conversely the formula gives the restriction law. The proof uses the semigroup/composition property of conformal maps and the multiplicative avoidance probability.
- **Proposition 4.1 (PDF pp. 16–17).** The filled Brownian excursion measure in $\mathbb H$ is a chordal restriction sample with exponent 1. The excursion is described by the SDE $dB=dW+i\,dt/\operatorname{Im}B$; its avoidance probability is $\Phi_A'(0)$.

## SLE characterizations and constructions

- **§5 (PDF pp. 19–22).** Loewner change-of-domain calculations show locality for SLE only at $\kappa=6$ (Proposition 5.1); the derivative martingale condition singles out SLE$_{8/3}$ and exponent $5/8$ (Propositions 5.2–5.3). For the displayed generalized martingale the parameters are $\alpha=(6-\kappa)/(2\kappa)$ and $\lambda=(8-3\kappa)(6-\kappa)/(2\kappa)$, in the paper’s convention and range.
- **Theorem 6.1 (PDF pp. 23–25).** Chordal SLE$_{8/3}$ has chordal restriction law $P_{5/8}$. The proof applies Itô’s formula to the conformal derivative observable, obtains a bounded martingale, and identifies the terminal avoidance event.
- **Theorem 7.3 (PDF pp. 29–30).** For the finite-parameter construction take $0<\kappa\le8/3$ and set $\alpha_\kappa=(6-\kappa)/(2\kappa)$ and $\lambda_\kappa=(8-3\kappa)(6-\kappa)/(2\kappa)$. The paper constructs $\Xi(\kappa)$ by adding to an independent chordal SLE$_\kappa$ an independent Poisson cloud of Brownian bubbles with intensity $\lambda_\kappa\mu\times dt$; its filled hull has law $P_{\alpha_\kappa}$. The printed theorem states the interval $[0,8/3]$, but the displayed formulas have denominator $2\kappa$; at $\kappa=0$ I do not substitute into them or claim a finite $\alpha_\kappa$ (the endpoint can only be read formally as a limit). Thus the stated finite-$\alpha$ construction has $\kappa>0$, and the SLE parameter varies with the restriction exponent. At $\kappa=8/3$, $\lambda_\kappa=0$ and $\alpha_\kappa=5/8$, giving the SLE$_{8/3}$ case. Conversely, for $\alpha>5/8$ the construction uses $\kappa=6/(2\alpha+1)$, not a fixed SLE$_{8/3}$ plus bubbles. The paper's 4/3 boundary statement here is about this hull/bubble construction, not the SLE$_6$ trace.
- **§8 (PDF pp. 31–43).** One-sided restriction laws $P^+_\alpha$ exist for all $\alpha>0$; Theorem 8.4 identifies the SLE$(8/3,\rho)$ family and its parameter relation. Corollary 8.6 rules out two-sided restriction for $\alpha<5/8$.
- **§9 (PDF pp. 44–51).** The paper compares SLE$_6$ **hulls** and Brownian hulls at specified stopping/exit events. Theorem 9.1 is the full-plane stopped-on-exiting-domain hull identity; Theorem 9.3 identifies a chordal SLE$_6$ hull stopped at a test hull with the filled reflected Brownian hull at reflection angle $2\pi/3$. Corollary 9.5 gives an approximation by reflected Brownian motion on the past hull. These are hull/frontier statements at the stated stopping times, not equality of full traces.

## Object distinctions and limits

The chordal SLE$_6$ trace has dimension $7/4$ (Beffara, M0179); $4/3$ is the Brownian frontier dimension (M0175) and also appears for appropriate hull boundaries here. Section 9 compares filled hulls/frontiers, and §9.4 distinguishes pioneer points from the SLE$_6$ trace. None of these results evaluates the infimum over Jordan arcs contained in Brownian trace (B061). Do not transfer a hull law to a trace law, or an avoidance exponent to an arc-dimension infimum.
