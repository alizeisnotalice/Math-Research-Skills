# M0172 — The Dimension of the Frontier of Planar Brownian Motion

**Reading record:** Entire 19-page PDF read (PDF pp. 1–19; printed pages follow the ECP pagination). Title and author verified on PDF p. 1. Independent review: pending.

## Exact theorem and hypotheses

For planar Brownian motion, let the frontier be the boundary of the unbounded connected component of the complement of the path range (equivalently, the outer boundary of its filled hull, with the time interval specified in the paper). Lawler's Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2) gives
\[
  \dim_H(\mathrm{Frontier})=2(1-\alpha)\quad\text{a.s.},
\]
where \(\alpha\) is the two-sided disconnection exponent. The proof uses the sharp exponent estimate \(q(n)\asymp n^{-2\alpha}\), with constants independent of integer scale \(n\), for the probability that two independent planar Brownian paths started at unit separation both reach distance \(n\) without disconnecting the origin from infinity (definition and normalization PDF pp. 2–4). At publication, the paper reports Werner's bound giving a dimension strictly greater than 1.015; its principal statement is expressed using \(\alpha\), not the subsequently established numerical value.

## Proof chain and quantitative interfaces

1. Define a finite-scale frontier event and \(q(n)\), then establish the disconnection exponent via submultiplicative/Markov scale comparisons (PDF pp. 2–6). The relevant asymptotic is two-sided comparability, not merely a logarithmic exponent.
2. Approximate frontier points by the set of times at which Brownian motion is within a prescribed scale of its past/future path and the corresponding two-sided non-disconnection event holds (PDF §§2–3).
3. The first-moment estimate gives the upper Hausdorff bound from the probability of a frontier interval at scale \(r\); a two-point estimate controls pairs separated by \(s\) using the corresponding scale ratio and the non-disconnection probability (PDF §§3–4).
4. A Frostman/energy argument applied to the random measure on approximate frontier times yields the matching lower bound. The proof needs the uniform finite-scale estimates and the conditioning/Markov decomposition stated there; replacing them by the exponent alone without uniform constants is insufficient for the second-moment step.

The result is an almost-sure Hausdorff dimension, not a Hausdorff-measure normalization. The exponent is dimensionless; scale-comparison constants are not asserted to be universal.

## Critical distinction for B061

This theorem concerns one geometrically defined subset: the frontier/outer boundary. B061 instead defines an infimum over **all Jordan arcs contained in** the Brownian trace and containing at least two distinct points. A dimension theorem for the frontier neither bounds that infimum in the required direction nor identifies the admissible set family. In particular, the value 4/3 from later work on the frontier is not the answer to B061. Likewise, a theorem about the supremum of dimensions of Jordan arcs (Burdzy's labyrinth dimension) addresses the opposite optimization direction.

## Scope / migration limits

- This paper's theorem depends on the two-sided disconnection exponent and its sharp scale estimates; do not state the numerical 4/3 from this 1996 article alone.
- The frontier is part of the Brownian trace, but an arbitrary frontier subset is not automatically a Jordan arc. Conversely, the minimizing Jordan arc in B061 need not be on the frontier.
- No conclusion about the Jordan-arc infimum follows without a separate structural argument.
