# M0171 — Percolation dimension of fractals

**Reading record:** Entire 6-page PDF read (PDF pp. 1–6; printed pp. 1–6). PDF title/author verified from p. 1. Independent review: pending.

## Precise definitions and theorem

For connected (A\subset\mathbb R^2) and distinct (x,y\in A), Burdzy writes \(\mathcal F_A(x,y)\) for the connected subsets of (A) containing (x,y). The lower and upper path dimensions are the infimum and supremum over distinct endpoint pairs of \(\inf_{B\in\mathcal F_A(x,y)}\dim_H B\); when those agree, the common quantity is called the percolation dimension (PDF p. 2, Definition 3). The same page defines boundary dimension from the homogeneous Hausdorff dimensions of boundaries of complementary components.

The main theorem (PDF pp. 2–6) says that for every \(1\le\alpha\le\beta\le\gamma\le2\), there is a compact, connected, locally connected planar set \(F\) with percolation dimension \(\alpha\), boundary dimension \(\beta\), and homogeneous Hausdorff dimension \(\gamma\). This is a construction theorem about deterministic compacta; it is not a theorem giving the percolation dimension of Brownian trace.

## Proof chain

1. (pp. 2–3) Build Jordan arcs of prescribed Hausdorff dimension \(\alpha\) by successive grid-segment replacement, with scale parameters \((N_k,n_k)\) satisfying \(|\alpha-\log N_k/\log n_k|<2^{-k}\). The covering count gives the limiting dimension.
2. (p. 3) Transfer the construction into a Jordan domain with a conformal map; Hausdorff dimension is preserved by the conformal map in this setting.
3. (pp. 3–4) Decompose a model domain into countably many arcs with only countably many intersections, and replace these by arcs of designated dimensions \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), preserving the prescribed topology.
4. (pp. 4–5) Iteratively insert small copies inside complementary Jordan domains. The radii and covers are chosen so the added \(\alpha\)-dimensional pieces have summable \(\gamma+2^{-n}\) covering costs. This controls the final set’s Hausdorff dimension from above; a dense union of the \(\gamma\)-dimensional pieces gives the reverse bound. Boundary pieces give boundary dimension \(\beta\).
5. (pp. 5–6) The dense connected union of \(\alpha\)-dimensional pieces gives the upper path-dimension bound. For the lower bound, a Jordan separator between two points forces every connected subset of the final set joining them to contain an arc from one of the constructed barrier families, of dimension at least \(\alpha\); nested-copy cases reduce to the same separator argument.

No Brownian probability estimate or numerical constant is asserted. The dimension parameters are exact.

## Relevance and migration limits for B061

Burdzy’s 1990 definition minimizes over **connected subsets** \(B\), whereas the current B061 prompt asks for the infimum over **Jordan arcs** contained in the planar Brownian trace and containing two distinct points. Do not silently identify those admissible families: prove an equivalence for the random trace/infimum before importing the old definition. The article’s historical remark on p. 2 that intuition suggests strict inequalities for Brownian trace, and that an earlier frontier conjecture was 4/3, is explicitly intuition/history, not a result of this theorem. Keep B061’s Jordan-arc infimum separate from the Brownian frontier, which is a boundary set.
