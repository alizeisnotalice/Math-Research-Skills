## Exact results and scope

Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3) proves the half-plane formula for all $k\ge2$ and nonnegative weights:
\[
\widetilde\xi(w_1,\ldots,w_k)=
\frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^k\sqrt{24w_j+1}-(k-1)\right)^2-1}{24}.
\]
Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 3) gives the full-plane exponent, when $w_1,w_2\ge1$ and $w_3,\ldots,w_k\ge0$:
\[
\xi(w_1,\ldots,w_k)=
\frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^k\sqrt{24w_j+1}-k\right)^2-4}{48}.
\]
In particular, substituting $k=2$ and $w_1=w_2=1$ into Theorem 1.1 gives the half-plane value $\widetilde\xi(1,1)=10/3$; the corresponding full-plane exponent is $\xi(1,1)=5/4$, not $10/3$. The paper's Theorem 1.2 also gives the displayed function $\eta(x)=((\sqrt{24x+1}-1)^2-4)/48$ for $x\ge\widetilde\xi(1,1)=10/3$ (PDF p. 3); this eta threshold is a half-plane/two-sided exponent interface and must not be relabeled with the plane $\xi$. Proof strategy (PDF pp. 3–4): compute two-sided $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ exponents in a half-plane, transfer the $\mathrm{SLE}_6$ values to Brownian half-plane exponents by the universality argument, and use cascade relations.

This paper itself explicitly leaves the exponents $\xi(n,w)$ with integer $n\ge1$ and $w\in(0,1)$ unresolved; it refers to the separate analyticity paper arXiv:math/0005295 for completion by analytic continuation (PDF p. 4). Therefore this source alone does not justify all disconnection exponents at weight zero and is not a standalone derivation of the Brownian frontier dimension.

## Migration limits

The results concern Brownian intersection exponents defined from packets of independent paths, not the dimension of the Brownian trace or the infimum over Jordan subarcs. The SLE transfer requires the stated conformal-invariance/restriction/excursion setup and cascade identities; do not substitute a frontier statement or a Jordan-arc statement for these exponent formulas.
