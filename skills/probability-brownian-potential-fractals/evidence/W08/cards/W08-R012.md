# W08-R012 — Neumann eigenfunctions and Brownian couplings

**Reading record:** Full 13-page review (PDF pp. 1–13), read. Identity verified on PDF p. 1. Review pending.

## Scope and useful interfaces

This is a review of probabilistic methods around the Neumann hot-spots problem, not a new general coupling theorem. It fixes the heat-equation convention $\partial_tu=\Delta u$ with inward Neumann normal convention and gives the generic eigenfunction expansion $u(t,x)=c_1+c_2\phi_2(x)e^{-\mu_2t}+R(t,x)$ under suitable domain/spectral hypotheses (PDF pp. 1–2). A coupling comparison of $E_xu_0(X_t)$ and $E_yu_0(Y_t)$, when valid for an appropriate family of pairs, can imply an order relation on a second eigenfunction (PDF pp. 7–9).

The review summarizes the mirror coupling and its hinge geometry (PDF pp. 9–11), plus scaling couplings and their time-change issue (PDF pp. 10–11). It distinguishes convergence-to-boundary/hot-spots arguments from finite-time coalescence: the coupling time may be infinite, and some spectral arguments use relative-position constraints rather than meeting.

## Limits for use

- The review’s hot-spots statements concern specific domain classes, including convex symmetric and lip domains as summarized in Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 4); it also describes counterexamples with holes and leaves convex-domain questions open (PDF pp. 4–5).
- It does not prove A020’s requested acute-triangle exponential tail, does not identify all couplings with the mirror coupling, and is not the primary source for W08-R002’s geometric hypotheses.
- The PDE eigenvalue is for Δ. If a reflected Brownian generator is taken as ½Δ, rates in a probabilistic statement acquire the corresponding factor; do not merge conventions without an explicit check.
