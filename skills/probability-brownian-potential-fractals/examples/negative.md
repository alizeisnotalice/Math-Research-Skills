# Missing-condition cases

## Disconnection is not nonintersection

Non-disconnection is the event that 0 lies in the unbounded component of the complement; disconnection is its complement. PR-f7109db62ff7 and PR-58f59904e178 concern the exact event and own-radius stopping rules in their admission rows. These events are not interchangeable with mutual nonintersection.

For M0196, replacing F_(R+1) with a connector confined to the unit disk misses a surrounding loop outside that disk. Do not take a finite-n exact exponent from the large-n limit, reverse the prescribed limit order, or insert Proposition 2’s spider argument.

## A local loop-erasure lemma is not a global path theorem

PR-1018a43b4e34 admits a two-time density for a fixed pair of disjoint nested separators and its corresponding first-hit stops. Applying it to an arbitrary finite separator family requires unadmitted consistency and weak-limit steps. A radial-SLE2 identification or chordal-SLE2 dimension does not by itself give a full Brownian loop-erased arc dimension.

## Keep M0224 within the read units and admitted theorem interfaces

The book record is four complete units and 107 physical pages, not a whole-book read. The admitted use includes positive-alpha energy and occupation-measure dimension methods, the specified transient d>=3 compact-target Martin comparison from 0 with A avoiding 0, and the already checked Kaufman all-subsets result. Do not use a planar killed-process Martin kernel, general Wiener criterion, last-exit formula, general intersection-dimension formula, blanket Frostman route, or all of Appendix 11 as high-qualified source support.

## A spectral lower bound is not a survival upper bound

From a bounded Neumann eigenfunction and correct coupled marginals one can obtain a lower bound for P(T>t) when the initial mode difference is nonzero. This prevents an upper tail from decaying too rapidly. It does not prove a coupling exists, an upper bound for P(T>t), or an acute-triangle rate.

## Keep coupling classes separate

- The disk synchronous noncoalescence result is about that same-driver pair; it does not disprove mirror or unrestricted couplings.
- The simultaneous product-boundary event in the disk is not simultaneous charge by the two original local-time regulators.
- The mirror inefficiency result for a strictly acute triangle with pairwise distinct angles concerns mirror coupling only.
- In Atar’s rectangle theorem, Condition 2.2 checks each primitive face normal. A diagonal n=(1,1)/sqrt(2) from a new rotated smooth face can project e1 to (1/2,-1/2), outside the positive cone; this is not one of the rectangle’s primitive face normals. The normalized diagonal sum of the two active coordinate normals at a rectangle corner is an active-normal combination, and the theorem does not separately require every such combination to preserve the positive cone. Thus this rotated-face obstruction does not contradict the rectangle positive case.
- Eberle’s Euclidean reflection direction is in transformed Brownian coordinates when the noise matrix is not identity.
- A one-particle Skorokhod solver or marginal existence theorem does not verify a pair’s Brownian drivers in the common filtration.
