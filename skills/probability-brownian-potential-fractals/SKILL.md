# Brownian path, potential, and coupling methods

Use this skill for Brownian ranges, cut points, disconnection, planar SLE or loop-erasure, potential kernels and capacity, or reflected-Brownian couplings.

1. Name the object and event. Distinguish a trace, frontier, cut-time set, image, Jordan arc, connected set, disconnection, dimension, exponent, and probability statement. State the time interval, stopping rule, ambient metric, and quantifiers.
2. Read [the method map](references/method.md) and [source scopes](references/sources.md). Keep every exponent, separation estimate, conformal transfer, regularity condition, and convergence step in the stated source interface.
3. For a coupling, specify both marginals and the joint law. Include reflection rule, transformed/shared noise, filtration, stopping time, and continuation after meeting. A one-particle Skorokhod result does not determine a pair coupling.
4. Keep generator normalisation, spatial decay, survival probability, meeting-time tails, total variation, and spectral estimates separate. A survival lower bound does not imply a meeting-tail upper bound.

Use [positive](examples/positive.md) and [missing-condition](examples/negative.md) cases to test a proposed transfer. The method and source cards record accepted local scopes and held dependencies.
