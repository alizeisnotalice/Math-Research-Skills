# Method checklist: flows, dimensions, and measure classes

## 1. Type the objects before calculating

Keep these objects separate:

1. A tree or tree law `T`, with its offspring/branching assumptions.
2. A boundary `∂T` and its sigma-field, usually generated by cylinder sets.
3. A boundary probability `μ` (for example, a random-walk exit law or a normalized positive flow).
4. A coding map `π: ∂T → E` and its pushforward `ν=π#μ`.
5. A metric on `E` and the boundary/cylinder scale used to measure dimension.

For two codes, define `ν_i=π_i#μ_i` as measures on one common target `E`. Check measurability, surjectivity only if needed, endpoint identifications, and whether a common cylinder filtration exists. Two different trees can code the same compact set while producing different measures.

## 2. Flow and conductance bookkeeping

For each vertex `v`, record the cylinder mass `μ([v])`, child transition probabilities `q(v,w)`, and normalization:

`μ([root])=1`, `μ([w])=μ([v]) q(v,w)`, `Σ_{w child of v}q(v,w)=1`.

For a conductance construction, distinguish conductance, current, a consistent normalized cylinder probability, and a finite-energy unit flow. A consistent cylinder probability follows from root mass one and normalized child probabilities; it does not itself require finite energy. Finite energy is the extra hypothesis in the transience/positive-capacity route, where the unit-flow root flux and conservation conditions must be checked. If a flow is random, specify the sigma-field, tree law, marked ray, and conditional versus annealed measure.

The accepted canonical Rousselin source is the 37-page arXiv:1708.06965v2 paper, and this use is limited to its marked-GW flow-rule interfaces in Theorems 2.2 and 3.2. Preserve `p₀=0`, `p₁<1`, finite mean offspring, iid marks independent of the GW tree, and the source's positive measurable subtree-consistent flow. For Theorem 3.2, match `φ(T)=h(Γ_root, Σ_i φ(T[i]))` and the source-defined induced flow; an arbitrary consistent flow plus the three displayed `h` identities does not establish invariance of the `κ(φ(T))` density. Check those identities and the finite positive normalizer. Theorem 2.2 requires a `Θ`-stationary probability absolutely continuous with respect to GW and gives equivalence, ergodicity/uniqueness in that class and exact dimension in the natural common-prefix ultrametric. This is not a theorem about arbitrary pushforwards on an externally fixed Cantor set. The distinct 22-page arXiv:1711.07920v1 paper is read but has no accepted G25-use credit; do not transfer its proof or caveats. The printed local quotient issue in Theorem 5.2 of the 37-page paper belongs to a separate later dimension-drop argument: R14 checked a derived coboundary repair for that local step only, and this defect/repair is not used in the present Theorems 2.2/3.2 interface.

## 3. Entropy rate and exact dimension

A typical symbolic route is

`−log μ([ξ₁…ξₙ]) / n → h`, `−log diam([ξ₁…ξₙ]) / n → χ`, `dim μ = h/χ`.

Every arrow needs hypotheses:

- The ray/shift law must be stationary for an entropy-rate theorem. For an almost-sure constant limit from a Shannon–McMillan–Breiman theorem, require ergodicity; in a nonergodic law, state the conditional information limit on ergodic components.
- Verify finite one-step or conditional entropy/information integrability. Infinite branching or zero transition probabilities need separate handling; `−log 0=+∞` cannot be silently averaged.
- Verify `0<χ<∞` for the chosen metric, including a Jacobian or cylinder contraction exponent where applicable.
- To pass from cylinders to metric balls, prove a bounded geometry, separation, bounded distortion, or another valid cylinder-ball comparison. Overlaps and endpoint identifications can invalidate a direct cylinder formula.
- A multifractal spectrum requires a separate moment, large-deviation, or pressure argument. One exact-dimension result gives one typical exponent, not the spectrum.

The accepted Rousselin interface is the canonical marked-GW flow-rule route in Theorems 2.2/3.2 above, with its own iid marks, flow identities, stationary-law and ultrametric hypotheses. The PTN2026 Chapter 16 source provides capacity/energy and electrical-network interfaces, including a specific open two-coding question; it does not answer that question.

For additional model-specific benchmarks, LPP1995 and Lin treat harmonic flows on infinite supercritical Galton–Watson trees, while Curien–Le Gall treat critical finite-height trees and their continuum reduced-tree limit. Their common-prefix or continuum-tree metric and stationary-ray hypotheses must be stated separately. Do not transfer one model's conductance recursion or dimension constant to another. See the two canonical exact-use cards（本机附件：`evidence/usage_cards/G25-harmonic-canonical-extensions.md`）.

### IFS coding entropy and Lyapunov scale

Feng–Hu define projection entropy on a symbolic IFS coding and account for information lost under the target coding sigma-field. For an ergodic invariant code on an `m`-conformal IFS, Theorem 2.8 gives `dim μ = h_π / ∫λ dm`; the nonergodic local limit is `E(f|I)` conditional on the invariant sigma-field, not automatically a constant. The physical p.5 printed local-entropy display omits that outer expectation; use the conditional form in the proof development (Propositions 4.10, 4.12 and 4.14). In a product, Theorem 2.11 requires coordinate conformality under the common symbolic index law and uses successive coordinate projection-entropy differences and ordered Lyapunov exponents. Its physical p.47 product-proof line for (C2) prints an extra factor; R13 derived the intended inequality from the preceding mass bound, a local repair rather than a source claim. Apply only after verifying the invariant symbolic law, code, conformality, and local cylinder-to-ball scale. ### CP-chain and tree-morphism entropy transfer

Hochman–Shmerkin Theorem 1.9 gives an arbitrary-measure b-adic scenery conditional-entropy lower-dimension bound with loss `C_{d,k}/log b`; it does not assume a CP-chain. The separate §8 ergodic CP-chain average carries the finite-scale `O(1/q)` loss. Theorem 4.4 turns almost-sure lower conditional child entropy along a `ρ`-tree morphism into an image lower-dimension bound `α/log(1/ρ)`, and Theorem 5.4 transfers Lipschitz maps with an additive entropy error. Product-projection theorems require their specified invariant product actions or regular separated IFS/Gibbs measures and minimality. Lemma 7.9 gives exact dimension for a typical source measure of an ergodic CP-chain; projection theorems need their own upper bridge before claiming exact image dimension. Proposition 8.4 additionally requires strong generation and uniform derivative approximation; Theorem 1.13 uses an essential-infimum expected-derivative control. The physical p.36 nonergodic proof has a local tail-probability typo (printed limit 0; context requires 1). See the Hochman–Shmerkin source card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-ffe34e1b8d1959fa-HOCHMAN-SHMERKIN-LOCAL-ENTROPY.md`）.

These sources give dimension information. They do not imply that equal-dimensional measures on a common coding target are equivalent or singular, and neither source supplies an A147 separator.


### Additional model-restricted entropy and dimension routes

For a transient generating walk on a finite free product of finite groups, the Traffic Equations determine the Markovian-multiplicative boundary measure and the paper states HD(μ∞)=h/γ in the common-prefix metric. The Stationary Traffic Equations are a separate prerequisite for shift-stationarity/ergodicity; the formula is not a general SMB theorem.

For invariant percolation walks on a non-elementary hyperbolic group, exact dimension is `h/l` in the source quasi-metric `ρ=exp(−Gromov product)`; under a genuine `ε`-visual metric `ρ_ε≈ρ^ε`, it is `h/(εl)`. Apply only under supercritical Bernoulli percolation or the stated ergodic-invariant, indistinguishable-cluster and positive-speed assumptions. Do not identify this random cluster with an externally specified coding tree.

A Schottky dimension gap requires the source's separated half-planes and unique distinguished-letter prefix condition; a stable-tree separator requires the prior harmonic-typical exponent in addition to the present paper's branching-typical local dimensions. These are model-specific, not pairwise measure-class results for arbitrary coded measures. See the wave11 use card（本机附件：`evidence/usage_cards/G25-wave11-free-product-random-graph-dimension.md`）.


## 4. Equivalence, absolute continuity, and singularity

To prove `ν₁ ⟂ ν₂`, exhibit a Borel `B⊂E` with `ν₁(B)=1`, `ν₂(B)=0`. A typical-set separator based on a statistic is valid only when the statistic is defined on the same `E`, is measurable, and has different almost-sure limits under the two measures.

To prove `ν₁ ≪ ν₂`, construct or identify `dν₁/dν₂`; a finite-level likelihood martingale must have the needed uniform integrability or converge under an applicable differentiation theorem. To prove equivalence, also establish positivity of the derivative `ν₂`-almost surely. A sequence of cylinder ratios alone is not enough without the shared filtration and convergence hypotheses.

Kakutani’s Hellinger criterion is for infinite product measures. Before using it, prove the relevant coordinates are independent under each measure and that the coded sigma-field is the product sigma-field being compared. Tree dependence, random environments, or a shared geometric coding do not automatically give a product decomposition.

### Hyperbolic-group boundary comparison (conditional model route)

BHM Corollary 1.6 gives an exact equivalence criterion for the hitting measures of two finitely supported symmetric random walks generating the same non-elementary hyperbolic group: equivalence is equivalent to the stated cross-entropy/Green-speed limit and to uniform two-sided comparability of the Green functions. Tanaka Theorem 5.8 gives a second sufficient route: if each walk satisfies h_i=l_i v on the same group and visual boundary, each harmonic measure is comparable to the common D=v/ε Hausdorff reference, hence the two are equivalent. Tanaka’s Theorem 3.6 also compares probabilities satisfying the same shadow/Gibbs estimate for a fixed potential. Dussaule–Yang give a distinct exact-dimension route on one common Floyd/Bowditch boundary only in the safely closed range `λ∈λ₀,1)`: if two admissible finite-first-moment walks have different `h_i/l_i` at the same λ and metric, singularity follows from the two exact-dimensional conclusions and a Hausdorff-dimension argument (derived, not their printed theorem). Their printed all-λ Floyd clause is excluded from positive use below `λ₀` because the later standing hypotheses and final proof use the restricted range; the separate end-boundary all-λ result is independent. Le Prince constructs an existential symmetric finitely supported walk whose harmonic measure is singular to boundary Hausdorff measure; do not apply that existence result to a specified walk. The separate Le Prince 2007 Proposition 2.1 gives only `overline{dim}_P ν≤h/(l log a)` for a matched hyperbolic-boundary walk and visual metric. Its proposed use requires finite first displacement moment, `H(μ)<∞`, `0<l<∞`, semigroup support, and the a.s. limits invoked in the proof; finite displacement moment does not automatically imply finite entropy for every proper action. R13 identified a p. 9 entropy-event threshold typo and derived the `h+ε` repair, while p. 10 fixed radius constants affect only `O(1/n)`. The probability-ratio lemma cites unread Kaimanovich (1998), and the Kingman/Avez and visual-metric results remain external. Its small-dimension construction is existential, and Corollary 5.1 compares one such measure only with Hausdorff reference. R13 v22 accepted the Le Prince 2007 G25.02 use only for Proposition 2.1’s guarded upper pointwise-dimension bound. This does not give exact dimension, paired-walk equivalence/singularity, or an A147 conclusion. All routes require a genuinely common group and boundary metric. A common measurable map pushes equivalence/absolute continuity forward, but singularity requires a Borel isomorphism on relevant full-measure sets or a direct target-space separator. Failure of an equivalence criterion is not a singularity theorem. See the [hyperbolic-boundary comparison card（本机附件：`evidence/usage_cards/G25-hyperbolic-boundary-comparison.md`）, BHM card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-e2ab8790e911ef09-BHM-HYPERBOLIC-GROUP-COMPARISON.md`）, Tanaka card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-4870403890d0e4d5-TANAKA-HYPERBOLIC-BOUNDARY-SPECTRUM.md`）, Dussaule–Yang card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-c9838fae60c2262f-DUSSAULE-YANG-RELATIVE-HYPERBOLIC-DIMENSION.md`）, and Le Prince card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-b05233923e45b9d0-LE-PRINCE-HYPERBOLIC-SINGULARITY.md`）.

Dimension is a coarse invariant. Two measures with equal exact dimension may be mutually singular; see the Bernoulli digit example in [negative.md](../examples/negative.md). Conversely, equal dimensions do not establish singularity either.

### Base-change coding and a common-target separator

Benjamini–Peres Example 6 gives one concrete interval-tree base-change example. For `A={Σ ε_n 10^{-2n}: ε_n∈{0,…,90}}`, its base-10 and base-100 tree walks induce harmonic probabilities on the same `A`. Under the base-100 law the base-100 digits are iid uniform with frequency `1/91` for digit 90; under the base-10 law they are iid and that frequency is `>1/40`. Therefore the Borel set where the limiting frequency exceeds `1/40` separates the measures. The threshold comparison is independently checked with SymPy; the source provides both stochastic bounds. Use only when the target digit coding is unique/Borel and the source's two iid laws and frequency separation actually apply. R13 accepted this restricted G25.06 use for the worked example; it does not transfer to a general finite-type coding problem. It does not prove finite-type transducer transfer and does not solve the binary/ternary middle-third problem A147. See the Benjamini–Peres card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-ef8f977a8e39168a-BENJAMINI-PERES-BASE-CHANGE-SINGULARITY.md`） and exact separator check（本机附件：`evidence/linked_material/aa06a021013db0b3_benjamini_peres_example6_separator_check.py`）.

## 5. Nonlinear harmonic set functions

Kaufman–Llorente–Wu study nonlinear `F`-potential theory on a regular tree. Their `ω_F` can fail additivity and can assign positive value to a union of two zero sets. Do not apply probability-only tools such as Radon–Nikodym derivatives, SMB, or Kakutani to `ω_F` unless a separate theorem proves it is an additive probability in the exact setting.

## 6. Branching-measure dimensions and spectra (model-specific)

For the generation-uniform branching measure on a Galton–Watson boundary, Liu (2001) writes cylinder mass as `μ([v])=m^{-|v|}W_v`; the paper’s random measure has total mass `W`, so it is not generally a probability. On survival `0<W<∞`; for radius `r=e^{-n}`, replacing `μ` by `μ/W` changes the local-dimension quotient by `log W/n→0`. The cylinder-tail estimates in Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 and the all-ray local-dimension statements in Theorem 4.1 require the stated no-leaf, nonconstant-offspring, finite `E[N log N]`, and stronger tail/moment hypotheses as applicable. Under Theorem 4.1’s `E[N^{1+δ}]<∞` and condition (3.3), both local dimensions equal `α` on every ray iff `p₊=p₋=∞`, and the lower local dimension equals `α` on every ray iff `p₊=∞`; here `p₋=−log P(N=1)/log m` when positive, else `∞`, and `p₊=sup{q≥1:E[N^q]<∞}`. Its upper-only criterion and sharper upper uniform bound are conjectures. The exact-dimension statement for a typical ray is imported from earlier work, and the paper’s later gauge/density argument has a separate dependency; do not present these as unconditional consequences of the displayed cylinder identity. This is the exact-dimension/cylinder-mass interface, not a general stationary-ergodic Shannon–McMillan–Breiman theorem. See the Liu 2001 card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-8a95f8be4c98be4c-LIU2001-BRANCHING-MEASURE-LOCAL-DIMENSIONS.md`）.

Kinnison–Mörters (2010) treats two specific probability laws on the same Galton–Watson boundary: the generation-uniform branching measure and a forward-only walk choosing a child uniformly. Under all-moment assumptions and the Schröder condition `0<P(N=1)<1`, Theorem 2.1 gives a joint local-dimension constraint and Theorem 2.2 gives joint spectra. This “visibility” law is not automatically undirected simple-random-walk harmonic measure; its multifractal theorem is not a general measure-class criterion. See the Kinnison–Mörters card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-13fbec7a861f433d-KINNISON-MORTERS2010-JOINT-MULTIFRACTAL.md`）.

Liu (1993), Proposition 4.1(0) (physical pp. 24–25 / printed pp. 23–24), gives the size-biased typical-ray local dimension under `E[Z(log Z)^2]<∞`; Theorem 2 (physical p. 6 / printed p. 5) gives `H^{φ_θ}(K)=(r_{1/θ})^θ W` for the branching-set gauge. Its random branching measure has total mass `W`, not necessarily one, and the theorem concerns the support gauge rather than harmonic-measure equivalence or a multifractal spectrum. Root's IDR-20261115-004 groups its same-title 1996 journal publication in one conservative work family; only the complete 1993 artifact is read and credited, with the 1996 text unread and un-compared. See the Liu 1993 card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-df42b1d9c8bf10aa-LIU1993-BRANCHING-SET-GAUGE.md`）.

These branching results can be used only when the target model has the matching Galton–Watson law, measure construction, boundary metric and moment assumptions. They supply no bridge to arbitrary deterministic coding trees or two fixed Cantor pushforwards. Equal local dimensions or a joint spectrum do not imply that two measures are equivalent or singular.

## 7. Source-use boundary

The current cards give narrow interfaces only: Rousselin’s random-environment exact-dimension theorem; LPP1995/LPP1996 and Lin's biased or simple-walk Galton–Watson harmonic-flow methods; Curien–Le Gall's distinct critical-tree dimension mechanism; the R13-v8-accepted Liu/Kinnison–Mörters branching-measure dimension and spectrum uses; Liu (1993)'s R13-accepted corrected support-gauge interface, counted as one conservative work family with only the 1993 artifact read; PTN2026 Chapter 16 capacity/energy and open Question 16.7; KLW2003 as a negative-scope comparison; the conditional BHM/Tanaka equivalence routes; Dussaule–Yang’s common-Floyd exact-dimension route for `λ≥λ₀`; and Le Prince’s existential Hausdorff-singularity construction.
