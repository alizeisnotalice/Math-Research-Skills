## Exact fixed-k model

Let `C_j` be the number of `j`-cycles. Every invariant subset is a union of complete cycles, so the event is

`E_k(C) = {there exists a_1,…,a_k with 0≤a_j≤C_j and Σ j a_j=k}`.

For fixed `k`, `C_1,…,C_k` converge jointly to independent `Z_j~Poisson(1/j)`. Hence

`p(k) = P(E_k(Z))`.

The finite-`n` relation is also exact after conditioning: Arratia–Barbour–Tavaré (1997), equations (2)–(5), gives independent `Z_j~Pois(1/j)` and `T_n=Σ_{j≤n}jZ_j` with `(C_1(n),…,C_n(n)) =_d (Z_1,…,Z_n | T_n=n)`. Its normalization is `P(T_n=n)=e^{-H_n}`. Therefore the finite permutation event uses the *conditioned* Poisson vector; the unconditioned vector gives the fixed-coordinate limit, not the finite-`n` law. See the ABT1997 use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-8d97b493a6e9793f.md`） for the exact formula, complete-read record, and zero-transfer limits.

Equivalently, let `Ω_k` be the countable set of vectors `a∈N_0^k` with no subvector `a'≤a` satisfying `Σ j a'_j=k`. Then

`p(k)=1−Σ_{a∈Ω_k} ∏_{j=1}^k exp(−1/j)(1/j)^{a_j}/a_j!`.

The probability sum converges because its nonnegative terms are probabilities of disjoint Poisson count vectors and their total is at most one. Although `Ω_k` is infinite (for `k=3`, all `(0,a₂,0)` lie in it), reachability depends only on `min(Z_j,floor(k/j))`. Assigning the Poisson tail at `floor(k/j)` to the saturated state gives a finite product state space for exact arithmetic or dynamic programming.

Eberhard–Ford–Green (2015), physical pp. 1–3, gives this exact cycle-sum event and fixed-`k` limit. Their Theorem 1.1 gives only a uniform two-sided order bound for the finite-`n` probability. Their Proposition 1.3 compares that probability with `k^{-1}E|L(X_k)|` uniformly for `1≤k≤n/2`; the proof uses a permutation sieve and bounds the size of the random reachable sumset. Their paper explicitly leaves adjacent monotonicity open (physical p. 4), so its order estimate does not resolve that question.


## Exact typed-cycle law under standardization (Guerder, 2026; accepted scoped G27.01 use)

For i.i.d. discrete values `G_1,…,G_n` with masses `p_i`, `std(G)` uses stable tie-breaking to produce a permutation. For a primitive word necklace `w` of length `j`, let `D_w` count cycles whose successive sampled values have type `w`. Guerder’s Theorem 1.1 (physical pp. 2, 11–15) gives the exact joint tail `P(D_(w_s)≥ℓ_s ∀s)=∏ p_(w_s)^{ℓ_s}` for distinct types when `Σ |w_s|ℓ_s≤n`, and zero otherwise. The proof inserts one typed cycle while preserving the standardized order on the complementary labels. Do not read this as finite-`n` independence of ordinary cycle counts.

For a fixed non-Dirac atom law, Theorem 1.2/Corollary 1.3 give fixed-cutoff independent `Geo_0(1−p_w)` limits by primitive type and geometric-sum limits for ordinary counts. The printed theorem omits this boundary: if `p=δ_a`, then `std(G)=id`, `D_(a)=c_1=n`, and `Geo_0(0)` is not a probability law; require `max_i p_i<1`. Under the separate small-frequency assumption `max_i p_i^(n)→0`, Theorem 1.4 gives a fixed-coordinate independent `Pois(1/j)` limit. Its mixed-moment proof uses the typed tail law and primitive-necklace weight sums. Therefore a fixed-target bounded-multiplicity reachability event transfers to the Poisson model only in the small-frequency regime; the fixed-atom model has a different limit. R14 independently completed the insertion/deletion inverse and the fixed non-Dirac and small-frequency fixed-cutoff proofs; see the proof note（本机附件：`evidence/derived_proofs/guerder_independent_short_cycle_proof.md`）. This source supplies no growing-coordinate rate, Feller coupling, or adjacent-target comparison. **Complete 35-page read; R14 accepted this scoped method for one work credit.** See the L14 use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-c151b24b7be7bba9.md`）.

## Multi-target cycle-partition reachability (EFK, 2016; Proposition 3.1 only, accepted by R14)

For fixed `m≥3`, Eberhard–Ford–Koukoulopoulos define `L_m(c)` by assigning every cycle, within its multiplicity, to exactly one of `m` labeled bins. A target vector `k_1,…,k_m` with `Σ_i k_i=n` is in `L_m(c)` exactly when the full cycle type partitions into `m` disjoint invariant sets of those sizes. Proposition 3.1 (physical p. 10) compares the uniform-permutation target-hit probability to `E|L_m(X_{k_1})|/k_1^(m−1)`, where `X_j` are independent `Pois(1/j)` for `j≤k_1`; the upper bound has factor `(k_(m−1)/k_1)^m`, and the balanced two-sided comparison requires `k_(m−1)≤c k_1`. R14 independently completed the p. 10–15 proof (see completion note（本机附件：`evidence/derived_proofs/efk_independent_local_global_completion.md`）): the p. 12 largest-index split is an inequality after dropping `exp(−(H_k−H_ell))`, not equality; the p. 14 truncation factor is `1−exp(2m)/M`, not `1−h exp(2m)/M`; and a separate bounded-`k` argument is needed where `h=floor(k/(4M))` may be zero. The completion uses EFG15b Proposition 2.1, already counted as a separate source/work. The later Theorem 1.4 `δ_m` order proof is outside this use and cites additional Kou10 Lemmas 4.4, 3.7 and 3.10. This method is a finite-permutation vector-hit comparison, not independent Poisson cycle counts for the permutation itself, and gives no adjacent-target sign. Full 34-page read; R14 accepted this scoped Proposition 3.1 use as one work. See the EFK use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-23e5783355f841bc.md`）.

Arratia–Tavaré (1992), physical pp. 4–5, states an independent-Poisson cycle process with `Z_j~Pois(θ/j)` and the sharp cutoff `d_b(n)→0` exactly when `b=o(n)`. At `θ=1`, the invariant-`k`-set event depends only on the first `k` cycle counts, so its probability differs from the independent-Poisson reachable-subset-sum probability by at most `d_k(n)`. This gives a finite-`n` approximation for fixed `k` and for `k=o(n)`; it does not justify a uniform Poisson approximation throughout `k≤n/2`. Their coupling theorem is attributed to Arratia–Barbour–Tavaré [6], rather than reproved in this article. See the L14 use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-9b2f026174c43847.md`）.

## Rough-cycle fixed-set comparison (Qian, 2026; scoped)

Qian’s Proposition 3.1 (physical pp. 6–10) gives, for a uniform permutation in `S_n`, `1≤k≤n/2`, and `1≤r≤0.04k`,

`R_r(n,k)=P(no cycle has length <r AND an invariant k-set exists) ≍ E|L(X_{r,k})|/(rk)`,

where the auxiliary `X_j` are independent `Pois(1/j)` counts and `L` allows multiplicities `0≤a_j≤X_j`. Lemma 2.1 gives `1/(2r)≤P(no cycle<r)≤1/r`, so conditional probability is obtained only by dividing the joint estimate by this exact roughness probability. The admitted interface is Proposition 3.1 plus that division. Theorem 1.1’s two-regime formula is a printed statement, but R14 did not certify its full §4 proof: the all-`t` replacement of harmonic mass by `log(k/r)` in Lemma 4.2 fails, and the proposed restricted-`t`/tail completion is not certified. See the Qian card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-192b0fee45246f94.md`） and R14 proof-boundary note（本机附件：`evidence/derived_proofs/qian_scoped_proof_and_auxiliary_boundary.md`）. This is not a verdict that Theorem 1.1 is false. The comparison is two-sided order and does not compare adjacent targets.

## Conjugation-invariant fixed-coordinate Poisson criterion (Benaych-Georges, 2009)

For a conjugation-invariant sequence of permutation laws, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3 combine normalized local-agreement probabilities with finite odd/even Bonferroni truncations to obtain joint independent `Pois(1/ell)` limits for any fixed list of cycle counts. Proposition 2.1 gives a concrete infinite allowed-cycle set `A`: with `q=gcd(A)`, `u_m=|S^A_{qm}|/(qm)!`, and `u_m/u_{m-1}→1`, the fixed-coordinate limit holds along admissible degrees. The exact local event uses actual degree `N=qm` and local probability `u_{m-p/q}/u_m · (N-p)!/N! ~ N^(-p)`. For each fixed target, point-mass convergence on the countable state space implies total-variation convergence of the finite count vector, so the bounded-multiplicity Boolean event transfers. There is no quantitative growing-coordinate rate, and finite allowed `A` has a different scaling. See the BG card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-deb0a9373615992a.md`） and its accepted R14 scope（本机附件：`evidence/admissions/EXSRC-deb0a9373615992a.json`）.

## Weighted-cycle fixed-coordinate Poisson criterion (Ercolani–Ueltschi, 2011)

For cycle-weighted permutations with law proportional to `∏_j θ_j^{C_j}`, Proposition 2.1(c) gives exact joint factorial moments `(h_(n−Σj j k_j)/h_n)∏(θ_j/j)^{k_j}`. For fixed finite nonnegative `θ_j`, eventual `h_n>0`, and `h_(n−1)/h_n→1`, Corollary 2.2 yields fixed-coordinate independent `Pois(θ_j/j)` limits. The printed proof cites Wormald; R14 independently completed the used implication from the factorial identity by bounding `h_(n−s)/h_n≤C_εe^{εs}`, applying dominated convergence to the finite-coordinate PGF, then tightness and uniqueness on the countable lattice. This also yields fixed-coordinate TV and transfers the fixed-`k` reachability event. In the uniform case `θ_j=1,h_n=1` exactly. Theorem 5.1’s algebraic-weight saddle-point chain is read but excluded from this interface. No finite-`n` ordinary-cycle independence, growing-coordinate error, or adjacent-target comparison follows. The L14 use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-a4bbc39a9c64074c.md`） records the conditions and R14 completion; R14 accepted this scoped method use for one work credit.
- Saturated dynamic programming gives exact finite-`k` calculations. It cannot prove monotonicity for every integer `k`.
- A combinatorial or analytic asymptotic may identify scale and periodic modulation. To infer monotonicity, derive the adjacent difference itself or prove a sufficiently strong adjacent-uniform remainder.
- A coupling proof must map the full cycle-count law and preserve or dominate the event. Pointwise inclusion of support labels without matching probabilities is insufficient.

**Derived finite-state algorithm.** For each `j≤k`, retain states `0,…,floor(k/j)` and merge all counts `≥floor(k/j)` at the last state. The state weight is `P(Z_j=r)` for `r<floor(k/j)` and `P(Z_j≥floor(k/j))` at the cap. Iterate a subset-sum Boolean DP over cycle lengths. This computes `p(k)` exactly from Poisson masses/tails; a numerical truncation of those tails must carry an explicit error and is not exact arithmetic by itself.


## Source-backed asymptotic method (scope-limited)

Green–Sawhney (2026), physical pp. 2–3, 12–17, proves a smooth log-periodic asymptotic for the fixed-`k` Poisson-cycle limit. Their proof changes from the exact `Pois(1/j)` cycle multiset to a dyadic multiset with multiplicities `Pois(n/(j H_k))`, samples it via two independent Poisson walks, and conditions on almost-positive bounded paths. For the conditional hit probability they prove separated-target `ℓ`-wise approximate independence using a rank-profile count and a Fourier local limit estimate, then apply truncated inclusion–exclusion or a second-moment bound (Lemma 11.2 and Proposition 12.1, pp. 49–72). Endpoint decoupling yields an `n`-independent periodic profile (pp. 78–79), and bounded-Lipschitz convergence of two measures completes the asymptotic (pp. 80–88).

Section 10 (pp. 45–47) evaluates the Fourier coefficients of the periodic kernel through a Mellin/Gamma integral and contour rotation. This is a G27.04 Mellin/Fourier use, not a saddle-point proof. Appendix A's ladder-height renewal theorem supports walk estimates; it is not a renewal equation for `p(k)` and does not establish the log-periodic main term through G27.05. The article provides no adjacent-difference expansion, sign estimate, or one-sided coupling. Its `o(1)` is relative to `p(k)`, so it cannot be differenced to settle monotonicity. The local proof route has two qualifications from independent review: the printed common-input mismatch bound in Lemma 5.7 (p. 20) is false, but a separately derived auxiliary maximal-coupling replacement preserves the needed laws and restores the downstream E3 rate `O(R²L³/n)` (pp. 70–72); and the p. 59 high-frequency retained-decay step needs a separately derived completion from Appendix C.1 (p. 112), the endpoint/scale bounds (p. 49), and radial integration. These are local proof supplements, not claims printed at the cited steps or a full re-audit of the main theorem. The detailed hypotheses and derivations are in the Green–Sawhney use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-bc148d3c8b95d714.md`）.

## Flajolet–Sedgewick (2009): labeled cycle-class EGFs

Flajolet–Sedgewick, *Analytic Combinatorics*, §II.1–II.2 and §II.4 (printed pp. 96–106, 119–125; assigned artifact physical pp. 112–122, 135–141), gives the labeled constructions `SET(B) ↦ exp(B(z))` and `CYC(B) ↦ log(1/(1−B(z)))` for component classes with no size-zero object. Every permutation is a set of labeled cycles, `P ≅ SET(CYC(Z))`, so `P(z)=exp(log(1/(1−z)))=1/(1−z)`. More generally, for cycle-length set `A⊆Z_{>0}` and total cycle-count set `B⊆Z_{≥0}`, Proposition II.4 gives `P^{(A,B)}(z)=β(α(z))`, with `α(z)=Σ_{a∈A}z^a/a` and `β(t)=Σ_{b∈B}t^b/b!`; the labeled count is `n![z^n]P^{(A,B)}(z)`. This is a G27.03 identity for classes restricting every cycle and/or their total count. It does not directly encode the A176 event that some subcollection of cycles sums to `k`; a Boolean reachability state is still required. The three complete section ranges, exact assumptions, coefficient check, and page coverage are in the L14 Flajolet use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/PR-04c3bacb4c77.md`）.

## Arratia–Tavaré (1994): conditioned cycle counts and event transfer

For labelled assemblies, the independent-coordinate law conditioned on total weight gives an exact permutation cycle model: with `m_j=(j−1)!` and `x=1`, independent `Z_j~Pois(1/j)` satisfy `(C_1(n),…,C_n(n)) =_d (Z_1,…,Z_n | Σ jZ_j=n)` (Theorem 1, physical pp. 6–8). For any `B⊂[n]`, Theorem 3 and equations (33)–(34) express `dTV(C_B,Z_B)` exactly through the scalar weighted sums `R_B=Σ_{j∈B}jZ_j` and `S_B=Σ_{j∉B}jZ_j` (physical pp. 11–13). Since the invariant-`k`-set event is measurable in `C_1,…,C_k`, this gives a data-processing error bound for its probability; the article cites the earlier Arratia–Tavaré cycle-structure paper for the quantitative `d_b(n)≤F(n/b)` bound and `b=o(n)` cutoff (physical pp. 15, 36), so do not attribute that proof to this source. Theorem 6 transfers approximation through a moderate-probability event with an explicit `1/p` loss (physical pp. 35–36); require `p` to dominate the incoming TV error.

For G27.03 only, the labelled-assembly exponential formula is `P̂(s,θ)=exp(θM̂(s))`; for permutations `M̂(s)=−log(1−s)` (equations (118)–(122), physical pp. 45–46). This supports exact cycle-count/class enumeration, but does not directly compute the Boolean event that some subcollection of cycles sums to `k`. The source also warns against replacing existence probabilities by expected representation counts: the reader-derived double count gives expected number of invariant `k`-subsets equal to one for each `0≤k≤n`, which is not `P(Y_{n,k}≥1)`. See the L14 AT94 use card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-fc298728139c2929.md`）.


## Najnudel–Pitman (2020): Feller cycle-spacing coupling

For Ewens parameter `θ>0`, independent Bernoulli indicators `B_i~Bernoulli(θ/(θ+i−1))` generate a finite Ewens permutation through the Feller construction; its `ℓ`-cycle count equals the count of `1 0^(ℓ−1) 1` spacings in the padded Bernoulli string (physical pp. 1–2). Infinite spacing counts are independent `Pois(θ/ℓ)`, and a lower-record/inter-record-stretch construction explains the Poisson process (Theorems 1.1–1.3 and Corollary 1.4, pp. 2–8). Thus for `θ=1`, fixed-`k` cycle coordinates and any event measurable in them transfer to the independent Poisson limit. The paper points to its references for quantitative `k=o(n)` error bounds; do not claim a new explicit rate from this source. Its coupling across Ewens parameters is not monotone for the finite cycle partition: increasing `θ` can add/delete/shorten cycles. This supplies no adjacent-target comparison for `p(k+1)` versus `p(k)`. See the L14 Najnudel–Pitman card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-2f7d8ed00ea40adc.md`）.

## Ewens permutations conditioned to exclude macroscopic cycles

Betz–Mühlbauer–Schäfer–Zeindler, Theorem 2.8 (physical pp. 6–7), gives a quantitative G27.01 interface under a different model: Ewens permutations with fixed parameter `θ>0`, conditioned on having no cycle longer than `α(n)`, where `n^a1≤α(n)≤n^a2` for fixed `0<a1≤a2<1`. Define `x_(n,α)>0` by `n=θΣ_{j≤α(n)}x_(n,α)^j` and `μ_j(n)=θx_(n,α)^j/j`. If `b=o(α(n))`, the joint law of the first `b` cycle counts is within `O(n^ε(α(n)/n)^(5/12))` in total variation of independent `Pois(μ_j(n))` for every fixed `ε>0`; choose `ε<5(1−a2)/12` to make this error vanish. In the smaller range `b=o(α/log n)`, the paper gives the additional rate `O(α/n + b log n/(n^(5/12)α^(7/12))`. Since `x_(n,α)→1`, fixed coordinates have limiting means `θ/j`; every fixed-target bounded-multiplicity reachability event therefore transfers to this Poisson model. The derivation uses exact conditioning on the weighted Poisson total and a one-dimensional local-ratio estimate via a saddle-point expansion. R14 found the printed Lemma 3.10 (3.42) true-root approximation false in the standing range; both Theorem 2.8 rates are retained only with R14’s independent comparison of the two actual saddle roots, direct `ρ=log n` Chernoff truncation, and corrected central ratio. Do not describe the printed Lemma 3.10 step as verified. Do not conflate Theorem 2.8’s n-dependent means with Theorem 2.7’s separate `θ=1`, `Pois(1/j)` comparison, or either with the unconditioned `S_n` law. This model-specific TV method supplies no adjacent-target inequality. The exact repair and source caveat are in the L14 card（本机附件：`evidence/source_cards/EXSRC-2a23911f92ee017a.md`） and R14’s derived proof note; R14 accepted the scoped Theorem 2.8 G27.01 use, retaining both rates only with the independent true-root repair.


## Conditional-cycle approximations and audited proof limits

Arratia–Stark–Tavaré (1995), Theorem 3, approximates the first `b` assembly component counts under its Δ-domain and `sup_i iλ_i<∞` hypotheses for `b=o(n/log n)`. At the uniform-permutation specialization `θ=1`, its first-order term vanishes, leaving only the stated `o(b/n)` conclusion; use Arratia–Tavaré (1992) for the sharper `b=o(n)` cutoff. The 1995 proof chain starts at physical p. 23 (printed p. 1369), and its scoped use is accepted by R14.

Arratia–DeSalvo (2016) gives an exact conditional-i.i.d. representation for assemblies conditioned on size and number of components; for permutations this is a cycle-count model conditioned on exactly `k` cycles, distinct from the unconditioned A176 law. Use finite size sets with any `x>0`; for infinite size sets the normalizer must satisfy `0<M(x)<∞`.
