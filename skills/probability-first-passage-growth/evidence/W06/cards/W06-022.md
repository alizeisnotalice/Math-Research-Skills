## Model, hypotheses, and exact conclusions

The model is undirected edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^2\). The paper permits either of two hypothesis packages. **A1′:** product/i.i.d. continuous weights and \(\mathbb E[(\min_{1\le i\le4}t_i)^2]<\infty\), where the four variables are the edges incident to the origin. **A2′:** translation-ergodic law with all square-lattice symmetries, \(\mathbb E t_e^{2+\epsilon}<\infty\) for some \(\epsilon>0\), bounded limit shape, the stated unique-passage-time condition, and upward finite energy \(\mathbb P(t_e\ge\lambda\mid\text{other weights})>0\) whenever \(\mathbb P(t_e\ge\lambda)>0\). A1′ or A2′ ensures unique finite geodesics (PDF pp. 2–3).

Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 3–4, abbreviated as Theorem 1.1 on p. 2) fixes a **deterministic** direction \(\theta\). Let \(S_\theta\) be the sector of contact angles of the tangent line to the limit shape at the boundary point \(v_\theta\). Assume the shape boundary is differentiable at \(v_\theta\) and at both endpoint contact directions of \(S_\theta\) (the authors note that endpoint limit points of extreme points suffice). Almost surely: (i) from every \(x\in\mathbb Z^2\) there is an infinite geodesic directed in \(S_\theta\), and every sequence of point-to-endpoint geodesics whose endpoints are directed in \(S_\theta\) converges to it; (ii) all such sector-directed geodesics coalesce; (iii) there is no bigeodesic with one end directed in \(S_\theta\). In particular, for each fixed deterministic direction, no bigeodesic has an end in that direction under these assumptions. This is not a simultaneous all-directions event.

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 5 and proof pp. 25–26) gives sector Busemann limits. For any \(x,y\) and any possibly random sequence \(x_n\) directed in \(S_\theta\),
\[
B(x,y)=\lim_n[T(x,x_n)-T(y,x_n)]
\]
exists almost surely. If \(\rho\) is the normal vector defining the tangent support line, then \(\mathbb E B(0,x)=\rho\cdot x\), and for every \(\varepsilon>0\) only finitely many \(x\) violate \(|B(0,x)-\rho\cdot x|\le\varepsilon\|x\|_1\). Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 5–7) also resolves the BKS midpoint question under the same differentiability assumptions: \(\mathbb P(\lfloor n/2\rfloor e_1\text{ lies on some geodesic from }0\text{ to }ne_1)\to0\).

## Proof chain

Section 4 (PDF pp. 11–23) constructs leftmost/rightmost sector geodesics by ordering planar paths and taking limits of finite geodesics to extremal boundary points. A trapping argument confines subsequential limits to the shape-contact sector; unique passage times prevent crossings. Coalescence of the extremal full-plane geodesics uses the Licea–Newman argument and a Burton–Keane counting contradiction; the upward finite-energy condition supplies a high-weight barrier when weights are not unbounded (Propositions 4.8–4.11). The half-plane versions are needed to order paths.

Section 5 (PDF pp. 23–25) defines left/right half-plane Busemann functions. Planar path crossing gives \(\Delta_H(0,e_1)=B_H^L(0,e_1)-B_H^R(0,e_1)\le0\); if left/right rays differ with positive probability, uniqueness forces strict inequality in expectation, and ergodicity gives distinct linear slopes. But shape differentiability identifies both slopes with the same unique supporting normal, a contradiction. Thus the left/right rays agree. Their coalescence produces endpoint-independent sector Busemann limits. A mass-transport/tree argument then rules out an infinite backward branch, hence a bigeodesic with one sector-directed end (proof p. 26). The midpoint corollary translates repeated midpoint passage into a bigeodesic with a sector-directed end (pp. 6–7).

## Transfer boundary

The conclusion is two-dimensional and needs shape differentiability at the base point and both sector endpoints, plus one of A1′/A2′. It rules out a bigeodesic with an end in each fixed deterministic direction, not all directions on one probability-one event and not bigeodesics without direction assumptions. Its directed-LPP comparisons in Section 3 are background only; no higher-dimensional or directed-LPP transfer is asserted.
