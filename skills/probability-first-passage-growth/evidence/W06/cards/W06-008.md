# W06-008 — Bertrand–Pertinand, *Dimension Improvement in Dhar's Refutation of the Eden Conjecture*

**Source.** Quentin Bertrand, Jules Pertinand, *Dimension improvement in Dhar's refutation of the Eden conjecture*, arXiv:1705.08143v2 (last revised 31 Jan 2018), 11-page PDF; the official arXiv record gives related journal DOI 10.1016/j.physleta.2018.01.025. Local copy: evidence/fulltexts/W06-008.pdf. All 11 PDF pages were read. The theorem relies on finite recursive computations described in the paper; the code was not independently re-run here.

## Exact result and proof route

For the Eden model on the unoriented hypercubic lattice, equivalently FPP with i.i.d. rate-one exponential **edge** weights, Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2) proves \(\mu_{\rm axis}^{22}<\mu_{\rm diag}^{22}\), so the limiting shape is not Euclidean in dimension 22. This agrees with archived C100's edge-weight model. The paper's abstract says the conjecture fails in dimensions superior to 22, but the theorem explicitly displayed and checked here is the strict comparison at \(d=22\). Since this card does not verify a separate extension argument for every \(d>22\), it records only \(d=22\), not a theorem for all \(d\ge22\).

The comparison has two distinct inputs: a rigorous analytic lower bound \(\mu_{\rm diag}^d\ge0.3313\ldots/\sqrt d\) imported from Couronné–Enriquez–Gerin, and an upper bound on \(\mu_{\rm axis}^d\) computed by Dhar's one-way infection recursion. For a unidirectional infection crossing \(n\) coordinate hyperplanes, the memoryless exponential clocks give a finite recursion for maximal expected residual infection times \(T_{i_0,\dots,i_{n-1}}\) over infected cluster sizes. In the one-plane case, the boundary size is bounded below by \(s_i=\lceil2(d-1)i^{(d-2)/(d-1)}\rceil\) as printed; the recursion has form \(T_i\le1+s_iT_{i+1}/(s_i+i)\). For several planes, the recurrence bounds infection transitions between cluster-size vectors and uses disjoint straight paths for terminal boundary data. The finite arrays are evaluated in C++; the table on PDF p. 9 reports upper bounds for dimensions 2,3,4,5,22,25,30,35 and \(n\le5\). The dimension-22 axis upper bound lies strictly below the imported diagonal lower bound.

The paper notes that this strategy also reaches dimension 16 only after assuming a monotonicity of successive expected increments that the authors could not prove; that observation is not a theorem. The growth rule is the edge-clock rule: each infected vertex infects each neighbor at rate one, so a healthy site with \(k\) infected neighbors has rate \(k\). Do not substitute the survey's different vertex-weight Eden rule, which chooses uniformly among distinct healthy boundary sites.

## Transfer limits

This proves a non-ball limit shape for the archived edge-FPP model in dimension 22. It does not establish the \(d\ge2\) universal claim in C100, and this card does not infer a theorem in every higher dimension from Theorem 1 alone. The method depends on exponential memorylessness, a one-way comparison process, and finite computational bounds; its output is not an explicit time constant or a stochastic simulation result. The local data contain the article, but its C++ implementation is not bundled, so only the paper's reported finite recursion values are recorded.
