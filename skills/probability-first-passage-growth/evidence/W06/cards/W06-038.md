# W06-038 — Aldous, *A discrete Hammersley process as an extreme case of oriented percolation flows*

**Source and reading record.** David Aldous, undated web note; complete local HTML text read. Original page: https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/hammersley_flow.html. The page gives no publication date, journal, formal theorem numbering, or proof of its stated limit claims. Local snapshot: evidence/W06/data/aldous-hammersley-oriented-flow.html; it is the source, not a PDF.

## Model and statements

For integer \(n\ge2\), use the directed square grid \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^2\), with each horizontal edge oriented right and each vertical edge oriented up. Edges are independently open with probability \(p\in(0,1)\), otherwise closed; give them capacities 1 and 0, respectively. The source set is the left and bottom boundary excluding the far upper/right endpoints:
\[
S_n=\{(0,j):0\le j\le n-2\}\cup\{(i,0):1\le i\le n-2\}.
\]
The sink set is the full top side together with the right side below the top-right corner:
\[
T_n=\{(i,n-1):0\le i\le n-1\}\cup\{(n-1,j):0\le j\le n-2\}.
\]
The note defines \(V(n,p)\) as the maximum number of edge-disjoint open directed paths from \(S_n\) to \(T_n\), equivalently the integral max flow with these capacities. It states, without proof in the note, that a deterministic \(v(p)\) exists with
\[
V(n,p)/(2n)\longrightarrow v(p)\quad\text{in }L^1,
\]
and that \(v(p)=0\) for \(p<p_*\), \(v(p)>0\) for \(p>p_*\), and \(v(p)\to1\) as \(p\uparrow1\), where \(p_*\) is the oriented-percolation threshold. The note makes no claim about \(v(p_*)\).

It then states a complement relation, up to negligible boundary terms, between \(1-v(p)\) and the normalized number \(U(n,p)/(2n)\). The note describes \(U(n,p)\) as the minimum number of edge-disjoint oriented paths, with the same source/sink boundary sets, that cover all closed edges. This motivates a continuum approximation: when \(p\uparrow1\), closed edges are heuristically treated as a Poisson point process of rate \(\delta=2(1-p)\) per unit area in a square of side \(n\). If \(U^*(n,\delta)\) is the minimum number of oriented continuum paths covering all Poisson points, the page writes \(U^*(n,\delta)\overset d=U^*(\delta^{-1/2}n,1)\), which has the spatial scaling reversed. The note invokes the Hammersley-process asymptotic \(U^*(m,1)=(1+o(1))m\), while explicitly saying it is unsure whether that statement is written explicitly in the cited literature.

## Source-level scaling and coefficient defects

The web note itself writes the inverse-square-root scaling displayed above. For intensity δ on a square of side n, multiplying both coordinates by a sends intensity to δ/a². The intensity-one image therefore uses a=√δ and side √δ n, giving U*(n,δ) equal in law to U*(√δ n,1). This is a dimension correction to the source text, not a verbatim source statement.

There is also a separate coefficient inconsistency. With the note's normalization U/(2n), the corrected scaling and a putative limit U*(m,1)/m→c yield deficit c√δ/2. The note's displayed c=1 would give √δ/2, rather than its stated conjecture √δ. Its conjectural coefficient would require c=2. A rigorous continuum path-cover constant, for example through a precise Dilworth/Hammersley connection, must be independently sourced before using that coefficient. This card does not claim to prove that asymptotic constant or the discrete/continuum limit interchange.

## Conjecture and proof gap

Despite the two transcription/coefficient inconsistencies above, the note explicitly states the following conjecture
\[
1-v(p)\sim\sqrt{2(1-p)}\qquad(p\uparrow1).
\]
This is **not proved** by the note. Aldous identifies the interchange of \(n\to\infty\) with \(p\uparrow1\) as the difficult point: the Poisson/Hammersley approximation must have errors controlled uniformly at the scale of the proposed square-root deficit. The page reports simulations by Jason Lenderman as supportive, but includes no data or error analysis. The existence of \(v(p)\) at each fixed \(p\), even accepted as stated, does not justify this two-limit asymptotic.

## Relation to archived A030 and validation boundary

This is the primary source for archived A030's precise question and candidate constant. The finite max-flow definition is rigorous, while the square-root asymptotic is expressly conjectural. The local MATLAB check in evidence/W06/fpp_flow_exact_small.m exhaustively enumerates all \(2^4=16\) edge states for \(n=2\) and \(2^{12}=4096\) states for \(n=3\), computes the exact finite-state expectation polynomial (evaluated in floating point at listed \(p\)'s), and confirms the graph's source/sink convention. It is finite-volume numerical evidence only; it neither proves the fixed-\(p\) limit nor the \(p\uparrow1\) coefficient.

**Transfer boundary.** The note gives the Hammersley/Poisson analogy and a named open obstruction, not a coupling theorem or a quantitative error bound. Any proof of A030 still needs an \(n\)-uniform comparison between discrete closed-edge covers and continuum Poisson covers strong enough to exchange limits at the square-root scale. The note itself offers no such estimate.

## Follow-up literature check — 2026-11-14

The complete Aldous–Diaconis article (W06-039, *Probability Theory and Related Fields* 103 (1995), 199–213) has now been read. Its Theorem 5 proves the unit-intensity Poisson longest-chain constant c=2. Conditional on M points in a square, their coordinate ranks form a uniform permutation; Dilworth’s theorem identifies the minimum number of increasing chains covering those points with the longest decreasing subsequence, which has the same distribution as the LIS after reversing values. Since M is Poisson with mean m², this proves U*(m,1)/m→2 in probability for the continuum point-cover definition used in this note. Thus the continuum coefficient needed by the corrected heuristic is now sourced: c√δ/2=√δ.

This update supplies the continuum path-cover constant that was previously left unverified. It does not change the source-level fact that Aldous’s web note itself does not prove the constant. The discrete model imposes edge-disjoint paths, and W06-039 proves no uniform coupling or discretization error for the Bernoulli lattice as p approaches one. The conjecture 1−v(p)~√(2(1−p)) therefore remains unproved by the evidence in this library.
