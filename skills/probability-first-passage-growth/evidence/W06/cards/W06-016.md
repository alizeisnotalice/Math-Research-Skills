## Model and theorem

The network is undirected nearest-neighbor edge-FPP on the rescaled graph \(\mathbb Z^d/n\), \(d\ge2\), with i.i.d. nonnegative edge capacities of law \(\Lambda\). The continuum domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\) is bounded, open, connected, and has piecewise \(C^1\) boundary; \(\Gamma^1,\Gamma^2\) are disjoint relatively open source/sink boundary pieces. \(\phi_n\) is the maximum capacity flow between their discrete approximations. The normalization is surface order \(n^{d-1}\).

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3)** assumes \(\int e^{\theta x}\,d\Lambda(x)<\infty\) for some \(\theta>0\) and \(\Lambda(0)<1-p_c(d)\). For each \(\lambda<\phi_\Omega\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d-1}}\log\mathbb P\{\phi_n\le\lambda n^{d-1}\}<0.
\]
Here \(\phi_\Omega\) is the anisotropic continuum cut value formed from directional cylinder-flow surface tension \(\nu(v)\) and finite-perimeter separating sets. The paper states the sharp zero-capacity threshold for positivity, \(\phi_\Omega>0\) iff \(\Lambda(0)<1-p_c(d)\). Constants in the exponential bound depend on the law, dimension, domain/boundary data, and the chosen subcritical level; no universal rate is given.

## Proof chain and imported input

Sections 2.1–2.5 define cylinder flows, invoke max-flow/min-cut, recall the surface tension \(\nu\), and define the anisotropic BV cut functional. The lower-deviation input is explicitly imported from Rossignol–Théret: for a fixed cylinder, an abnormally small flow has probability bounded by \(K'\exp[-K n^{d-1}\mathcal H^{d-1}(A)]\) under finite mean and the zero-mass condition. It is not a new cylinder LDP proved in this article.

Theorem 4 (PDF p. 8; Zhang, cited and adapted) bounds the number of edges in a minimal cutset: under an exponential moment and \(\Lambda(0)<1-p_c(d)\), for \(\beta\) large and \(n\) large,
\[
\mathbb P\{|E_n|\ge \beta n^{d-1}\}\le C_1e^{-C_2\beta n^{d-1}}.
\]
Section 4 combines this estimate with compactness to restrict a low-capacity cut to a finite-perimeter macroscopic separator. Section 5 selects a finite cover of its reduced boundary using Vitali/Besicovitch and differentiation estimates, classifies interior versus boundary balls, and defines local events \(G(x_i,r_i,v_i)\) expressing closeness to a flat cut plus an abnormally low local cost. The physical p. 16 reduction is a **union bound**:
\[
\mathbb P\{V(E_n)\le \lambda n^{d-1},\; \mathcal L^d(E_n\triangle F)\le\varepsilon_F\}
\le \sum_{i\in I\cup J\cup K}\mathbb P\{G(x_i,r_i,v_i)\}.
\]
No independence of the ball events is claimed. Section 6 (“Surgery in a ball to define an almost flat cutset”) modifies each local cut into a cylinder cut: the symmetric-difference error controls the number of added boundary edges. Conditional on the interior event, the capacities of these connector edges are independent of the interior configuration; this is used to compare a local \(G\)-event to a low cylinder-flow event or an exceptional large-sum event, not to multiply probabilities across balls.

Section 7 bounds each local event using the imported cylinder lower-deviation estimate and an exponential-moment bound for the connector-capacity sum; the number of possible slab positions contributes only a factor of order \(n\), absorbed by the surface-order exponential decay. Applying the covering union bound from p. 16 and calibrating the geometric and deviation margins makes the cost deficit \(\phi_\Omega-\lambda>0\) survive all covering, surgery, and connector errors. The independent parallel-layer product mechanism belongs to the companion upper-deviation paper W06-017, not this lower-deviation proof. This remains an undirected continuum-domain proof, not a directed Bernoulli lattice approximation.

## Transfer boundary

The theorem concerns a fixed capacity law and the limit \(n\to\infty\), with continuum domain regularity and an exponential moment. It neither establishes a \(p\uparrow1\) limit nor supplies a Poisson/Hammersley interface, square-root normalization, or uniform two-parameter error bound required by archived A030. Use it as an anisotropic min-cut and surface-order lower-tail comparator only.
