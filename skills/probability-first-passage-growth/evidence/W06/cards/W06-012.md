## Model and theorems

The ambient graph \((V,E)\) is an induced subgraph of \(\mathbb Z^2\) whose vertex set and complement are both infinite and connected. A simple boundary \(\{v_n\}\) is obtained along the doubly infinite dual boundary. Passage times are nonnegative edge weights; a geodesic is a finite minimizing path. The boundary sequence need not be a deterministic ray in a direction.

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 4).** For every configuration \(\omega\) and all \(x,y\in V\), \(B(x,y)=\lim_n[\tau(x,v_n)-\tau(y,v_n)]\) exists. The authors explicitly leave the analogous arbitrary-direction, full-plane assertion open.
- **Theorem 1.3 (PDF pp. 4–5).** If the law admits finite geodesics a.s., the directed finite-geodesic graphs to \(v_n\) converge almost surely, and every directed path in the limiting graph is a geodesic.
- **Theorem 1.5 (PDF p. 6).** In the upper half-plane, under either (A) product weights with continuous marginals, or (B) restriction of a symmetric, translation-ergodic full-plane law with an upward finite-energy property, unique passage times, a finite \((2+\alpha)\)-moment, and bounded limit shape, the limiting graph has out-degree one, no undirected circuits, finite backward clusters, and all forward infinite paths coalesce. The theorem does **not** identify their asymptotic direction as \(e_1\).

## Proof chain and limits

In §2 (PDF pp. 6–9), a Jordan-curve crossing argument makes the boundary Busemann differences monotone and bounded below by \(-\tau(x,y)\). For arbitrary \(x,y\), the proof removes a finite connected set, isolates the component containing all sufficiently distant boundary vertices, and uses a finite cut interface to express passage times as minima over finitely many boundary entrance sites. Limits of the interface terms yield the Busemann difference.

Section 3 (PDF pp. 10–13) proves local convergence of geodesic graphs by stabilizing the finite set of minimizing boundary vertices. In §4 (PDF pp. 13–24), Kingman's theorem gives densities \(\alpha_k,\beta_k\) for disjoint geodesics; a positive density of disjoint paths at height zero forces \(\alpha_0>0\). Planarity relates \(\alpha_k-\beta_k\) to \(\alpha_0\), while a local edge-increase modification creates a positive density of paths counted by \(\beta_k\) under the assumption of noncoalescence, a contradiction. The unbounded-support case uses a simpler large-edge modification. This is a half-plane argument, not a generic full-plane coalescence theorem.

Appendix A (PDF pp. 24–25) proves that the dual edge boundary is one simple doubly infinite path. Appendix B (pp. 25–27) proves finite geodesic existence for product measures: if zero-weight probability is below 1/2, truncate at 1 and use a shape lower bound to keep minimizers in a finite box; otherwise use infinitely many zero circuits in annuli to replace escaping path segments without increasing cost. Appendix C (pp. 27–34) adapts Wehr–Woo's no-bigeodesic proof. Under (A), supercritical edges of weight at most \(D\) yield open half-circuits and a cost \(O(Dn^{3/2})\) separator, contradicting the \(\Omega(n^2)\) weight forced by a bigeodesic. Under (B), the boundary second-moment estimate and Borel–Cantelli give the contradiction. The moment/finite-energy alternatives are used differently and must remain separate.

## Transfer boundary

The result supplies an exact monotone Busemann construction and half-plane coalescence framework for C093/C097. The arbitrary-subgraph Busemann limit is deterministic in the weight configuration, but the coalescence theorem needs its separate stochastic assumptions. No direction claim or all-directions full-plane limit follows.
