## Model and hypotheses

Undirected nearest-neighbor edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), with i.i.d. nonnegative edge weights \(t_e\). The product law satisfies
\[
\text{(A1)}\quad \mathbb E Y^2<\infty,\quad Y=\min(t_1,\dots,t_d),\qquad
\text{(A2)}\quad \mathbb P(t_e=0)<p_c(d).
\]
A2 gives finite geodesic existence a.s.; A1+A2 yield the norm time constant \(\mu\). For the full shape theorem, the paper separately notes the usual sufficient condition that the minimum of \(2d\) i.i.d. weights has finite \(d\)-th moment.

Proposition 1.1 (PDF p. 2) gives, for \(\|x\|_1>1\),
\[
\mu(x)\le \mathbb ET(0,x)\le\mu(x)+C_1\sqrt{\|x\|_1\log\|x\|_1}.
\]
Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 4) proves a Gaussian lower-tail bound at scale \(\sqrt{\|x\|_1}\):
\[
\mathbb P\{T(0,x)-\mathbb ET(0,x)\le-t\sqrt{\|x\|_1}\}\le e^{-c t^2},\qquad t\ge0,
\]
for a constant \(c>0\) depending on the model assumptions.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2) gives an almost-sure outer shape rate under A1+A2:
\[
\frac{B(t)}t\subset\bigl(1+C_2t^{-1/2}(\log t)^{1/2}\bigr)B_0\quad\text{for all large }t.
\]
If, in addition, \(\mathbb E t_e^\alpha<\infty\) for some \(\alpha>1+1/d\), then the inner bound is
\[
\bigl(1-C_3t^{-1/2}(\log t)^4\bigr)B_0\subset\frac{B(t)}t\quad\text{for all large }t.
\]
Constants \(C_1,C_2,C_3,c\) are finite and may depend on the distribution and dimension; no universal numerical value is claimed.

## Proof dependencies

Section 2 (PDF pp. 4–8) uses the entropy method. Tensorization plus a block-tensorization argument localizes entropy to edge neighborhoods; a resampling inequality bounds it by the weighted number of pivotal edges. The expected pivotal count is controlled by splitting edges into those below a multiple of \(T\) and an exceptional surplus. Kesten's exponential small-time estimate for long self-avoiding paths, available under A2, gives an exponential tail for that surplus. The Herbst argument turns the resulting entropy inequality into Theorem 2.3 and then the Gaussian lower tail. Proposition 1.1 combines that tail with Alexander's approximation of subadditive functions.

For the outer shape rate, Section 3.1 (PDF pp. 9–10) applies a union bound over lattice endpoints and Borel–Cantelli to the Gaussian lower tail and the expectation approximation. For the inner bound, Sections 3.1–3.2 (PDF pp. 10–12) combine Zhang's concentration inequality for passage between logarithmic boxes with Ahlberg's upper-tail estimate for low-moment weights. Summability uses \(\mathbb E Z^{2d+2+\delta}<\infty\) for \(Z\) the minimum of \(2d\) weights, which follows from \(\mathbb E t_e^\alpha<\infty\), \(\alpha>1+1/d\). The logarithmic powers differ between outer and inner inclusions and should not be merged.

## Transfer boundary

These are quantitative rates for undirected i.i.d. edge-FPP under explicit moment and subcritical-zero-mass assumptions. They do not prove fluctuation-exponent existence or values, do not identify the exact time constant, and do not apply to critical zero percolation. Sublinear deviation bounds are not polynomial lower-fluctuation bounds and cannot establish the moment-exponent equality requested in C090 by themselves.
