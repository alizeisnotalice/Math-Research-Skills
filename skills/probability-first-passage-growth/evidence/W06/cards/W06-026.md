## Model and theorem

The model is i.i.d. undirected edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), with nonnegative edge-weight law \(\mu\). **Theorem 1.1 (PDF physical p. 3)** assumes
\[
\mathbb E[t_e^2(\log t_e)_+]<\infty,\qquad \mu(\{0\})<p_c(d).
\]
Then for \(\|x\|_1>1\) (with small endpoints absorbed into constants),
\[
\operatorname{Var}\tau(0,x)\le C(\mu,d)\frac{\|x\|_1}{\log\|x\|_1}.
\]
The law may be discrete, absolutely continuous, singular continuous, or a mixture; no density or log-Sobolev assumption is imposed. \(C\) is distribution- and dimension-dependent. This is an upper bound, not a polynomial fluctuation exponent or a matching lower bound.

## Proof chain

Section 1 states the model and theorem and previews the method. Section 2 recalls entropy tensorization and the Falik–Samorodnitsky inequality. In §3.1 the passage time is approximated by an average \(F_m\) of translates with \(m\) on the order of \(\|x\|_1^{1/4}\); §3.2 applies the entropy inequality and martingale decomposition, with geodesic-intersection and Kesten path estimates controlling the relevant influences. The averaging error is smaller order than the final \(n/\log n\) bound.

Section 4 revisits the Benaïm–Rossignol approach for continuous laws; it is explanatory and independent of the proof of the general-distribution theorem. Section 5 supplies a lower bound for the variance contribution through a Newman–Piza resampling argument: in directions/geodesic configurations where passage times are not trapped at the minimum, geodesics must use a positive density of sufficiently large edges. Shape comparison under strictly more variable laws gives the required count.

Section 6 proves the general-law result. In §6.1 each weight is represented by an infinite sequence of Bernoulli variables through the generalized inverse of \(\mu\). In §6.2 Bonami’s inequality reduces entropy to discrete derivatives, and the proof bounds those derivatives by indicators/penalties of edges in geodesics. The lattice-animal estimate in §6.2.3 controls the random geodesic sums under the \(2+\log\) moment condition; §6.2.4 completes the entropy estimate, and §6.3 concludes Theorem 1.1. The quantity formed from the minimum of the \(2d\) incident weights and its tail enter the comparison; subcritical zero mass is needed for the geodesic/path estimates.

## Transfer boundary

The conclusion is the \(n/\log n\) variance upper bound under the explicit moment and subcritical-zero assumptions. It is not an equality of exponents, does not imply a \(n^{2/3}\) variance law, and does not cover critical Bernoulli FPP.
