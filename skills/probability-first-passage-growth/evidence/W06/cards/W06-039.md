## Model and exact claims

Let P be a unit-intensity homogeneous Poisson point process in the positive quadrant. Let L(x,t) be the maximum number of Poisson points on a coordinatewise nondecreasing (up-right) path from (0,0) to (x,t). For the square, g(t)=E L(t,t) is superadditive, and the paper defines c=lim g(t)/t. Theorem 5(a) proves **c=2**. Theorem 5(b) proves that along a characteristic line x=at, for every fixed a>0, the particle configuration in a fixed local window converges in distribution to a homogeneous Poisson process of rate a^(-1/2).

In §2.1, conditional on the number M(x,t)~Poisson(xt) of points in a rectangle, ordering by one coordinate makes the ranks of the other coordinate a uniform permutation. Its longest increasing subsequence (LIS) equals the longest up-right chain. Superadditivity plus the subadditive ergodic theorem gives L(t,t)/t→c for the Poisson model; de-Poissonization then gives n^(-1/2) E L_n→c and n^(-1/2)L_n→c in probability for the LIS L_n of a uniform permutation. The paper does not claim an almost-sure limit for the fixed-n permutation model.

## Proof chain for the exact constant

1. **Poissonization and scaling (§§2.1–2.3, local physical pp. 3–8).** The uniform-permutation representation, superadditivity and space-time scaling reduce the asymptotics to c. The hydrodynamic PDE in §2.3 is expressly a heuristic; it predicts c=2 but is not the proof.
2. **Stationary full-line process (§2.4, local physical pp. 8–11).** The graphical construction extends Hammersley’s particle process to the full line. Lemma 7 classifies finite-intensity, translation-invariant stationary laws as mixtures of Poisson processes; Lemma 8 supplies space-time interchange.
3. **Upper bound c≤2 (§2.5, local physical p. 11).** Couple the empty-half-line process with a stationary rate-b full-line process. The expectation comparison gives E L(x,t)≤bx+t/b; optimizing b=√(t/x) gives 2√(tx), hence c≤2.
4. **Local coupling and derivative limit (§2.6, local physical pp. 11–13).** Monotone couplings under time and spatial shifts give local increment estimates. Superadditivity and scaling imply the derivative asymptotic used to make diagonal-window distributions tight and identify their mean intensity.
5. **Lower bound and local equilibrium (§2.7, local physical pp. 14–15; equations (20)–(21) on p. 15).** Every subsequential diagonal local limit is stationary and translation invariant, hence conditionally Poisson with random rate Λ. Space-time interchange implies Λ has the same law as 1/Λ. Since x↦x and x↦1/x are oppositely monotone, Λ and Λ^(-1) are negatively correlated, so \(1=E[\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}]\le(E\Lambda)(E\Lambda^{-1})=(E\Lambda)^2\); hence EΛ≥1. Lemma 11 and Fatou's lemma give c≥2EΛ≥2. Together with the upper bound, c=2; equality forces Λ=1 almost surely, identifying the local limit as rate-one Poisson. Scaling yields rate a^(-1/2) for every a>0.

## Exact continuum cover corollary and interface

The paper proves a longest-chain theorem, not a max-flow theorem. A separate finite-poset argument gives the relevant continuum chain-cover constant: conditional on M Poisson points in a square, the minimum number of coordinatewise increasing chains partitioning the points equals the poset width by Dilworth’s theorem. The width is the longest decreasing subsequence of the associated uniform permutation; reversing the value order makes its distribution equal to the LIS distribution. Thus the minimum chain-cover count has the same law as L_M, and with M~Poisson(t²) its count divided by t converges in probability to 2. This is an exact continuum point-set/chain-cover statement, derived from the article’s LIS theorem plus Dilworth; it is not an assertion about every lattice flow formulation.

For W06-038, the article therefore validates the continuum Poisson/Hammersley constant **c=2** (so the note’s needed cover coefficient is mathematically available after fixing the note’s scaling algebra). It does **not** provide a uniform coupling from rare closed edges on the directed Bernoulli lattice to a Poisson point process, control discretization and boundary errors uniformly as p→1, or justify swapping the box-size and near-one limits. Those discrete-to-continuum and limit-interchange steps remain unproved here; W06-038 remains conjectural as a discrete statement.

## Transfer boundary

The model is a planar Poisson point process and an associated Hammersley interacting-particle process. Its exact constant 2 transfers to continuum monotone point-chain covering through the finite-poset argument above. It does not alone prove the square-root coefficient or asymptotic for an oriented Bernoulli edge max-flow/min-cut, and it says nothing about undirected edge-FPP geodesics or KPZ fluctuation exponents.
