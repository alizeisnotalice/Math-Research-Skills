# W06-006 — Damron–Lam–Wang, *Asymptotics for 2D Critical First Passage Percolation*

**Source.** Michael Damron, Wai-Kit Lam, Xuan Wang, arXiv:1505.07544v2 (15 Aug 2015), 28-page PDF. Local copy: evidence/fulltexts/W06-006.pdf. The full 28-page text, including the proof and point-to-point corollaries, was read.

## Exact model and main results

The model is **two-dimensional square-lattice** FPP with i.i.d. nonnegative edge weights, \(F(0^-)=0\), and \(F(0)=p_c=1/2\). Let \(\rho(F)=\lim_n T(0,\partial B(n))\), where \(B(n)=[-n,n]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Define \(\eta_0=\sup\{\eta>0:E[t_e^{\eta/4}]<\infty\}\).

- Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 3): if \(\eta_0>1\), the mean of \(T(0,\partial B(2^n))\) is bounded above and below, up to positive constants depending on \(F\), by \(\sum_{k=2}^n F^{-1}(p_c+2^{-k})\). The lower bound is stated without the same moment assumption. Corollary 1.3 gives the exact criterion \(\rho(F)<\infty\) a.s. iff this inverse-quantile series converges; if it diverges, the passage time tends to infinity a.s.
- Theorem 1.5 (p. 4): if \(\eta_0>2\), \(\operatorname{Var}(T(0,\partial B(2^n)))\) is bounded above and below by constants times \(\sum_{k=2}^n[F^{-1}(p_c+2^{-k})]^2\).
- Theorem 1.6 (p. 4): under \(\eta_0>2\) and divergence of the first inverse-quantile sum, if the squared series converges then the centered boundary passage times converge a.s. and in \(L^2\) to a mean-zero finite-variance random variable. If the squared series diverges, the centered and variance-normalized passage times converge in distribution to standard normal.
- Corollaries 5.11–5.13 (PDF pp. 25–27) extend corresponding mean/variance/limit statements to \(T(0,x)\), with \(\|x\|_\infty\to\infty\); in the finite-square-series regime the point-to-point limit is a sum of two independent copies of the boundary limit.

## Proof dependencies

The authors couple \(t_e=F^{-1}(\omega_e)\), with \(\omega_e\) i.i.d. uniform, to critical Bernoulli percolation and invasion percolation (PDF pp. 6–8). They compare the optimal FPP path to the path constrained inside the invasion cluster. A near-critical parameter \(p_k\) is chosen through correlation length \(L(p_k)\asymp2^k\); the paper uses planar RSW, arm estimates, and exponentially decaying invasion outlet/circuit tails. Lemma 3.1 (pp. 9–14) bounds annular passage-time moments by \(C(a_k^r+e^{-\lambda k})\), where \(a_k=F^{-1}(p_c+2^{-\epsilon_0 k/2})\); a scale-comparison lemma transfers the exponent change to the theorem's dyadic inverse-quantile series. The variance/CLT proof uses the innermost critical open circuit \(C_k\), a filtration by its interior (p. 16), Kesten–Zhang martingale increments \(\Delta_k\) independent of terminal scale, RSW lower variance contributions, exponential strong mixing between annuli, and McLeish's dependent-array CLT (pp. 17–25). The comparison between circuit and box-boundary passage times is uniformly bounded in \(L^r\) (Lemma 5.10, pp. 23–24).

## Transfer limits

This result is not a solution to the archive's **three-dimensional** Bernoulli critical problem C091: it depends on two-dimensional planar duality, RSW, and invasion-percolation estimates. In the special 2D Bernoulli(0/1) law, the inverse-quantile sum is a constant multiple of \(n\), so this theorem predicts linear growth in \(\log_2\) radius; the exact logarithmic mean for that law was already known from Chayes–Chayes–Durrett. Do not infer the same order in \(d=3\), nor for arbitrary critical weight laws.
