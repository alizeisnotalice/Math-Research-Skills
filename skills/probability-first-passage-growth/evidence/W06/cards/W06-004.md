# W06-004 — Coupier, *Multiple geodesics with the same direction*

**Source.** David Coupier, arXiv:1104.1321v1 (7 Apr 2011), 10-page PDF. Local copy: `evidence/fulltexts/W06-004.pdf`. All 10 PDF pages and the proof/reference list were read.

## Model and theorem

The model is **directed last-passage percolation**, not undirected FPP: i.i.d. vertex weights on \(\mathbb N^2\) are exponential with rate 1; admissible paths take east/north steps; passage value is the maximum sum. Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 3–4) states: (1) a.s. the random set of directions with two infinite geodesics is dense and countable in \([0,\pi/2]\); (2) a.s. no direction has more than two infinite geodesics; (3) for each fixed deterministic direction \(\alpha\), a.s. there is exactly one geodesic with direction \(\alpha\). Part (3) is a per-direction probability-one statement; it coexists with (1) because the exceptional direction set is random and dense. Theorem 2 states positive-probability (n)-coexistence for every finite (n), where the sources are ((0,n-1),(1,n-2),\ldots,(n-1,0)).

## Proof dependencies

- The cited Ferrari–Pimentel input gives (i) every infinite geodesic has a direction, (ii) each deterministic direction has at least one ray a.s., and (iii) uniqueness for almost every deterministic direction. The proof uses the east/north planar ordering and competition interface.
- Proposition 3 (PDF pp. 4–7) is the key local modification: any event (S) measurable through the diagonal last-passage values (G(z), |z|=m), and weights outside that diagonal, if it has positive probability, can be intersected with any positive-probability finite tree-shape event (B) while retaining positive probability. The proof conditions on the diagonal vector (G_m=X_m+Y_m); the independent exponential diagonal increments (X_m) have a positive density. For a positive-measure set of (G_m), choose (Y_m\in[0,\varepsilon]^{m+1}) and diagonal values in a small rectangle so the desired signs of neighboring last-passage differences force the chosen tree. Lemma 5 constructs such a prescribed finite tree inductively, with ε updated by ε_{k+1}=(4k+2)\varepsilon_k.
- For part 2, three same-direction rays with positive probability can be locally forced through three diagonal sites with only the middle subtree crossing the middle site. That subtree would be unbounded but have zero density between rays of the same direction, contradicting Coupier–Heinrich's zero-one/density result (Theorem 2 of the cited paper).
- For part 3, if two rays share any fixed deterministic direction with positive probability, locally force them through different children of the origin. This makes the competition interface have an atom at that direction, contradicting Ferrari–Pimentel's atomless direction law. Combine with existence of at least one ray in every deterministic direction.
- For part 1, take two rays with distinct endpoint directions enclosing a nonempty interval. Their highest/lowest infinite branches have ordered directions; any gap would force a forbidden directional ray between them. Thus the interval contains a direction with two rays. Rational intervals give density, while mapping each exceptional direction to its unique bifurcation vertex gives countability.
- For Theorem 2, choose (n) deterministic directions, a large diagonal where the corresponding rays hit distinct sites with positive probability, then use Proposition 3 to force the finite geodesics to emanate from the (n) prescribed source sites. Each source then has an infinite descendant tree.

## Transfer limits

All results rely on integrable directed LPP structure, exponential weights, the TASEP/competition-interface coupling, and planar order. They do not prove the corresponding statement for undirected FPP rays (including C097 or C098), nor do they imply ordinary FPP KPZ exponents. Quantifier order is essential: “for every fixed direction, almost surely” is weaker than “almost surely, for every direction.”
