# W06-005 — Tessera, *Speed of Convergence in First Passage Percolation and Geodesicity of the Average Distance*

**Source.** Romain Tessera, arXiv:1410.1701v3 (11 May 2015), 20-page PDF. Local copy: evidence/fulltexts/W06-005.pdf. All 20 PDF pages were read.

## Results and assumptions

- Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3): on any Cayley graph of \(\mathbb Z^d\) from a finite generating set, assume edge weights have an exponential moment (A1) and the average metric satisfies \(\bar d(x,y)=E d_\omega(x,y)\ge a d(x,y)\) for some \(a>0\) (A2). Then a deterministic norm and random finite constants give an a.s. limit-shape error of order \(\sqrt{n\log n}\) on both sides: \(B_{\|\cdot\|}(0,n-C\sqrt{n\log n})\cap\mathbb Z^d\subset B_\omega(0,n)\subset B_{\|\cdot\|}(0,n+C\sqrt{n\log n})\). In the standard Cayley graph A2 follows if \(\nu(\{0\})<p_c\); the paper does not claim this exact criterion for every graph.
- Proposition 1.2 (pp. 4, 8–9): if graph balls have polynomial volume growth \(|B(o_n,r_n)|\le Kr_n^d\), Talagrand concentration plus a union bound and Borel–Cantelli controls all pairs in a ball by \(C\sqrt{r_n\log r_n}\) eventually a.s.
- Proposition 1.7 (pp. 6, 8–9): under the same concentration/bi-Lipschitz hypotheses, the average distance is strongly asymptotically geodesic with \(N(r)=c\sqrt{r/\log r}\). The argument chooses an environment where the random metric is uniformly close to its average metric on a large ball, takes a random-metric geodesic, and finds a point near the requested fraction along it. A2 prevents that geodesic from exiting the ball; exponential edge tails control its largest edge.
- Proposition 1.8 (pp. 7, 13–18): for an invariant metric on \(\mathbb Z^d\), a quantitative strong-asymptotic-geodesic estimate with rate \(N\gtrsim\phi\) is equivalent to a norm limit shape with Hausdorff error \(O(n/\phi(n))\), for the stated increasing sublinearly-doubling \(\phi\). The proof uses the abelian-group structure and Carathéodory convexification; it is geometric and independent of FPP randomness.

## Proof dependencies / constants

The concentration inequality quoted from Talagrand is \(P(|d_\omega-\bar d|\ge u)\le C_1\exp[-C_2\min(u^2/\bar d,u)]\). The volume-growth union bound applies it to at most \(K^2r^{2d}\) ordered pairs and makes the failure probability summable. The average-metric estimate is then a geometric transfer through geodesicity of \(d_\omega\); the final square-root-log rate is not obtained from Kingman's theorem. The constants depend on the concentration constants, growth bound, and bi-Lipschitz comparison.

## Transfer limits

The theorem gives a rate for convergence to an unspecified norm, not an explicit time constant or fluctuation exponent. It requires an exponential moment and positive linear expected distance. It does not address critical FPP when the time constant vanishes. The paper's results for general polynomial-growth graphs should not be conflated with its specific norm-rate equivalence for invariant metrics on \(\mathbb Z^d\).
