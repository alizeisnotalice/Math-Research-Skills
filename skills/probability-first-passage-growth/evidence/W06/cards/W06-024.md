## Model and theorem

This is **site/vertex-weight FPP on the triangular lattice** \(\mathbb T\), embedded with vertex set \(\mathbb Z^2\) and the six triangular-lattice neighbors. The vertex weights \(t_v\) are i.i.d. with distribution function \(F\), \(F(0)=p_c=1/2\). Write
\[
I=\inf\{x>0:F(x)>1/2\},\qquad B(n)=\{x:\|x\|_\infty\le n\},\quad\partial B(n)=\{x:\|x\|_\infty=n\}.
\]
Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3) proves
\[
\frac{T(0,\partial B(n))}{\log n}\longrightarrow \frac{I}{2\sqrt3\,\pi}\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
For six i.i.d. copies \(t_1,\ldots,t_6\) of a vertex weight, if \(\mathbb E[\min(t_1,\ldots,t_6)^2]<\infty\), then
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(T(0,\partial B(n)))}{\log n}\longrightarrow I^2\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt3\,\pi}-\frac{1}{2\pi^2}\right).
\]
Remark 1 (PDF p. 3) gives the mean limit \(\mathbb ET(0,\partial B(n))/\log n\to I/(2\sqrt3\pi)\) under the weaker first-moment condition \(\mathbb E\min(t_1,\ldots,t_6)<\infty\). These moment conditions are stated to be optimal in the sense that the corresponding moments of \(T\) are finite exactly when those of the minimum of six weights are finite.

## Proof dependencies

For the law of large numbers (PDF pp. 4–5, 12–14), couple \(t_v\) above the Bernoulli critical weights \(t_v^B=I\mathbf 1_{\{t_v>0\}}\). This gives the lower bound from the Bernoulli theorem of Yao. To obtain the matching upper bound, use the almost surely nested outermost closed circuits (Lemma 2.1, pp. 7–8) and their RSW-based size/separation controls (Lemma 2.2). A “good circuit” construction (Lemma 2.4, pp. 8–9) supplies many separated attachment points; conditional Chernoff bounds then build an infinite path crossing successive circuits with all but finitely many positive-weight vertices at weight at most \(I+a_j\), where \(a_j\downarrow0\) (Lemma 3.2, pp. 9–12). Comparing this path to the Bernoulli geodesic makes the excess passage cost at most \(\epsilon\,O(\log n)+o(\log n)\); send \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).

For the variance limit (PDF pp. 5–6, 14–28), decompose the centered passage time into martingale differences indexed by nested annuli with innermost open circuits. Compare each increment to the Bernoulli increment. Good-circuit paths and planar multi-arm probability estimates show that the squared increment discrepancy tends to zero in Cesàro average; the annular passage-time moment bounds allow the passage from circuit endpoints to \(\partial B(n)\). Thus the variance differs from the Bernoulli variance by \(o(\log n)\), and the exact constant is imported from Yao's Bernoulli result. The critical-percolation inputs are specific: triangular-lattice RSW, BK, and exact arm exponents/CLE\(_6\) results (used around Lemma 5.3, PDF pp. 22–25).

## Transfer boundary

The authors explain that their method extends to a broad class of two-dimensional lattices **conditional on existence** of the Bernoulli limits, but does not prove those limits or their exact values there (Remark 1.2, p. 3). The result concerns a two-dimensional triangular lattice with vertex weights and critical zero mass. It does not imply matching logarithmic estimates for 3D Bernoulli edge-FPP at \(p_c(3)\) in C091; dimension, lattice, weight placement, and percolation technology all differ.
