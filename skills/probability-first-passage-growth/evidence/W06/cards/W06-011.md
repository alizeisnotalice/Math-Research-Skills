## Model and main theorem

This is i.i.d. undirected edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^2\). After scaling the minimum weight to 1, let \(\mathcal M_p\) be the class with support in \([1,\infty)\) and \(\mu\{1\}=p\), where \(p\in[\vec p_c,1)\) and \(\vec p_c\) is the oriented bond-percolation threshold. For \(p>\vec p_c\), oriented edges of weight 1 generate the flat edge of the limit shape; at \(p=\vec p_c\) the endpoint is \(N_{\vec p_c}=(1/2,1/2)\).

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 4)** states that for every \(\mu\in\mathcal M_p\), \(p\in[\vec p_c,1)\), the limit-shape boundary is differentiable at the flat-edge endpoint \(N_p\). By symmetry the reflected endpoint is also differentiable. Corollary 2.1 (p. 4) deduces infinitely many extreme points accumulating at \(N_p\), hence the limit shape is not a polygon.

Theorem 2.2 (PDF p. 6) states that every \(\mu\in\mathcal M_p\), \(p\in[\vec p_c,1)\), admits coexistence of **infinitely many species** in the associated edge-FPP competition model. In the paper's definition, coexistence means positive probability that all species have infinite occupied sets for some finite or countably infinite choice of distinct starting sites; the theorem is not merely a two-species assertion. Theorem 2.3 (PDF p. 6) separately says that if \(\mu\) is not purely atomic, the infection graph has infinitely many ends almost surely. These are FPP infection-time results; a Markov “uniform choice among frontier sites” interpretation is specific to exponential **vertex** clocks and is not inferred here for general \(\mu\).

## Fluctuation consequences and assumptions

Let \(w_\theta=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), and \(\theta_p\) be the percolation-cone boundary angle.

- **Theorem 2.5 (PDF p. 7; proof §4, pp. 20–27):** if \(\mu\in\mathcal M_p\), \(\theta\in[0,\theta_p)\), \(\mathbb E\tau_e^2<\infty\), and for some \(\kappa<1\), \(\mathbb E\tau(0,nw_\theta)-ng(w_\theta)<n^\kappa\) for all large \(n\), then \(\operatorname{Var}\tau(0,nw_\theta)\ge C_\theta\log n\). Corollaries 2.2 and 2.3 are both stated on p. 7: the first supplies the non-random-fluctuation premise for all such \(\theta\) under an exponential moment, while the second gives the horizontal direction \(\theta=0\) under only a second moment.
- **Theorem 2.6 (PDF p. 8; proof §5, pp. 27–31):** under \(\mu\in\mathcal M_p\) and a finite second moment, the exponent definitions (2.3)–(2.4) satisfy \(\chi_\theta\ge(1-\xi_\theta)/2\), for \(\theta\in[0,\theta_p)\). This is a relation, not a determination of either exponent.
- **Theorem 2.7 (PDF p. 8; brief proof/attribution p. 9):** with \(\mathbb E e^{\beta\tau_e}<\infty\) for some \(\beta>0\), if \(\theta\in[0,\theta_p)\) is a direction of curvature (a containing Euclidean ball touches the shape on the ray in that direction), then \(\operatorname{Var}\tau(0,nw_\theta)\ge C_\theta n^{1/4}\). The source says this follows as in Newman–Piza using Kesten/Alexander shape-fluctuation bounds; it is not a general polynomial lower bound for every direction.

## Proof mechanisms actually used

For differentiability, suppose there were a second tangent line at \(N_p\). A linear functional \(f\) then has a positive transverse slope. Embed oriented percolation in the edges at the minimum weight. In the supercritical case, a positive-density sequence of finite-energy “good” configurations creates bypass paths with passage time equal to the oriented path length but a strictly larger \(f\)-coordinate. The expected \(f\)-advance per layer then contradicts the almost-sure time-constant/shape limit. At \(p=\vec p_c\), independently sprinkle edges with weights in \((1,K]\) to make an auxiliary oriented-percolation parameter \(\tilde p>\vec p_c\); run the bypass argument and send \(\tilde p\downarrow\vec p_c\). The positive bypass probability and the inequality \(a\ge 3/C_f\) keep the contradiction strict. The paper also sketches the analogous site and directed variants in Remark 8; those are separate models.

For Theorem 2.5, proved in §4 (PDF pp. 20–27), Newman–Piza martingale influences give a variance lower bound from edges on geodesics whose weights can be changed. Since a geodesic may otherwise use only weight-1 edges, Lemma 9 forces linearly many edges with weight at least \(y>1\) outside the cone. Proposition 2 (in §4, including the support-gap argument on physical p. 23) localizes them to disjoint annuli, using a strict support-line gap for a complementary shape arc and the non-random fluctuation estimate. Summing the squared influence probabilities over logarithmically many annuli gives the \(\log n\) bound. Theorem 2.6 is proved in §5 (PDF pp. 27–31) by applying the influence inequality to geodesic confinement and shape geometry; Theorem 2.7 is stated on p. 8 and its one-paragraph proof on p. 9 imports the cited shape estimate under curvature.

## Transfer boundary

All shape and bypass arguments here are planar and use the oriented-percolation atom at the minimum. The variance lower bounds are not fluctuation-exponent values. The growth-model results do not collapse vertex-clock Eden and edge-clock exponential FPP into one transition rule. The paper supplies no directed-LPP Busemann theorem and no result for dimensions \(d\ge3\).
