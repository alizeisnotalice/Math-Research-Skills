## Model and unconditional theorem

This is **undirected bond FPP** on (ℤ^d), with i.i.d. nonnegative, nondegenerate edge weights (F). If (λ) is the smallest point of the support, “admissible” means (F(λ)<p_c(d)). For (a_n(h)), the passage time from (0) to (ne_1) restricted to (ℤ\times[-h,h]^{d-1}), and (h_n) integer, **Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 3–4)** assumes (E[ω^p]<\infty) for some (p>2) and admissible (F), and gives
\[
h_n=o(n^\alpha),\qquad
\alpha<\frac{1}{d+1+2(d-1)/(p-2)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{a_n(h_n)-Ea_n(h_n)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(a_n(h_n))}}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]
For all finite moments this yields every strict (α<1/(d+1)). The same CLT holds for the paper’s point-to-point cylinder time (t_n(h_n)) and side-to-side crossing time (T_n(h_n)). When (h_n=O(1)), the zero-mass condition (F(λ)<p_c(d)) can be dropped.

**Proposition 1.3 (PDF p. 4)** adds, when (h_n\to\infty), (Ea_n(h_n)/n\to\nu(e_1)) and (c_1n/h_n^{d-1}\le\operatorname{Var}(a_n(h_n))\le c_2n), with positive constants depending on (d,F). For fixed (h), the mean and variance per unit length have positive limits; **Proposition 1.4** gives a Brownian functional limit for fixed cylinder width when (E[ω^p]<\infty), (p>2).

The generalization, **Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 7)**, uses a nondecreasing sequence of finite connected graphs (G_n\subset\mathbb Z^{d-1}), with diameter (d_n), edge count (k_n=O(d_n^\theta)), (θ\ge1), and (E[ω^p]<\infty). For fixed (G_n=G), admissibility is unnecessary. For growing (G_n), admissibility and
\[
d_n=o(n^\alpha),\qquad \alpha<\frac1{2+\theta+2\theta/(p-2)}
\]
give the normalized CLT for (a_n(G_n)), also (t_n(G_n)), (T_n(G_n)). Substituting (θ=d-1) recovers Theorem 1.1’s exponent.

## Conditional wider-cylinder result

Theorem 8.10 and Corollary 8.11 (PDF p. 32) are **conditional**. They assume the paper’s fluctuation and transversal exponents (χ,ξ) with uniform exponential-moment controls (Assumptions 8.1–8.2), the quadratic shape regularity at (e_1) in Assumption 8.3, and monotonicity in width of the fixed-length cylinder variance for all sufficiently large lengths (Assumption 8.8). Under these assumptions, the CLT holds for (h_n=o(n^\alpha)) with (α<ξ) if (χ>0), and (α<1/(d-1)) if (χ=0); in (d=2,3) the stated corollary gives all (α<ξ). These assumptions are not proved in the paper. The largest unconditional width exponent remains open.

## Proof mechanism and constants

For the unconditional result, the authors first pass from endpoint-to-endpoint to side-to-side crossing times; the mismatch is dominated by sums of at most diameter-many vertical connector weights. A martingale/Efron–Stein argument bounds central moments through the number of pivotal edges on a minimizing path. Kesten’s exponential estimate for unusually long cheap paths controls that path length. A lower variance bound of order (n/k_n) is paired with an upper moment estimate. The crossing time is split into independent blocks; because the one-level Lyapunov estimate loses powers under these nonmatching bounds, the proof recursively reblocks at scales (n^{\beta_i}). Rosenthal’s inequality controls each independent block sum, while the connector error is dominated by sums of (m d_n) edge weights. Solving the exponent constraints and sending the recursion depth to infinity yields (1/(2+\theta+2\theta/(p-2))).

For the conditional threshold result, alternating long and short blocks separate longitudinal time fluctuations from boundary-height mismatch. Shape regularity and transversal tails bound the mismatch; assumed variance monotonicity supplies a lower bound at wider widths; recursive moment bounds and Lyapunov finish the CLT. Section 10 reports simulations (Bernoulli edges, 1,000 observations per configuration) for Conjecture 1.2/10.1; they are numerical support only, not proof of the threshold.

## Limits of use

The rigorous small-width theorem is for a specified undirected edge model and finite (p>2); it does not identify the maximal CLT width. Its exponent is a sufficient width range, not a sharpness claim. The wider (ξ)-threshold is conditional on unproved exponent/shape/variance assumptions. Directed vertex-LPP results and KPZ scaling heuristics do not remove those conditions.
