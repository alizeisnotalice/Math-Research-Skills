## Model and main results

This is nearest-neighbor **edge** FPP on \(\mathbb Z^2\), with independent edge weights that use law \(F_-\) on the left side of the vertical interface and law \(F_+\) on the right. The environment is not translation-invariant across that interface. Let \(Y_\pm\) be the minimum of the four incident weights at a vertex in the corresponding homogeneous half-plane, and let \(\mu_\pm\) be the homogeneous time-constant norms.

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 2–3).** If \(\mathbb E Y_-\) and \(\mathbb E Y_+\) are finite, then for every fixed \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), \(T(0,nx)/n\to\bar\mu(x)\) almost surely and in \(L^1\), for a deterministic function \(\bar\mu\).
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 3–4).** If \(\mathbb E Y_-^2,\mathbb E Y_+^2<\infty\), convergence is uniform radially: \(\sup_{\|z\|_1\ge n}|T(0,z)-\bar\mu(z)|/\|z\|_1\to0\) a.s. If also \(\max(F_-(0),F_+(0))<p_c(2)\), the associated asymptotic shape is a compact convex set with nonempty interior and the shape theorem follows.
- **Theorem 1.4 / Propositions 4.2–4.3 (PDF pp. 4–5, 12–16).** The inhomogeneous time constant is subadditive and homogeneous. For directions in either half-plane it is the infimum over interface contact points of the sum of the interface cost and the relevant homogeneous half-plane norm. The shape is obtained by convexifying the two half-plane shapes and allowing the vertical-axis segment determined by \(\bar\mu(e_2)\). This formula permits a vertical “pyramid” defect; it does not say the defect is always strict.
- **Theorem 1.5 (PDF pp. 4–5, 18–20).** \(\bar\mu(e_2)\le\min\{\mu_-(e_2),\mu_+(e_2)\}\). Equality follows under the stated concave-order comparison (“more variable” means lower expectation for every concave nondecreasing test function), not merely from a variance comparison.
- **Theorem 1.6 (PDF pp. 5, 19–20).** Gives nondegenerate pairs for which the inequality is strict, so the interface creates a pyramid. The construction first compares a deterministic unit environment with a nondegenerate law of the same homogeneous speed, uses repeated low-cost detours with positive frequency, and then perturbs the deterministic law to a nondegenerate one.

## Proof dependencies and constants

Section 2 (PDF pp. 6–8) imports homogeneous FPP estimates: the time-constant seminorm and its positive-norm criterion, a quantile/uniform coupling, and half-plane upper and lower deviation controls. Section 3 (pp. 8–12) splits a passage path at its last interface contact, producing an infimum formula; a Kingman limit along the interface supplies \(\bar\mu(e_2)\). Upper bounds use a fixed contact point and half-plane concentration plus Borel–Cantelli; lower bounds union-bound over possible contacts using the imported lower-tail estimate. Section 4 (pp. 12–16) proves homogeneity, subadditivity, Lipschitz control and the convex-shape formula. Section 5 (pp. 16–18) upgrades radial convergence to a shape theorem using a finite directional net and connector-tail bounds. Section 6 (pp. 18–20) couples environments by quantiles and replaces edge weights one at a time: passage time as a function of one edge is a minimum of increasing affine functions, hence concave and nondecreasing, which permits the concave-order comparison. Section 7 (pp. 20–21) treats random columnar defects by a positive-density favorable-block argument and subadditivity.

Appendix A sketches the homogeneous half-plane deviation input using increasing-width cylinders, renewal cross-sections and a direction net; its cited lower-tail ingredient remains imported. Constants depend on the laws and dimension; the paper does not give a universal numerical constant for the shape or interface comparison.

## Transfer boundary

This is a useful inhomogeneous shape/interface comparator for C081/C084, but it is not a closed-form time-constant example for a single i.i.d. law, nor does it prove strict convexity or rule out every flat segment for the archive's law class. The different laws on the two sides and the special vertical interface are essential.
