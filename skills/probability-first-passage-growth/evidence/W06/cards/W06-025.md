## Model and theorem

This is undirected nearest-neighbor edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), with i.i.d. nonnegative weights \(t_e\). Let \(T(0,x)\) be the point-to-point passage time and \(F\) the edge-weight law. Theorem 1.1 (PDF physical p. 3) gives two distinct conclusions:

1. If \(F(0)<p_c(d)\) and \(\mathbb E e^{2\alpha t_e}<\infty\) for some \(\alpha>0\), then constants \(c_1,c_2>0\), depending on the model, satisfy for all \(\|x\|_1>1,\lambda\ge0\),
\[
\mathbb P\{|T(0,x)-\mathbb ET(0,x)|\ge
\lambda\sqrt{\frac{\|x\|_1}{\log\|x\|_1}}\}\le c_1e^{-c_2\lambda}.
\]
2. If \(F(0)<p_c(d)\) and \(\mathbb E[t_e^2(\log t_e)_+]<\infty\), then the same scale and exponential-in-\(\lambda\) bound hold for the **lower tail only**:
\[
\mathbb P\{T(0,x)-\mathbb ET(0,x)\le
-\lambda\sqrt{\frac{\|x\|_1}{\log\|x\|_1}}\}\le c_1e^{-c_2\lambda}.
\]

The theorem is a concentration inequality around the mean; it does not identify a polynomial variance exponent or a KPZ exponent. The stronger exponential-moment hypothesis is needed for the two-sided claim, while the lower-tail result uses the stated \(2+\log\) moment condition.

## Proof dependencies

Section 1 specifies the model, assumptions, main result, and proof outline. The authors reduce concentration of \(T(0,x)\) to variance/entropy estimates for \(e^{\lambda F_m}\), where \(F_m\) averages passage times over a block of starting points. Section 2 proves the required comparison between the averaged and point-to-point variables and recalls the Falik–Samorodnitsky entropy inequality. Section 3 linearizes the passage time under edge resampling and bounds the influence terms using geodesic structure and Kesten-type control of long low-cost paths.

Section 4 represents each edge weight as a quantile transform of an infinite sequence of Bernoulli variables. Bonami–Gross two-point log-Sobolev controls the discrete derivatives. Positive and negative \(\lambda\) require separate derivative estimates because \(e^{\lambda F_m}\) changes monotonicity; §4.2 and §4.3 keep the corresponding exponential-moment versus \(2+\log\)-moment assumptions separate. Section 5 turns geodesic-edge sums into greedy lattice-animal estimates using \(w_e=1-\log F(t_e)\), whose tail has an exponential moment under the quantile construction. The resulting entropy bounds, followed by the exponential-concentration lemma, prove Theorem 1.1 in §6. Appendix A fills in the technical comparison/derivative arguments and A.3 records an entropy derivation of the classical Gaussian concentration bound under exponential moments.

## Transfer boundary

The estimate applies to i.i.d. undirected edge-FPP with subcritical zero mass and the displayed moment assumptions. Its threshold is \(\lambda\sqrt{n/\log n}\), a subdiffusive concentration scale; it is not a proof that this is the typical fluctuation scale. It cannot be used to infer \(\chi=1/3\), a variance asymptotic, or a result at critical zero mass.
