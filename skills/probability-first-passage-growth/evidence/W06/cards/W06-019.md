## Model and exponent definitions

This is undirected nearest-neighbor edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), with i.i.d. nonnegative weights. The paper assumes a **nearly-gamma** law in the sense of Benaïm–Rossignol and a finite moment generating function near zero for Theorem 1.1. Nearly-gamma means a continuous density \(h\) on its support interval and the stated normal-quantile derivative bound; the paper lists exponential, gamma, beta and bounded-uniform laws as examples.

The theorem distinguishes upper and lower exponent indices. \(\chi_a,\xi_a\) are the least exponents such that for every larger exponent there is an exponential-moment bound, uniformly over nonzero endpoints, for \(|T(0,v)-\mathbb ET(0,v)|/|v|^{\chi'}\) and geodesic deviation \(D(0,v)/|v|^{\xi'}\). \(\chi_b,\xi_b\) are the greatest exponents for which, for every smaller exponent, variance and mean deviation have uniform polynomial lower bounds at sufficiently large distance. These are definitions/indices, not assumed limits of logarithmic ratios.

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 3–4)** proves
\[
0\le\chi_b\le\chi_a\le\tfrac12,\qquad 0\le\xi_b\le\xi_a\le1,
\qquad \chi_a\ge 2\xi_b-1.
\]
If the upper and lower indices coincide, \(\chi_a=\chi_b=\chi\) and \(\xi_a=\xi_b=\xi\), then \(\chi=2\xi-1\). It does not establish that these equalities of indices hold, nor does it give the numerical values \(\chi=1/3,\xi=2/3\).

## Proof dependencies

Section 2 (PDF pp. 4–8) is the proof sketch: it previews the exponent cases, the norm approximation, curvature geometry, and the block argument. Section 3 (pp. 8–10) proves the a priori bounds by nondegeneracy, path geometry, and the nearly-gamma concentration estimate. Section 4 (pp. 10–15) applies Alexander's subadditive approximation, with an exponential concentration bound, to control \(h(x)=\mathbb ET(0,x)\) by its time-constant norm \(g(x)\). Section 5 (pp. 15–17) obtains two curvature directions for an arbitrary norm using inscribed/circumscribed Euclidean balls; this is geometric and does not assert strict convexity of the random limit shape.

Section 6 (pp. 17–19) combines strict curvature in one direction with the norm approximation and exponential deviations to exclude geodesics wandering beyond \(n^{\xi'}\), proving \(\chi_a\ge2\xi_b-1\). For the reverse inequality under coincident indices, §7 (pp. 19–25) partitions a cylinder into alternating long/short blocks; bounded-curvature errors and concentration make the passage time close to a sum over separated blocks. Harris–FKG nonnegative covariance then forces a variance contradiction. Section 8 (pp. 26–28) handles \(\chi=1/2\) via an iterative variance bootstrap combined with the BKS \(n/\log n\) variance upper bound. Section 9 (pp. 28–31) handles \(\chi=0\) with a local edge-resampling/weight-perturbation construction that makes a large deviation of the geodesic likely, contradicting the assumed wandering upper tail. Constants are distribution- and dimension-dependent; no universal numerical constants are claimed.

## Transfer boundary

This is a conditional exponent relation for ordinary edge-FPP under the paper's exponent definitions and law assumptions. It is not a proof of KPZ exponents, exponent existence, or equality of upper/lower exponent indices. For C087/C090/C094, use it only after checking each definition and conditional hypothesis.
