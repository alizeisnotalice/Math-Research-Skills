## Framework and main results

The model is a directed random path in an ergodic environment: finite admissible step set \(R\), strictly positive walk kernel \(p(z)\), stationary shifts \(T_x\), and a potential \(V\) satisfying the paper's class-L uniform ergodic/integrability condition. The results cover positive-temperature directed polymers and zero-temperature directed LPP, with general step sets and potential dependence. They are **not** undirected edge-FPP results.

- **Theorem 2.4 (PDF pp. 8–9).** Point-to-level and point-to-point limits exist under ergodicity and \(V\in L\), and the point-to-level limit is the supremum over velocities of point-to-point free energy plus tilt.
- **Theorem 3.2 (PDF pp. 10–11).** For point-to-level zero-temperature LPP, \(g^\infty_{pl}(h)=\inf_{F\in K_0}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_\omega\max_{z\in R}\{V_0(\omega,z)+h\cdot z+F(\omega,0,z)\}\); a minimizing centered stationary \(L^1\) cocycle exists. The positive-temperature formula replaces max by log-sum-exp.
- **Theorem 3.4 (PDF pp. 11–13).** A stationary \(L^1\) cocycle adapted to the base potential gives a minimizer/corrector when the tilt difference is orthogonal to the affine hull of the step set; the essential supremum becomes an almost-sure identity. Uniform sublinearity of centered cocycles (Appendix A) closes the passage from one-step identity to asymptotic growth.
- **Theorem 4.4 (PDF p. 17).** Under \(V_0\in\mathcal L\), ergodicity of \(\mathbb P\), and the separate finiteness premise \(g_{pl}^{\beta}(0)<\infty\), tilt–velocity convex duality yields the point-to-point cocycle variational formulas for \(\xi\in\operatorname{ri}U\), for \(0<\beta\le\infty\). The infimum can be restricted to cocycles whose mean tilt is dual to \(\xi\), and a minimizing cocycle exists. This explicit finiteness premise must not be dropped or inferred from class \(\mathcal L\) alone.
- **Theorems 5.1–5.2 (PDF pp. 19–23).** If paths between fixed endpoints have equal length, Busemann limits exist, and the stated expectation-convergence condition holds, point-to-point or point-to-level Busemann functions extend to stationary integrable cocycles adapted to the potential and minimize the corresponding variational formula. The expectation condition is separate; existence of a pointwise limit alone is not the full integrability/duality conclusion.
- **Theorems 7.2–7.3 (PDF pp. 26–27).** Zero-temperature alternatives optimize expected potential over invariant environment/path measures absolutely continuous in the environment marginal; the point-to-point formula is stated under the stronger directed i.i.d. \(L^{d+\epsilon}\) assumptions.

## Proof dependencies and examples

Section 2 (pp. 5–9) builds point-to-level/point-to-point free energies and takes the zero-temperature limit. Section 3 (pp. 9–15) proves the cocycle infimum by importing the positive-temperature variational formula, then uses a cocycle adaptedness identity and a uniform ergodic theorem for the corrector. Section 4 (pp. 15–18) uses convex duality to pass from tilt to velocity. On p. 17, the proof uses \(\xi\in\operatorname{ri}U\) to bound relevant tilts, restrict the infimum to a compact set, and use continuity of \(g_{pl}^{\beta}\) to obtain a dual tilt. Section 5 (pp. 18–23) establishes cocycle stationarity/additivity by telescoping Busemann gradients; integrability follows from uniform integrability and the L1 shape bound, while the additional expectation limit proves duality. Section 6 (pp. 23–25) illustrates with log-gamma polymer and exponential corner growth. Section 7 (pp. 25–32) gives invariant-measure/entropy formulas and passes to zero temperature; the point-to-point formula's scope is narrower because it uses a full quenched LDP. Section 8 (pp. 32–38) reduces finite periodic environments to Perron–Frobenius/max-plus eigenvalue theory. Appendix A (pp. 38–38) states the uniform ergodic theorem for centered cocycles and a uniform-integrability lemma. References occupy pp. 39–41.

## Transfer boundary

This is a broad proof-method source for directed LPP/polymer variational formulas and cocycle solutions (C081/C093). For Theorem 4.4, retain all four inputs: \(V_0\in\mathcal L\), ergodicity, \(g_{pl}^{\beta}(0)<\infty\), and \(\xi\in\operatorname{ri}U\); class-\(\mathcal L\) membership does not license omitting the stated finiteness premise. Also keep finite-step, relative-interior, and moment/integrability conditions visible. Its framework can guide a proof but does not directly compute a nondegenerate undirected FPP time constant or establish undirected-FPP Busemann existence.
