## Model and main theorem

For \(d\ge2\), capacities \((t(e))\) are i.i.d. nonnegative edge variables with common law \(\Lambda\) on the rescaled nearest-neighbor graph \(\mathbb Z^d/n\). The domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\) is bounded, open, connected and Lipschitz; its boundary \(\Gamma\) is covered by finitely many oriented \(C^1\) hypersurface patches meeting transversely. Two disjoint relatively open boundary pieces \(\Gamma^1,\Gamma^2\) have relative boundaries of \(\mathcal H^{d-1}\)-measure zero and positive separation. The discrete network \(\Omega_n\) approximates \(\Omega\), and \(\phi_n\) is the maximum flow from \(\Gamma^1_n\) to \(\Gamma^2_n\), with conservation at interior vertices and capacities \(0\le g(e)\le t(e)\).

Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3) assumes an exponential moment \(\int e^{\theta x}\,d\Lambda(x)<\infty\) for some \(\theta>0\). There is a deterministic finite constant \(\phi_\Omega\ge0\) such that
\[
\frac{\phi_n}{n^{d-1}}\longrightarrow\phi_\Omega\qquad\text{almost surely},
\]
and \(\phi_\Omega>0\) iff \(\Lambda(0)<1-p_c(d)\), where \(p_c(d)\) is the bond-percolation threshold. The normalization is surface order. The limit is the continuous anisotropic min-cut value: the surface tension \(\nu(v)\) is the directional cylinder-flow cost per unit \((d-1)\)-area, and \(\phi_\Omega\) is the infimum of the corresponding \(\nu\)-weighted perimeter over separating cuts (definitions and variational formula in PDF pp. 5–8). It is not generally an isotropic Euclidean area constant.

## Proof architecture and source attribution

The paper's §3 is titled **State of the art**. Section 3.1, “Existing laws of large numbers” (PDF pp. 9–11), reviews earlier straight-cylinder and tilted-cylinder max-flow limits. Section 3.2, “Large deviations for \(\phi_n\)” (beginning on physical p. 12; physical p. 14 is still in this subsection), presents two existing estimates: Theorem 9 attributes the surface-order lower-tail bound to companion paper [7], and Theorem 10 attributes the volume-order upper-tail bound to companion paper [8]. It explains how these results give the needed almost-sure bracketing by Borel–Cantelli. These deviation theorems are cited inputs/overview here, not newly proved exponential-deviation results of this LLN paper.

Section 4 is **Polyhedral approximation** and begins on physical p. 18. Theorem 11 states that for each finite-perimeter set \(F\subset\Omega\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a polyhedral set \(P\) whose boundary is transverse to \(\Gamma\), respects the source/sink boundary inclusions, satisfies \(\mathcal L^d(F\triangle(P\cap\Omega))<\varepsilon\), and has \(I_\Omega(P)\le I_\Omega(F)+\varepsilon\). The proof spans §4 (PDF pp. 18–34) and uses finite covers of reduced-boundary patches, the Besicovitch differentiation and Vitali covering theorems, local flat-disc/cylinder comparisons, and boundary-collar modifications to push interfaces inside \(\Omega\). Its role is to prove equality of the unrestricted continuum cut value \(\phi_\Omega\) and the polyhedral value \(\widetilde\phi_\Omega\). Together with the existing large-deviation inputs summarized in §3.2, this identifies the normalized discrete max flow's limit. Do not attribute the companion large-deviation proofs or a new discrete-cut LDP to §4.

The positivity criterion in Theorem 1 follows from the corresponding positivity of directional surface tension exactly below the zero-capacity percolation threshold. The source is an undirected capacity network in a regular continuum domain.

## Transfer boundary for A030

A030 concerns a directed Bernoulli edge-flow on a specific oriented lattice with an additional \(p\uparrow1\) limit exchange. This source has an undirected network, a continuum domain, i.i.d. nonnegative capacities, and a fixed-law limit \(n\to\infty\) under exponential moments. Neither its surface-order min-cut formula nor its positivity threshold supplies the directed Poisson/Hammersley approximation or the square-root coefficient required by A030. Use as a max-flow/min-cut and surface-tension comparator only.
