## Model and theorem

This is **directed vertex LPP on \(\mathbb Z^2\)**: nonnegative i.i.d. vertex weights \(\omega_x\) with general common law \(F\); paths are up/right and passage values are maximized. No exponential weight law is assumed. Let \(g_F\) be the 1-homogeneous concave time constant. The law has mean \(m\), variance \(\sigma^2>0\), and moment-generating function \(M(t)=\mathbb E e^{t\omega_0}\) finite on a neighborhood of zero, as in (8). Its Cramér rate function is \(I(a)=\sup_t\{at-\log M(t)\}\). The exponential/geometric shape formula is a comparison benchmark, not the model assumption.

Theorem 2.2 (PDF p. 6) assumes a family of **pre-Busemann functions** satisfying stationarity, additivity, past independence, recovery, and expectation duality (Definition 2.1, PDF pp. 4–6). If
\[
\frac{s\log 4}{1+s}<I\!\left(\frac{g_{\rm Exp}(1,s)}{1+s}\right)\quad\text{for every }s\in(0,1),
\qquad g_{\rm Exp}(1,s)=m(1+s)+2\sigma\sqrt{s},
\]
then for every member \(B^u\) of that family,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(B^u(e_2,0),B^u(0,e_1))\le 0.
\]
Lemma 2.3 (PDF pp. 6–10) gives the underlying equivalence: domination \(g_F(x)\le g_{\rm Exp}(x)\) for all \(x\in\mathbb R_+^2\) is equivalent to nonpositive covariance for all adjacent increments of the pre-Busemann family; reversing both inequalities gives the analogous positive-correlation criterion.

Proposition 2.6 (statement on PDF p. 7; proof in §3.4, pp. 16–18) verifies the rate-function condition for Bernoulli \(F=\mathrm{Ber}(p)\) when \(p_*<p<1\), with \(p_*\approx0.6504\). The threshold is defined through \(s_*(p)\), where \(p+2\sqrt{p(1-p)s}/(1+s)=1\), and the unique \(p_*\) solving \(\log4=(1+p)/(1+s_*(p))\). The paper proves sufficiency, not necessity: plots/simulations suggest broader validity, but are not promoted to a theorem.

## Proof dependencies and constants

For the covariance identity, write \(\gamma(s)=g_F(1,s)\), take a Legendre transform \(f(a)=\sup_{s>0}(\gamma(s)-sa)\), and use \(\gamma(s)=\inf_{a>m}(sa+f(a))\). The recovery property expresses adjacent increments through \(\omega_0\) and positive/negative parts of a neighboring increment. Past independence then yields the exact identity
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(B^u(0,e_1),B^u(e_2,0))=-\sigma^2+(f(a)-m)(a-m).
\]
Thus the covariance sign is equivalent to comparing \(f(a)\) with \(m+\sigma^2/(a-m)\); Legendre duality converts this to the global time-constant comparison.

For the rate-function estimate, Theorem 2.4 (statement on PDF p. 6; proof in §3.2, pp. 10–15) shows that if \(s>0\), \(K>m\), and \(s\log4/(1+s)<I(K/(1+s))\), then \(g_F(1,s)\le K\). It truncates weights using the local mgf, replaces all up/right paths by a coarse-grained class, and counts this class at exponential rate \((s+r)\log4/((1+s)(1+r))\). A Cramér upper-tail bound for a fixed candidate path supplies the competing rate \(I(K/(1+s))\); choosing rational \(r>0\) with a strict rate gap makes the union bound vanish. The covariance/time-constant equivalence is proved separately in §3.1 (PDF pp. 7–10). Bernoulli verification in §3.4 differentiates the explicit binary rate function, bounds logarithms, and solves the threshold equation above. §3.3 proves Proposition 2.5, ruling out the proposed convex-order route by Jensen equality and matching first and second moments.

Appendix A (PDF pp. 18–19) records conditions under which actual directional Busemann limits exist; the main theorem is deliberately proved for the larger pre-Busemann class and does not require a proved limit along every direction. Appendix B (PDF pp. 19–20) explains a **heuristic/conditional** KPZ implication: nonpositive covariances for all separated increments would give a linear variance bound and suggest \(2\chi\le\xi\); Theorem 2.2 only controls one adjacent pair type and does not prove those global covariance hypotheses or KPZ exponents. References occupy PDF pp. 20–22.

## Transfer boundary

The theorem is specific to directed up/right LPP with i.i.d. vertex weights on \(\mathbb Z^2\). It does not establish negative correlations, Busemann existence, or KPZ identities for undirected edge-FPP. It is a model-specific comparison source for C097/C098 only. The Bernoulli threshold is sufficient, not a characterization; \(p\le p_*\), and non-Bernoulli laws not satisfying the rate criterion, remain outside the theorem.
