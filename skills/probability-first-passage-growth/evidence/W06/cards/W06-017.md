## Model and theorem

The model is undirected nearest-neighbor edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^d/n\), \(d\ge2\), with i.i.d. nonnegative capacities \(\Lambda\) and maximum flow \(\phi_n\) between discrete approximations of two disjoint relatively open boundary sets \(\Gamma^1,\Gamma^2\) of a bounded connected domain \(\Omega\) with piecewise \(C^1\) boundary. In this paper the source and sink must be positively separated: \(d(\Gamma^1,\Gamma^2)>0\). Assume \(\int e^{\theta x}\,d\Lambda(x)<\infty\) for some \(\theta>0\).

**Theorem 1 (PDF physical p. 3)** defines a finite threshold \(\widetilde\phi_\Omega\) and proves that for every \(\lambda>\widetilde\phi_\Omega\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1{n^d}\log\mathbb P\{\phi_n\ge\lambda n^{d-1}\}<0.
\]
Thus this is a volume-order upper deviation for a surface-order flow. The constant is the infimum over **polyhedral** separators transverse to \(\Gamma\) that separate source from sink, with the anisotropic cost induced by cylinder surface tension. The companion LLN papers show its relevance as the almost-sure flow limit under added geometric assumptions; Theorem 1 alone is not that LLN and does not prove equality with an unrestricted finite-perimeter cut value.

## Proof architecture

Sections 2.1–2.3 define cylinder/max-flow objects, summarize existing cylinder laws and large-deviation inputs, and place the theorem in the companion-paper project. In particular, the cylinder-flow upper-deviation estimate is cited as an existing input (Theorem 8 in the article’s numbering); the bounded-domain proof builds on it rather than reproving the cylinder theorem.

Section 3 defines \(\widetilde\phi_\Omega\). Section 4 sketches the proof. Section 5 proves finiteness by constructing a polyhedral separator; §6 chooses a slightly enlarged domain \(\Omega'\) containing the discrete approximants; §7 constructs many pairwise disjoint cuts in \(\Omega_n\). Section 8 covers the relevant polyhedral interface by finitely many cylinders whose normals are nearly constant, and a cut in each cylinder plus connector edges yields a global source–sink cut. Section 9 bounds the cardinalities of the connector sets \(W(k)\) and \(M(k)\), keeping their total edge count small relative to the main cylinder pieces.

In §10 the authors calibrate the errors. The connector edge locations are chosen from the environment inside one layer while the connector capacities are independent of that layer; exponential moments and Cramér’s theorem bound their total cost. For each parallel cut layer, the cylinder upper-deviation input controls the sum of local cylinder flows. Because the constructed cut layers are disjoint, a deviation above the polyhedral cost across the full domain forces too many independent layer failures, yielding the \(e^{-c n^d}\) scale. The exponential-rate constant depends on the fixed gap above \(\widetilde\phi_\Omega\), \(d,\Lambda\), and domain geometry.

## Transfer boundary

This is fixed-law, undirected capacity max-flow in a regular continuum domain. It supplies neither directed edge-disjoint Bernoulli flow estimates nor a square-root constant or \(p\uparrow1\) two-limit interchange. Do not confuse its polyhedral variational threshold with the unrestricted continuum cut value established by the separate LLN/polyhedral-approximation paper W06-015.
