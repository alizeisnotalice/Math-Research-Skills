# W06-003 — Benjamini–Kalai–Schramm, *First Passage Percolation Has Sublinear Distance Variance*

**Source.** Itai Benjamini, Gil Kalai, Oded Schramm, arXiv:math/0203262v5 (25 May 2007 version; manuscript originally dated 21 Oct 2002), 11-page PDF. Local copy: `evidence/fulltexts/W06-003.pdf`. All 11 PDF pages, including the proof and reference list, were read.

## Exact theorem

For (d>1), independent edge weights taking exactly (a) or (b), each with probability (1/2), where (0<a<b<\infty), Theorem 1 (PDF p. 1) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var} T(0,v)\le C(d,a,b)\,\frac{\|v\|_1}{\log\|v\|_1},\qquad \|v\|_1\ge2.
\]
This is a uniform variance upper bound for this two-point law. It is not a polynomial improvement (O(n^{1-\epsilon})) and does not establish the conjectural (n^{2/3}) variance in (d=2).

## Proof chain and constants

1. Apply Talagrand's discrete product-space variance inequality (equation (2), PDF p. 2): 
\(\operatorname{Var}f\le C\sum_j\|\rho_j f\|_2^2/[1+\log(\|\rho_j f\|_2/\|\rho_j f\|_1)]\).
2. For the geodesic length \(|\gamma|\), the deterministic estimate (T(0,v)\le b\|v\|_1) and each edge weight at least (a) give 
\(|\gamma|\le(b/a)\|v\|_1\), hence \(\sum_eP(e\in\gamma)=E|\gamma|\le(b/a)\|v\|_1\) (PDF pp. 5–6).
3. Symmetry is insufficient to bound individual influences, so translate both endpoints by a random vector (z(x)) built from (d) independent copies of a triangular-wave function (g_m\) on (m^2) bits. This function changes by at most one under a one-bit flip and has point masses at most (c/m) (Lemma 3, pp. 5–6). Set (m=\lfloor\|v\|_1^{1/4}\rfloor); the randomized endpoint perturbation changes passage time by at most (2mdb).
4. If flipping an edge increases the perturbed passage time, that edge is pivotal on every geodesic. The random shift and the (O(m)) diameter of the affected edge neighborhood yield influence (I_e\le O(m^{1-d})=O(\|v\|_1^{-1/4})) (pp. 6–7). Summing influences gives (O(\|v\|_1)), so the Talagrand denominator is of order (log\|v\|_1); the auxiliary endpoint bits contribute (O(m^2)=O(\|v\|_1^{1/2})).
5. Talagrand then bounds variance of the perturbed distance by (O(\|v\|_1/\log\|v\|_1)). The perturbation error contributes (O(m\|v\|_1^{1/2})=O(\|v\|_1^{3/4})) in the displayed comparison as controlled in the proof, which is lower order than the target for large \(v\); finite small \(v\) are absorbed into (C(d,a,b)) (equation (9), p. 6; conclusion p. 7).

The article also proves a circumference-variance estimate on a product of a finite vertex-transitive graph and a cycle (Theorem 2, PDF pp. 3–4); its explicit bound depends on (a,b,n,|V(H)|). It illustrates symmetry-based influence counting, but is not needed for the point-to-point theorem.

## Transfer limits

The Bernoulli two-point product-space inequality and the bounded-difference influence method are specific to this distribution. Later general-distribution results (e.g. Damron–Hanson–Sosoe) use different entropy/coupling hypotheses. The bound is sublinear by a logarithm only; do not infer a fluctuation exponent.
