# W06-001 — *50 Years of First-Passage Percolation* (selected chapters)

## Read locations and claims

- **PDF pp. 8–19, printed §§2.1–2.3.** The time-constant theorem assumes (E[min(	au_1,ldots,	au_{2d})]<\infty); subadditivity and stationarity/ergodicity give (T(0,nx)/n\to\mu(x)) a.s. and in (L^1). The moment is the minimum of (2d) independent weights, not (E\tau_e<\infty). The norm is nondegenerate iff (F(0)<p_c(d)). The shape theorem additionally needs (E[\min(\tau_1^d,\ldots,\tau_{2d}^d)]<\infty) and (F(0)<p_c(d)). This does not compute \(\mu(e_1)\): the explicit time-constant question remains open (Question 1, PDF p. 11; **C081**).
- **PDF pp. 34–45, selected passages in §§3.1–3.3 (not the p.46 continuation/proof); pp. 65–72, §§3.6–3.7.** Kesten's linear variance upper bound is presented under (E\tau^2<\infty), (P(\tau=0)<p_c), and non-degenerate weights; a lower bound also holds there. Damron–Hanson–Sosoe improve the upper bound to (C\|x\|_1/\log\|x\|_1) under (P(\tau=0)<p_c) and (E[\tau^2(\log\tau)_+]<\infty). Their route combines Falik–Samorodnitsky with spatial averaging / greedy-animal control; it gives sublinear variance, not a power-law exponent. In (d=2), Newman–Piza give a logarithmic variance lower bound under the stated second-moment, non-degeneracy, and atom/percolation alternatives, with an extension outside the oriented percolation cone for the relevant class. The survey's Questions 13–17 (PDF pp. 36–38, 54–55) ask for exponent existence/values/positivity, high-dimensional behavior, and moment-definition equivalence; none follows from these logarithmic bounds (**C087–C090**).
- **PDF pp. 73–77, §4.1; pp. 90–106, §§4.4–4.5; pp. 107–127, §§5.1–5.6.** A geodesic is a finite path attaining the infimum. In (d=2), geodesics between every pair exist a.s. for arbitrary edge-weight law; in (d>2), arbitrary nonnegative finite i.i.d. weights are left open (Question 21, p. 73; **C093**). Under (F(0)<p_c), the survey gives exponential control of very long self-avoiding geodesics: (P(m(x)\ge M\|x\|_\infty)\le e^{-c\|x\|_\infty^{1/d}}), using a supercritical comparison cluster, Antal–Pisztora intrinsic-distance bounds, and Kesten's small-time path estimate. This does not cover the critical zero-cluster case.
- **PDF pp. 90–98, §§4.4–4.5.** In (d=2) with continuous weights, Licea–Newman prove that there is a deterministic full-Lebesgue-measure set (D\) such that for each \(\theta\in D\), a.s. no two distinct rays from a fixed vertex have direction \(\theta\); their planar ordering/Fubini argument does not establish the assertion for every direction (Question 27; **C097**). Their same-direction coalescence result is also for \(\theta\in D\), in dimension two and under the same continuity setting (Theorem 4.22, pp. 96–97); higher-dimensional extension is Question 29 (**C098**). Do not reverse the quantifiers: “for each deterministic \(\theta\in D\), almost surely” is not “almost surely for all \(\theta\).” Bigeodesic nonexistence is conditional on further direction/shape hypotheses (Question 31).
- **PDF pp. 107–127, §§5.1–5.6.** For a fixed infinite geodesic (R=(x_0,x_1,\ldots)), (f_R(x)=\lim_n[T(x,x_n)-T(x_0,x_n)]) exists by bounded monotonicity. Its increments are additive, bounded by passage time, and recover the geodesic's segment costs. The survey distinguishes actual directional Busemann limits from subsequential/distributional constructions. Hoffman’s argument (translation ergodicity, unique finite geodesics, a (2+\delta) moment, bounded shape) assumes a globally well-defined Busemann function, applies ergodicity and symmetry to force zero mean linear increments, then contradicts a positive time constant along an infinite ray; hence that global uniqueness event has probability zero and distinct infinite geodesics occur. Differentiability of the limit shape gives directed-ray/coalescence conclusions only with the additional hypotheses stated in the relevant theorems; existence of a Busemann limit is not automatic.
- **PDF pp. 128–130, §§6.1–6.2; also checked pp. 131–137, §§6.3–6.5.** In §6.1 the survey's Eden process chooses uniformly among the distinct uninfected boundary vertices. Richardson's identity (6.1) couples its discrete-time clusters to site FPP with i.i.d. exponential vertex weights; memorylessness gives its Markov property. This is **not** the edge-clock rule in the C100 archive: under i.i.d. exponential edge weights, every infected-to-healthy boundary edge has its own rate-one clock, so a healthy vertex with k infected neighbors is infected at total rate k and is chosen with probability proportional to k. Theorem 6.2's cited high-dimensional result and Question 33 concern the survey's vertex/uniform-frontier version, and are not by themselves a proof for archived edge-FPP C100. For that archived edge model use W06-007/008; see their separate exact scopes. Equal-speed coexistence is linked to distinct directional geodesics. For unequal exponential rates, the cited result is non-coexistence for all but at most countably many values of the varying rate; the all-unequal-rates statement is still Question 34, and monotonicity in the slower rate is Question 35 (**C101–C102**). A statement for almost every parameter is not a statement for every parameter.
- **PDF pp. 138–145, §7.1.** Max flow equals the minimum cut capacity in the finite network. The survey's classical FPP max-flow limits concern undirected capacities and surface-order cuts; the critical positivity threshold and moment/box-growth conditions matter. These theorems do not imply Aldous's directed Bernoulli percolation flow asymptotic (**A030**).
- **PDF pp. 146–148, open questions, and cited bibliography pp. 149–160.** Checked the question locations above and references for source tracing. The volume has 160 PDF pages; no claim is made that all 160 were read.

## Transfer boundaries

Separate undirected i.i.d. edge FPP, vertex exponential Eden growth, directed exponential LPP, stationary non-i.i.d. cocycles, and directed Bernoulli max flow. The shape theorem proves existence of a deterministic norm/shape under its moment and positivity assumptions; it does not determine exact constants, fluctuation exponents, smoothness everywhere, or geodesic uniqueness in exceptional directions. An integrable LPP formula cannot be substituted for an undirected FPP theorem.
