## Model and theorem scope

This is **directed vertex** last-passage percolation (corner growth) on \(\mathbb Z^2\), with up-right steps \(e_1,e_2\) and i.i.d. vertex weights. The standing assumption (2.1) is nondegenerate weights, \(\mathbb E|\omega_0|^{2+\epsilon}<\infty\), and a finite deterministic lower bound \(\omega_0\ge c\). Cocycles are constructed on an extended environment space. This is not undirected edge-FPP.

- **Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 8–9).** For every starting vertex and \(\xi\in U\), there are semi-infinite geodesics directed in the two endpoint sets \(U_\xi^\pm\); every semi-infinite geodesic is \(U_\xi\)-directed for some \(\xi\). Strict concavity makes all such geodesics directed. If the vertex-weight law is continuous and the relevant point and endpoints of the linear segment are differentiability points of the shape, the \(U_\xi\)-directed geodesic from each site is unique, all coalesce, and each site has finitely many ancestors in the directed tree.
- **Theorem 2.2 (PDF pp. 8–9).** For noncontinuous laws, leftmost/rightmost geodesics replace uniqueness; under the stated one-sided endpoint/differentiability assumptions they coalesce and finite leftmost/rightmost geodesics converge to the corresponding infinite ones when endpoint directions approach the prescribed interval.
- **Theorems 2.3–2.4 (PDF pp. 9–10).** Busemann limits exist when the limiting shape has an exposed differentiability direction or a maximal linear segment with differentiable endpoints. The resulting cocycle recovers weights; under the differentiability assumptions, all geodesics with the stated asymptotic direction are Busemann geodesics.
- **Theorems 2.6 and 2.8 (PDF pp. 11–12; proofs §5, pp. 25–29).** With continuous vertex weights and differentiability at endpoints of all shape facets, the competition interface has an a.s. limiting direction \(\xi_*\), an exposed point; its atoms are precisely the nondifferentiability directions of the shape. There is a countable family of starting points where two directional geodesics split and never meet again.

## Proof dependencies and version boundary

Section 3 (PDF pp. 14–16) constructs stationary cocycles and Busemann functions from queueing fixed points. Section 4 (pp. 16–25) selects paths by following minimal gradients, with a tie-breaker for atoms; recovery gives the geodesic property, while cocycle monotonicity and one-sided continuity order paths across directions. The coalescence theorem (Theorem 4.5, p. 20) is stated with a sketch: stationarity would force many disjoint paths, while a local weight modification creates an impossible positive-density family. Its detailed coalescence proof is the v1 Appendix A material explicitly omitted from v2; it is not in the v2 “Appendix A: Auxiliary technical results.” That v2 Appendix A is present on physical p. 30 and was read: Theorem A.1 states a uniform ergodic theorem for stationary \(L^1\) cocycles under its growth-envelope condition, and Lemma A.2 gives deterministic monotonicity inequalities for point-to-point passage-time gradients. The no-backward-geodesic/finite-ancestor argument (Theorem 4.6, pp. 21–24) is a lack-of-space tree count. Section 5 (pp. 25–30) characterizes the competition interface by the sign change of cocycle increments and sandwiches it between directional cocycle geodesics. Section 6 (p. 30) derives the explicit geometric-weight interface law from independent geometric Busemann increments.

Exact exponential LPP formula: for \(\xi=(s,1-s)\), \(\alpha(\xi)=\sqrt{s}/(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{1-s})\), Busemann edge increments are exponential with rates \(\alpha\) and \(1-\alpha\), yielding \(g_{pp}(s,1-s)=1+2\sqrt{s(1-s)}\). This is a directed integrable benchmark, not an undirected-FPP time constant.
