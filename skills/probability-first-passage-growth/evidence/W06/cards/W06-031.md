## Model and hypotheses

The base model is directed corner-growth/last-passage percolation on (ℤ^2), with up-right steps and i.i.d. nondegenerate **vertex** weights (ω_x). Assumption (2.1) requires (E|\omega_0|^p<\infty) for some (p>2) and a finite lower bound (ω_0\ge c) a.s.; the lower bound is used to invoke queueing results proved for nonnegative weights. The article is about general, not necessarily integrable, vertex laws; its exponential/geometric formulas are special cases.

## Main conclusions

- **Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 10):** for exposed differentiability directions, or directions on a maximal linear segment whose endpoints are differentiability points, there is a common full-probability event on which every sequence of endpoints in the corresponding sector has Busemann limits (B(x,y)=\lim_n(G_{x,v_n}-G_{y,v_n})). The mean increments equal the gradient of (g_{pp}). A dual-tilt point-to-line limit is also given.
- **Theorem 3.2 (p. 11):** if the point and relevant segment endpoints satisfy the stated differentiability condition, the centered Busemann cocycle minimizes the point-to-point and point-to-line variational formulas. For this cocycle the maximum over the two outgoing directions equals the variational value almost surely, so the essential supremum is removed.
- **Theorem 5.2 (pp. 19–20):** queueing fixed points construct stationary (L^1) cocycles on an extended space, with recovery (ω_x=B(x,x+e_1)\wedge B(x,x+e_2)), mean vector (-h_\pm(\xi)=\nabla g_{pp}(\xi\pm)), monotonicity in direction, and one-sided continuity. These stationary cocycles exist even where pointwise Busemann convergence is not asserted.
- **Theorem 5.3 (p. 20):** differentiability of (g_{pp}) at endpoints of its linear segments ensures the cocycles are measurable with respect to the original environment (up to completion).
- **Theorems 5.6–5.7 (pp. 22–24):** stationary cocycles minimize the point-to-line and point-to-point variational formulas; at nondifferentiable exposed directions, convex mixtures and conditional expectations are needed.
- **Theorem 6.1 / Corollary 6.2 (pp. 24–25):** without extra regularity, cocycles sandwich liminf/limsup Busemann increments. Differentiability at the relevant endpoints collapses this to a limit. The percolation-cone specialization is separately stated in Theorems 3.3–3.5, with bounded vertex weights and oriented-site-percolation density (p_1>p_c).

## Proof mechanism

Queue fixed points and tandem-queue/Burke-type stationarity produce boundary increments coupled to the original bulk weights. Cocycles extend these increments by additivity; recovery supplies the max-plus recursion for stationary LPP. Queue stationarity gives the increment means, monotonicity, and directional continuity. Convex duality identifies the variational values. For Busemann limits, compare ordinary point-to-point LPP with the stationary boundary model, use ergodic cocycle sublinearity and moment-based maximal estimates to bound liminf/limsup by one-sided cocycles, then use shape differentiability to identify them. The exactly solvable exponential/geometric laws yield explicit product increments, but those laws are not assumed for the general construction.

## Transfer limits

This is **directed vertex LPP**, not undirected edge FPP. It does not prove all-direction Busemann convergence without the stated shape regularity, nor does its stationary-cocycle construction by itself imply every geodesic coalescence claim. For planar undirected FPP use the separate Damron–Hanson hypotheses and proof, not a direct transfer from this paper.
