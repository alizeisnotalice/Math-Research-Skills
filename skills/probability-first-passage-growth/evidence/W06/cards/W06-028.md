## Model and main theorems

Undirected edge-FPP on \(\mathbb Z^2\), with i.i.d. nonnegative weights satisfying (EXP) \(\mathbb E e^{\alpha t_e}<\infty\) for some \(\alpha>0\) and (ABS) absolute continuity. Thus finite geodesics are unique a.s. Write \(B_G\) for the deterministic limit shape and \(\mathrm{Sides}(B_G)\) for its number of sides/extreme points (infinite if not polygonal).

- **Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 2–3; proof §3.1, pp. 29–34).** If \(\mathrm{Sides}(B_G)>32\), then for \(0<\epsilon\le1/17\), \(\delta\ge0\), and \(\|y\|\ge2\), all geodesics from the \(\ell^\infty\)-box of radius \(\|y\|^{1/8-\epsilon}\) near 0 to the translated box near \(y\) have symmetric difference at most \(\|y\|^{1-\delta}\), except with probability at most \(C\log^2\|y\|/\|y\|^{\epsilon-\delta/8}\). The theorem is a finite-geodesic coalescence estimate, not an infinite-ray theorem.
- **Theorem 1.2 (PDF pp. 3–4; §3.2, pp. 34–35).** If \(\mathrm{Sides}(B_G)>40\), then for vertices \(u,v,z\), with \(D_z^{u,v}=\min(\|u-z\|,\|v-z\|)\), \(\mathbb P\{z\in\gamma(u,v)\}\le C\log^2(D_z^{u,v}+2)/(D_z^{u,v})^{1/16}\). Under the weaker >32 condition the paper gives (3.12) with an arbitrarily small loss in the power and a cubic logarithm.
- **Theorem 1.3 (p. 3; §3.3, pp. 35–36).** Under >40 sides, the probability that the origin lies on some geodesic between \((-n,s)\) and \((n,s)\), for an arbitrary integer \(s\), is \(O(\log^2 n/n^{1/24})\).
- **Theorem 1.4 (p. 5; §3.4, pp. 37–40).** Under only (EXP),(ABS), the expected proportion of \(\Lambda_n\) covered by geodesics from 0 to \(\partial\Lambda_{4n}\) is at most \(C\log^2n/n^{1/8}\); no explicit limit-shape-side assumption is needed because a 45-degree rotated lattice handles the exceptional \(\ell^1\)-ball shape.
- **Theorem 1.5 (p. 5; §5, pp. 48–51).** For weights distributed as \(1+\varepsilon X\), with \(X\in[0,1]\), \(\operatorname{Var}X=\sigma^2>0\), and sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) depending on \(\sigma\), the limit shape has at least \(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\log\log(1/\varepsilon)\) sides.

## Proof mechanism and limits

The core is Proposition 1.7, “attractive geodesics” (§2, PDF pp. 14–29). The introduction/background discussion in §1 continues through physical p. 14. With high probability, any path spending enough time in a narrow tube about a reference geodesic must share an edge with it, provided the reference geodesic has bounded slope and the scale constraints (1.5) hold. A sufficient local event compares the least edge-disjoint restricted passage time with the reference subgeodesic time plus a connector cost. Talagrand concentration gives control of many reference subsegments; a Mermin–Wagner style perturbation gives a lower fluctuation/tail bound for the restricted passage time; independence is used only for a spaced subfamily of disjoint slabs, and Harris's inequality handles conditioning on the reference geodesic. The perturbation transfers a Gaussian-shift estimate through monotone maps of the absolutely continuous weight law.

Section 3 traps nearby endpoint geodesics between ordered reference paths, derives the midpoint influence estimate by spatial averaging and translation invariance, and proves the two density estimates. Section 4 turns limit-shape geometry into bounded-slope and wrong-direction estimates: a strict norm gap between directions is transferred to passage times via a shape/time-constant approximation and Talagrand concentration. Section 5 proves the many-sides result by comparing a two-route upper bound, based on Berry–Esseen, with a counting/union-bound lower bound for near-shortest paths; a strict convexity gap then rules out two directions lying on the same flat edge.

Constants depend on the weight law (and, in intermediate estimates, slope/parameter choices). The BKS exponent 1/16 is not claimed optimal; the paper says 2/3 is expected. The power-law influence theorem needs >40 sides, while the >32 hypothesis suffices only for the stated coalescence theorem/variant. The paper's higher-dimensional attractive-geodesic proposition does not yield a higher-dimensional coalescence result because planarity supplies ordering.

## Transfer boundary

This directly strengthens planar C098/C099 and provides conditional fluctuation-related control for C087. It does not establish exponent values or transfer to d≥3. For this result, “continuous i.i.d. planar edge-FPP with exponentially integrable weights plus the stated shape-side count” is part of the theorem, not optional context.
