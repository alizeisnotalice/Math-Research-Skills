# W06-002 — Hoffman, *Coexistence for Richardson type competing spatial growth models*

The full text and all 10 PDF pages, including proof and references, were read. Exact publisher record/version still pending bibliographic review.

## Theorem and hypotheses

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 4) states (P(C(0,e_1))>0) for two competing infections projected from the *same* stationary FPP environment, assuming the measure μ is “good”: (i) totally ergodic under every lattice translation; (ii) invariant under the symmetries of (\mathbb Z^d); (iii) a finite ((d+\epsilon))-moment on each edge for some ε>0; (iv) finite energy; (v) the one-edge conditional law given all other edges is a.s. absolutely continuous with support ([0,\infty)); and (vi) bounded limit shape. The finite-energy/conditional-density assumptions imply unique finite geodesics and unique passage-time comparisons. The conclusion is positive probability, not almost-sure coexistence.

Theorem 1's shape input (PDF p. 2) assumes stationary ergodicity and finite ((d+\epsilon))-moments; its displayed conclusion is a probability (>1-\epsilon) outer/inner approximation at all sufficiently large times. The proof itself needs bounded shape plus the stated ergodicity and comparison conditions; the authors explicitly say several assumptions are convenient and not necessary.

## Proof dependency chain

1. For a ray (G=(v_n)), define (B_G(x,y)=\lim_n[T(x,v_n)-T(y,v_n)]). The limit exists because each of the two normalized differences relative to (v_0) is bounded by (T(x,v_0)) and monotone (PDF p. 5).
2. Suppose a single (B(x,y)) is well-defined independently of the ray. Symmetry and additivity force (E B(0,v)=0) (Lemma 1, p. 6). Total ergodicity and Birkhoff then give (B(0,mv)/m\to0) in probability, uniformly over finite sets of directions after a covering argument (Lemmas 2–3, pp. 6–7).
3. Bounded nonempty limit shape yields constants (0<k_1<k_2<\infty) such that with probability approaching one, (k_1< T(0,x)/\|x\|_1<k_2) for all large \(\|x\|_1\) (Corollary 1, p. 6). Along an infinite geodesic, (B(0,v_n)=T(0,v_n)); this contradicts the uniform sublinear bound if (\epsilon<\min(k_1,1)/3). Thus the event that all ray Busemann functions agree has probability zero (Lemma 4, pp. 7–8).
4. On a positive-probability event choose adjacent (0,e_1), two rays and a deterministic gap (r>0) with (B_{G_0}(0,e_1)<r<B_{G_1}(0,e_1)). Raise all edges incident to (e_1) by (r). Finite energy plus the conditional density/support condition gives this local modification positive probability; the two rays remain geodesics and their Busemann signs straddle zero. Then each infection wins infinitely many vertices, yielding coexistence (pp. 8–9).

## Exact limits and caveats

- The moment is (d+\epsilon) on an individual edge, stronger than the usual minimum-of-(2d)-edges shape-theorem condition; do not silently weaken it.
- The source uses stationary, possibly dependent edge environments, not only i.i.d. weights.
- The local perturbation relies on positive conditional probability for the required interval; absolute continuity/support and finite energy are not cosmetic.
- The argument establishes coexistence for a pair of nearby sources after lattice symmetry; it does not settle unequal growth rates.
- This source supports the distinction between “multiple infinite geodesics” and “positive-probability coexistence”; the implication uses the local edge modification.
