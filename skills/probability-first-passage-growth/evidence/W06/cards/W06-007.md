# W06-007 — Couronné–Enriquez–Gerin, *Construction of a Short Path in High-Dimensional First-Passage Percolation*

**Source.** Olivier Couronné, Nathanaël Enriquez, Lucas Gerin, *Construction of a short path in high-dimensional First Passage Percolation*, arXiv:1008.5069v2 (last revised 23 Feb 2011), 7-page PDF; *Electronic Communications in Probability* 16 (2011), 22–28, DOI 10.1214/ECP.v16-1595. Official arXiv record confirms title, authors, v2 date and journal reference. Local copy: evidence/fulltexts/W06-007.pdf. All 7 PDF pages were read.

## Results

The model is standard undirected FPP on \(\mathbb Z^d\) with i.i.d. Exp(1) **edge** weights; passage time is the sum of crossed edge weights. In the associated continuous-time growth, each infected-to-healthy boundary edge has a rate-one residual exponential clock. Thus a healthy vertex adjacent to the infected cluster along \(k\) edges has total infection rate \(k\); at a jump, it is selected with probability proportional to this number of incident boundary edges. This is the edge-clock Eden/Richardson rule and matches the edge-weight law in archived C100. It differs from the survey's site-FPP Eden process, which chooses uniformly among distinct healthy boundary vertices. Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 2–4) constructs a path from the origin to the hyperplane \(H_1=\{x_1=1\}\), with \(\ell+1\) edges, by recursively selecting low order-statistic edges in \(H_0\). It proves
\[
\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{d}{\log d}\inf_{\ell,p_1,\ldots,p_\ell} E[\tau(P(\ell,p_1,\ldots,p_\ell))]\le \frac e4.
\]
The argument takes \(\ell=\lfloor\log d\rfloor-A\), \(p_i=\lfloor e^i\rfloor\), then lets \(A\to\infty\). Repeating independent slab crossings and using the law of large numbers gives \(\mu(d)\le(e/4+o(1))\log d/d\), a constructive upper bound; Dhar's sharper constant \(1/2\) is cited, not reproved.

Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 5–6) gives a lower bound on the diagonal time constant:
\[
\mu^\star(d)\ge \frac{\alpha_\star^2-1}{2\sqrt d}\approx\frac{0.3313}{\sqrt d},
\]
where \(\alpha_\star>0\) is the nonzero solution of \(\coth\alpha=\alpha\). The proof counts self-avoiding paths that first hit the diagonal, bounds a fixed path's Gamma lower tail by \((ae/k)^k\), applies a first-passage union bound, optimizes over the length ratio \(\alpha=k/(n\sqrt d)\), and uses Borel–Cantelli. Corollary 4 (PDF p. 6) states non-Euclidean shape for \(d\ge35\); the displayed numerical comparison is at \(d=35\), using Dhar's upper estimate \(0.93\log(2d)/(2d)\) and Theorem 2's lower estimate. For C100 this card relies at minimum on the explicit strict comparison at \(d=35\); it does not extrapolate that comparison to smaller dimensions.

## Transfer limits

The article does not address dimensions \(2\le d<35\), and its asymptotic path construction is a high-dimensional rate bound, not an exact time constant. The 2015 survey's Eden question refers to its site-FPP/uniform-frontier version; it must not be silently identified with the archived edge-FPP C100 model. The edge-FPP counterexample is independently lowered to dimension 22 in W06-008. The exponential-weight and high-dimensional assumptions are essential; simulation is not a substitute for the analytic diagonal lower bound.
