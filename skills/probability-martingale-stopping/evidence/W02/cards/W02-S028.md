## Measure-valued martingale formulation

The original problem (PDF pp. 1–2) fixes a continuous bounded $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$, terminal law $\mu\in\mathcal P(\mathbb R_+)$ with finite first moment, and a continuous martingale $M$ in a Brownian-generated filtration with $M_T\sim\mu$, optimizing a stopping payoff over stopping times in $[s,T]$. For each such model, the conditional law $\xi_t=\mathcal L(M_T\mid\mathcal F_t)$ is a measure-valued martingale with $\xi_0=\mu$, $\xi_T=\delta_{M_T}$, and $M_t=\int x\,\xi_t(dx)$. This changes the transport problem to an infinite-dimensional controller-stopper problem.

For a finite-support terminal law, the conditional probabilities satisfy a Brownian control representation $d\xi^n_t=w^n_t dW_t$, with $\sum_n w^n_t=0$, and a coordinate that reaches 0 or 1 must have zero diffusion coefficient so the probability vector remains in its simplex. A strictly increasing time change converts the terminal horizon into an unbounded controller problem with bounded control disk; the paper then recursively handles the lower-dimensional simplex faces. Lemmas 2.1–2.3 (PDF pp. 3–5) establish continuity and the time-change/control representation. The nonnegative-Lipschitz assumption in Lemma 2.1 is a technical condition for that continuity argument; it should not be silently attached to every theorem stated for the original bounded-continuous reward.

## Finite-dimensional obstacle PDE

For a law supported on $k+1$ points, Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 6, proof pp. 6–12) identifies the finite-dimensional value $V_\alpha$ as the unique continuous viscosity solution on the simplex of the obstacle equation

\[
\min\!\left\{-\sup_{w\in D_c^{k+1}}\frac12\operatorname{tr}(ww^\top D_\xi^2V_\alpha),\;V_\alpha-\bar f\right\}=0,
\]

with Dirichlet boundary data given by the value on lower-dimensional faces. After removing one redundant simplex coordinate, the solution is the concave envelope of the projected payoff. The proof uses stochastic Perron sub- and supersolutions, comparison for the obstacle PDE, and a concave-envelope argument using Itô/Tanaka and the recursive boundary values. It is a finite-support approximation result, not a general smooth solution formula.

Section 4 (PDF pp. 13–16) gives call-spread and Brownian-exit examples; they illustrate the framework and do not supply a universal optimizer for arbitrary $\mu$ and $f$. Appendix A (PDF pp. 16–17) proves the continuity lemma via time rescaling, coupling/disintegration and optional sampling; references are pp. 17–18.

## Limits on use

The result depends on the Brownian-generated filtration, terminal-law constraint, stopping horizon and finite-support reduction used for the viscosity theorem. Keep the controller and stopper roles distinct. The concave-envelope representation belongs to the projected finite-dimensional problem. See `reading_coverage.json` for section-level proof coverage.
