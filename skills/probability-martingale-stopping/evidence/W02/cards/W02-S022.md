## Weak formulation and hypotheses

The problem is the three-dimensional incompressible nonhomogeneous Navier–Stokes system on a bounded smooth domain (D\subset\mathbb R^3), with no-slip boundary condition, density equation (d\rho+\operatorname{div}(\rho u)dt=0), and momentum equation forced by Brownian noise and small/large Lévy jumps (PDF pp. 1–3, equations (1.1)–(1.6)). The martingale-solution definition requires a filtered probability space, a (d)-dimensional standard Brownian motion (W), a time-homogeneous Poisson random measure (\pi), and progressively measurable ρ,u with

\[
\rho\in L^\infty(\Omega;L^\infty(0,T;L^\infty(D))),\qquad
u\in L^4(\Omega;L^\infty(0,T;H))\cap L^2(\Omega;L^2(0,T;V)).
\]

The weak density identity is tested against (H^1(D)); the weak momentum identity is tested against (V) and separates the compensated integral over {|z|<1} from the uncompensated integral over {|z|\ge1}. The paper assumes (W) independent of the compensated Poisson random measure; (\mu(\{0\})=0), (\int_Z(1\wedge|z|^2)\mu(dz)<\infty), and (\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^p\mu(dz)<\infty) for every (p\ge1). The filtration is right-continuous and complete, with null sets in (\mathcal F_0) (PDF p. 3).

Assumption (A) requires continuous (f,g) that are Lipschitz in (u) in (L^2(D)) and have linear (L^2)-growth. Assumption (B) requires the integrated (L^2(D)) squared differences of (F,G) over the small- and large-jump regions to be bounded by (C\|u-v\|_2^2), and for each (p\ge2) the corresponding integrated (p)-power norms to be bounded by (C(1+\|u\|_2^p)) (PDF pp. 3–4, (1.7)–(1.8)). Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 4) assumes additionally (u_0\in L^2(D)), (\rho_0\in L^\infty(D)), and (0<m\le\rho_0\le M); it concludes existence of a martingale solution, not pathwise uniqueness or uniqueness in law.

## BDG statement and localization in the existence proof

Lemma 2.4 (PDF p. 7) quotes a two-sided BDG inequality: for a real-valued square-integrable càdlàg martingale (M) with (M_0=0), each fixed (p\ge1) admits a finite (C_p), independent of (M), such that

\[
C_p^{-1}\,\mathbb E\langle M\rangle_T^{p/2}
\le \mathbb E\max_{0\le t\le T}|M_t|^p
\le C_p\,\mathbb E\langle M\rangle_T^{p/2}.
\]

The source calls its angle-bracket process “the quadratic variation” but does not define it here, so its notation is ambiguous for jump martingales. The displayed two-sided BDG statement is standard for the optional quadratic variation $[M]$ (with the necessary moment integrability); treating the source's symbol as $[M]$ is an explicit clarification, not a definition supplied by the paper. Do not silently read it as the predictable bracket $\langle M\rangle$: for a one-step martingale with $\Delta M=\pm q^{-1/2}$ each with probability $q/2$ and $0$ otherwise, $\langle M\rangle_1=1$ while $\mathbb E\sup_{t\le1}|M_t|=\sqrt q\to0$. Thus no universal lower-BDG constant can use the predictable bracket for all such jumps at $p=1$. By contrast, $\mathbb E\sqrt{[M]_1}=\sqrt q$ in this example. The source gives no numerical value for $C_p$; its displayed class is scalar real-valued càdlàg martingales, not a sharp-constant assertion or dimension-free vector inequality. The same preliminaries give the Lévy-type Itô formula (Lemma 2.3, PDF pp. 6–7), with predictable-integrability requirements for small jumps and a separate predictable large-jump term.

For the Galerkin velocity (u_n), the proof applies Itô's formula to the density-weighted kinetic energy (\int_D\rho_n|u_n|^2dx). It localizes at the first time (\tau_N) that (\|\sqrt{\rho_n}u_n\|_{L^2(D)}\ge N) (PDF p. 10, (3.11)); Brownian and jump martingales are bounded using BDG, the growth assumptions, and Young's inequality. After Gronwall and then (N\uparrow\infty), (3.29) gives, uniformly in (n),

\[
\mathbb E\sup_{s\le t}\|\sqrt{\rho_n(s)}u_n(s)\|_2^2
+2\nu\,\mathbb E\int_0^t\|\nabla u_n(s)\|_2^2ds\le C.
\]

Higher moment estimates (PDF pp. 14–19) and negative-Sobolev time-increment bounds (3.62), (3.66), PDF p. 20, yield tightness. The compactness step uses Aubin–Simon and Jakubowski–Skorokhod; the limit is identified by proving that the limiting (W) is Brownian and (\pi) remains a time-homogeneous Poisson random measure. Deterministic nonlinear terms use strong/weak convergence and uniform integrability; the stochastic terms are passed directly using mollification, integration by parts, Poisson-integral isometry/test duality, and Vitali convergence (PDF pp. 23–29, especially pp. 26–29). The authors do not use a general martingale representation theorem as the final identification step.

## Scope boundary

This is a specialized PDE existence theorem with bounded positive initial density, Lipschitz/linear-growth external forcing, and the stated Lévy-measure moments. Its BDG lemma and stopping-time use illustrate a proof pattern; the article does not supply a general martingale-problem existence theorem, optional-stopping theorem, uniqueness result, or sharp BDG constant. It is auxiliary only for the broader C112 martingale-problem dossier: the conditioned model's state-dependent generator, tightness, uniqueness, and variance interfaces are not shown to match these hypotheses.
