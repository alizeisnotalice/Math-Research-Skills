## Sub-ψ condition and main line-crossing theorem

For discrete (T=\mathbb N), or continuous (T=(0,\infty)), let (S,V) be real adapted processes with (S_0=V_0=0), (V_t\ge0). In continuous time the paper assumes the usual hypotheses on the filtration and càdlàg (S); its intrinsic-time process (V) is adapted, nondecreasing, and càdlàg. Definition 1 (PDF pp. 9–10) says ((S,V)) is (l_0)-sub-ψ if for each (\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{\max})), some supermartingale (L_t(\lambda)) satisfies (L_0(\lambda)\le l_0) and
\[
\exp\{\lambda S_t-\psi(\lambda)V_t\}\le L_t(\lambda)\quad\text{a.s. for all }t.
\]
The sub-ψ property belongs to the pair ((S,V)); (V) can be adapted rather than predictable. The exponential process need only be dominated by a supermartingale, not itself be a supermartingale.

Define (D(b)=\sup\{\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\max}):\psi(\lambda)/\lambda\le b\}), with the empty supremum set to zero. Theorem 1(a) (PDF pp. 11–12) gives for any (a,b>0)
\[
\mathbb P\big(\exists t\in T:S_t\ge a+bV_t\mid\mathcal F_0\big)\le l_0e^{-aD(b)}.
\]
This part does not require the CGF-like conditions. If ψ is CGF-like (strictly convex, twice continuously differentiable, ψ(0)=ψ'(0+)=0, and unbounded on its domain), Theorem 1(b)–(d) are equivalent formulations using the Legendre–Fenchel transform \(\psi^*\), the slope transform \(s(u)=\psi(\psi^{*\prime}(u))/\psi^{*\prime}(u)\), and intrinsic-time restrictions. In particular, part (d) separately bounds \(\{V_t\ge m,\ S_t\ge x+b(V_t-m)\}\) with the source's two-case boundary depending on (s(x/m)\); it is not an unconditional identity between all forms.

Proof of (a), PDF pp. 13–15: apply Ville's inequality to the dominating nonnegative supermartingale to obtain \(\mathbb P(\sup_t e^{\lambda S_t-\psi(\lambda)V_t}\ge e^z\mid\mathcal F_0)\le l_0e^{-z}\). On (S_t\ge a+bV_t), any \(\lambda\) with \(\psi(\lambda)\le b\lambda\) forces the exponential above (e^{a\lambda}); optimizing over the feasible λ gives (D(b)). When ψ is CGF-like, convex duality shows parts (a)–(d) equivalent. The proof requires only Ville plus these convex-transform identities; no optional stopping on a newly defined crossing time is needed in the master-theorem proof.

## Useful regimes and limits

For scalar sub-Gaussian (S) with \(\psi(\lambda)=\lambda^2/2\), (V_t) its stated variance proxy, and (l_0=1), the line-crossing consequence is \(\mathbb P(\exists t:S_t\ge a+bV_t)\le e^{-2ab}\). The paper also develops sub-Bernoulli, sub-Poisson, sub-gamma, sub-exponential, self-normalized, matrix (factor (l_0=d)), smooth Banach-space (factor (l_0=2)), continuous-time, and sequential-testing applications. Their (V), scale, and leading factor differ; see Facts 1–2, Lemma 3, and Corollaries 1–12. For continuous local martingales, Corollary 11(a) gives the Brownian line-crossing probability, with equality when the quadratic variation diverges to infinity.

The line-crossing inequality is one-sided. For a left-tail bound, verify the relevant assumption for (-S). A matrix maximum-eigenvalue result carries its dimension factor and trace-exponential domination; it is not a dimension-free scalar theorem. Self-normalized versions may use observed squares and can have weaker moment requirements, but only under the matching condition in Section 3. Do not infer a true-martingale or optional-stopping identity for (S) merely from the sub-ψ condition.
