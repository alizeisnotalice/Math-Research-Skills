## Finite-horizon dual problem and optimal martingales

For an adapted integrable reward $Z_0,\ldots,Z_J$, define the Snell envelope by $Y_J^*=Z_J$ and $Y_j^*=\max\{Z_j,\mathbb E[Y_{j+1}^*\mid\mathcal F_j]\}$. The martingales in the dual problem start at zero. The discrete Rogers dual value is

\[
Y_0^*=\inf_{M_0=0}\mathbb E\!\left[\max_{0\le j\le J}(Z_j-M_j)\right],
\]

and the martingale part $M^*$ of the Snell-envelope decomposition $Y_j^*=Y_0^*+M_j^*-A_j^*$ is surely optimal. The zero-start restriction is material: if unrestricted constant shifts are admitted, the displayed infimum can be sent to $-\infty$ even for a constant reward.

The paper distinguishes weak optimality (the conditional expected path maximum equals the Snell value) from sure optimality (the path maximum equals it almost surely). Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 2.7 (PDF pp. 6–7; proofs in §4, pp. 11–14) characterize weakly optimal martingales as $M=M^*-S$, where $S$ is a zero-start martingale satisfying the two interval constraints (2.9)–(2.10) around successive optimal stopping times. Corollary 2.8 adds the exact zero-increment and endpoint constraints characterizing sure optimality. These are finite-discrete-time characterizations, not continuous-time optional-stopping criteria.

## Randomized dual perturbation

On a product extension, let $\eta$ be a conditionally centered perturbation and form the pseudo-martingale $\widetilde M_j=M_j-\eta_j=M_j^*-S_j-\eta_j$. Proposition 3.1 (PDF p. 9) gives the dual lower bound $\mathbb E\max_j(Z_j-\widetilde M_j)\ge Y_0^*$. If $S=0$ and

\[
\eta_j\le Y_j^*-Z_j+A_j^*\quad\text{a.s. for every }j,
\]

then the pathwise maximum equals $Y_0^*$ almost surely. Theorem 3.2 assumes $M\in\mathcal M^{\circ,0}$ and $\eta_j=\xi_j(Y_j^*-Z_j+A_j^*)$, with the $\xi_j$ i.i.d. in $(-\infty,1]$, independent of the original filtration, mean zero, and having a joint continuous density supported on that set with positive density at 1. Under these assumptions, any non-Doob weakly optimal candidate that differs from $M^*$ with positive probability becomes strictly suboptimal after this randomization. Corollary 3.3 states that the randomized dual maximum has zero variance iff the original candidate is $M^*$.

The proof uses the Doob decomposition, optional sampling for the zero-start martingale $S$, the envelope constraints on $\eta$, and positive density near 1 to force strict loss for a non-Doob candidate. Numerical examples in §5 (PDF pp. 19–26) illustrate variance reduction but are not proof of the structural results.

## Limits on use

All claims are for a finite discrete exercise grid and the paper's integrability/optimal-time setup. Randomization occurs on an auxiliary product space and creates a pseudo-martingale for the dual calculation; do not conflate it with a new adapted martingale on the original filtration. The dual admissible martingales are zero-start. See `reading_coverage.json` for the proof-section map.
