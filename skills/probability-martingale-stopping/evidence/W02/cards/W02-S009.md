## Theorem 2.1: two-sided exponential moment and optimal exponent

Let \((X_i,\mathcal F_i)_{i\ge1}\) be supermartingale differences and \(S_k=\sum_{i=1}^kX_i\). For \(0<\alpha<1\), assume
\[
\sup_i\mathbb E\exp\{|X_i|^{2\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\}\le C_1<\infty.
\]
Then for every \(x>0\),
\[
\mathbb P\{\max_{k\le n}S_k\ge nx\}
\le C(\alpha,x)\exp\{-(x/4)^{2\alpha}n^\alpha\},
\]
where
\[
C(\alpha,x)=2+35C_1\left(\frac{1}{x^{2\alpha}16^{1-\alpha}}+
\frac1{x^2}\left(\frac{3(1-\alpha)}{2\alpha}\right)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\right).
\]
For \(x=1\) the theorem gives the stated order \(O(e^{-c n^\alpha})\). The exponent \(\alpha\) is optimal even for stationary martingale differences: the authors construct a stationary Rademacher sequence multiplied by a heavy-tailed random scale, satisfying the moment condition but with a matching lower tail along all sufficiently large \(n\) (PDF p. 2, proof pp. 5–6).

### Proof chain

For a threshold \(u\), split each increment into its centered small part \(X_i1_{|X_i|\le u}-\mathbb E[X_i1_{|X_i|\le u}|\mathcal F_{i-1}]\), its centered large part, and the predictable drift (nonpositive for a supermartingale). Apply the bounded-increment maximal Azuma inequality to the small part; use the martingale L² maximal inequality and the exponential-moment tail estimate for the large part. For the crossing threshold \(z>0\) in \(\mathbb P\{\max_{k\le n}S_k\ge z\}\), the proof takes \(t=1/\sqrt2\) and \(u=(z/(4\sqrt n))^{1-\alpha}\) (PDF p. 5); on writing \(z=nx\), this becomes \(u=(x\sqrt n/4)^{1-\alpha}\). The matching lower example uses a single random scale shared across independent Rademacher signs; it shows sharpness of the exponent, not a universal exact prefactor.

## Theorem 2.2: one-sided exponential moment and conditional-second-moment clock

With \(\langle S\rangle_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\mathbb E[X_i^2|\mathcal F_{i-1}]\), replace the two-sided moment assumption by
\[
\sup_i\mathbb E\exp\{(X_i^+)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\}\le C_1<\infty.
\]
Then for all \(x,v>0\),
\[
\mathbb P\{\exists k\le n:S_k\ge x,\ \langle S\rangle_k\le v^2\}
\le \exp\!\left\{-\frac{x^2}{2(v^2+x^{2-\alpha}/3)}\right\}+nC_1e^{-x^\alpha}.
\]
The bracket is the predictable sum of conditional second moments (it is a sum of conditional variances only when the increments have conditional mean zero); the large-jump remainder is explicit. The proof truncates above \(u\), applies the one-sided bounded-increment Freedman lemma (Lemma 4.1, PDF p. 7) to the truncated supermartingale, then bounds the chance of any discarded positive jump by \(nC_1e^{-u^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}}\); choosing \(u=x^{1-\alpha}\) gives the display (pp. 7–8).

## Corollary 2.1: add an exponential moment of the total bracket

If, in addition,
\[
\mathbb E\exp\{(\langle S\rangle_n/n)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\}\le C_2,
\]
then, for \(x>0\),
\[
\mathbb P\{\max_{k\le n}S_k\ge nx\}
\le \exp\!\left\{-\frac{x^{1+\alpha}n^\alpha}{2(1+x/3)}\right\}
+(nC_1+C_2)e^{-x^\alpha n^\alpha}.
\]
The proof splits according to whether \(\langle S\rangle_n\le nv^2\), applies Theorem 2.2 on the first event and exponential Markov on the second, then chooses \(v=(nx)^{(1-\alpha)/2}\) (PDF p. 8). The stationarity construction again proves exponent optimality under the displayed assumptions.

## Scope limits

These are one-sided maximal deviation bounds for discrete-time supermartingale differences. The two-sided increment moment in Theorem 2.1 and positive-part moment in Theorem 2.2 are different hypotheses; the latter needs the predictable variance clock and carries a union-bound tail term. Neither theorem supplies a continuous-time result or an exact BDG constant. Section coverage is in `reading_coverage.json`.
