## Full-document map and mathematical evidence

- **§1, “A quick summary of some parts of measure theory” (PDF pp. 2–5).** Builds from algebras and σ-algebras to probability spaces, measurable random variables and expectations. Theorem 1.6 gives equivalent continuity/additivity characterizations; its proof partitions an increasing union into disjoint differences and uses complements for decreasing events. The notes define generated σ-algebras and state Carathéodory extension without proof.
- **§2, “Conditional expectation and a toy example” (pp. 5–7).** Theorem 2.1 states existence of an integrable, sub-σ-algebra-measurable version (V=E[X\mid\mathcal G]), characterized by (E[V;G]=E[X;G]) for every (G\in\mathcal G), unique a.s. Finite-partition examples compute conditional averages and illustrate independence from a σ-algebra. The tower rule is stated for (mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal H); its proof is left to the reader.
- **§3, “Probability toolbox” (pp. 8–12).** Proves the two Borel–Cantelli directions, with independence required for the divergent-sum converse; monotone, Fatou and dominated convergence; Tonelli for nonnegative sums and Fubini under (E\sum_k|X_k|<\infty); Jensen, Hölder, Lyapunov and Minkowski. The arguments use tail events and continuity of probability, simple-function approximation/truncation, monotone limits, convex supporting lines and Hölder. These are discrete-sum formulations.
- **§4, “Modes of convergence” (pp. 13–15).** Defines weak, in-probability, a.s. and (L^p) convergence. Theorem 4.2 gives an a.s. Cauchy/tail-supremum criterion. Theorem 4.4 proves a.s. → probability, (L^p\to X) → probability for (p>0) by Markov, and probability → weak convergence via tight truncation and uniform continuity on compact intervals. Examples show reverse implications can fail; e.g. a.s. convergence need not imply (L^1) convergence.
- **§5, “Martingales, stopping times” (pp. 15–18).** The setting is discrete time with a filtration ((\mathcal F_n)); martingales are adapted, (L^1) at every time and satisfy (E[M_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=M_n) a.s. A predictable (C_n) is (\mathcal F_{n-1})-measurable, and the discrete stochastic integral is ((C\bullet X)_n=\sum_{k=1}^n C_k(X_k-X_{k-1})). Theorem 5.9 preserves supermartingales for bounded nonnegative (C), and martingales for bounded signed (C); the text also gives the (L^2)-integrability relaxation. For a stopping time (T\in\{0,1,\ldots,\infty\}), {(T\le n)}\in\mathcal F_n and (X^T_n=X_{T\wedge n}); Theorem 5.11 proves stopped-process preservation by the predictable indicator (1\{k\le T\}).

  Theorem 5.12 (PDF p. 16) gives sufficient conditions for (E[X_T]\le E[X_0]) for a supermartingale, with equality for a martingale: (i) (T) bounded; (ii) (X) bounded and (T<\infty) a.s.; or (iii) deterministic bounded increments |(X_n-X_{n-1})|\le K and (E T<\infty). The proof first applies the stopped-process result to (T\wedge n); it passes to the limit by eventual equality, dominated convergence, or the bound |(X_{T\wedge n}-X_0)|\le KT, respectively. These are sufficient hypotheses, not a claim that a.s. finite stopping alone gives equality. Theorem 5.14 relaxes conditions for (X\ge0): any a.s.-finite stopping time for a nonnegative supermartingale gives (E[X_T]\le E[X_0]) by Fatou. Lemma 5.15 shows that a uniform conditional probability 
  \(P(T\le n+N\mid\mathcal F_n)\ge\varepsilon\quad\text{a.s. for every }n\ge0\)
  implies a geometric tail and (E T\le N/\varepsilon). The simple symmetric walk hitting (+1) is a counterexample to unrestricted optional-stopping equality: (T<\infty) a.s., (S_T=1), but (E T=\infty). Exponential martingales then show (P(T<\infty)=1) and (E[\alpha^T]=(1-\sqrt{1-\alpha^2})/\alpha), (0<\alpha<1).
- **§6, “Martingale convergence” (pp. 19–20).** A predictable on/off transform gives ((b-a)E U_N[a,b]\le E[(X_N-a)^-]) for a supermartingale. Hence an (L^1)-bounded supermartingale converges a.s. to a finite limit. For an (L^2) martingale, disjoint increments are orthogonal; the Pythagorean identity gives (\sup_n E M_n^2<\infty\) iff (\sum_{k\ge1}E(M_k-M_{k-1})^2<\infty\), and in this case (M_n\to M_\infty) a.s. and in (L^2).
- **§7, “Doob decomposition” (pp. 20–21).** For an adapted (L^1) process, define (A_0=0), (A_n=\sum_{k=1}^n E[X_k-X_{k-1}\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]) and (M_n=X_n-X_0-A_n). The proof checks predictability and the martingale difference identity, and uniqueness follows because every decomposition must have these conditional drift increments; (X) is a submartingale iff (A) is a.s. nondecreasing. For an (L^2) martingale with (M_0=0), the predictable compensator in (M^2=N+A) is denoted (\langle M\rangle), with (A_n-A_{n-1}=E[(M_n-M_{n-1})^2\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]) and (EM_n^2=EA_n). This is the discrete predictable bracket construction stated in these notes.
- **§8, “Uniform integrability” (pp. 21–23).** Defines (p)-th-power UI by 
  \(\lim_{c\to\infty}\sup_n E(|X_n|^p;|X_n|\ge c)=0.\)
  Lemma 8.2 proves absolute continuity of the integral using a contradiction sequence, Borel–Cantelli and Fatou. Theorem 8.3 assumes (p\ge1), (X,X_n\in L^p) and (X_n\to X) in probability. It states (L^p) convergence iff (X_n) is (p)-th-power UI, equivalently convergence of (E|X_n|^p) to (E|X|^p); a uniform (q)-moment bound for some (q>p) or an (L^p) dominator are sufficient conditions. The notes give only partial proofs: the UI directions are worked for (p=1), the norm implication, higher-moment-to-UI and domination-to-(L^p) are shown, and the converse of Scheffé’s theorem is explicitly skipped. Do not attribute a complete proof of every stated equivalence to this source.
- **§9, “Uniformly integrable martingales” (pp. 23–25).** Theorem 9.1: a UI martingale converges a.s. and in (L^1), and (M_n=E[M_\infty\mid\mathcal F_n]) a.s.; the proof combines (L^1)-boundedness/upcrossings with the (L^1) consequence of UI and checks conditional-expectation identities on events in (\mathcal F_n). Theorem 9.2 (Lévy upward) applies to (\xi\in L^1) and an increasing filtration, giving (E[\xi\mid\mathcal F_n]\to E[\xi\mid\mathcal F_\infty]) a.s. and in (L^1). The section also proves Kolmogorov’s 0–1 law for independent-variable tail σ-algebras, states/proves Lévy downward convergence for decreasing σ-algebras, and derives the strong law for i.i.d. (L^1) variables using (\mathcal G_{-n}=\sigma(S_n,S_{n+1},\ldots)), the conditional expectation (E[X_1\mid\mathcal G_{-n}]=S_n/n), and tail triviality.
- **§10, “Doob’s submartingale inequality” (pp. 25–26).** For a nonnegative discrete submartingale (Z), (c>0) and integer (n>0), Theorem 10.1 states
  \[
  P\{\max_{0\le k\le n}Z_k\ge c\}\le\frac{E(Z_n;\max_{0\le k\le n}Z_k\ge c)}{c}\le\frac{EZ_n}{c}.
  \]
  The proof partitions the crossing event into disjoint first-hit events (F_k\in\mathcal F_k); conditional submartingality gives (E(Z_n;F_k)\ge E(Z_k;F_k)\ge cP(F_k)), and summation proves the bound. Convex transforms (g(M_n)) are submartingales when integrable by conditional Jensen. The cashier example obtains a finite-horizon ruin bound using the squared simple walk.
- **§11, “A discrete Black–Scholes option pricing formula” (pp. 26–29).** The finite model has independent binary ω-values, stock returns (R_n\in\{a,b\}), bond growth (B_n=(1+r)^nB_0), and (-1<a<r<b<\infty); the probability (p=(r-a)/(b-a)\in(0,1)) makes (E R_n=r). A strategy is predictable and self-financing, with nonnegative wealth and call payoff ((S_N-K)^+). Lemma 11.3 proves by the two next-step states that every martingale has a unique predictable representation against (Z_n=\sum_{k\le n}(\omega_k-2p+1)). Discounted self-financing wealth is therefore a martingale; conditional replication gives the unique initial price
  \[
  x=(1+r)^{-N}E_p[(S_N-K)^+].
  \]
  The proof obtains nonnegative stock holdings from the coordinatewise monotonicity of the call payoff. This conclusion is for the stated finite binary model and strategy definition; it is not a continuous-time pricing theorem.
- **References (PDF p. 29).** Cites Shiryaev, *Probability* (2nd ed., 1996), and Williams, *Probability with Martingales* (1991).

## Transfer limits

The stopping times, transforms, convergence and stochastic integrals in §§5–7 are discrete-time results. No continuous-time optional-stopping theorem, local-martingale limit passage, Itô integral construction, or general Doob–Meyer theorem is proved here. In particular, a.s. finiteness alone is insufficient for the equality case of Theorem 5.12; verify its integrability/uniform-integrability hypotheses separately before transferring a stopping identity.
