# W02-S017 — Optional sampling inequalities for demimartingales

## Source and reading scope

Milto Hadjikyriakou and B. L. S. Prakasa Rao, “Doob-type optional sampling theorems for demimartingales with applications to associated sequences,” arXiv:2507.05023v4. The local PDF's arXiv margin identifies the v4 submission as 6 August 2026; the title page itself is dated August 7, 2026 (PDF p. 1). I read the complete original PDF, physical pp. 1–16 (including references).

## Definitions and theorem conditions

A sequence of integrable random variables \((S_j)\) is a demimartingale if
\[
\mathbb E[(S_{j+1}-S_j)f(S_1,\ldots,S_j)]\ge0
\]
for every componentwise nondecreasing \(f\) for which the expectation exists; for a demisubmartingale, \(f\) is additionally required nonnegative (Definition 1.2, PDF p. 1). This is an inequality against monotone functions of the past, not a conditional-expectation identity with respect to a filtration. Martingales are examples, but the converse fails (Remark 1.4, p. 2).

For a bounded stopping time \(\tau\le M\), Theorem 2.1 (p. 6) assumes \(1_{\{\tau=k\}}\) is componentwise nondecreasing in \((S_1,\ldots,S_k)\) for \(k<M\). Then \((S_\tau,S_M)\) is a demisubmartingale and \(\mathbb E S_\tau\le\mathbb E S_M\). A related form, Theorem 2.3 (pp. 6–7), assumes \(1_{\{\tau<i\}}\) is componentwise nondecreasing in \((S_1,\ldots,S_{i-1})\), for each \(2\le i\le M\), and yields the same expectation inequality. The proof expands
\[
S_M-S_\tau=\sum_{i=2}^M(S_i-S_{i-1})1_{\{\tau<i\}},
\]
then applies the defining demisubmartingale inequality termwise. Corollaries 2.2 and 2.4 apply these results to \(\tau\wedge j\) (pp. 6–7).

The unbounded-time Doob-type results (Section 3) are distinct from classical OST. For a demimartingale, Theorem 3.1 (pp. 9–10) assumes \(1_{\{\tau\le j\}}\) is componentwise nondecreasing in \((S_1,\ldots,S_j)\), plus any one of:

1. \(\tau\) is bounded;
2. \(\tau<\infty\) a.s. and the whole sequence is uniformly bounded a.s.; or
3. \(\mathbb E\tau<\infty\) and \(|S_k-S_{k-1}|\le M\) a.s. for all \(k\ge2\), for a constant \(M>0\).

It concludes \(\mathbb E S_\tau\le\mathbb E S_1\). Under the same monotonicity condition, Theorem 3.2 (p. 10) replaces these integrability alternatives by nonnegativity of the demimartingale and \(\tau<\infty\) a.s.; Fatou gives the same inequality. For a **demisubmartingale**, Theorem 3.3 (p. 10) reverses both the direction of monotonicity (the indicator is componentwise nonincreasing) and the conclusion: under one of conditions (1)–(3), \(\mathbb E S_\tau\ge\mathbb E S_1\).

## Proof chain and examples

For bounded \(\tau\), compare the stopped value with a deterministic terminal value by summing increments times the pathwise event indicator. The monotonicity hypothesis makes the indicator an admissible test function in the demi(sub)martingale definition. For unbounded \(\tau\), first apply the bounded result to \(\tau\wedge n\), giving a uniform expectation comparison. Since \(\tau\wedge n\to\tau\) and \(S_{\tau\wedge n}\to S_\tau\) a.s., bounded convergence handles (1)–(2); under (3), \(|S_{\tau\wedge n}-S_1|\le M(\tau-1)\), an integrable dominating bound. The nonnegative case uses Fatou instead (pp. 9–10). The proof thus depends on the stopping-rule monotonicity and the stated convergence/integrability route; it is not a filtration-only stopping theorem.

For a first upper crossing \(\tau_a=\inf\{n\ge1:S_n\ge a\}\wedge M\), \(1_{\{\tau_a<i\}}=1_{\{\max_{k<i}S_k\ge a\}}\) is nondecreasing, so the bounded comparison applies (Remark 2.6, pp. 8–9). The paper also derives a maximal negative-excursion bound for a demisubmartingale (Theorem 4.1, pp. 10–11), and a dyadic Borel–Cantelli argument gives \(\max_{k\le n}(-S_k)^+/[n(\log n)^{1+\epsilon}]\to0\) a.s. for the **uncentered partial sums** \(S_k=\sum_{i=1}^kX_i\) of positively associated \(X_i\) with \(\sup_i\mathbb E X_i^+<\infty\) and \(\mathbb E X_i\ge0\) (Corollary 4.3, pp. 11–12; checked in the original page image). Generalized Wald inequalities for associated variables follow by centering the sums to obtain demi(sub)martingales (Theorems 4.5–4.6, pp. 13–14).

## Failure boundary and transfer warning

Do not substitute this for martingale optional stopping: demimartingales need not be martingales, do not carry a conditional-mean-zero increment property, and need not have associated increments. The stopping event must satisfy the specified componentwise monotonicity at every relevant time; an arbitrary stopping time does not qualify. The unbounded results require one of the exact integrability/boundedness conditions above (or nonnegativity for Theorem 3.2). Results for associated sequences additionally use positive association and, in the convex-transform result, nonnegative variables, integrability of \(g(X_n)\), convex strictly increasing \(g\) with \(g(0)=0\), and a bounded stopping time. Those conditions do not follow merely from the term “martingale.”
