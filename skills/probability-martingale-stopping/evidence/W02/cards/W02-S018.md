# W02-S018 — Sokol (2012), Proving existence results in martingale theory using a subsequence principle

Reading evidence: all 16 PDF pages read; arXiv:1205.2482v2.

Standing setup (PDF p. 3): the filtered probability space satisfies the usual conditions. For A_0=0, locally integrable means the total variation process is locally integrable after a localizing sequence. Theorem 2.3, PDF pp. 7–8 (the construction begins on pp. 3–7): a locally integrable càdlàg adapted finite-variation process A has a predictable locally integrable càdlàg finite-variation compensator Π*_pA, unique up to indistinguishability, such that A−Π*_pA is a local martingale.

Proof chain: construct discrete compensators on dyadic grids for bounded jump processes; obtain L2 bounds; use a Hilbert-space convex-combination/subsequence lemma; identify the predictable increasing limit using a limsup and predictable-process characterization; extend by monotone convergence, jump decomposition, and localization. For uniqueness, the difference of two predictable compensators is itself a predictable finite-variation local martingale; the theorem cited in the paper makes that difference constant, hence zero from its initial value. This does not claim every finite-variation local martingale is predictable.

Theorem 3.2, PDF pp. 8–12: for a local martingale M starting at zero there is an increasing càdlàg [M] with M²−[M] a local martingale and Δ[M]=(ΔM)². The proof first handles bounded martingales by dyadic squared increments and an L2 subsequence argument, then uses the decomposition into locally bounded and finite-variation pieces and localizes.

Section 4, pp. 12–14, sketches a route from predictable process theory and compensators to quadratic variation and construction of integrals with locally bounded predictable integrands. This is a roadmap, not a complete integral-domain or norm-estimate theorem. Distinguish [M] from predictable bracket ⟨M⟩.
