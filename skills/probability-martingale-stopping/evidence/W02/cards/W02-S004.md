## Model and averaging limit

The paper studies a deterministic fast–slow system on (M\times S), with compact (M=\mathbb T^1), (S=\mathbb T^d),
\[
F_\varepsilon(x,\theta)=(f(x,\theta),\theta+\varepsilon\omega(x,\theta)),\qquad \partial_xf>\lambda>2.
\]
For each frozen (\theta), the expanding map has a unique SRB measure (m_\theta), smooth parameter dependence, and exponential decay of correlations on the stated test spaces. Initial laws are generated from standard pairs; the theorem's displayed simple case fixes the slow coordinate (\theta_0) and uses a (C^1) density for the fast coordinate. These are deterministic-dynamics assumptions that supply mixing and a way to recover conditioning in the limit.

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 5) says the interpolated slow path laws converge weakly to the trajectory of
\[
\dot\Theta=\bar\omega(\Theta),\quad \Theta(0)=\theta_0,\qquad
\bar\omega(\theta)=\int\omega(x,\theta)\,m_\theta(dx).
\]
Proof, developed in Sections 3–5 (pp. 7–13): standard-pair conditioning gives a uniform averaging estimate for generator tests; the interpolated paths are uniformly Lipschitz, hence tight; every subsequential limit solves the first-order martingale problem; Lipschitz continuity of \(\bar\omega\) and Gronwall give uniqueness, so the whole family converges.

## Fluctuation CLT and covariance

For \(\zeta^\varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon^{-1/2}(\Theta^\varepsilon(t)-\Theta(t))\), Theorem 2.2 (PDF pp. 5–6) states weak convergence to the zero-mean Gaussian process solving
\[
d\zeta=D\bar\omega(\Theta(t))\zeta\,dt+\sigma(\Theta(t))\,dB_t,\qquad \zeta(0)=0,
\]
where (B) is standard (d)-dimensional Brownian motion. With \(\widehat\omega(x,\theta)=\omega(x,\theta)-\bar\omega(\theta)\), the Green–Kubo covariance is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2(\theta)={}&m_\theta(\widehat\omega\otimes\widehat\omega)\\
&+\sum_{k\ge1}m_\theta\!\left(\widehat\omega\circ f_\theta^k\otimes\widehat\omega\right)
+\sum_{k\ge1}m_\theta\!\left(\widehat\omega\otimes\widehat\omega\circ f_\theta^k\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The matrix is symmetric and nonnegative, so its symmetric nonnegative square root \(\sigma\) exists. Lemma 7.5 (PDF pp. 21–22) states uniform strict positivity on \(s\in[0,T]\) if every nonzero direction \(v\) has a centered scalar projection \(\langle v,\widehat\omega(\cdot,\theta)\rangle\) that is not a smooth coboundary for the frozen map. The proof shows the degeneracy implication: if \(v^\top\sigma^2(\theta)v=0\), then that projected observable is an \(L^2(m_\theta)\) coboundary, and Livsic regularity upgrades its transfer function to \(C^1\). Thus one must exclude a coboundary in every nonzero direction; it is not enough to say the entire vector observable is not a vector-valued coboundary.

Proof chain, Sections 7.1–7.3 (PDF pp. 15–23): first prove tightness for the rescaled fluctuations with moment estimates; then show the generator-test martingale-problem errors vanish, with the drift (D\bar\omega(\Theta(t))\zeta) and diffusion matrix above; uniqueness of that time-inhomogeneous linear martingale problem identifies every limit with the SDE. Itô's formula verifies that the SDE law solves the problem, and the characteristic-function argument shows the solution is centered Gaussian. The paper's proof explicitly treats the base dynamics as deterministic and reconstructs the needed limiting conditional identities through its standard-pair setup.

## Scope and transfer boundary

This is an explanatory, specialized result for a uniformly expanding fast map with a slowly varying parameter and standard-pair initial laws. The estimates rely on mixing, smooth dependence of (m_\theta), tightness, and uniqueness; it is not a generic martingale CLT. For a new application, establish its own mixing and moment estimates, identify its actual Green–Kubo/cross-covariance structure, and prove tightness and martingale-problem uniqueness. A nonzero scalar projection of the centered observable being a coboundary can create a degenerate diffusion direction.
