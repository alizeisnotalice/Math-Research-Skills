## Classical Lq Itô isomorphism

Let \(N\) be a Poisson random measure on \(\mathbb R_+\times J\) with intensity \(dt\,d\nu\), where \((J,\mathcal J,\nu)\) is \(\sigma\)-finite, and let \(\widetilde N=N-dt\,d\nu\). Assumption 3.2 requires that for \(s<t\), counts on \((s,t]\times A\) are \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable and independent of \(\mathcal F_s\). For a simple adapted \(L^q(S)\)-valued integrand \(F\), Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3) states, for \(1<p,q<\infty\), \(t>0\), and \(B\in\mathcal J\),
\[
\left(\mathbb E\sup_{0<s\le t}
\left\|\int_{(0,s]\times B}F\,d\widetilde N\right\|_{L^q(S)}^p\right)^{1/p}
\simeq_{p,q}\|F1_{(0,t]\times B}\|_{I_{p,q}}.
\]
The equivalence constants depend on \(p,q\), not on dimension; they are not claimed sharp. The norm \(I_{p,q}\) is the appropriate intersection/sum of three spaces, selected by the relative positions of \(p,q,2\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
2\le q\le p & S_q^p\cap D_{q,q}^p\cap D_{p,q}^p\\
2\le p\le q & S_q^p\cap(D_{q,q}^p+D_{p,q}^p)\\
1<p<2\le q & (S_q^p\cap D_{q,q}^p)+D_{p,q}^p\\
1<q<2\le p & (S_q^p+D_{q,q}^p)\cap D_{p,q}^p\\
1<q\le p\le2 & S_q^p+(D_{q,q}^p\cap D_{p,q}^p)\\
1<p\le q\le2 & S_q^p+D_{q,q}^p+D_{p,q}^p.
\end{array}
\]
Here the square-function norm is \(\|F\|_{S_q^p}=(\mathbb E\| (\int |F|^2dt\,d\nu)^{1/2}\|_{L^q}^p)^{1/p}\); the other two are the mixed norms \(D_{q,q}^p=(\mathbb E(\int\|F\|_{L^q}^qdt\,d\nu)^{p/q})^{1/p}\) and \(D_{p,q}^p=(\int\mathbb E\|F\|_{L^q}^pdt\,d\nu)^{1/p}\). The estimate with \(q=1\) is upper-only; no two-sided isomorphism is asserted there because \(L^1\) is not UMD.

## Proof route and noncommutative extension

The proof first applies Doob's maximal inequality to the norm of the integral, then the UMD decoupling inequality to replace the compensated Poisson integral by an independent-copy version. The independent sum is estimated by the paper's vector-valued Rosenthal inequalities (Theorem 1.2, pp. 6–16); conditional expectation onto simple partitions and duality recover the lower estimate (PDF pp. 19–24). The assumptions are first stated for elementary/simple adapted integrands; general integrands follow by completing their normed domain, as the paper explains on pp. 2–3.

Sections 4–7 extend the Rosenthal and Itô-isomorphism arguments to noncommutative \(L^q\)-spaces. Theorem 7.1 (PDF pp. 41–43) replaces the classical square-function term with both row and column square functions in the six corresponding regimes. Appendix A (pp. 44–46) proves the UMD decoupling step; Appendix B (pp. 46–49) supplies remaining Rosenthal regimes; references are pp. 49–51.

Do not cite this result as the scalar continuous-martingale BDG inequality: its integrator is a compensated Poisson random measure, its natural domain is an intersection/sum of mixed norms, and constants are equivalence constants. Nor does the theorem automatically extend beyond UMD/Lq settings. Section-by-section physical page coverage is in `reading_coverage.json`.
