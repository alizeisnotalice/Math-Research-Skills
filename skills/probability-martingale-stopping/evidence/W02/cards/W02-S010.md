## Theorem 2.1: upper-tail crossing bound with unbounded negative jumps

Let \((\xi_i,\mathcal F_i)_{i=0}^n\) be real supermartingale differences on a probability space, \(\xi_0=0\), with \(\{\varnothing,\Omega\}=\mathcal F_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal F_n\), so \(\mathbb E[\xi_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le0\). Assume \(\mathbb E\xi_i^2<\infty\) for every \(1\le i\le n\). Write \(S_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\xi_i\), \(\langle S\rangle_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\), and for \(y\ge0\)
\[
G_k^y=\sum_{i=1}^k\left(\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i\le y\}}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]+\xi_i^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i>y\}}\right).
\]
For \(x,y\ge0\), \(v>0\), and \(k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\),
\[
\mathbb P\{\exists k:S_k\ge x,\ G_k^y\le v^2\}\le B_1(x,y,v)\le B_2(x,y,v),
\]
where for \(y>0\),
\[
B_1=\exp\!\left(\frac{x}{y}-\left(\frac{x}{y}+\frac{v^2}{y^2}\right)\log\!\left(1+\frac{xy}{v^2}\right)\right),\qquad
B_2=\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2(v^2+xy/3)}\right),
\]
and at \(y=0\) both continuous limits are explicitly \(\exp(-x^2/(2v^2))\) (Theorem 2.1, PDF pp. 4, 7–9). Since \(G_k^y\le H_k^y:=\sum_{i\le k}(\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]+\xi_i^2\mathbf1_{\{|\xi_i|>y\}})\), this strengthens the cited Dzhaparidze–van Zanten bound. If \(\xi_i\le a\) for all \(i\), then \(G_k^a=\langle S\rangle_k\); no lower bound on \(\xi_i\) is needed for that reduction (pp. 2–4).

## Proof chain

For \(\lambda>0\), define a normalized product with one-step factor proportional to \(\exp\{\lambda\xi_i-\tfrac12(\lambda\xi_i)^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i>y\}}\}\). Lemma 4.1 (pp. 7–8) bounds its conditional normalizer by
\[
\exp\!\left(\frac{e^{\lambda y}-1-\lambda y}{y^2}\,\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i\le y\}}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\right),
\]
with coefficient \(\lambda^2/2\) at \(y=0\). This follows from monotonicity of \((e^z-1-z)/z^2\) on the lower-truncated part, the inequality \(e^{z-z^2/2}\le1+z\) on the upper tail, conditional expectation and \(1+u\le e^u\). The normalized product is a nonnegative mean-one martingale; stopping it at the first \(k\) with \(S_k\ge x,G_k^y\le v^2\) gives a change-of-measure identity. On the stopped event, the likelihood ratio is bounded by \(\exp\{-\lambda x+[(e^{\lambda y}-1-\lambda y)/y^2]v^2\}\). Optimizing at \(\lambda=y^{-1}\log(1+xy/v^2)\) gives \(B_1\); the elementary bound \(e^t-1-t\le t^2/[2(1-t/3)]\) yields \(B_2\) (pp. 8–9).

Theorem 2.2 (pp. 4–5, 10–11) gives a non-square-integrable counterpart. For \(1<\beta<2\), assume \(\mathbb E|\xi_i|^\beta<\infty\) and the same supermartingale-difference setup. Put
\[
G_k^0(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^k\left(\mathbb E[(\xi_i^-)^\beta\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]+(\xi_i^+)^\beta\right).
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb P\{\exists k\le n:S_k\ge x,\ G_k^0(\beta)\le v^\beta\}
\le \exp\{-C_\beta(x/v)^{\beta/(\beta-1)}\},\quad
C_\beta=(\beta-1)\beta^{-\beta/(\beta-1)}.
\]
The proof repeats the stopped exponential change of measure, using the exact pointwise inequality
\[
\exp\{z-(z^+)^\beta\}\le 1+z+(z^-)^\beta,\qquad z\in\mathbb R,
\]
for \(1<\beta<2\) (Lemma 5.1, PDF p. 10). Substituting \(z=\lambda\xi_i\) with \(\lambda\ge0\) gives \((\lambda\xi_i)^+{}^\beta=\lambda^\beta(\xi_i^+)^\beta\) and \((\lambda\xi_i^-)^\beta=\lambda^\beta(\xi_i^-)^\beta\). Thus the conditional normalizer is at most \(\exp\{\lambda^\beta\mathbb E[(\xi_i^-)^\beta\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\}\). The event bounds the exponent by \(-\lambda x+\lambda^\beta v^\beta\); minimizing gives the stated constant and power (pp. 10–11). This is not the different (false) rearrangement \(e^z-(z^+)^\beta\le1+z+(z^-)^\beta\).

## Scope and failure boundaries

The event is an **upper crossing** of a supermartingale and a variance/truncated-moment budget at the same stopping index. The quadratic theorem controls the positive-large-jump tail by realized squares and the remaining part by predictable conditional second moments; swapping \(\{\xi_i\le y\}\) for a symmetric absolute-value truncation changes the theorem. The \(\beta\)-moment version requires \(1<\beta<2\), not merely a first moment. These bounds are not two-sided without applying a separate argument to \(-S\), which generally changes the one-sided supermartingale assumption. For conditionally symmetric martingale differences, Theorem 2.3 instead uses absolute truncation \(M_k^y\) and a different Sason-type bound (pp. 3–5, 11–14); it is not the assumption set of Theorem 2.1.
