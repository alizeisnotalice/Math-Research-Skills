## Main continuous-time UMD BDG theorem

For a UMD Banach space (X), an (X)-valued local martingale (M) with (M_0=0), and (t\ge0), Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3; restated as Theorem 5.1, pp. 17–19) states that the covariance bilinear form \([[M]]_t\) is well-defined and bounded almost surely, and for every (1\le p<\infty),
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|M_s\|^p\ \bar\sim_{p,X}\ \mathbb E\,\gamma([[M]]_t)^p.
\]
The constants in \(\bar\sim_{p,X}\) depend only on (p) and (X). For a nonnegative symmetric bilinear form (V:X^*\times X^*\to\mathbb R), \(\gamma(V)\) is the (L^2)-norm of the associated (X)-valued Gaussian random variable, characterized by \(\mathbb E\langle\xi,x^*\rangle^2=V(x^*,x^*)\). This is a Banach-valued covariance norm, not a scalar substitution of ([M]).

Proof of Theorem 5.1: first project to finite-dimensional subspaces and refine time partitions. Discrete UMD-valued BDG compares the maximal martingale sums with Gaussian randomizations; the associated finite-partition covariance forms converge to \([[M]]_t\) in the required (L^p\) Gaussian norm using scalar bracket convergence. For infinite-dimensional (X), UMD implies reflexivity; separability reduces the dual to an increasing union of finite-dimensional subspaces. Apply the finite-dimensional result to the projections, use monotone convergence and continuity of the Gaussian characteristic, then localize a general local martingale. The finite-dimensional projection and localization steps are essential to the extension.

## Continuous and purely discontinuous cases

Theorem 6.1 (PDF pp. 21–22) extends the same equivalence to (0<p<\infty) **for continuous local martingales** in UMD (X). For (p<1), the proof uses continuity/predictability of the increasing process \(\gamma([[M]])\) and Lenglart's inequality after the (p\ge1) case; this range is not asserted for arbitrary jump martingales.

For a purely discontinuous UMD-valued martingale, Theorem 6.5 (PDF p. 22) gives, for (1\le p<\infty),
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{s\le t}\|M_s\|^p\ \bar\sim_{p,X}\
\mathbb E\| (\Delta M_s)_{0\le s\le t}\|_{\gamma(\ell^2([0,t]),X)}^p.
\]
The jump sequence has the stated Gaussian-radonifying norm; it is not interchangeable with the continuous bracket term. Continuous plus purely discontinuous decompositions cover general UMD-valued local martingales (Remark 6.6, p. 22).

## Stochastic-integral estimate and domain

For a Hilbert space (H), UMD Banach space (X), (H)-valued local martingale \(\widetilde M\), and elementary predictable \(\Phi:\mathbb R_+\times\Omega\to\mathcal L(H,X)\), Theorem 7.3 (PDF pp. 25–26) gives for (1\le p<\infty)
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{s\le t}\Big\|\int_0^s\Phi\,d\widetilde M\Big\|^p
\ \bar\sim_{p,X}\
\mathbb E\|\Phi q_{\widetilde M}^{1/2}\|_{\gamma(L^2([0,t],[\widetilde M];H),X)}^p.
\]
Here ([\widetilde M]) is the quadratic variation and (q_{\widetilde M}) its quadratic-variation derivative as defined in the paper. Corollary 7.4 extends the integral to strongly predictable \(\Phi\) when the paper's expected \(\gamma\)-integrability condition is finite; it gives a martingale integral and the same estimate. The identity \(\langle\Phi\cdot\widetilde M,x^*\rangle=(\Phi^*x^*)\cdot\widetilde M\) is part of the construction. Predictability and the displayed integrability condition are not optional.

## Transfer boundary

Do not use scalar Doob/BDG constants for arbitrary Banach-valued martingales, replace the Gaussian covariance characteristic by a scalar bracket, or transfer the (p<1) result from continuous paths to arbitrary jumps. Theorem 7.3 starts with elementary predictable integrands; the larger Corollary 7.4 domain needs strong predictability and finite \(\gamma\)-integrability. This paper supports the UMD and stochastic-integration interface, not a general Banach-space BDG theorem without geometric assumptions.
