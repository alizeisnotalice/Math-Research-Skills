## Main martingale normal-approximation bounds

Let $(\xi_i,\mathcal F_i)_{i=0}^n$ be martingale differences on an increasing filtration with trivial $\mathcal F_0$, and set $S_0=0$, $S_k=\sum_{i\le k}\xi_i$, and $\langle S\rangle_k=\sum_{i\le k}\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$. The array may depend on $n$. Conditions (A1)–(A2), stated on PDF p. 3, are:

- For some $\epsilon\in(0,1/2]$, all $k\ge2$ and $1\le i\le n$ satisfy
  \[
  \left|\mathbb E[\xi_i^k\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\right|\le \tfrac12 k!\epsilon^{k-2}\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\quad\text{a.s.}
  \]
- For some $\delta\in[0,1]$, $|\langle S\rangle_n-1|\le\delta^2$ a.s.

Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 5) gives, for every $x\in\mathbb R$,
\[
\left|\mathbb P(S_n\le x)-\Phi(x)\right|
\le C(1+x^2)\left(\epsilon|\log\epsilon|+\frac{\delta}{1+|x|}\right)e^{-\widehat x^2/2},
\qquad
\widehat x=\frac{2|x|/\sqrt{1+\delta^2}}{1+\sqrt{1+2|x|\epsilon/(1+\delta^2)}} ,
\]
where $C$ is an absolute constant. Theorem 2.3 (PDF p. 7) strengthens the upper tail: for $x\ge0$,
\[
\mathbb P(S_n>x)\le(1-\Phi(\widehat x))\left[1+C(1+\widehat x)(\lambda^2\epsilon+\lambda\delta^2+\epsilon|\log\epsilon|+\delta)\right],
\]
with $\widehat x$ as above and
\[
\lambda=\frac{2x/(1+\delta^2)}{1+2x\epsilon/(1+\delta^2)+\sqrt{1+2x\epsilon/(1+\delta^2)}}\in[0,\epsilon^{-1}).
\]
The cited normal-range consequence holds for $0\le x\le\min\{\epsilon^{-1/3},\delta^{-1}\}$, with relative error of order $(1+x^3)(\epsilon|\log\epsilon|+\delta/(1+x))$; no asymptotic equality is asserted outside that range.

If (A2) is unavailable, Corollary 2.2 (PDF p. 6; proof pp. 16–17) gives a nonuniform error with an additional bracket-fluctuation term:
\[
\left|\mathbb P(S_n\le x)-\Phi(x)\right|
\le C(1+x^2)\epsilon|\log\epsilon|e^{-\check x^2/2}
 +C\bigl(\mathbb E|\langle S\rangle_n-1|+\epsilon^2\bigr)^{1/3}e^{-x^2/6},
\quad \check x=\frac{2|x|}{1+\sqrt{1+2|x|\epsilon}}.
\]
The proof stops at the last time the predictable bracket is at most one, augments the probability space with independent Rademacher increments (and a final variance-filling increment) to obtain unit bracket, and transfers the bound back using an $L^2$ estimate, Chebyshev, and an optimized smoothing threshold. This is a separate result; it does not remove the bracket-error term.

## Proof mechanism and applications

Theorem 2.3 is proved by exponential change of measure. The positive multiplicative martingale
\[
Z_k(\lambda)=\prod_{i\le k}\frac{e^{\lambda\xi_i}}{\mathbb E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]}
\]
defines $d\mathbb P_\lambda=Z_n(\lambda)d\mathbb P$. Under $\mathbb P_\lambda$, decompose $S_k=Y_k(\lambda)+B_k(\lambda)$ into centered tilted differences and drift. Lemmas 4.1–4.2 bound the drift and predictable cumulant from (A1)–(A2); choose $\lambda$ by optimizing the cumulant exponent. Lemma 4.3 controls the tilted centered terminal sum by a normal law, then an integrated positive-tail comparison yields the sharpened Gaussian-tail factor. Theorem 2.1 follows by comparing $\widehat x$ with $|x|$, using the normal-tail bounds, and applying the argument to $-S$ for the opposite tail. The tilted CLT ingredient in Appendix A uses Lemma A.1 from Fan–Grama–Liu (2013), cited as reference [13]; that lemma is imported, not reproved here.

Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 8; proof p. 18) transfers Theorem 2.1 to a least-squares regression estimator. It assumes independent random design variables, a design sequence independent of the noise, martingale-difference errors with constant conditional variance $\sigma^2$, a maximum-leverage bound, and conditional Bernstein moments. The proof enlarges the filtration to reveal the whole design, normalizes the estimator error into martingale differences, verifies unit predictable bracket and (A1), and applies Theorem 2.1.

Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 9; proof pp. 18–19) treats independent, nondegenerate, symmetric $\xi_i$ under the self-normalized bound $|\xi_i|/\sqrt{[S]_n}\le\epsilon$ a.s., where $[S]_n=\sum_i\xi_i^2$. Reveal all squared increments at time zero and normalize by $\sqrt{[S]_n}$; conditional symmetry and $\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}$ give the exponential-mgf control. Its scope is this independent symmetric setting, not arbitrary dependent self-normalized martingales.

## Scope limits

The sharp exponential-in-$x$ nonuniform rate uses the conditional Bernstein moment condition and an almost-sure predictable-bracket bound. Without the latter, Corollary 2.2 retains a bracket-moment error; its second exponential is exactly $e^{-x^2/6}$, while only the first uses $\check x$ from (13). The paper's regression and self-normalized conclusions require their distinct design and symmetry assumptions. Its Cramér expansion is normal-range only; the bound on all $x$ is a Berry–Esseen inequality, not a relative tail expansion on all scales. Full physical-page coverage is in `reading_coverage.json`.
