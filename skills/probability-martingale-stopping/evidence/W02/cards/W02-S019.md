# W02-S019 — Rémillard & Vaillancourt, Central limit theorems for martingales-I: continuous limits

Reading evidence: all 18 pages of arXiv:2301.07267v3 (18 Jan 2024) read. The present card has been corrected against PDF pp. 2–5, not just the earlier summary.

## Standing setup and Hypothesis 2.1 (PDF p. 2)

The filtered probability space satisfies the usual conditions. For fixed dimension d, M_n=(M_n^i)_{i=1}^d is a sequence of square-integrable càdlàg F-martingales, M_n(0)=0. The matrix predictable bracket is A_n=⟨M_n⟩=(⟨M_n^i,M_n^j⟩)_{i,j}; this is distinct from the optional matrix quadratic variation [M_n].

Hypothesis 2.1 requires all of the following:

(a) For each fixed n≥1 and each coordinate i, A_n^{ii}(t)→∞ almost surely as t→∞.

(b) There is a D-valued matrix process A such that: (i) A_n converges to A in finite-dimensional distributions and A^{ij}=0 for every i≠j; (ii) for every t≥0 and every i, E A_n^{ii}(t)→E A^{ii}(t)<∞; (iii) for each i, A^{ii}(t)→∞ almost surely as t→∞.

These are part of the theorem’s hypotheses, not consequences. In particular the limiting bracket is diagonal; the coordinate martingales do not have a nonzero limiting cross-bracket under this theorem.

## Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 3)

Assume Hypothesis 2.1 and that A is continuous everywhere. Also require J_t(M_n) converges in law to zero for every t>0, where J_t is the largest Euclidean jump on [0,t]. There must exist an F-adapted sequence of D-valued square-integrable martingales B_n, B_n(0)=0, such that: (1) (A_n,B_n) converges weakly in D×D to (A,B), B is Brownian with respect to its natural filtration, and A is measurable with respect to the path sigma-field generated by B; and (2) for every i,j and t≥0, ⟨M_n^i,B_n^j⟩_t converges in law to zero.

Then (M_n,A_n,B_n) converges weakly to (M,A,B), where M is a continuous square-integrable martingale in the enlarged filtration and has predictable bracket A. Coordinatewise, M^i=W^i∘A^{ii}; W is a standard d-dimensional Brownian motion independent of B and A. PDF p. 3 also states the time-changed martingale setup and proof begins immediately; the proof continues through PDF pp. 3–5.

## Proof chain (PDF pp. 3–5)

1. Convergence of A_n to a continuous A and the bracket Cauchy–Schwarz increment inequality yield C-tightness of the bracket coordinates.
2. Compensator moment convergence, bounded-time increment estimates and the vanishing-largest-jump condition yield C-tightness of M_n and of the joint processes.
3. Apply Skorokhod representation along a subsequence and pass martingale identities to any limit using uniform integrability; the diagonal bracket identities pass using the expectation convergence in Hypothesis 2.1(b)(ii).
4. Pass the cross-bracket identities with B_n using Kunita–Watanabe bounds and the stated cross-bracket convergence. The limit M and B are continuous martingales with bracket A, bracket t for B, and zero cross-bracket.
5. Lévy characterization/orthogonal Brownian representation gives M=W∘A with W independent of B; because A is B-measurable, W is independent of A too. Every subsequential limit has the same law.

## A127 transfer boundary

This theorem is not a general vector martingale CLT with arbitrary correlated limiting bracket. If two leaf-height martingales in A127 have a nonzero limiting cross-bracket A^{12}, Hypothesis 2.1(b)(i) fails and Theorem 2.1 cannot be silently applied. Under the theorem's representation (M^i=W^i(A^{ii})), condition on the whole path A: the Brownian coordinates (W^i,W^j) are independent and centered, so for (i\ne j), \(\mathbb E[M_s^iM_t^j\mid A]=0\) for any fixed s,t. Hypothesis 2.1(b)(ii) gives finite \(\mathbb E A^{ii}(s)\) and \(\mathbb E A^{jj}(t)\), hence the coordinates are square-integrable at these times and their cross-covariance exists and is zero. This does **not** imply independence after mixing over random clocks. For example, if T is uniform on \(\{1,4\}\), independent of standard normals Z1,Z2, then X=\(\sqrt T Z_1\), Y=\(\sqrt T Z_2\) satisfy E[XY]=0 but E[X²Y²]=17/2 whereas E[X²]E[Y²]=25/4; the shared-clock mixture is dependent. This finite-mixture calculation is illustrative, not the conditional-expectation proof. For A127 one must establish the actual joint bracket limit and choose a CLT whose hypotheses accommodate it, in addition to proving the martingale approximation, random circular separation control, jump condition, and UI for the normalized covariance.
