## One-sided conditional moment condition and main expansion

Let $(\xi_i,\mathcal F_i)_{i=0}^n$ be square-integrable martingale differences, with $X_k=\sum_{i\le k}\xi_i$ and predictable bracket $\langle X\rangle_k=\sum_{i\le k}\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$; the triangular array may depend on $n$. Conditions (A1)–(A2), PDF p. 3, require fixed $\rho\in(0,1]$, $\epsilon_n\in(0,1/2]$, $\delta_n\in[0,1/2]$, and
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[|\xi_i|^{2+\rho}e^{\epsilon_n^{-1}\xi_i^+}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}\right]
\le \epsilon_n^\rho\,\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}],\qquad
|\langle X\rangle_n-1|\le\delta_n^2\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
This is one-sided exponential control: the positive part has the exponential moment, while the negative part is controlled only by the conditional $(2+\rho)$ moment. In particular (A1) need not imply a two-sided conditional Cramér condition. The paper also gives a sufficient route to (A1): an upper bound $\xi_i\le\gamma_n$ plus a conditional $(2+\rho)$-to-second-moment ratio bound, with the stated smallness assumptions (PDF p. 3, (2.3)).

Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 3–4) states that for some $\alpha>0$ and all $0\le x\le\alpha\epsilon_n^{-1}$,
\[
\left|\log\frac{\mathbb P(X_n>x)}{1-\Phi(x)}\right|
\le c_{\alpha,\rho}\left(x^{2+\rho}\epsilon_n^\rho+x^2\delta_n^2+(1+x)(\epsilon_n^\rho+\delta_n)\right),\quad 0<\rho<1,
\]
while for $\rho=1$ the bound is
\[
\left|\log\frac{\mathbb P(X_n>x)}{1-\Phi(x)}\right|
\le c_\alpha\left(x^3\epsilon_n+x^2\delta_n^2+(1+x)(\epsilon_n|\log\epsilon_n|+\delta_n)\right).
\]
The constants depend on the range parameter and, for $\rho<1$, on $\rho$. The extra $\epsilon_n|\log\epsilon_n|$ at $\rho=1$ is necessary under these hypotheses: the paper invokes Bolthausen’s bounded-difference example to rule out replacing it by $\epsilon_n$ (PDF p. 4).

Corollary 1 (PDF p. 4) gives relative-tail convergence only on the reduced ranges $x=o(\min\{\epsilon_n^{-\rho/(2+\rho)},\delta_n^{-1}\})$ for $0<\rho<1$, and $x=o(\min\{\epsilon_n^{-1/3},\delta_n^{-1}\})$ for $\rho=1$. Corollary 2 (PDF pp. 4–5) yields an MDP only for Borel sets in $[0,\infty)$, under $\max\{\epsilon_n,\delta_n\}\to0$, $a_n\to\infty$, and $a_n\epsilon_n\to0$, with rate function $x^2/2$. The authors explicitly warn that this half-side result does not give the usual MDP on negative-side Borel sets.

## Proof mechanism and applications

Section 4 (PDF pp. 9–12) uses the positive exponential martingale
\[
Z_k(\lambda)=\prod_{i\le k}\frac{e^{\lambda\xi_i}}{\mathbb E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]},\qquad d\mathbb P_\lambda=Z_n(\lambda)d\mathbb P,
\]
and decomposes $X_n=B_n(\lambda)+Y_n(\lambda)$ into tilted drift and a centered martingale. Under (A1)–(A2), Lemma 2 controls $|B_n(\lambda)-\lambda|$ by $\lambda\delta_n^2+c\lambda^{1+\rho}\epsilon_n^\rho$, and Lemma 3 controls the predictable log-cumulant around $\lambda^2/2$ by $c(\lambda^{2+\rho}\epsilon_n^\rho+\lambda^2\delta_n^2)$. Lemma 4 supplies a Berry–Esseen bound under the tilted measure, with the distinct $\rho<1$ and $\rho=1$ rates.

Theorem 1 is proved in Section 5 (PDF pp. 12–16) by matching the tilt to the level. Proposition 1 uses the upper-drift equation $\lambda+\lambda\delta_n^2+c\lambda^{1+\rho}\epsilon_n^\rho=x$, then integrates the tilted positive tail and compares it to $1-\Phi(\lambda)$ and $1-\Phi(x)$. Proposition 2 uses the lower-drift equation $\lambda-\lambda\delta_n^2-c\lambda^{1+\rho}\epsilon_n^\rho=x$ and splits into small- and large-$\lambda$ cases so that the tilted CLT error can be absorbed into a Gaussian-tail lower bound. Their combination gives the absolute logarithmic-ratio estimate in Theorem 1. The proof of Lemma 4 is not included: it is deferred to the supplemental article [13].

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 6; proof pp. 18–20) transfers the $\rho=1$ result to a quantile coupling with a Gaussian. Under the conditional two-sided Sakhanenko condition $\mathbb E[|\eta_i|^3e^{K|\eta_i|}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le L\mathbb E[\eta_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$ and a bounded total conditional-variance deviation, the quantile-coupled $W_n=H_n(\Phi(Z))$ obeys $\sqrt n|W_n-Z|/\log n\le2D(W_n^2+1)$ on the stated event $|W_n|\le\alpha\sqrt n$ for $n\ge n_0$; the paper also gives an exponential tail bound for the scaled coupling error. The proof combines a martingale exponential bound with Theorem 1 and Mason–Zhou's coupling theorem.

Theorem 3 (PDF p. 7; proof pp. 21–22) handles interlaced block sums of a possibly nonstationary $\beta$-mixing sequence. It assumes mean zero, a $(2+\rho)$ block-moment bound, a linear lower bound on block variances, stretched-exponential $\beta(n)\le a_1e^{-a_2n^\tau}$, and a one-sided increment bound $\eta_i\le c_3$. For $\rho\in(0,1)$, $m=\lfloor n^\alpha\rfloor$, $k=\lfloor n/(2m)\rfloor$, and $Y_j=\sum_{i=1}^m\eta_{2m(j-1)+i}$, the theorem gives a log tail-ratio bound of order $(1+x)^{2+\rho}/n^{\rho(1/2-\alpha)}$ on $0\le x=o(\min\{n^{1/2-\alpha},n^{\alpha\tau/2}\})$. The proof uses Berbee coupling to independent block copies, applies Theorem 1, and bounds the coupling/bracket error.

Theorem 4 (PDF p. 8; proof pp. 22–23) gives the $\psi$-mixing analogue under the same block moment/variance and one-sided upper-bound assumptions, with $\tau_n^2=\psi(m)+n\psi(m)^2+k\psi(m)^{1/2}\to0$. Its logarithmic tail-ratio error has the Theorem 1 block term plus $x^2\tau_n^2+(1+x)(n^{-\rho(1/2-\alpha)}+\tau_n)$ for $\rho<1$; for $\rho=1$ the block terms are $x^3/n^{1/2-\alpha}$ and $(1+x)\log n/n^{1/2-\alpha}$. The proof replaces the independent coupling with a covariance bound and a martingale-difference approximation; it supplies the proof for $\rho<1$ and says the $\rho=1$ case is similar.

## Scope limits

The main theorem is an upper-tail result under a one-sided exponential condition; it does not by itself control $\mathbb P(X_n<-x)$. The negative tail would require a corresponding assumption on $-\xi_i$. The bracket must be almost surely close to one. The coupling and mixing theorems have additional hypotheses and separate proof dependencies. The main tilted CLT lemma is deferred to the supplement, so this paper alone is not a self-contained proof of every step in Theorem 1. See `reading_coverage.json` for the complete 24-page map.
