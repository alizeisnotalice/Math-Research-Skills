## Countable stochastic-convolution maximum

Work in a 2-smooth Banach space $X$ with smoothness constant $D$, with contractive $C_0$-semigroups $S_j$ and progressive $\gamma(H_j,X)$-valued integrands $\psi_j$ that are stochastically integrable on $[0,T]$. Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 7–9) gives, for $p\in[1,\infty)$,

\[
\left\|\sup_{j\ge1}\sup_{t\le T}
\left\|\int_0^t S_j(t-s)\psi_j(s)\,dW_j(s)\right\|_X\right\|_{L^p}
\le 10D\left\|\sup_{j\ge1}\sqrt{p+\log j}
\left(\int_0^T\|\psi_j(s)\|_{\gamma(H_j,X)}^2ds\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^p}.
\]

The proof starts from an exponential tail estimate for a single stochastic convolution, obtains a three-stopping-time good-$\lambda$ inequality, applies a union bound over $j$, and then uses Burkholder's distribution-function lemma. The logarithmic factor records the countable family; the bound does not assert independence among the $W_j$.

For a sequence of zero-start $X$-valued discrete martingales $f_j(i)$ with conditionally symmetric increments, define

\[
d^*(f_j)=\sup_i\|\Delta f_j(i)\|_X,\qquad
s(f_j)=\left(\sum_i\mathbb E[\|\Delta f_j(i)\|_X^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Theorem 3.6 (PDF p. 10) states

\[
\left\|\sup_j f_j^*\right\|_{L^p}
\le13\left\|\sup_j(p+\log j)d^*(f_j)\right\|_{L^p}
+14D\left\|\sup_j\sqrt{p+\log j}\,s(f_j)\right\|_{L^p}.
\]

Here $f_j^*=\sup_i\|f_j(i)\|_X$. The first term has $p+\log j$, without a square root; the second has its square root. The proof uses a discrete Burkholder–Rosenthal good-$\lambda$ argument and conditional symmetry. Do not omit the starting-at-zero or conditional-symmetry assumptions.

## Parameter-indexed regularity and applications

Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 12–13) treats an exponentially stable semigroup, $\|S(t)\|\le e^{-at}$ for $a>0$, and gives a long-time Ornstein–Uhlenbeck stochastic-convolution estimate with factor $18D\sqrt{p+\log(1+aT)}a^{-1/2}$ multiplying $\left\|\sup_{t\le T}\|f(t)\|_{\gamma(H,X)}\right\|_{L^p}$. This is the theorem's printed general formula, visually checked on the original PDF p. 12. Its proof rescales to $a=1$, first treats integer $T$, and uses the countable-time estimate with $\log T$ internally; rounding the horizon and the theorem statement give the displayed $\log(1+aT)$ factor. The introduction's scalar Hilbert-space example has the same logarithmic scale but is not the source of the general theorem statement.

Sections 5–8 build admissible generalized moduli, relate Minkowski and doubling dimensions to a Ciesielski-type embedding, and derive a Kolmogorov–Chentsov theorem. In Theorem 8.2, a metric parameter space of finite Minkowski dimension $d$ and finite doubling dimension, $p>d$, and $d/p<\alpha<1$ yield a continuous modification with any $0<\beta<\alpha-d/p$, with the stated metric-dependent constant.

Theorem 9.1 (PDF pp. 23–25) assumes a 2-smooth $X$, cylindrical Brownian noise, a family of progressive $\gamma$-radonifying integrands indexed by a metric space of finite Minkowski dimension and finite doubling dimension, and the paper's generalized Hölder-norm integrability condition. It produces a continuous modification of $I_\Phi(x)=\int_0^T\Phi(x)(t)dW(t)$ and bounds the generalized Hölder seminorm by a constant depending on the metric/modulus times the integrand norm $K(\Phi)$. Theorem 10.1 (PDF pp. 26–27) applies this to the one-dimensional parabolic Anderson model for $p>4$ and $U_0\in L^p(\Omega;C^2([0,1]))$, deriving a logarithmically corrected space-time modulus; Appendix A supplies Green-kernel regularity estimates.

## Limits on use

The constants and geometry are for 2-smooth Banach spaces and the stated $\gamma$-radonifying stochastic-integral domains. The Hölder theorem needs its metric dimension, doubling and generalized-modulus hypotheses. These are not scalar sharp BDG constants, and the parabolic Anderson application is one-dimensional.
