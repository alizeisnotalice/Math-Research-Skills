## Setup: e-variables, test supermartingales, and two type-II errors

The general setup (Definition 2.1, PDF p. 5) is a filtered measurable space with simple null (P_0) and alternative (P_1), trivial (\mathcal F_0), and (P_1\ll P_0) on every finite (\mathcal F_t), with finite (\mathrm{KL}(P_1|_{\mathcal F_t}\|P_0|_{\mathcal F_t})) (Assumption 2.3, PDF p. 6). The i.i.d. model (Definition 2.2, PDF p. 6) is invoked only where explicitly stated. An e-variable satisfies (E_{P_0}E\le1); a conditional e-variable satisfies (E_{P_0}[E_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le1). Their product (W_0=1, W_t=\prod_{n\le t}E_n) is a nonnegative (P_0)-supermartingale (Definitions 2.7–2.9 and Lemma 2.10, PDF p. 7).

For (\ell=\log(1/\alpha)), the rejection time is (\tau_\alpha=\inf\{t:W_t>1/\alpha\}). Ville's inequality gives (P_0(\tau_\alpha<\infty)\le\alpha) (Theorem 2.12, PDF p. 7). The fixed-horizon type-II error is (\gamma_t(\alpha)=P_1(W_t\le1/\alpha)); the sequential error is (\bar\gamma_t(\alpha)=P_1(\tau_\alpha>t)=P_1(\max_{n\le t}W_n\le1/\alpha)\), with (\bar\gamma_t\le\gamma_t) (Definition 2.13 and Lemma 2.14, PDF pp. 7–8). This distinction is used throughout.

## Main findings and proof chains

**Mean e-power alone gives no uniform finite-horizon type-II guarantee.** Theorem 3.7 (PDF pp. 3, 12–13) constructs, for every (c>0,\alpha\in(0,1),\eta\in(0,1)), and (t\in\mathbb N), a pair of simple hypotheses and a betting sequence with (E_{P_1}[\log E_n\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]=c) exactly at every step, yet (\bar\gamma_t(\alpha)\ge1-\eta). The construction makes the first bet catastrophically negative on an event of alternative probability (1-\eta), with crash size (M=(t-1)\tilde u+\ell); later increments cannot recover past the threshold by time (t). Each bet remains an exact conditional e-variable under (P_0). The failure is uniform over testing problems; Remark 3.8 (PDF pp. 14–15) explicitly does not rule out model-dependent bounds. This does not contradict eventual rejection for a fixed i.i.d. e-variable with positive e-power: Proposition 3.10 uses the strong law (PDF p. 15). Theorem 3.7 and Proposition 3.10 therefore have different quantifiers.

**A lower-tail exponent controls the error.** Define (\Lambda_s(E)=-\log E_{P_1}[E^{-s}]), (\Lambda(E)=\sup_{s\ge0}\Lambda_s(E)), and (\Lambda_s^{(t)}=-t^{-1}\log E_{P_1}[W_t^{-s}]), (\Lambda^{(t)}=\sup_s\Lambda_s^{(t)}) (Definition 2.18, PDF p. 8). For any test supermartingale, Markov's inequality gives

\[
\bar\gamma_t(\alpha)\le\gamma_t(\alpha)\le\exp\{s\ell-t\Lambda_s^{(t)}\}\qquad(s\ge0)
\]

(Lemma 2.22, PDF p. 9). In the i.i.d. product case, Proposition 2.23 identifies (\Lambda(E)) with the fixed-horizon type-II exponent under the additional conditions (E_{P_1}|\log E|<\infty), positive e-power, and 0 interior to the effective Cramér-rate domain; only the lower bound on the sequential exponent is asserted there (PDF pp. 9–10).

**Conditional Hölder gives the exact exponent ceiling.** In the i.i.d. model put (R=dP_1/dP_0), (Z_\beta=E_{P_0}[R^\beta]), and (\lambda(\beta)=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_\beta=D_{1-\beta}(P_0\|P_1)), for (0<\beta\le1). The flattened e-variable is (R_\beta=R^\beta/Z_\beta). For (s>0), set (\beta=1/(1+s)). Theorem 4.10 (PDF p. 19) proves

\[
\Lambda_s(E)\le\lambda(\beta),
\quad\text{with equality iff }E=R_\beta\;P_0\text{-a.s.}
\]

by factoring (E^{1-\beta}(RE^{-s})^\beta=R^\beta), applying Hölder with conjugate exponents (1/(1-\beta),1/\beta), using (E_{P_0}E\le1), and then analyzing the equality case. The arbitrary-filtration step (Theorem 4.9, PDF pp. 18–19) conditions Hölder on (\mathcal F_{n-1}), uses the one-step change-of-measure identity (E_{P_1}[Y\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]=E_{P_0}[R_nY\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]), and obtains (E_{P_1}[E_n^{-s}\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]\ge e^{-\lambda_n(\beta)}). Proposition 4.11 (PDF pp. 20–21) multiplies these bounds step by step; equality characterization in the i.i.d. model is only (P_1)-a.s. in general, strengthened to (P_0)-a.s. only under (P_0\sim P_1) (Remark 4.12, PDF p. 21).

As (\beta\downarrow0), (\lambda(\beta)\to\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_1)). Corollary 4.13 gives the arbitrary-filtered-space ceiling (\Lambda^{(t)}\le t^{-1}\sum_{n\le t}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{P_1}\mathrm{KL}_n), where (\mathrm{KL}_n=-E_{P_0}[\log R_n\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]) (PDF p. 21). In the i.i.d. model, Theorem 4.25 (PDF p. 25) proves

\[
\sup_{W}\Lambda^{(t)}=\sup_{E\in\mathcal E}\Lambda(E)=\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_1)
\]

for each finite (t), with the supremum approached as (\beta\downarrow0) by the flattened family (R_\beta). If (P_0\sim P_1), (\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_1)<\infty), and (P_0\ne P_1), no test supermartingale attains the ceiling at any finite (t) (Propositions 4.24–4.25, PDF p. 25). The proof combines the conditional Hölder upper bound, the explicit flattened lower-bound witness, and strict decrease of the Rényi profile; non-attainment additionally uses finite-tilt attainment for nonconstant wealth. Proposition 4.15 (PDF p. 22) separately uses the Chernoff–Stein lemma to show that in the i.i.d. model the asymptotic exponent of either type-II error cannot exceed (\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_1)). These are exponent results, not finite-(t) equality of type-II errors.

**At a chosen finite horizon, the best flattening depends on (t).** Under finite (\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_1)), finite (\mathrm{KL}(P_1\|P_0)), and nonconstant (\log R), Proposition 5.5 (PDF p. 29) gives the exact error of the i.i.d. (R_\beta) design:

\[
\gamma_t(\alpha)=P_1(G_t\le B_t(\beta)),\quad
G_t=\sum_{n\le t}\log R(X_n),\quad B_t(\beta)=\ell/\beta-t\lambda(\beta).
\]

Thus minimizing $B_t$ minimizes the exact fixed-horizon error over this family, but the error minimizer need not be unique because $P_1(G_t\le B_t)$ is only nondecreasing in its threshold. Theorem 5.6 (PDF pp. 29–31) gives a unique threshold minimizer $\beta^*(t)$ when $\ell/t$ lies in the range of $g$, characterized by $g(\beta^*(t))=\ell/t$, where $g(\beta)=\beta\kappa'(\beta)-\kappa(\beta)$ and $\kappa=\log Z$. The growth-optimal design $R$ ($\beta=1$) is the threshold minimizer exactly at $t=\ell/\mathrm{KL}(P_1\|P_0)$; on either side its threshold is strictly beaten by sharpening ($\beta^*>1$) or flattening ($\beta^*<1$). Its exact error is weakly beaten there, and is strictly beaten only when $P_1$ charges $(B_t(\beta^*(t)),B_t(1)]$ (Remark 5.7, PDF p. 31). The remark's lattice example has threshold crossover at $t=148.8$, tied exact errors for integer $t=149,\ldots,191$, first strict improvement at $t=192$, and further isolated ties. If an interior minimizer does not exist, the infimum is approached at the endpoint of the admissible $\beta$-range. For the asymptotic rate, the theorem additionally assumes $E_{P_0}[R^{-\delta}]<\infty$ for some $\delta>0$ and $V_0=\mathrm{Var}_{P_0}(\log R)\in(0,\infty)$; it then states $\beta^*(t)\sim\sqrt{2\ell/(tV_0)}$ and $\gamma_t(\alpha)\le\exp\{-t\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_1)+\sqrt{2\ell tV_0}+O(1)\}$. The proof needs a genuine two-sided neighborhood of 0 in the cumulant generating function to justify the $O(1)$ remainder. A general matching Bahadur–Rao second-order expansion is explicitly conjectured/left unproved (Remark 5.9, PDF p. 31).

For a separate useful finite-horizon lower bound, Lemma 5.10 (PDF p. 32) assumes an i.i.d. design with (c=E_{P_1}\log E>0), (v=\mathrm{Var}_{P_1}(\log E)<\infty), and (c\ell>2v); it gives (\bar\gamma_t(\alpha)\ge1-2v/(c\ell)) whenever (t\le\ell/(2c)). The proof centers the log-wealth random walk and applies Kolmogorov's maximal inequality. This is not a bound from e-power alone: finite variance is essential to this stated route.

## Appendices and scope limits

Appendix A (PDF pp. 38–44) extends selected results to arbitrary filtrations. Conditional exponent sharpness requires Assumption A.8, that the conditional Rényi integrals are deterministic at each step; without it the average essential-supremum ceiling is an upper bound whose sharpness is open (Remarks 4.14 and 6.4). An information-spectrum hypothesis is separately needed to bind the actual type-II exponent in the general filtered case (Proposition A.19). Appendix B (PDF pp. 45–47) restates cited tools—Ville, Hölder, conditional Hölder, Kolmogorov, Cramér, Chernoff–Stein, Rényi monotonicity, and variational characterization—rather than proving the cited source results there. The article is an oracle result for simple (P_0,P_1); its flattened designs require the true (R), and the composite-alternative case is not addressed (Remark 6.1, PDF p. 35). The sequential asymptotic exponent is not generally identified, and the general non-i.i.d. sharpness, exact second-order behavior, and general Bahadur–Rao converse remain open/unstated (Remarks 6.2–6.4, PDF pp. 35–36).
