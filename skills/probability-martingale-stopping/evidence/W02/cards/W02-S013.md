## Theorem and assumptions

Let $(\xi_i,\mathcal F_i)_{i=0}^n$ be martingale differences with $\mathcal F_0$ trivial, $X_k=\sum_{i\le k}\xi_i$, and predictable quadratic characteristic $\langle X\rangle_k=\sum_{i\le k}\mathbb E[\xi_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$. The triangular array may depend on $n$. Conditions (A1)–(A2), PDF pp. 2–3, require constants $c_0,c_1>0$ and $\delta\ge0$ such that
\[
\sup_{1\le i\le n}\mathbb E[e^{c_0\sqrt n|\xi_i|}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le c_1\quad\text{a.s.},\qquad
\|\langle X\rangle_n-1\|_\infty\le\delta^2\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 3) says that there is a positive absolute $\alpha_0$ such that for all $0\le x\le\alpha_0\sqrt n$ and $\delta\le\alpha_0$,
\[
\frac{\mathbb P(X_n>x)}{1-\Phi(x)}
=\exp\!\left\{\theta c_{\alpha_0}\!\left(\frac{x^3}{\sqrt n}+x^2\delta^2+(1+x)\left(\frac{\log n}{\sqrt n}+\delta\right)\right)\right\},
\]
and the analogous lower-tail ratio $\mathbb P(X_n<-x)/\Phi(-x)$ has the same form; $|\theta|\le1$. The constant $c_{\alpha_0}$ depends on the chosen range parameter. In particular the normal relative-tail approximation follows uniformly for $0\le x=o(\min\{n^{1/6},\delta^{-1}\})$ as $\min\{n,\delta^{-1}\}\to\infty$. This is an expansion with an explicit error in the logarithmic ratio, not a bound valid with vanishing relative error over the full $O(\sqrt n)$ range.

Under $\delta\to0$, $a_n\to\infty$, and $a_n n^{-1/2}\to0$, the paper states the MDP for every Borel set $B\subset\mathbb R$ (PDF p. 3, immediately after (9)): with speed $a_n^2$ and good rate $I(x)=x^2/2$, the lower bound uses $\inf_{x\in B^o}I(x)$ and the upper bound uses $\inf_{x\in\overline B}I(x)$. Equations (6) and (7) separately control positive and negative tails. There is no restriction to $[0,\infty)$ and no warning here that negative-side sets fail.

## Proof route and dependency boundary

Section 3, “Sketch of the proof” (PDF pp. 3–5), introduces the positive exponential martingale
\[
Z_k(\lambda)=\prod_{i\le k}\frac{e^{\lambda\xi_i}}{\mathbb E[e^{\lambda\xi_i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]},
\qquad d\mathbb P_\lambda=Z_n(\lambda)d\mathbb P,
\]
for $|\lambda|\le c_0\sqrt n$. Under the tilted law it defines $b_i(\lambda)=\mathbb E_\lambda[\xi_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]$, $\eta_i(\lambda)=\xi_i-b_i(\lambda)$, and decomposes $X_n=B_n(\lambda)+Y_n(\lambda)$. Lemma 3.1 (PDF p. 4) obtains conditional moment bounds from (A1). Lemmas 3.2–3.3 (stated p. 4) control the tilted drift and predictable log-cumulant; Lemma 3.4 (p. 4) supplies a Berry–Esseen bound for the tilted martingale. The proof of Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 5) is a short reduction to Fan–Grama–Liu (2013), replacing that source’s lemmas by Lemmas 3.2–3.4 here. The paper does not reproduce the complete tail-ratio proof or the proof of Lemma 3.4.

The two additional detailed proofs are printed after the acknowledgements/references: the proof of Lemma 3.2 is PDF p. 6 and uses the conditional moment series, the bracket error (A2), and bounds on the tilt normalizer; the proof of Lemma 3.3 is PDF p. 7 and uses a second-order expansion of $\log(1+u)$ plus the same conditional moment bounds. The bibliography is on PDF p. 5. The note has only Sections 1, 2, and 3; it contains no beta-mixing or psi-mixing application and no Sections 3.2–3.3.

## Scope limits

The conditional Cramér assumption controls $|\xi_i|$ on both sides and is distinct from S014’s one-sided Sakhanenko condition. S013 has a two-sided tail expansion and an MDP for all Borel subsets of $\mathbb R$; S014 instead proves a positive-tail expansion and its stated “half-side” MDP on $[0,\infty)$. Bracket closeness in S013 is an almost-sure condition. This short note does not contain the complete proof of its main theorem or the proof of Lemma 3.4; it points to the 2013 article for those details.
