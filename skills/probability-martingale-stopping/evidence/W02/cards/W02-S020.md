## Semimartingale representation and stochastic integration

For a predictable function \(\xi\) in the universal class \(U\), compatible with a \(\mathbb C^m\)-valued semimartingale \(X\) (meaning \(\xi(\Delta X)\) is finite-valued a.s.), the representation is
\[
\xi\circ X=D\xi(0)\!\bullet X+\tfrac12D^2\xi(0)\!\bullet[X,X]^c+
(\xi-D\xi(0)\,\widehat{\mathrm{id}})\!\ast\mu^X
\]
with real-coordinate lifts for complex-valued processes. Here \(U\) requires zero value at the origin, twice real differentiability there, locally bounded first and second derivatives, and a locally bounded second-order remainder near zero (Definition 2.2–2.3, PDF p. 8). Proposition 2.6 (p. 8) recovers ordinary locally bounded predictable stochastic integration: for locally bounded predictable \(\zeta\), \(\zeta\mathrm{id}\in U\) and \(\zeta\bullet X=(\zeta\mathrm{id})\circ X\). Thus the integral is defined under the standard semimartingale-integrability condition for the integrand; local boundedness is a sufficient condition used here, not a claim that every predictable integrand is integrable.

For smooth \(f\), Proposition 2.7 gives the Itô change-of-variables representation; compositions remain in \(U\) and satisfy \(\psi\circ(\xi\circ X)=\psi(\xi)\circ X\) when both sides are compatible (Theorem 2.8, pp. 9–10). Drift evaluation uses the predictable characteristics and a truncation function; at predictable jump times the compensator increment is \(\mathbb E_{t-}[\xi_t(\Delta X_t)]\), while the quasi-left-continuous component is handled by the Itô-type formula (Proposition 2.12 and Remark 2.13, pp. 9–10).

## Theorem 3.1: multiplicative compensator

Let \(Z\) be a complex-valued semimartingale absorbed at zero if it hits zero. Let \(\tau_Z^c\) be the first continuous hit of zero (the open stochastic interval is handled via an announcing sequence). Assume the stochastic logarithm \(L(Z)\) is special on \([[0,\tau_Z^c[[\) and
\[
\Delta B^{L(Z)}\ne-1\quad\text{on }[[0,\tau_Z^c[[.
\]
Then \(\mathcal E(B^{L(Z)})\) does not vanish there and
\[
M=\frac{Z}{\mathcal E(B^{L(Z)})}\,1_{[[0,\tau_Z^c[[}
\]
is a local martingale on that stochastic interval (Theorem 3.1(1), PDF p. 11). If additionally \(M\) is special, it is a local martingale on the whole positive line (part (2)); a sufficient example given is that \(Z\) is special and \(\liminf_{t\uparrow\tau_Z^c}|\mathcal E(B^{L(Z)})_t|>0\) on \(\{\tau_Z^c<\infty\}\). The specialness condition on \(M\) cannot be dropped for the global conclusion: Examples 3.8 construct a case where the interval-local martingale fails to be a global local martingale, and even one where \(L(Z)\) is special before continuous zero but \(Z\) itself is not special (pp. 13–14).

Proof dependency: use \(L(\mathcal E(B^{L(Z)}))=B^{L(Z)}\), so the stochastic logarithm of the ratio has zero predictable drift. The drift formula for represented processes (Proposition 2.12) and the jump restriction \(\Delta B^{L(Z)}\ne-1\) make the compensator cancel; hence the ratio is a local martingale. This excludes the value \(-1\), which prevents the denominator \(1+\Delta B^{L(Z)}\) from vanishing; it is not an order condition, since the source permits complex-valued processes. To extend across \(\tau_Z^c\), specialness permits localization and passage of conditional expectations to the limit (p. 11). If \(Z\) can jump to zero, the multiplicative compensator after that jump is not unique (Remark 3.4, p. 12).

## Girsanov transfer and independent-increment case

Theorem 5.4 (pp. 22–23) assumes \(Z_0=1\), \(L(Z)\) special before \(\tau_Z^c\), \(\Delta B^{L(Z)}\ne-1\), and that \(M=Z/\mathcal E(B^{L(Z)})\,1_{[[0,\tau_Z^c[[}\) is a real-valued nonnegative uniformly integrable martingale. Define \(dQ/dP=M_\infty\). For a P-semimartingale \(V\), Q-specialness is equivalent to P-specialness of the density-weighted process \(VM\), and also to P-specialness of \(V+[V,L(Z)]\) on the specified stopped intervals. The Q compensator satisfies
\[
B^V_Q=\frac{1}{1+\Delta B^{L(Z)}}\!\bullet B^{V+[V,L(Z)]}
\]
on \([[0,\tau_Z]]\cap[[0,\tau_Z^c[[\); corresponding local-martingale equivalences hold (Theorem 5.4). The proof is a composition of the standard density Girsanov theorem, integration by parts for \(VM\), and semimartingale-representation composition (pp. 22–23). These stopping intervals and the nonnegative UI density are part of the theorem, not cosmetic conditions.

For a semimartingale \(Y\) with independent increments, Theorem 4.1 (pp. 14–16) states that before the deterministic first time \(\tau\) at which \(P(\Delta Y_t=-1)=1\), \(Y_T\) is special iff \(\mathbb E|\mathcal E(Y)_T|<\infty\); in that case \(\mathbb E[\mathcal E(Y)_t]=\mathcal E(B^Y)_t\) up to \(T\). On \([[0,\tau[[\), specialness iff finite absolute expectation at every time, with the same expectation identity, and \(\mathcal E(Y)\) is a local martingale iff it is a martingale. The proof uses independent increments to factor expectations and construct a normalized martingale, then Theorem 3.1 (pp. 15–16). This is the setting where the compensator is deterministic and yields actual expectations; a general local martingale alone need not have this property.

## Transfer limits

Use the representation calculus to simplify stochastic-integral and jump-compensator calculations when the integrability, compatibility, and predictable-characteristic assumptions are available. Do not infer that every stochastic integral is covered by the locally bounded case, that a local martingale is a true martingale, or that a pre-zero interval result extends beyond continuous hitting of zero. The filtration is right-continuous but need not be complete; some debut times in the paper are only treated up to an almost surely equal stopping/predictable time (PDF p. 7). Complex-valued bracket and derivative expressions use the paper’s real-coordinate convention and should not be copied as scalar real formulas without reduction.
