## Discrete-time decomposition

Assume $\mathcal F_0$ is trivial and for every $k\ge1$ there is an $\mathcal F_k$-measurable uniform $U_k$, independent of $\mathcal F_{k-1}$; the paper identifies this with conditional atomlessness of $\mathcal F_k$ given $\mathcal F_{k-1}$. For an $\mathbb R^m$-valued $L^2$ martingale $X$ with $X_0=0$, Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 1) constructs $L^2$ martingales $Z^n$, $Z^n_0=0$, such that $X_k=\sum_n Z^n_k$, each increment $Z^n_k-Z^n_{k-1}$ is independent of $\mathcal F_{k-1}$, and for each fixed $n$ the increments over $k$ form an independent system. The series converges in $L^2$ at each $k$, with energy identity

\[
\|X_k\|_2^2=\sum_n\|Z^n_k\|_2^2.
\]

For each fixed time $k$, the proof invokes the construction from [4] to write $X_k-X_{k-1}=\sum_n Y_k^n$ in $L^2$, where each $Y_k^n$ is $\mathcal F_k$-measurable, independent of $\mathcal F_{k-1}$, and has conditional mean zero; it states $\|X_k-X_{k-1}\|_2^2=\sum_n\|Y_k^n\|_2^2$. Setting $Z_k^n=\sum_{s\le k}Y_s^n$ gives the martingale components and the theorem’s energy identity. A successive conditional-expectation/characteristic-function calculation proves that, for each fixed $n$, the sequence $(Y_k^n)_k$ is independent across time. This proof does not claim independence across $n$. The best-independent-approximation theorem and its residual-energy identities are quoted from the earlier paper [4], not proved in this note; I do not infer an additional cross-$n$ conditional-independence or orthogonality assertion.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 2), the best-independent-$L^2$-approximation lemma that powers the construction, is explicitly attributed to the author's earlier paper [4]; this note states it and lists its consequences but does not prove the lemma. Its assumptions include conditional atomlessness and zero conditional mean. Do not present that lemma as proved here.

## Closed martingales and Brownian representation

Section 2 assumes a bounded $L^2$ martingale, hence a terminal $X_\infty$ with $X_k=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_k]$. Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 3–4) proves $X_\infty=\sum_n Z^n_\infty$ in $L^2$ and preserves its squared norm, using the preceding increment decomposition and an $\varepsilon$-tail argument. Conditional atomlessness remains in force.

For the natural filtration of one-dimensional Brownian motion on $[0,1]$, Proposition 3.2 identifies a scalar $L^2$ martingale with independent increments as one of the form

\[
dY_t=\phi_t f(t)\,dW_t,
\]

where $f\in L^2[0,1]$ is deterministic and nonnegative and $\phi$ is predictable with $|\phi|=1$; the converse gives Gaussian independent increments. Theorem 3.4 (PDF p. 6) then represents any scalar $L^2$ martingale in the Brownian filtration, starting at zero, as an $L^2$-convergent sum of Gaussian martingales of this form, preserving terminal $L^2$ energy. The proof invokes the Brownian martingale representation theorem and applies the conditional-atomless $L^2$ decomposition to the predictable integrand.

Theorem 5.4 (PDF p. 10) is the $d$-dimensional analogue: $dY^n_t=f_n(t)O_n(t)dB_t$, with deterministic symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix $f_n\in L^2[0,1]$, predictable orthogonal-matrix process $O_n$, and an $L^2$-convergent sum with energy equality; each $O_n(t)dB_t$ is a $d$-dimensional Brownian motion. The proof uses the Kunita–Watanabe representation and a matrix polar-decomposition argument. The paper notes that the summands need not be mutually independent (Section 4, PDF p. 7).

## Limits on use

This is an $L^2$ structural decomposition under atomless innovations, not a distribution-free martingale inequality, not a general predictable-representation theorem, and not a claim that the component martingales are mutually independent. The Brownian results are tied to the natural Brownian filtration on the specified finite horizon. Consult `reading_coverage.json` for the complete page map.
