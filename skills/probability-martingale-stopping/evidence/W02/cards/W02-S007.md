## Setup and Theorem 2.1: general conditional exponential normalizer

The paper works in discrete time with real supermartingale differences (xi_i,F_i) for i=1,...,n: xi_i is F_i-measurable, E[xi_i|F_{i-1}]≤0, and F_0 is trivial in the stated setup. Let S_k=sum_{i=1}^k xi_i, predictable conditional-square sum <S>_k=sum_{i=1}^k E[xi_i^2|F_{i-1}], and realized square sum [S]_k=sum_{i=1}^k xi_i^2 (PDF p. 1).

Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 4–5) assumes nonnegative F_{i-1}-measurable V_{i-1}, a fixed lambda>0, and nonnegative functions f(lambda), g(lambda), with for every i=1,...,n
E[exp{lambda xi_i-g(lambda)xi_i^2}|F_{i-1}]≤1+f(lambda)V_{i-1}.

For x,v,w>0 it gives the finite-horizon simultaneous crossing bound

P{there is k in {1,...,n}: S_k≥x, [S]_k≤v^2, sum_{i=1}^k V_{i-1}≤w}
≤ exp{-lambda x+g(lambda)v^2+n log(1+f(lambda)w/n)}
≤ exp{-lambda x+g(lambda)v^2+f(lambda)w}.

The theorem is a conditional-mgf criterion; it does not require V to be the conditional variance, and the exponent parameter is any fixed value for which the displayed condition holds. It controls an upper crossing with a realized-square budget and a separate accumulated V-budget. The bound is one-sided.

### Proof mechanism

The proof (PDF pp. 13–14) forms the nonnegative multiplicative likelihood martingale

Z_k(lambda)=product_{i=1}^k exp{lambda xi_i-g(lambda)xi_i^2}/E[exp{lambda xi_i-g(lambda)xi_i^2}|F_{i-1}],

stops it at the first index satisfying all three event constraints, and uses Z_{T wedge n} as a change-of-measure density. On T=k, the inverse density contributes exp{-lambda S_k+g(lambda)[S]_k+Xi_k(lambda)}, where Xi_k is the sum of conditional log-normalizers. Jensen and the hypothesis bound this sum by k log(1+(f(lambda)/k)sum_{i≤k}V_{i-1}); the event constraints and k≤n then yield the n log(1+f(lambda)w/n) bound. Finally log(1+u)≤u gives the simpler exponential bound. This is a finite discrete stopping/change-of-measure proof, not a continuous-time or optional-stopping statement at an unbounded horizon.

## Concentration corollaries and their distinct assumptions

- **Corollary 2.2 (PDF p. 5; proof pp. 14–15):** Assume E[(xi_i^-)^3]<∞ and set L_k=sum_{i≤k}E[(xi_i^-)^3|F_{i-1}]. Then for x,v,w>0,
  P{there is k≤n: S_k≥x, [S]_k≤v^2, L_k≤w}
  ≤ exp{-lambda_* x+lambda_*^2 v^2/2+lambda_*^3 w/3},
  where lambda_*=2x/(v^2+sqrt(v^4+4wx)). The proof inserts the pointwise inequality exp{z-z^2/2}≤1+z+(z^-)^3/3 into Theorem 2.1 using g(lambda)=lambda^2/2, f(lambda)=lambda^3/3 and V_{i-1}=E[(xi_i^-)^3|F_{i-1}]. It then compares the optimized expression with the paper's Bennett/Bernstein bounds B_1 and B_2. No beta-moment theorem is asserted here.
- **Corollary 2.3 (PDF pp. 5–6; proof pp. 15–16):** Under the lower-increment condition xi_i≥−1, the upper crossing with [S]_k≤v^2 has probability at most (1+x/v^2)^{v^2}e^{-x}, and hence at most the paper's B_1(x,1,v) and B_2(x,1,v). This uses a lower bound on increments and a realized-square budget; it is different from Freedman's usual upper-increment/conditional-variance form.
- **Corollary 2.4 (PDF pp. 5–6; proof p. 16):** Under the conditional Bernstein condition E[xi_i^ell|F_{i-1}]≤(ell! epsilon^{ell-2}/2)E[xi_i^2|F_{i-1}] for every integer ell≥2, use <S>_k as the budget. With lambda_*=(2x/v^2)/(2x epsilon/v^2+1+sqrt(1+2x epsilon/v^2)) in (0,epsilon^{-1}), the bound is exp{-lambda_*x+n log(1+lambda_*^2v^2/[2n(1-lambda_*epsilon)])}, then the usual Bennett bound B_1(x,epsilon,v). The finite-n logarithmic factor is retained in the sharper first bound.
- **Corollary 2.5 (PDF pp. 6–7; proof pp. 16–17):** For conditionally symmetric differences, define V_k^2(y)=sum_{i≤k}E[xi_i^2 1{|xi_i|≤y}|F_{i-1}]. For y>0 and v^2≤ny^2, the crossing probability with V_k^2(y)≤v^2 is bounded by either of the paper's optimized cosh-exponential expressions plus P(max_{i≤n}xi_i>y). This is a truncation of the increments; the large positive-tail event remains explicit. Do not remove it or assume bounded jumps unless separately justified.
- **Theorem 2.6 (PDF p. 7; proof pp. 17–18):** If positive F_{i-1}-measurable V_{i-1} satisfy E[e^{lambda xi_i}|F_{i-1}]≤exp{f(lambda)V_{i-1}} for a fixed lambda>0, then P{there is k≤n:S_k≥x, sum_{i≤k}V_{i-1}≤v^2}≤exp{-lambda x+f(lambda)v^2}. This is an accumulated intrinsic-budget crossing bound and has no [S]_k condition.
- **Corollary 2.7 (PDF pp. 7–8; proof pp. 18–19):** If xi_i≤U_{i-1} for nonnegative F_{i-1}-measurable U_{i-1}, set C_{i-1}^2=E[xi_i^2|F_{i-1}] when E[xi_i^2|F_{i-1}]≥U_{i-1}^2, and otherwise C_{i-1}^2=(U_{i-1}+E[xi_i^2|F_{i-1}]/U_{i-1})^2/4. Then E[e^{lambda xi_i}|F_{i-1}]≤exp{lambda^2 C_{i-1}^2/2} and
  P{there is k≤n:S_k≥x, sum_{i≤k}C_{i-1}^2≤v^2}≤exp{-x^2/(2v^2)}.
  If E[xi_i^2|F_{i-1}]≥U_{i-1}^2 for every i, C_{i-1}^2 is just the conditional second moment and the result can be stated with <S>_n≤v^2. The proof uses a conditional mgf lemma derived from the one-sided upper bound and second moment, followed by Theorem 2.6 and optimization over lambda. The variance proxy is not automatically the conditional variance in the other branch.

## Applications and proof coverage

Section 3 (PDF pp. 9–13) applies the inequalities to least-squares estimates in linear regression (Theorems 3.1–3.4), autoregressive estimates (Theorems 3.5–3.6), and a Galton–Watson offspring-mean estimator (Theorems 3.8–3.9). The proof strategy represents each estimation error as a martingale sum, verifies the matching conditional Bernstein, sub-Gaussian, or one-sided-increment condition, and invokes Corollary 2.4 or 2.7. The regression results include a conditional-Bernstein bound and a one-sided bounded-noise Gaussian tail; the autoregression/branching conclusions retain their stated predictable or population-dependent budgets.

Section 4 (PDF pp. 13–17) proves Theorem 2.1 and Corollaries 2.2–2.5 by change of measure, the cubic negative-part inequality, a scalar lower-increment lemma, conditional Bernstein series, and symmetric truncation. Section 5 (pp. 17–19) proves Theorem 2.6 and Corollary 2.7 via the same stopped exponential process and a one-sided bounded-increment conditional mgf estimate. Section 6 (pp. 19–21) proves the regression and autoregression claims by explicit martingale-difference representations and applies the corollaries conditionally to the branching estimator. References occupy pp. 21–22.

## Transfer boundaries

Choose the result by matching both the crossing budget and the conditional exponential assumption: Theorem 2.1 uses [S] plus a separate V-sum, Corollary 2.4 uses predictable conditional squares under a Bernstein moment condition, Corollary 2.5 requires conditional symmetry and retains a large-jump probability, and Corollary 2.7 uses its piecewise C^2 proxy under a one-sided upper increment bound. These are not interchangeable. The arguments are finite and discrete-time; they do not imply continuous-time inequalities without an appropriate theorem. The paper does not state S010's G_k^y/B_1/B_2 truncation theorem or its 1<beta<2 moment theorem.
