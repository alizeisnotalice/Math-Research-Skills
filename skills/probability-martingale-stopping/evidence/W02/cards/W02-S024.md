# W02-S024 — Bañuelos & Osękowski, *Stability in Burkholder’s Differentially Subordinate Martingales Inequalities and Applications to Fourier Multipliers*

## Identity, setup, and reading evidence

The local source is arXiv:1609.08672v1, 42 physical pages. I read pp. 1–42 and visually checked original PDF pp. 5–9 and 37–38 for the transform statements, constants, Bellman hypotheses, and Riesz-transform representation. Hash is recorded in `sources.json`; independent review is pending.

The martingale framework is a complete probability space with filtration satisfying the usual conditions; \(X,Y\) are adapted càdlàg martingales with values in a separable Hilbert space \(H\). The square bracket is coordinate-summed, \([X,X]=\sum_j[X^j,X^j]\), and \(\|X\|_p=\sup_{t\ge0}\|X_t\|_{L^p}\). Differential subordination means \([X,X]-[Y,Y]\) is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Orthogonality means \([X,Y]\) is constant. When terminal values appear, the hypotheses include Lp-boundedness so the terminal limits exist.

## Sharp Burkholder bounds

For \(p^*=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\}\), Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 7) gives
\[
\|Y\|_p\le(p^*-1)\|X\|_p,\qquad 1<p<\infty,
\]
sharp even for discrete scalar transforms. For real-valued orthogonal, differentially subordinate martingales, Theorem 2.2 (PDF p. 7) gives the sharp
\[
\|Y\|_p\le\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2p^*}\right)\|X\|_p.
\]

Theorem 2.3 (PDF pp. 7–8) is a stability statement, not a stronger universal norm inequality. If \(1<p<2\) and \(\|Y\|_p\ge((p-1)^{-1}-\varepsilon)\|X\|_p\), then
\[
\big\||Y_\infty|-(p-1)^{-1}|X_\infty|\big\|_p\le c_p\varepsilon^{1/2}\|X\|_p,
\quad
c_p=\frac{\left(\frac p{p-1}\right)^{(3-p)/2}}{\sqrt{1-p(1-1/p)^{p-1}}}.
\]
If \(2<p<\infty\) and \(\|Y\|_p\ge(p-1-\varepsilon)\|X\|_p\), then
\[
\big\||Y_\infty|-(p-1)|X_\infty|\big\|_p\le c_p\varepsilon^{1/p}\|X\|_p,
\quad
c_p=(p-1)\left(\frac{2pe}{(p-2)(e-2)}\right)^{1/p}.
\]
The orders in \(\varepsilon\) are optimal. At \(p=2\), the paper constructs a pair showing that no positive power stability estimate of this form holds. Theorem 2.4 gives the corresponding orthogonal scalar statements with \(\tan(\pi/(2p))\) for \(p<2\), \(\cot(\pi/(2p))\) for \(p>2\), and again no such stability at \(p=2\) (PDF pp. 8–9).

## Bellman proof route

Theorem 2.5 (PDF p. 9) gives a sufficient condition: a continuous function \(U:H\times H\to\mathbb R\), bounded on bounded sets, of class \(C^1\) on \(H\times H\setminus\{|x||y|=0\}\), and of class \(C^2\) on an exhausting sequence of open connected sets \(S_i\) (the union of their closures is \(H\times H\)). For each \(i\), a nonnegative measurable \(c_i\) on \(S_i\) must satisfy, for every \((x,y)\in S_i\), \(h,k\in H\),
\[
\langle U_{xx}h,h\rangle+2\langle U_{xy}h,k\rangle+\langle U_{yy}k,k\rangle
\le-c_i(x,y)(|h|^2-|k|^2)
\]
and, for each \(i,n\), the annular bound \(\sup\{c_i(x,y):(x,y)\in S_i,\ 1/n\le |x|+|y|<n\}\le M_{i,n}<\infty\) holds. Then for any \(t\ge0\) and any Hilbert-valued martingale pair \(X,Y\) with \(Y\) differentially subordinate to \(X\), there is a nondecreasing sequence of stopping times \(\tau_n\uparrow\infty\) such that \(\mathbb E U(X_{\tau_n\wedge t},Y_{\tau_n\wedge t})\le\mathbb E U(X_0,Y_0)\). Theorem 2.6 instead assumes superharmonicity and convexity in the second coordinate for real orthogonal martingales. Sections 2.2–2.6 (PDF pp. 9–30) construct sharp discrete martingale pairs and the Bellman functions \(U_p,V_p\), then use localization, Fatou, explicit majorization and convexity. Sharpness examples include stopped Brownian motion in sectors.

## Scalar Riesz transforms and C207 boundary

Equation (1.6) (PDF p. 5) recalls the sharp scalar component estimate, citing Iwaniec–Martin [32]:
\[
\|R_jf\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^d)}\le
\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2p^*}\right)\|f\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^d)},\quad 1<p<\infty,
\]
with sharp constant for every coordinate \(j\) and dimension \(d\). Theorem 1.2 on PDF pp. 5–6 is a *stability* theorem for near-extremizers; it is not the statement of (1.6). Theorem 3.5 and Lemma 3.7 in §3.3 (PDF pp. 37–38) prove that stability result using the background-radiation Brownian representation: Theorem 3.5 identifies the limit \(T^y_{A_j}f\to R_jf\) a.e. as \(y\to\infty\), and Lemma 3.7 supplies the Bellman integral inequality before Fatou's lemma passes to the limit. The sharp scalar value is also the constant in the orthogonal martingale inequality of Theorem 2.2, which the paper cites as known. This scalar benchmark implies only a lower bound for the vector operator norm in C207 (already in dimension one); applying componentwise inequalities gives at best dimension-dependent bounds such as \(\sqrt d\,c_p\) for \(p\ge2\). This paper/card does **not** prove the exact dimension-free bound for \(\| (\sum_{j=1}^d|R_jf|^2)^{1/2}\|_p\) required by C207. A vector Bellman or equivalent sharp vector-valued argument and its sharpness construction remain unproved.

Sections 3.1–3.2 (PDF pp. 31–37) transfer differential-subordination estimates to Fourier multipliers through Lévy-process representations and laminates; §3.4 (pp. 38–40) proves sharpness, with d=1 reduced to the Hilbert transform by dilation.
