# Method and assumption checks

## Martingale transforms and stopping

A discrete X is a martingale only after verifying integrability, adaptedness, and E[X_n|F_{n−1}]=X_{n−1}; for a supermartingale replace equality by ≤. A predictable C_n is F_{n−1}-measurable; check integrability of C_n times the increment. Stopping uses C_n=1{n≤T}.

Safe optional-stopping routes include bounded T; a UI stopped family and L1 passage; a nonnegative supermartingale plus Fatou under the theorem’s assumptions; or bounded increments with E[T]<∞. If only T<∞ a.s. is known, check UI. Almost-sure convergence does not imply L1 convergence without UI.

## Maximal and tail inequalities

Doob’s weak inequality follows by partitioning according to the first crossing time. Freedman uses centered increments bounded in absolute value and the predictable sum of conditional variances (or conditional second moments if the increments are not conditionally centered). Keep the Fan–Grama–Liu theorems distinct: S007 Theorem 2.1 assumes a conditional exponential normalizer and caps both realized squares and an adapted V-sum; S007 Corollary 2.7 uses an upper increment bound and a piecewise C² proxy from conditional second moments; S010 Theorem 2.1 uses its one-sided positive-jump truncation budget. None of these budgets may be substituted for another, and S010’s truncation/β-moment statements are not S007 results. For Howard et al.'s line-crossing theorem, record the adapted pair \((S,V)\), initial values, nondecreasing intrinsic-time process, ψ-domain, and leading factor \(l_0\). The key assumption is that for each λ the exponential \(e^{\lambda S_t-\psi(\lambda)V_t}\) is dominated by a nonnegative supermartingale initialized at most \(l_0\); it need not itself be a supermartingale. The bound is one-sided, \(l_0e^{-aD(b)}\), for crossing \(S_t\ge a+bV_t\). In continuous time use the source's usual-filtration and càdlàg hypotheses. Keep the time horizon, boundary, variance proxy, and factor visible.

For unbounded increments, use an explicit tail/moment theorem or truncate and bound the remainder. Do not silently apply the bounded form.

Fan–Grama–Liu S009 supplies two distinct unbounded-increment routes. Theorem 2.1 uses a two-sided exponential moment and gives a maximal (n x) deviation with optimal exponent (n^\alpha). Theorem 2.2 instead assumes an exponential moment of (X_i^+), controls a crossing under the predictable bracket ⟨S⟩, and retains the explicit (nC_1e^{-x^\alpha}) large-jump term; Corollary 2.1 additionally assumes an exponential moment of the terminal bracket. Do not confuse either with S007's conditional exponential normalizer or S010's different truncation quantities.

For one-sided martingale Cramér moderate deviations, S014 requires the conditional positive-part exponential moment in (A1) and an almost-sure final predictable-bracket error in (A2); it controls only the upper tail unless an analogous condition is imposed on negative increments. The ρ=1 logarithmic remainder is part of the stated bound, and the proof of its tilted Berry–Esseen lemma is deferred to the cited supplement. S013 is a distinct absolute conditional-Cramér route: Theorem 2.1 controls both tails and its MDP display covers all Borel sets in R. Do not import S014’s half-side scope into S013, or S013’s two-sided result into S014.

For a quantitative finite martingale CLT, S016 requires the conditional moment comparison
\(\mathbb E(|\xi_i|^{2+\rho}|\mathcal F_{i-1})\le\epsilon^\rho\mathbb E(\xi_i^2|\mathcal F_{i-1})\)
for each increment. Its exact-rate theorem also needs an almost-sure bracket error bound; the random-bracket version adds the (L^p) bracket error and (\mathbb E\max_i|\xi_i|^{2p}). The proof fills a stopped bracket with independent Rademacher increments for the random-bracket result. This is a Berry–Esseen statement, not a martingale functional CLT or an OST identity.

## BDG and stochastic integrals

Record p, continuity/jumps, scalar/vector geometry, initial condition, localization, and the square function/bracket. UMD-valued results use a Gaussian-radonifying covariance norm, not scalar [M]. In the cited Yaroslavtsev result, general UMD-valued local martingales have the \([[M]]\) characteristic for \(1\le p<\infty\); the extension to \(0<p<1\) is for continuous local martingales, while the purely discontinuous theorem is stated for \(1\le p<\infty\) with a Gaussian norm of the jump sequence. For an integral H·M first establish H is predictable and in the theorem’s integrability domain. The cited integral inequality starts with elementary predictable integrands; its strongly predictable extension needs finite γ-integrability. Brownian scalar and compensated-Poisson/vector-valued square functions need not have the same form. Existence of an integral does not imply a supremum estimate.

Distinguish true martingales, UI martingales, H¹ martingales, and local martingales. A bounded integrand preserves the martingale property under the applicable BDG/H¹ hypotheses; boundedness alone with a merely UI martingale integrator does not suffice. Herdegen–Herrmann construct a bounded deterministic integrand whose integral against a UI, non-H¹ martingale is strict local. For semimartingales, locally bounded predictable integrands are a sufficient stochastic-integrability class; do not infer integrability for arbitrary predictable H. Under multiplicative compensation and measure change, keep the pre-zero stochastic interval, the jump exclusion ΔB^{L(Z)}≠−1, specialness, and UI density in force. This excludes a zero denominator and is not an order condition for the paper's complex-valued processes.

Chen–Wang–Wang (S022) illustrate localization in a Galerkin energy estimate for a 3D stochastic Navier–Stokes existence proof. Lemma 2.4 (PDF p. 7) quotes a two-sided inequality for real-valued square-integrable càdlàg martingales starting at zero, $p\ge1$, with an unspecified $C_p$, but calls its angle-bracket process “quadratic variation” without defining the jump convention. For general jump BDG, write the optional bracket $[M]$ explicitly; treating S022’s symbol this way is a clarification, not a source definition. Do not use the predictable bracket in the lower inequality for arbitrary jumps: a one-step rare-jump martingale has predictable bracket 1 and $E\sup|M|=\sqrt q\to0$ at $p=1$ (exact checks in `evidence/W02/examples/s022_predictable_vs_optional_bdg.py`). The PDE proof then combines stopping, BDG, Young and Gronwall. Do not export that PDE theorem to a general martingale problem or uniqueness claim.

For compensated Poisson integration in (L^q(S)), Dirksen's Theorem 1.1 (S030) is an (L^p)-maximal Itô isomorphism for simple adapted integrands, (1<p,q<\infty), under independence of future Poisson increments from the past. The integrand norm changes across six (p,q,2) regimes and combines a square function with two distinct mixed (L^p/L^q) terms. The proof uses Doob, UMD decoupling, independent Rosenthal inequalities and duality. At (q=1) only the upper estimate is retained. This is not a sharp scalar BDG constant and does not extend by itself to arbitrary Banach spaces.

For Snell envelopes, Jacka–Norgilas (S026) assume (E\sup_{t\le T}|G_t|<\infty), a special-semimartingale decomposition, and (G\in S^1\cap H^1_{loc}) for the compensator theorem. Then (dA=\mu dD^-), (0\le\mu\le1). Their generator-domain result separately assumes a right process, payoff integrability and (g\in D(L)). Their smooth-fit statements apply to specified regular one-dimensional diffusions only.

For Hilbert-valued martingales, Bañuelos–Osękowski (S024) use differential subordination ([X,X]-[Y,Y]\) nonnegative and increasing. Sharp inequalities (\|Y\|_p\le(p^*-1)\|X\|_p\) and, in the real orthogonal case, (\cot(\pi/(2p^*))\|X\|_p\) feed their Bellman stability analysis. Their Riesz-transform result is for each scalar component (R_j); it does not prove the exact sharp dimension-free norm of the vector ( (R_1f,\ldots,R_df)\) in C207.


For a countable family of stochastic convolutions in a 2-smooth Banach space, Cox–van Winden (S025, Theorem 3.1) give a supremum estimate with $10D\sqrt{p+\log j}$ multiplying each integrand's pathwise $L^2_t\gamma$ norm. Their discrete martingale Theorem 3.6 assumes zero start and conditionally symmetric increments; the increment-maximum term is weighted by $13(p+\log j)$, while the conditional-square term is weighted by $14D\sqrt{p+\log j}$. Keep these factors distinct. Their metric Hölder theorem additionally needs finite Minkowski dimension, finite doubling dimension, and the stated admissible-modulus norm.

Delbaen (S021) decomposes $L^2$ martingales into independent-increment components only under conditional atomlessness of the innovations; the component martingales are not asserted mutually independent. The best-independent-(L^2) approximation lemma used by the main decomposition is attributed to an earlier source and is not proved in S021.

For test supermartingales under simple null and alternative hypotheses, Forré (S027) distinguishes fixed-horizon error from sequential crossing error. Ville controls type I; conditional Hölder yields a lower-tail-exponent ceiling on arbitrary filtrations, but the matching finite-horizon construction is i.i.d. and uses the true likelihood ratio. In Theorem 5.6, $B_t(\beta)$ has a unique minimizer $\beta^*(t)$ when the interior condition holds, and the threshold crossover for $R$ is exactly $t=\ell/\mathrm{KL}(P_1\|P_0)$. Since the error is a CDF at that threshold, the exact error can tie at other horizons; strict improvement requires positive $P_1$ mass on $(B_t(\beta^*(t)),B_t(1)]$ (Remark 5.7, p. 31). Conditional e-power by itself implies no uniform finite-horizon type-II bound, even though a fixed i.i.d. design with positive e-power rejects eventually. Do not transfer the i.i.d. equality or the simple-hypothesis oracle design to composite or general filtered models.

For finite-grid stopping, Belomestny–Schoenmakers (S029) distinguish weakly optimal martingales from surely optimal ones. Their Rogers dual ranges over zero-start martingales; Theorem 2.7 characterizes weak optimizers as perturbations of the Doob martingale, while Theorem 3.2 uses independent centered randomizers supported on $(-\infty,1]$ with joint density positive at 1 to make every non-Doob weak optimizer strictly suboptimal. The perturbation lives on an auxiliary product space. Bayraktar–Cox–Stoev (S028) use a conditional-terminal-law measure-valued martingale and, for finite-support terminal laws, reduce the stopping transport value to a simplex obstacle PDE whose projected solution is a concave envelope; this does not supply a general continuous-time Snell-envelope theorem.

## Nonclassical optional sampling

Demimartingale inequalities are defined by testing increments against componentwise monotone functions of the past, not by a filtration conditional-expectation identity. Their optional-sampling bounds require the stopping event indicators to have the exact componentwise monotonicity stated in the theorem. Do not apply these bounds to arbitrary stopping times or treat them as classical martingale OST.

For a localized process at a finite horizon, distinguish a genuine terminal martingale from a strict local martingale or a process that fails to be a semimartingale at an endpoint. Uniform integrability, endpoint integrability, and H¹ control are different requirements; record which one justifies the limit passage.

## Martingale problems

Define the test process, e.g. f(X_t)−f(X_0)−∫Lf(X_s)ds. A weak-limit proof separates tightness, convergence of coefficients/brackets/test functionals, UI for passing expectations, dense-time identities and path regularity, and uniqueness. In controlled weak-strong convergence results, also identify the control variable, a determining class, canonical test versions, continuity of test values and their products, and the uniform-integrability family. A martingale problem does not by itself prove tightness or uniqueness. Fixed-time jumps require the source's path-control and test-continuity hypotheses; weak J1 convergence alone is insufficient. Encode jumps and boundary conditions in the test class. For conditioned models, carry the conditioning law through all moment and compactness estimates. Feehan–Pop (S031) give a specialized PDE route for a time-inhomogeneous diffusion on a half-space with boundary-degenerate covariance $x_da$, inward normal drift and cycloidal/parabolic Hölder assumptions with linear growth. Their uniqueness argument invokes a Schauder theorem from companion paper [26], shifts the process by ε before applying Itô's formula, localizes, and sends ε down to zero. Their mimicking theorem matches one-time marginals under conditional drift/covariance and the same coefficient conditions; it does not match path laws or prove a generic conditioned-path martingale problem.

## A007: stopping-time convergence template (auxiliary)

Aldous, *Stopping Times and Tightness II*, Berkeley Technical Report 124 (1987 report version), supplies two conditional routes; it does not prove the polling limit. Proposition 1.2 (report PDF p. 3) applies to real-valued D([0,infinity), R) martingales: finite-dimensional convergence to a continuous limit plus UI of {M_n(t)} over n at each fixed t gives J1 weak convergence. For general processes, Proposition 2.2 (PDF p. 7) requires finite-dimensional convergence to a continuous limit and, for every L>0 and 0<epsilon<1/2, some 0<delta<=1 such that liminf_n Gamma_Xn(L,epsilon,delta)>0. Gamma is defined using the two conditional events X_n(T+delta')−X_n(T)<=epsilon and X_n(T+delta')−X_n(T)>=−epsilon given T<=L, uniformly over every stopping time T with P(T<=L)>epsilon and every 0<delta'<=delta. Lemma 2.3 (PDF p. 8) converts stopped-increment UI for a martingale into a positive Gamma bound.

The Proposition 2.2 proof is not a direct J1-coupling shortcut: section 3 first obtains convergence in measure and a Dudley d_L coupling (PDF p. 10); section 4 (PDF pp. 11–14) builds a moving-average crossing time and randomized short delay to contradict the positive Gamma bound if local supremum discrepancies persist. In A007 these are usable only after independently proving the process representation, finite-dimensional limit, fixed-time UI or full stopping-time criterion, and continuous-limit hypotheses. They supply no renewal-age tightness, stability, stationary-to-fluid exchange, or waiting-time law. See the [Aldous use card](../evidence/W02/cards/W02-CANON-ALDOUS-1987.md) and bundled canonical read evidence.
