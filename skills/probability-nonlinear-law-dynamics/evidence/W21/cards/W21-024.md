## Smoothing operator and fixedpoint criterion

Let `W_1,...,W_N` be jointly distributed nonnegative random variables with `P(W_i>0)>0` and `E W_i^γ<∞` for each `i` for some `γ>1`. For a probability law `μ` on `[0,∞)`, define

`Tμ = Law(Σ_i W_i X_i)`,

where `X_i` are iid with law `μ` and independent of the whole weight vector. No independence among the `W_i` is assumed. Put

`v(α)=log Σ_i E[W_i^α ; W_i>0]`.

**Theorem 1 (printed p.277; physical PDF p.3).** There is a nontrivial fixedpoint `μ≠δ_0` iff some `α∈(0,1]` satisfies `v(α)=0` and `v'(α)≤0`. The paper reduces this criterion to five intermediate results: construct a finite-mean fixedpoint when `v(1)=0,v'(1)<0`; use an associated size-biased random walk and harmonic-function/renewal arguments for necessity; reduce an interior root with negative derivative to the finite-mean case by replacing weights with powers and subordinating with a one-sided stable law; obtain the tangent-root case `v'(α)=0` by a rescaling/limit argument; and show any nontrivial fixedpoint forces a root in `(0,1]`.

**Theorem 2 (printed pp.280–281; physical PDF pp.6–7).** When `v` is not identically zero and a nontrivial fixedpoint exists, there is a unique root `α∈(0,1]` with `v(α)=0,v'(α)≤0`. The complete fixedpoint family is parametrized by positive smooth functions `p` with the source's complete-monotonicity condition; when the weight-log law is nonlattice, or `α=1`, this parameter reduces to a positive constant, while for lattice span `s>0` and `α<1` periodic parameters of period `s` are allowed. The parameter is recovered from the Laplace-transform asymptotic at zero. Also, if fixedpoint Laplace transform `φ` and initial Laplace transform `ψ` satisfy

`lim_{θ↓0} (1−φ(θ))/(1−ψ(θ)) = 1`,

then `T^n ψ -> φ`. At `α=1,v'(1)<0` this condition is equality of means; otherwise it requires the matching stable-tail asymptotic. The proof identifies subsequential limits of a normalized transform ratio as harmonic functions for the size-biased random walk, then uses a branching random walk to sandwich iterates between rescalings of the fixedpoint.

**Theorem 3 (printed p.281; physical PDF p.7; proof pp.25–27).** If `v(1)=0` and `v'(1)<0`, then for every fixedpoint and every `β>1`, the `β`th moment is finite iff `v(β)<0`. The proof uses the smoothing equation and moment inequalities for the forward implication, and an iterated moment bound starting from `δ_1` for the converse.

## Transfer boundary

The RDE has the exact independent-copy representation `X d= Σ_i W_i X_i`, together with nonnegative weights, a positive `γ` moment, and Laplace-tail/moment conditions. These are strong structural assumptions. A law that is stationary for a finite rank-selection Markov kernel is not a smoothing-transform fixedpoint unless this recursive-sum identity is derived. Fixedpoint existence/uniqueness/convergence claims do not transfer by analogy alone.
