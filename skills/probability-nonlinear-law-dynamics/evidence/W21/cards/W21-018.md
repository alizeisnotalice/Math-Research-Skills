## Operator and the support mechanism

Let `G` be a finite Abelian group, `|G|=m`, and `μ` a probability measure on `G`. The quadratic stochastic operator `V` on the simplex `S^G` is specified by the heredity coefficients

`p_{ij,k}=μ_{k−i−j}`,  so  `(Vx)_k = sum_{i,j in G} μ_{k−i−j} x_i x_j`.

Write `s(x)={i:x_i>0}`. A subgroup `U≤G` is `A`-invariant when `|U+A|=|U|`; equivalently, `A` lies in one coset of `U`.

**Proposition 3.6 (PDF p.6).** `s(Vx)=s(x)+s(x)+s(μ)`, so support size never decreases. Equality of support sizes holds exactly when `s(x)` is a coset of an `s(μ)`-invariant subgroup. Since support size is bounded, every orbit eventually has constant support size and cosets of a fixed invariant-subgroup size; this does not mean the coset itself is fixed.

**Theorem 3.9 (statement PDF p.6; proof pp.6–8).** For every `x in S^G`,

`V^n x − u(s(V^n x)) -> 0`,

where `u(B)` is uniform on the nonempty set `B`; the reference law is the uniform law on the support at time `n`, so the statement does not assert convergence to a fixed law when those supports move. Proposition 3.6 shows that after finitely many steps the support size is constant and each support is a coset of an `s(μ)`-invariant subgroup `U`. The coset can move: if `s(μ)⊂a+U`, then `g+U` is sent to `2g+a+U`. Order the `k=|U|` masses at each step as `v_1≥...≥v_k`. The proof applies the rearrangement inequality to the pair products in `(Vy)_j`; it lower-bounds the smallest new mass by `v_k+(v_1−v_k)v_k`, then bounds the remaining deficit from `1/k`. Unless the vector is already uniform, the minimum mass increases; the deficit tends to zero. Thus the distance from `V^n x` to the uniform law on its **current** support coset tends to zero. The coset dynamics may be periodic, as Theorem 3.12 records.

**Theorem 3.11 (as printed, PDF p.7).** For any norm on `R^G`, the source displays

`limsup_{n→∞} (1/n) log log ||V^n x−u(s(V^n x))|| ≤ log 2`.

This displayed expression is not real-valued once the error norm is in `(0,1)`, which eventually occurs by Theorem 3.9; it is also undefined if the error becomes exactly zero. Do not silently insert a minus sign or reverse the inequality and attribute the repaired expression to the paper.

**A valid rate consequence of the proof (not a correction of the printed theorem).** On the stable support cosets of size `k`, let `ε_n=max_{i∈s(V^n x)} |(V^n x)_i−1/k|`. The p.8 proof gives `ε_{n+1}≤k ε_n²`. Since Theorem 3.9 gives `ε_n→0`, choose `n_0` with `c=k ε_{n_0}<1`. Then `z_n=kε_n` obeys `z_{n+1}≤z_n²`, hence `ε_{n_0+r}≤k^{-1}c^{2^r}`. If no later error is zero, this proves

`liminf_{r→∞} (1/r) log(−log ε_{n_0+r}) ≥ log 2`.

If an error is zero, the orbit is already exactly uniform on its current support from then on. Norm equivalence in finite-dimensional `R^G` preserves this derived double-exponential upper bound up to constants. The rescaling identity and rate direction were checked symbolically in `checks/source_formula_audit.py`; the derivation above, not the computation, is the proof.

**Periodic orbits and regularity (Theorem 3.12, Proposition 3.13, PDF pp.8–9).** For an `s(μ)`-invariant subgroup `U`, `a in s(μ)`, and `g in G` whose doubling orbit `2^n g` is periodic modulo `U`, the uniform laws `u(2^n g−a+U)` form a periodic orbit. Conversely, the theorem says every periodic orbit is of this form. The operator is regular (every trajectory converges) iff for every such `U` and every `g in G` some `n` has `2^n g in U`; then all limits are uniform on a coset. Corollary 3.14 gives regularity for every `μ` when `|G|` is a power of two. Corollary 3.15 gives a nonregular example when `|G|` is not a power of two, using `μ=δ_0`.

Since interior points of the simplex have full support `G`, their supports remain `G`; Theorem 3.9 therefore yields convergence to the center `u(G)` for Lebesgue-almost every starting point. Boundary initial laws can instead approach a moving periodic sequence of coset-uniform laws or lie on a nontrivial periodic orbit; the paper distinguishes almost regularity from regularity and ergodicity.

## Transfer boundary

These conclusions require the exact group-indexed QSO coefficients above. The nested stationary-law map `theta -> pi(K_theta)` has not been shown to be a QSO, much less a `(G,μ)`-QSO. A finite simplex domain alone is not a sufficient identification. The paper is a positive example of how an invariant support stratification and explicit orbit algebra can prove almost-everywhere convergence once the special operator structure is established.
