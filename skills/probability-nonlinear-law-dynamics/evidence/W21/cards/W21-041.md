## Model and exact assumptions

Let $T=(T_j)_{j\ge1}$ be a nonincreasing null sequence of nonnegative random weights; define $m(s)=\mathbb E\sum_jT_j^s$ and $N=\#\{j:T_j>0\}$. The source's solution class is
\[
\mathcal M=\{f:\mathbb R\to[0,1]: f\text{ is nonincreasing and left-continuous},\ f(0)=f(0+)=1,\ 0<f(t)<1\text{ for some }t>0\}.
\]
The equation is
\[
f(t)=\mathbb E\prod_{j\ge1}f(tT_j),\quad t\ge0.
\tag{1}
\]
For a Laplace transform $f(t)=\mathbb E e^{-tX}$, this is $X\overset d=\sum_jT_jX_j$, with iid $X_j$ independent of $T$. For a left-continuous survival function it is $X\overset d=\inf\{X_j/T_j:T_j>0\}$, with empty infimum $+\infty$.

Conditions (2)–(3): $\mathbb EN>1$, and there is a minimal $\alpha>0$ with $m(\alpha)=1<m(\beta)$ for every $\beta\in[0,\alpha)$. In regular case (10) is $m'(\alpha)=\mathbb E\sum_jT_j^\alpha\log T_j\in(-\infty,0)$; (11) is $\mathbb E[W_1\log W_1]<\infty$, $W_1=\sum_jT_j^\alpha$; (12) is $\mathbb E\sum_jT_j^\alpha\log^-T_j<\infty$. Theorem 1.2 assumes (2),(3),(10),(11),(12); $r\ge1$ is maximal geometric span in (13), with $r=1$ nongeometric. The Biggins additive martingale $W_n=\sum_{|v|=n}L(v)^\alpha$ converges a.s. and in $L^1$ to nondegenerate $W$ under (10)–(11).

For boundary case replace (10) by (14), $m'(\alpha)=0$, and assume (16):
\[
\mathbb E\sum_jT_j^\alpha(\log T_j)^2\in(0,\infty),\qquad
\mathbb E[W_1(\log W_1)^2]+\mathbb E[\widetilde X\log\widetilde X]<\infty,
\quad \widetilde X=\sum_jT_j^\alpha\log^+T_j.
\]
Then derivative martingale $Z_n=\sum_{|v|=n}L(v)^\alpha(-\log L(v))$ converges a.s. to limit $Z$ positive on BRW survival (17).

## Main theorem / classification

Let $\mathcal H_r$ consist of positive multiplicatively $r$-periodic, **left-continuous** $h$ for which $t\mapsto h(t)t^\alpha$ is nondecreasing; in nongeometric case $r=1$, $h$ is positive constant. Theorem 1.2 (physical p.6) classifies every $f\in\mathcal M$:
\[
f(t)=\mathbb E\exp[-Wh(t)t^\alpha],\qquad h\in\mathcal H_r.
\]
Laplace-transform solutions exist only for $0<\alpha\le1$, and are exactly those $h$ for which $h(t)t^\alpha$ has completely monotone derivative (class $\mathcal P_r$). Theorem 1.5 (physical p.7) states the same classification in boundary case after replacing $W$ by $Z$, under (2),(3),(14),(16). This is not an unrestricted fixed-point theorem for arbitrary law-valued maps.

## Proof mechanism and conditions on reuse

Theorem 1.2 proof (Section 3, physical pp.11–16) begins with disintegration $M_n(t)=\prod_{|v|=n}f(tL(v))$, a positive bounded product martingale. Stopping-line convergence gives tameness near zero. Setting $F(t)=\mathbb E[-\log M(t)]$ yields smoothing harmonic equation $F(t)=\mathbb E\sum_jF(tT_j)$ and a $Ct^\alpha$ bound. The many-to-one change of measure (Lemma 2.1, p.9) turns $e^{\alpha x}F(e^{-x})$ into a bounded harmonic function of associated random walk; Choquet–Deny identifies a constant/geometric-periodic factor. Conditional expectations along BRW and additive martingale identify $M(t)=e^{-Wh(t)t^\alpha}$, and averaging recovers $f$.

Boundary proof, Section 4 (physical pp.17–24), states Proposition 4.1 for a **nontrivial centered** random walk of lattice span $d\ge0$ with $\mathbb E S_1^2<\infty$. Its function $G:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ must be right-continuous, satisfy the killed-half-line boundary condition $G(x)=0$ for $x\le0$ and the harmonic equation
\[
G(x)=\mathbb E\!\left[G(x+S_1)\mathbf1_{\{x+S_1>0\}}\right],\qquad x>0,
\tag{33}
\]
The prose just before Prop. 4.1 calls the growth condition “at most linear growth,” but the proposition's displayed condition is $\sup_{x\le0}|G(x)/(1+|x|)|<\infty$; because $G=0$ there, this display is vacuous on the positive half-line. Preserve this source-version mismatch; do not claim the printed proposition alone proves a general harmonic uniqueness theorem or silently repair its statement.

**Proof-supported restricted use.** Use the harmonic uniqueness conclusion $G(x)=\kappa(x)H(x)$ only after separately establishing
\[
\sup_{x>0}\frac{|G(x)|}{1+x}<\infty
\]
as well as the centered finite-second-moment walk, right-continuity, killed-boundary, and harmonicity assumptions above. This positive-side bound is the actual at-most-linear-growth gate used by the uniqueness proof; without it, do not use Proposition 4.1's harmonic uniqueness.

For the paper's own boundary classification (Theorem 1.5), Section 5 (physical pp.25–29) supplies that gate for its specific harmonic input rather than deriving a general correction to Prop. 4.1. It defines the truncated martingale limit $M^{(a)}$ and $F^{(a)}(t)=\mathbb E[-\log M^{(a)}(t)]$. Lemma 5.1 gives the pointwise domination $-\log M^{(a)}(t)\le C Z^{(a)}(t)$ (Eq. 51, p.28); uniform integrability of the trimmed martingale then yields $F^{(a)}(t)\le C t^\alpha H(-\log(t/a))$ for $t\le a$. The transformed function $g^{(a)}(x)=a^{-\alpha}e^{\alpha x}F^{(a)}(ae^{-x})$ is shown on physical p.29 to be right-continuous and killed-harmonic, with $g^{(a)}(x)\le C H(x)$. Since the source gives $H(x)\sim x$ at infinity (Eq. 36, p.18), this specific $g^{(a)}$ has the separately verified bound $\sup_{x>0}|g^{(a)}(x)|/(1+x)<\infty$. Thus Theorem 1.5 may use the proof-supported restricted lemma for this constructed input. The source p.29 step is rendered [here](../../../references/local-assets.md#asset-c231cca69cb5). This does not repair Prop. 4.1 generally; without an independently established positive-side bound, do not use its harmonic-uniqueness conclusion. The method still requires the weighted branching tree, many-to-one walk, and martingale/disintegration structure.
