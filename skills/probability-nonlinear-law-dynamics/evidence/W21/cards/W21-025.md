## Operator and hypotheses

Let `N<∞` almost surely and `A_i≥0` be random weights with `Σ_i A_i<∞` almost surely. For a probability law `μ` on `[0,∞)`, define

`Tμ = Law(Σ_i A_i Z_i)`,

where, conditional on the weights, the `Z_i` are iid with law `μ` and independent of the weights. Write `S(x)=Σ_i A_i^x`, `p(x)=E S(x)`, and `S=S(1)`. The paper imposes its nondegeneracy condition (H0). Its moment assumptions are

`(H1)  EN<∞,   E Σ_i A_i log⁺A_i<∞`,

and, for some `δ>0`,

`(H2)  E N^(1+δ)<∞,   E S^(1+δ)<∞`.

The H2 moment condition is equivalent to the corresponding moment bound for `Σ_i max(A_i,1)` as stated in the paper.

## Existence, tails, and iteration

**Theorem 0 (printed p.5; PDF p.6).** Under (H1), a nontrivial fixedpoint of finite mean exists iff `E[S log⁺S]<∞`, `p(1)=1`, and `p'(1)<0`.

**Theorem 1 (printed p.6; PDF p.7).** If `EN>1` and `inf_(x∈[0,1])p(x)≤1`, a nontrivial fixedpoint exists; the converse holds under (H1). Under (H1), existence is equivalent to `EN>1` plus this root condition.

**Theorem 2 (printed p.7; PDF p.8).** Assuming (H1), a nontrivial fixedpoint exists, and `α∈(0,1]` is the root with `p(α)=1`, `p'(α)≤0`, every nontrivial fixedpoint with Laplace transform `φ` has a slowly varying profile `ℓ` at zero such that

`(1−φ(t))/(t^αℓ(t)) → 1` if `α=1`, and `limsup_(t↓0)(1−φ(t))/(t^αℓ(t))≤1` if `α<1`.

Under (H2), one may take `ℓ(t)=c` when `p'(α)<0`, and `ℓ(t)=c|log t|` when `p'(α)=0`. Corollary 2 and §11 give the corresponding tail upper bounds and moment thresholds using the source's slowly-varying normalization; no tail formula is transferred here without preserving that normalization.

**Theorem 3 (printed p.8; PDF p.9).** Under the same root assumptions, if either `α=1` or (H2) holds and `φ` is the Laplace transform of a nontrivial fixedpoint while `η` is the Laplace transform of any initial probability law satisfying `1−φ(t)~1−η(t)` as `t↓0`, then `T^nη` converges weakly to the fixedpoint with transform `φ` (equivalently, its Laplace transforms converge pointwise).

The proof chain in §7 constructs the depth-`n` branching-tree representation of `T^nμ`, proves a maximal-product decay lemma from the condition `p(a)<1`, and establishes a comparison lemma that propagates small-`t` inequalities to all `t`. The regular-variation theorem supplies scaled fixedpoints bracketing `η`; order preservation and the comparison lemma squeeze the iterates, with the scale gap then sent to zero. This is a theorem for the exact independent-copy weighted-sum operator above.

**Theorem 4 (printed p.9; PDF p.10).** Under (H2), fixedpoints are in natural bijection with the source's profile class `B_(α,s)`. In the nonlattice case, or when `α=1`, the profile is constant; in the lattice case with `α<1`, it is a positive smooth periodic profile with derivative sign conditions. The profile is read from the small-argument Laplace asymptotics, with a separate `|log t|` normalization when `p'(α)=0`. Sections 8–11 then treat Kesten–Stigum/Seneta–Heyde limits, no-fixedpoint regimes, totality/convergence, and moments/tail behavior.

## Proof architecture and applications

Section 3 proves existence by truncating the offspring count/weights, constructing fixedpoints for truncated transforms, and taking a nondegenerate Laplace-transform limit. Section 4 introduces the tilted increment `X_α` and the Laplace deficit `D_α(x)=e^(αx)(1−φ(e^(−x)))`; a renewal/random-walk identity and lattice/nonlattice arguments provide necessary root conditions and asymptotics. Section 5 conjugates the transform by stable subordination; §6 transfers small-argument regular variation to tails. The applications in §2 cover Poincaré equations, Crump–Mode branching, and branching random walks.

## Transfer boundary

This paper is a broad RDE fixedpoint and iteration source, but its structure is specific: a random finite sum of conditionally iid copies with nonnegative weights, independence from those weights, and explicit `p(α)`, moment, and lattice hypotheses. No representation of A121/A122's stationary-law map as this `T` has been derived. A fixedpoint equation that merely resembles an RDE is not enough to transfer these existence, uniqueness, tail, or iteration theorems.
