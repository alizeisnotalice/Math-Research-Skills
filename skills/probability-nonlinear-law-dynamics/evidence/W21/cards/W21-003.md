## Operator and invariant laws

The paper studies a **continuous-time nonlinear Markov chain** on a finite state space `S={1,...,S}`. A law `m` evolves by

`d/dt Phi_t(m) = Phi_t(m) Q(Phi_t(m)),  Phi_0(m)=m`,

where each `Q(m)` is a conservative rate matrix. This is a nonlinear forward ODE / semigroup. It is not the nested stationary-law iteration `theta[n+1]=pi(K_theta[n])`.

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF p.4; proof in Appendix A, pp.14–15).** If every entry `Q_ij(m)` is Lipschitz in `m`, there is a unique nonlinear Markov semigroup with this generator. The proof forms the vector field `f_j(m)=sum_i m_i Q_ij(m)`, extends this Lipschitz vector field to Euclidean space, checks the simplex tangent-cone condition at each face using conservativity and nonnegative off-diagonal rates, then applies ODE existence/uniqueness, flow invariance, and continuous dependence.

An invariant law solves `m^T Q(m)=0`. For a fixed parameter `m`, let

`s(m) = {x in P(S): x^T Q(m)=0}`.

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p.4; proof pp.4–5).** If `Q` is continuous, an invariant law exists. For each fixed `m`, `s(m)` is nonempty, compact, and convex by finite-chain stationary theory; continuity makes its graph closed. Kakutani applied to `s` gives a fixed point `m in s(m)`.

**Theorem 3.2 (statement PDF p.5; proof pp.5–7).** If every `Q(m)` is irreducible, let `x(m)` be its unique stationary law and set `f(m)=x(m)-m`. If `f` is continuously differentiable and the paper's reduced `(S−1)×(S−1)` Jacobian matrix `M(m)` (the derivative along the simplex coordinates) is nonsingular for every `m in P(S)`, then the nonlinear chain has a unique invariant law. Proof: irreducibility puts every invariant law in the simplex interior; identify the simplex interior with an open subset of `R^(S−1)`; use the nonzero Jacobian determinant to give a constant local degree sign; homotope the reduced map to a reference point while keeping zero off the boundary; the Brouwer degree is `±1`, so there is exactly one zero.

## Long-time dynamics actually proved

Section 4 supplies two warnings. The three-state construction in §4.1 has periodic marginal distributions (PDF pp.8–9). The two-state example in §4.2 has an irreducible nonlinear generator at every law but three equilibria at first coordinate `1/4, 1/2, 3/4`; intervals of initial conditions converge to `1/4` or `3/4`, so irreducibility alone does not give strong ergodicity (PDF pp.9–10).

**Theorem 5.1 (PDF p.11).** For two states, if the scalar vector field

`f(m_1)=m_1 Q_11(m_1,1−m_1)+(1−m_1)Q_21(m_1,1−m_1)`

is continuous and the chain has a unique stationary point `(m_bar,1−m_bar)`, then the chain is strongly ergodic. The proof reduces the flow to `dot m_1=f(m_1)`, uses continuity and the absence of other zeros to get a constant sign on each side of `m_bar`, and uses conservativity for the inward endpoint signs; every scalar trajectory therefore converges to `m_bar`.

**Theorem 5.2 (statement PDF p.12; proof pp.12–13).** For three states, the rate equation reduces to a two-dimensional `C^1` vector field `f` on a bounded simply connected region `O` containing the simplex. Assume a unique stationary point `m_bar`, divergence `∂_1 f_1+∂_2 f_2` nowhere zero with one sign throughout `O`, and at `m_bar` either `det Df(m_bar)>0` or `(tr Df(m_bar))^2−4 det Df(m_bar)<0`. Then the chain is strongly ergodic. The proof uses flow invariance and compactness, Poincaré–Bendixson, Bendixson's criterion to rule out periodic orbits, and the local condition to rule out a saddle/homoclinic alternative; hence every omega-limit set is the unique equilibrium.

## Transfer boundary

The existence theorem concerns a continuous-time generator-valued map `Q(m)` and the equilibrium equation `m^TQ(m)=0`. The small-state criteria concern trajectories of the corresponding ODE. They can inform a target only after its exact update has been identified as this type of nonlinear Markov flow and all differentiability, irreducibility, uniqueness, and planar-dynamics assumptions have been verified. In particular, they do not prove convergence of `theta -> pi(K_theta)`.
