## Operator, topology, and theorem scope

The general recursion is an affine vector RDE sequence
\[
Y_n\stackrel d=\sum_{r=1}^K A_r(n)Y^{(r)}_{I_r(n)}+b_n,
\]
with independent recursive copies and a jointly specified random index/coefficient/toll tuple. After centering and matrix normalization, the paper writes
\[
X_n=C_n^{-1/2}(Y_n-M_n)\stackrel d=\sum_{r=1}^K A_r(n)X^{(r)}_{I_r(n)}+b(n).
\]
The limit operator is
\[
T(\mu)=\mathcal L\!\left(\sum_{r=1}^K A_r^*X^{(r)}+b^*\right),
\]
where the limiting coefficient/toll tuple and the iid law copies are independent; dependence among entries of the coefficient/toll/index tuple is allowed by the setup. The metric is the multivariate Zolotarev metric \(\zeta_s\). The notation \(\mathcal M_d^s(m,C)\) is overloaded by s (source equations (16)–(18), physical p.8): for \(2<s\le3\), it means finite s-th absolute moment, mean m, and covariance C; for \(1<s\le2\), finite s-th moment and mean m (C is ignored); for \(0<s\le1\), finite s-th moment only (both m and C are ignored). Thus the shorthand \(\mathcal M_d^s(0,I_d)\) in Theorem 4.1 imposes unit covariance only when \(2<s\le3\), mean zero without fixed covariance when \(1<s\le2\), and no mean/covariance constraint when \(0<s\le1\).

**Theorem 4.1 (exact transfer assumptions).** For a fixed finite \(K\) and \(0<s\le3\), assume the normalized sequence is s-integrable and:

1. \((A_1(n),\ldots,A_K(n),b(n))\to(A_1^*,\ldots,A_K^*,b^*)\) in \(L_s\);
2. \(\mathbb E\sum_{r=1}^K\|A_r^*\|_{op}^s<1\);
3. for each finite \(l\in\mathbb N\) and each \(r\),
   \[
   \mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{I_r(n)\le l\}\cup\{I_r(n)=n\}}\|A_r(n)\|_{op}^s]\to0.
   \]

Then \(\zeta_s(X_n,X)\to0\), where \(X\) is the unique fixed point of \(X\stackrel d=\sum_rA_r^*X^{(r)}+b^*\) in the piecewise-defined class \(\mathcal M_d^s(0,I_d)\) just described. Under the source normalization (22), for \(2<s\le3\) it centers by \(EY_n\) and uses the eventually positive-definite covariance; for \(1<s\le2\) it centers by \(EY_n\) and only requires a positive-definite scaling matrix; for \(0<s\le1\) no mean constraint is imposed and only a positive-definite scaling matrix is specified. Rendered original pp.8 and 11–12 were checked. Do not omit the small-index and self-index boundary condition (3), or replace the joint \(L_s\) convergence by weak convergence.

**Theorem 5.1 (univariate transfer).** For
\[
Y_n\stackrel d=\sum_{r=1}^K Y^{(r)}_{I_r(n)}+b_n,
\]
assume the recursive copies are independent of \((I^{(n)},b_n)\), \(P(I_r(n)=n)\to0\), and eventual positive variance. For functions \(f,g\) with \(g(n)>0\), require in \(L_s\)
\[
\left(g(I_r(n))/g(n)\right)^{1/2}\to A_r^*,\qquad
g(n)^{-1/2}\left(b_n-f(n)+\sum_r f(I_r(n))\right)\to b^*,
\]
and \(\mathbb E\sum_r(A_r^*)^s<1\), for some \(0<s\le3\). For \(2<s\le3\), also require \(EY_n=f(n)+o(\sqrt{g(n)})\) and \(\operatorname{Var}Y_n=g(n)+o(g(n))\); for \(1<s\le2\), require only the stated mean expansion; for \(0<s\le1\), Theorem 5.1 imposes no mean or variance asymptotic. Then \((Y_n-f(n))/\sqrt{g(n)}\) converges to the unique fixed point in the overloaded class \(\mathcal M_1^s(0,1)\): it has mean 0 and variance 1 only for \(2<s\le3\); for \(1<s\le2\) it has mean 0 but no fixed-variance constraint; for \(0<s\le1\) neither mean nor variance is constrained. The proof (physical p.21) takes \(M_n=EY_n\) for \(1<s\le3\) and \(M_n=f(n)\) for \(0<s\le1\), with \(\sigma_n=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y_n)}\) only for \(2<s\le3\) and \(\sigma_n=\sqrt{g(n)}\) for \(0<s\le2\). At \(s=2\), the variance is instead recovered as \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=\operatorname{Var}(X)g(n)+o(g(n))\), not as unit variance.

**Corollary 5.2 (normal limit).** For some \(2<s\le3\), if the preceding mean/variance and coefficient stabilization hold, the normalized toll tends to zero in \(L_s\), and
\[
\sum_r(A_r^*)^2=1\quad\text{a.s.},\qquad P(\exists r:A_r^*=1)<1,
\]
then \((Y_n-f(n))/\sqrt{g(n)}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\). The nondegeneracy clause and exact squared-coefficient normalization are part of the source statement.

## Proof chain actually read

- Section 3 proves affine-map invariance and contraction on fixed-moment classes using the \((s,+)\)-ideal property of \(\zeta_s\) (Lemmas 3.1–3.3, Corollary 3.4; pp. 6–10).
- For Theorem 4.1, the paper first obtains the limiting fixed point from the coefficient contraction. It builds an accompanying sequence \(Q_n\): finite-index recursive terms are retained, while indices past the cutoff use iid copies of the limit. The coefficient convergence plus the low-index/self-index boundary condition make \(Q_n\) close in \(L_s\) and then in \(\zeta_s\) to the limit. A recursive \(\zeta_s(X_n,Q_n)\) bound has a vanishing self-index coefficient and a limiting contraction coefficient. Boundedness followed by a limsup argument proves convergence (pp. 11–14).
- The univariate transfer theorem checks the vector theorem’s coefficient convergence by the two displayed stabilizations; its boundary condition follows by bounding the low-index term by \((l/n)^{s/2}\) and the self-index term by \(P(I_r(n)=n)\) (pp. 20–22).
- For the normal corollary, the zero-toll limit equation has standard normal as its fixed point under the exact variance identity; uniqueness follows from the fixed-point theorem (p. 22).

## Remaining paper coverage and usable interfaces

The full text also develops smoothing/local and global approximation metrics (Section 4.2, including Propositions 4.2/4.4 and Corollaries 4.3/4.5), random branching count \(K_n\) with \(K_n\to\infty\) (Theorem 4.6), algorithm/data-structure examples (Quickselect/Quicksort; m-ary search-tree path length and size; tries/digital trees; mergesort; maxima; recursive-tree path lengths), and multivariate urn/tree normal-limit examples (Section 5.4). Section 5.4 runs through physical p.39, where the references begin; the references occupy pp.39–41. The applications verify their own recurrence, toll, and moment expansions; they do not weaken Theorem 4.1’s hypotheses.

**Interface to W21.** This source can transfer a finite recursive distributional system to an affine limiting RDE when one has a genuine recursive decomposition, coefficient/toll convergence in the required moment metric, a strict operator-norm contraction, and control of small and full-size subproblems. It does not prove that a given nonlinear map on a simplex has such a recursive representation, identify a stationary kernel, or establish global attraction for an arbitrary law-update operator. Those bridges remain unproved until separately constructed.
