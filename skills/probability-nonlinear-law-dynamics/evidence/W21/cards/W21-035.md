## Model and generic operator

For a balanced urn with replacement matrix row sum \(K-1\ge1\), a tree embedding produces recursive systems for color counts. At each subtree split the random size proportions converge in all \(L_p\) to a Dirichlet vector \(D=(D_1,\ldots,D_K)\) with parameters \(((K-1)^{-1},\ldots,(K-1)^{-1})\). The limit operators are finite systems of affine RDEs
\[
X_i\stackrel d=\sum_{r=1}^{d'}A_{ir}Z_{ir}+b_i,
\]
where the child-law index is \(\pi(i,r)\), and coefficient/toll variables are independent of iid child samples. The paper works in product spaces of laws with prescribed moments, equipped with product metrics \(\zeta_s^\vee\) or complex \(\ell_2^\vee\), and proves three distinct regimes.

**Theorem 5.1, real nonnormal regime.** If all \(A_{ir},b_i\) are square-integrable real variables, \(Eb_i=0\), and
\[
\max_i\sum_{r=1}^{d'}E[A_{ir}^2]<1,
\]
then the real system map has a unique fixed point in \((\mathcal M_2^R(0))^d\). The proof conditions on coefficients and uses the \((2,+)\)-ideal property of \(\zeta_2\), giving contraction constant exactly \(\max_i\sum_rEA_{ir}^2\); Banach’s theorem applies on the complete fixed-mean, finite-variance product space.

**Theorem 5.2, normal regime.** If the real coefficients are in \(L_{2+\varepsilon}\) for some \(\varepsilon>0\), all tolls vanish, \(\sum_rA_{ir}^2=1\) a.s. for every equation i, and
\[
\min_iP\!\left(\max_r|A_{ir}|<1\right)>0,
\]
then for every \(\sigma^2\ge0\), the unique fixed point in \((\mathcal M_{2+\varepsilon}^R(0,\sigma^2))^d\) is \((N(0,\sigma^2),\ldots,N(0,\sigma^2))\). Proof chooses \(s=(2+\varepsilon)\wedge3>2\); the a.s. square-sum identity plus the positive-probability strict-shrink event imply \(\max_i\sum_rE|A_{ir}|^s<1\), so \(\zeta_s^\vee\) is contracting. The Gaussian tuple is checked directly to be fixed.

**Theorem 5.3, complex nonzero means.** For square-integrable complex coefficients and tolls, prescribed \(\gamma_i\in\mathbb C\) must satisfy
\[
Eb_i+\sum_r\gamma_{\pi(i,r)}E[A_{ir}]=\gamma_i.
\]
If \(\max_i\sum_rE|A_{ir}|^2<1\), the complex map has a unique fixed point in \(\prod_i\mathcal M_2^C(\gamma_i)\). The proof uses optimal couplings and independence to eliminate cross terms in the squared \(\ell_2\) cost, obtaining contraction factor \((\max_i\sum_rE|A_{ir}|^2)^{1/2}\).

## Urn regimes and exact example interfaces

- **Balanced urn tree split (Lemma 2.1).** The discrete subtree size proportions converge to the stated Dirichlet vector, in all finite moments; this supplies the random coefficients and tolls after scaling.
- **Deterministic two-color urn (Theorems 6.1–6.2).** Under the source’s balanced 2×2 replacement assumptions and \(bc>0\), if \(a-c>(a+b)/2\), the mean has a secondary order \(n^\lambda\), \(\lambda=(a-c)/(a+b)>1/2\); centering by the exact mean and scaling by \(n^\lambda\) gives a unique nonnormal RDE limit in the centered finite-variance product class. If \(a-c<(a+b)/2\), the variance is linear and the standardized limits are normal; proof uses \(\zeta_3\), since the \(\zeta_2\) coefficient limit is exactly 1. The boundary \(a-c=(a+b)/2\) has extra logarithmic variance factors and is discussed as still covered by their proof technique. The case \(bc=0\) is explicitly excluded and behaves differently.
- **Random two-color replacement (Theorems 6.4/6.6).** For Bernoulli replacement probabilities \(\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)\), the nonnormal regime is \(\alpha+\beta>3/2\) with exponent \(\lambda=\alpha+\beta-1>1/2\); normal regime is \(\alpha+\beta<3/2\) with variance normalization. At equality, logarithmic variance factors enter and the authors state their method still applies. The nonlinear two-color recurrences are kept as a system of marginal law recursions; no arbitrary joint coupling is assumed.
- **Cyclic urn (Theorem 6.10).** For m types and \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/m}=\lambda+i\mu\), m≥7 gives \(\lambda>1/2\). The fixed-point system has coefficients \(U^\omega,(1-U)^\omega\), \(U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\). Lemma 6.8 proves unique system fixed point in prescribed complex-mean classes using \(2/(1+2\lambda)<1\). Lemma 6.9’s scalar law is exactly
\[
X\stackrel d=U^\omega X+\omega(1-U)^\omega X',\qquad
X,X',U\ \text{independent},\quad U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1],
\]
with \(X,X'\) iid and law in \(\mathcal M_2^C(2/[m\Gamma(\omega+1)])\). Then \(\Lambda^{[j]}\stackrel d=\omega^{j-1}\Lambda\). Theorem 6.10 proves the actual distributional approximation in \(\ell_2\):
\[
\ell_2\!\left(X_n^{[j]},\operatorname{Re}\{e^{i(\mu\ln n+2\pi(j-1)/m)}\Lambda\}\right)\to0.
\]
The conclusion is asymptotically periodic after phase rotation, not convergence to one fixed real law. Its proof couples the discrete split \(I_n=\lfloor nU\rfloor\) to the fixed point, uses dominated convergence for coefficient mismatch, and a limsup contraction with factor \(\sqrt{2/(1+2\lambda)}<1\). A local SymPy check integrates \(\int_0^1u^{2\lambda}du\), recovering the displayed source contraction constant and the condition \(\lambda>1/2\); for the boundary example m=7 it evaluates to 0.8900837358252576. This is a symbolic/numerical consistency record only; the all-m proof remains the source’s inequality \(\cos(2\pi/m)>1/2\). ## W21 transfer boundary

These theorems establish fixed-point uniqueness and asymptotics only after the urn-derived affine RDE, scaling, moment classes, and coefficient identities are verified. The source emphasizes that a multivariate coupling of marginal counts may create artificial covariance assumptions; it avoids requiring such covariance in the marginal-system method. It does not prove a general law-operator result on a simplex or rule out periodic behavior in other nonlinear dynamics. In a W21 application, every coefficient/toll, independence relation, scaling, and fixed-moment class must be derived from the target recursion; the cyclic example specifically warns against assuming a single stationary limit when a rotating phase remains.
