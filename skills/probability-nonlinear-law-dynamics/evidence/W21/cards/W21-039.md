## Model and norm conventions

Let $P$ be a Markov transition operator on a general state space $\mathcal S$; row probability measures act on the right. The paper uses full variation norm $\|q\|=|q|(\mathcal S)$, twice the set-supremum convention for probability laws. For signed zero-mass $q$, the ergodicity coefficient is
\[
\tau(A)=\sup_{\substack{q\in M_0\\\|q\|=1}}\|qA\|
=\tfrac12\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal S}\|(\delta_x-\delta_y)A\|,
\]
where $M_0=\{q:q(\mathcal S)=0\}$; $\tau(A)\le1$ and $\tau(A_1A_2)\le\tau(A_1)\tau(A_2)$. In a finite state space this is the induced $\ell^1$ norm on signed row vectors and the maximum absolute row-sum norm on matrices.

The base chain is **uniformly ergodic** in the paper's sense if it has a unique invariant law $\pi$ and constants $C<\infty$, $0<\rho<1$ with
\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal S}\|\delta_xP^n-\pi\|\le C\rho^n
\quad(n\in\mathbb Z_+).
\tag{2.2}
\]
This implies $C\ge1$. Uniformity over starting states and the geometric rate are assumptions; uniqueness alone does not suffice.

## Theorem 3.1 and stationary-law sensitivity

Let $\widetilde P$ be another transition operator, $E=\widetilde P-P$, $p_n=p_0P^n$, $\widetilde p_n=\widetilde p_0\widetilde P^n$, and $z_n=\widetilde p_n-p_n$. Under (2.2), put
\[
\widehat n=\left\lceil\log_{\rho}(C^{-1})\right\rceil.
\]
Theorem 3.1 (physical p.3, printed p.1005; full statement and proof pp.3–5) gives
\[
\|z_n\|\le
\begin{cases}
\|z_0\|+n\|E\|,&n\le\widehat n,\\
C\rho^n\|z_0\|+
\left(\widehat n+C\rho^{\widehat n}
\frac{1-\rho^{n-\widehat n}}{1-\rho}\right)\|E\|,
&n\ge\widehat n+1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Corollary 3.1, (3.8) (physical p.4, printed p.1006; proof p.5/print 1007) says that **if** the perturbed chain has an invariant law $\widetilde\pi$, then
\[
\|\widetilde\pi-\pi\|\le
\left(\widehat n+\frac{C\rho^{\widehat n}}{1-\rho}\right)
\|\widetilde P-P\|.
\tag{3.8}
\]
The proof telescopes
\[
z_n=z_0P^n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\widetilde p_i E P^{n-i-1},
\]
uses zero-mass contraction by $\tau(\cdot)$, then sums the initial unit-bound segment and geometric tail $C\rho^i$. Uniform ergodicity of the perturbed chain is not assumed for this inequality; in finite state space an invariant law exists, but uniqueness is separate.

Theorem 3.2, (3.11) (physical p.5, printed p.1007) gives another bound: if $m\in\mathbb N$ has $\tau_m:=\tau(P^m)<1$ and $\widetilde\pi$ is any invariant law of $\widetilde P$, then
\[
\|\widetilde\pi-\pi\|\le
\frac{\|\widetilde P^m-P^m\|}{1-\tau_m}.
\tag{3.11}
\]
The paper also gives transient bounds in Theorems 3.2–3.3 and finite-state convergence estimates in Section 4; those are not needed for the transfer below.

## Conditional transfer to A121/A122

For A121's order-statistic kernel $K_\theta(x,\cdot)$, draw $k$ iid samples from $\theta$ and select the $j$-th nearest to current state $x$, with the specified tie-breaking randomness. Couple each sample under $\theta$ and $\eta$ maximally and use the same tie-break randomizer. The product coupling's mismatch probability is at most $k\,d_{TV}^{\rm usual}(\theta,\eta)$; common randomized selection cannot increase total variation. In C037's full-variation convention this proves
\[
\|K_\theta-K_\eta\|_{\mathrm{op}}
:=\sup_x\|K_\theta(x,\cdot)-K_\eta(x,\cdot)\|
\le k\|\theta-\eta\|.
\]
This is a derived coupling lemma, not a statement in C037. Combining with (3.8), **provided base kernel $K_\eta$ satisfies (2.2)** and $K_\theta$ has an invariant law, yields
\[
\|\pi(K_\theta)-\pi(K_\eta)\|
\le k\left(\widehat n_\eta+
\frac{C_\eta\rho_\eta^{\widehat n_\eta}}{1-\rho_\eta}\right)
\|\theta-\eta\|.
\]
Alternatively, if $\tau_m(K_\eta)<1$, telescoping powers in (3.11) gives constant $mk/(1-\tau_m(K_\eta))$. A global Lipschitz conclusion requires uniform constants (e.g. common $C,\rho$, or common $m$ and uniform positive gap $1-\tau_m$) over the parameter region. The row-TV estimate alone supplies no mixing constants. Near boundary parameter laws, irreducibility, aperiodicity, and uniform ergodicity remain to be proved. Continuity/Lipschitz control alone does not imply almost-everywhere convergence of the outer iteration.

An exact rational three-state, $k=3,j=2$ calculation is saved in ../reader_batches/L08_B05/calculations/c037_rank_kernel_tv.py and its JSON output. It yields $\|K_\theta-K_\eta\|_{\mathrm{op}}=13/27\le1=k\|\theta-\eta\|$ for the chosen laws and tie rule. This checks one finite example only; the coupling proof is general, and neither establishes (2.2).
