## Centered-law contraction

Let `D` be the probability laws on `R` with finite second moment and mean zero, equipped with the `W₂` metric. For independent `τ~Unif[0,1]` and iid `X, X̄~F∈D`, define

`S(F)=Law(τX+(1−τ)X̄+C(τ))`,

`C(u)=2u log u+2(1−u)log(1−u)+1` (with endpoint values by continuity). The paper checks `E C(τ)=0`, so `S` maps `D` to itself.

**Theorem 2.1 (printed p.91; PDF p.8).** `S` is a strict contraction on the complete metric space `(D,W₂)`, with the exact bound

`W₂(S(F),S(G))² ≤ E[τ²+(1−τ)²] W₂(F,G)² = (2/3)W₂(F,G)²`.

Thus the contraction factor is `√(2/3)`. Proof: couple an optimal `(X,Y)` for `F,G` with an independent copy `(X̄,Ȳ)`, use the same `τ` and additive `C(τ)` in both outputs, expand the squared difference, and use independence plus centering to remove the cross term. Since `Eτ²=E(1−τ)²=1/3`, the coefficient is `2/3`. The Banach fixedpoint theorem gives a unique centered finite-variance fixedpoint and geometric convergence of every `S^n(F)` to it. On PDF p.9, the source prints the Cauchy estimate `d(S^mF,S^nF)≤3d(F,SF)(2/3)^(m/2)` for `m≤n`, after bounding `Σ_{j=m}^{n−1}q^j` by `3q^m`, where `q=√(2/3)`. That displayed geometric-series inequality is not valid: already `1+q+q²+q³>3`. The valid generic bound is `Σ_{j=m}^{n−1}q^j≤q^m/(1−q)=(3+√6)q^m`. Thus the printed prefactor 3 is unsupported by this summation; replacing it by `3+√6` still proves the Cauchy property and geometric convergence. This local error does not affect the contraction theorem or Banach fixedpoint conclusion.

## Quicksort limit and tail argument

For randomized Quicksort, `X_n` counts comparisons and `Y_n=(X_n−E X_n)/n`. Conditioning on the uniform pivot rank `Z_n` gives independent left/right subproblem costs and the finite recursive law equation (1.2), with deterministic centering term `C_n(Z_n)`. As `n→∞`, `Z_n/n⇒τ` uniform and `C_n(⌈nτ⌉)→C(τ)` uniformly. Theorem 3.1 (printed pp.92–95; PDF pp.9–12) proves `Law(Y_n)→F*` in `W₂`, where `F*` is the unique fixedpoint of `S`: it compares the finite recursive operator with `S`, uses the uniform convergence of the toll term and a recursive square-error inequality, then applies a scalar sequence lemma to force the error to zero.

Section 4 proves uniform exponential-moment bounds: for each `L>0`, some `K_L` bounds `E exp(λY_n)` for all `n` and `|λ|≤L`. Passing to the limit gives finite Laplace transforms of the fixedpoint for every real parameter. With `E X_n/n∼2 log n`, Markov's inequality yields `P(X_n≥2E X_n)≤C(λ)n^(−2λ)` for every fixed `λ>0`, so this bad-performance probability decays faster than any fixed polynomial order. Section 5 gives higher-moment recurrences, an infinite-tree-sum representation, and a k-median variant under its corresponding pivot law.

## Transfer boundary
