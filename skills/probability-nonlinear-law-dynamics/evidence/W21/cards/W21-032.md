## Operator, topology, and exact scope

For a deterministic real sequence \(T=(T_j)_{j\ge1}\) and constant \(C\), the map is
\[
K(\mu)=\mathcal L\left(\sum_{j\ge1}T_jW_j+C\right),\qquad W_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mu.
\]
The countable sum is defined through convergence in distribution of partial sums; ordering/conditional convergence matters. Main results first take \(C=0\). Except the elementary cases \(T_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\), the paper’s standing nontrivial regime (A) requires \(0<\sup_j|T_j|<1\), \(T_j\to0\), and at least two nonzero weights. If a nontrivial fixed point exists, there is a unique characteristic exponent \(\alpha\in(0,2]\) satisfying \(\sum_j|T_j|^\alpha=1\).

The proof leaves ordinary Wasserstein/contraction iteration: it proves infinite divisibility via weighted branching, writes the Lévy measure as a \(\Lambda\)-harmonic measure for \(\Lambda=\sum_j\delta_{T_j}\), then applies Deny/Choquet–Deny decomposition on the closed multiplicative group \(G_\Lambda\) generated by the weights. The classification is in characteristic functions and depends on which group the weights generate; it is not one universal stable-law formula.

**Theorems 7.1–7.2, continuous groups.** For \(G_\Lambda=\mathbb R_*\), the fixed-point characteristic functions are:

- If \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), or \(\alpha\in(1,2]\) and \(\sum_jT_j\ne1\): \(\exp(-c|t|^\alpha)\), \(c\ge0\) (symmetric stable family).
- If \(\alpha\in(1,2]\) and \(\sum_jT_j=1\): \(\exp(i\gamma t-c|t|^\alpha)\), \(c\ge0,\gamma\in\mathbb R\).

For \(G_\Lambda=\mathbb R_+\): at \(\alpha=1\), \(\exp(i\gamma t-c|t|)\); for \(\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)\), the stable skew family \(\exp[-c|t|^\alpha(1+i\beta\operatorname{sgn}(t)\omega(t,\alpha))]\), \(\beta\in[-1,1]\), with the source’s location restriction \(\gamma=0\); at \(\alpha=2\), centered Gaussians \(\exp(-\sigma^2t^2/2)\). The formula distinguishes \(\alpha=1\), \(\alpha=2\), and the remaining indices.

**Theorem 7.3, discrete groups.** For \(G_\Lambda\) one of the three discrete multiplicative subgroups listed in the paper, the fixed characteristic functions for \(\alpha\in(0,2)\) have the exact form
\[
\varphi(t)=\exp\!\left(it\gamma+c\iint(e^{itsu}-1-it\chi(su))|u|^{-\alpha}\,\lambda_{G_\Lambda}(du)\,\nu(ds)\right),
\]
where \(\nu\) is a probability measure on \(\mathbb R_*/G_\Lambda\), \(c\ge0\), \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and \(\chi\) is the paper’s truncation function. The parameters must satisfy its exact drift condition: with
\[
F_{s,c}(x)=c\int(x\chi(su)-\chi(xsu))|u|^{-\alpha}\lambda_{G_\Lambda}(du),
\quad \gamma=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^n\left(\gamma T_j+\int F_{s,c}(T_j)\nu(ds)\right).
\]
At \(\alpha=2\), Theorem 7.3(b), PDF p.16, prints \(\exp(it\gamma-\sigma^2t^2/2)\) when \(\sum_jT_j=1\), but literally prints \(\exp(+\sigma^2t^2/2)\) when \(\sum_jT_j\ne1\). For \(\sigma^2>0\), the latter has modulus greater than 1 and cannot be a characteristic function. The immediately following source prose calls this branch centered normal, and its Gaussian classification elsewhere has the negative sign; the mathematically consistent consequence is the centered Gaussian \(\exp(-\sigma^2t^2/2)\). The positive exponent is recorded as a source display error, not silently corrected. Theorem 7.4 gives the exact discrete-group classifications for \(\alpha\in(0,2)\). Write \(M_\Lambda\) for all probability measures on the paper’s representative of \(\mathbb R_*/G_\Lambda\), \(M^s_\Lambda\) for symmetric ones, and \(M^0_\Lambda\) for the paper’s centered/weighted-centered class (defined separately at \(\alpha=1\) and \(\alpha\ne1\)). Then:

- D1: if \(\alpha>1\) and \(\sum_jT_j=1\), triples are \(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty)\times M_\Lambda\); if \(\alpha\le1\) or \(\sum_jT_j\ne1\), triples are \(\{0\}\times[0,\infty)\times M_\Lambda\).
- D2: if \(\alpha=1\) (so \(\sum_jT_j=1\)), triples are \(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty)\times M^0_\Lambda\); if \(\alpha\ne1\), every \((c,\nu)\in[0,\infty)\times M_\Lambda\) has exactly one allowed \(\gamma\).
- D3: if \(\alpha=1\), or if \(\alpha\ne1\) and \(\sum_jT_j=\sum_j\operatorname{sgn}(T_j)|T_j|^\alpha\), triples are \(\{0\}\times[0,\infty)\times M_\Lambda\). If \(\alpha\ne1\) and \(\sum_jT_j=1\), triples are \(\mathbb R\times[0,\infty)\times M^0_\Lambda\). If \(\alpha\ne1\) and \(\sum_jT_j\) exists in \(\mathbb R\cup\{\pm\infty\}\) but equals neither of those two sums, every \((c,\nu)\in(0,\infty)\times M_\Lambda\) has exactly one allowed \(\gamma\). If \(\alpha>1\) and \(\sum_jT_j\) does not exist, triples are \(\{0\}\times[0,\infty)\times M^0_\Lambda\).

Do not replace the quotient-space mixture/drift restriction with “all stable laws.”

**Nonzero constant.** Theorem 9.2: under (A) and convergence \(\sum_jT_j\to s\ne1\), fixed points for \(C\ne0\) are the corresponding zero-constant family shifted by \(C/(1-s)\). Theorem 9.3 gives no fixed point in its stated cases where \(\sum_jT_j\) fails to converge or equals 1 (including the paper’s named cases C1/D1 and \(\alpha=2\)).

**Theorem 9.4 (PDF p.26).** Under \(\alpha\in(0,2)\), \(\sum_jT_j=1\), and \(C\ne0\), there are three cases; (a) is continuous, not discrete. (a) If \(G_\Lambda=\mathbb R_+\) (C2), necessarily \(\alpha=1\). The theorem's displayed set is \(\exp(i\gamma t-c|t|(1+\beta\operatorname{sgn}(t)\omega(t,1)))\), with \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), \((\beta,c)\in\Theta_{\Lambda,C}\), and \(\Theta_{\Lambda,C}=\{(\beta,c)\in[-1,1]\times\mathbb R_+:(2\beta c/\pi)\sum_jT_j\log T_j=C\}\). The proof immediately below instead derives the standard stable characteristic function \(\exp(i\gamma t-c|t|(1+i\beta\operatorname{sgn}(t)\omega(t,1)))\); the displayed theorem formula omits this \(i\), so preserve it as a source statement/proof discrepancy rather than silently repairing it. (b) If \(G_\Lambda=r^{\mathbb Z}\) (D2), necessarily \(\alpha=1\); functions of (7.1) are allowed for \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), \((c,\nu)\in\mathbb R_+\times(M_\Lambda\setminus M^0_\Lambda)\), subject to \(C+c\int s\,\nu(ds)\sum_jT_j\log T_j=0\); the class is empty if \(\sum_jT_j|\log T_j|=\infty\). (c) If \(G_\Lambda=(-r)^{\mathbb Z}\) (D3), necessarily \(\alpha\ne1\); functions of (7.1) are allowed for \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), \((c,\nu)\in\mathbb R_+\times(M_\Lambda\setminus M^0_\Lambda)\), subject to
\[
C+c\int \operatorname{sgn}(s)|s|^\alpha\Delta(s)\,\nu(ds)\left(1-\sum_j\operatorname{sgn}(T_j)|T_j|^\alpha\right)=0,
\]
where \(\Delta\) is defined in (8.8). The proof obtains the D2/D3 drift equations from (9.5), equivalently (7.3) with \(C\) added; these are group-specific conditions, not an unrestricted stable-law classification.

## Proof chain actually read

1. Section 2 establishes closure under convolution/reflection and isolates elementary weight cases.
2. Section 3 obtains the exponent relation and weighted-branching representation. The iteration yields infinite divisibility; the Lévy measure satisfies a harmonic identity for multiplicative convolution and is controlled near zero/infinity.
3. Sections 4–6 derive the characteristic-function equation and use Deny/Choquet–Deny harmonic-measure structure to disintegrate over cosets of \(G_\Lambda\).
4. Sections 7–8 evaluate continuous and discrete group cases; the Lévy–Khintchine formula gives stable or group-periodic mixtures, while the drift condition filters which parameters are allowed.
5. Section 9 shifts or excludes fixed points when \(C\ne0\); Section 10 and Appendix A supply ancillary lemmas and the Deny/Choquet–Deny statements.

## W21 transfer boundary

This is a solution-classification method for a *linear weighted-sum RDE with deterministic coefficients* and an infinite-divisibility/harmonic-measure mechanism. It does not provide uniqueness in arbitrary moment classes by strict metric contraction, nor does it apply to random coefficients, nonlinear rational simplex updates, minima/maxima, or a stationary Markov kernel unless those are separately reduced to this exact weighted-sum structure and all convergence/drift conditions are proved. The classification can inform a candidate fixed-law analysis only after those prerequisites are established.
