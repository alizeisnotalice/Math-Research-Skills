## Nonlinear Markov update and sharp Dobrushin criterion

The discrete-time process has law-dependent transition kernel `P_μ` and law update

`N(μ) = μ P_μ`,  `μ_(n+1) = N(μ_n)`.

The paper uses `d_TV(μ,ν)=2 sup_A |μ(A)−ν(A)|`. Its global cross-state/law condition (2.3) is

`sup_(μ,ν) d_TV(P_μ(x,·), P_ν(y,·)) ≤ 2(1−α)` for all `x,y` and some `α>0`.

The same-state law-sensitivity condition (2.4) is

`d_TV(P_μ(x,·), P_ν(x,·)) ≤ λ d_TV(μ,ν)` for every `x,μ,ν`.

**Theorem 2.2 (PDF p.4; proof pp.6–7).** If `0≤λ≤α`, the nonlinear chain has a unique invariant law `π`. If `λ<α`,

`d_TV(μ_n,π) ≤ 2(1−(α−λ))^n`;

if `λ=α`, `d_TV(μ_n,π) ≤ 2/(λn)` for `n≥1`. The restriction is sharp: for every `0<α<λ≤1`, the paper constructs a two-state chain with a continuum of invariant laws, a countable-state chain with no invariant law, and a chain whose iterates fail to converge.

The proof first splits two laws into their common part and residuals. Condition (2.4) controls the common-mass contribution; condition (2.3) forces overlap between the residual transition mixtures. This gives the one-step estimate

`d_(n+1) ≤ d_n(1−α+λ−(λ/2)d_n)`,  `d_n=d_TV(μ_n,ν_n)`.

For `λ<α`, discard the last negative term and iterate the strict linear bound. At `λ=α`, the quadratic decrement gives the `2/(λn)` rate. Completeness of probability measures in total variation makes each orbit Cauchy; the same inequality gives invariance and uniqueness. The counterexamples in the second half of the proof show why the threshold cannot be relaxed.

## McKean–Vlasov perturbation result

For the SDE `dX_t=b(X_t,μ_t)dt+dW_t`, `μ_t=Law(X_t)`, the drift is `b_1(x)+εb_2(x,μ)`. The source assumes Lipschitz dependence in `x` and the truncated 2-Wasserstein distance `ρ_2(μ,ν)=(inf E(|X−Y|²∧1))^(1/2)`, a Veretennikov–Khasminskii dissipativity condition `⟨b_1(x),x⟩≤−r|x|` outside a ball, and a uniform bound `sup_(x,μ)|b_2(x,μ)|≤D`.

**Theorem 3.1 (statement PDF p.5; proof pp.9–15).** For sufficiently small `ε∈[0,ε_0]`, there is a unique invariant law `π_ε`; if `I(μ_0)=∫e^{|x|}μ_0(dx)<∞`, then

`d_TV(μ_t^ε,π_ε) ≤ C(ε)(1+I(μ_0))e^(−θ(ε)t)`.

Proof chain: choose a Lyapunov function `V` and a sampling time `T`; prove drift bounds for `V` and `V²`; obtain a local Dobrushin condition on a Lyapunov sublevel set by applying a diffusion Harnack inequality to the unperturbed process; use Girsanov and Pinsker to keep this local overlap under small bounded perturbations; apply the Hairer–Mattingly weighted-TV contraction lemma to `P_T^μ(ε)μ`. A uniform moment estimate extends the contraction from bounded-moment initial laws and the sampled semigroup to the claimed invariant law and continuous-time rate. The paper notes that the global condition (2.3) alone is insufficient for nonlinear chains; Example 2.1 already has a stationary law and a 2-cycle.

## Transfer boundary

The first theorem is a direct method template for a law-dependent kernel update `μ↦μP_μ`; its assumptions are quantitative and include both cross-state overlap and same-state law sensitivity. For A122, the equation `θK_θ=θ` has this invariant-law form when `P_μ=K_μ`, but no proof currently verifies (2.3)–(2.4) for the target kernels. It does **not** prove convergence of A121's outer map `θ↦π(K_θ)`. The SDE result concerns a continuous-time self-consistent diffusion with dissipative drift and small bounded interaction, not the finite-state stationary-law map.
