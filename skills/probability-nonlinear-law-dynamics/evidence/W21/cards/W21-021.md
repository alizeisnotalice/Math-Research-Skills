## Operator and majorization order

On \(S^{m-1}=\{x\ge0:\sum_i x_i=1\}\), the QSO is
\[
(Vx)_k=\sum_{i,j=1}^m p_{ij,k}x_ix_j,\quad
p_{ij,k}=p_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad \sum_kp_{ij,k}=1.
\]
Write \(x_{[1]}\ge\cdots\ge x_{[m]}\) for decreasing rearrangement. The paper calls \(V\) doubly stochastic when \(Vx\prec x\) for every \(x\in S^{m-1}\), i.e. every leading partial sum of the rearranged \(Vx\) is no larger than the corresponding partial sum of \(x\). This is a property of the full quadratic map, not of each coefficient slice separately.

For each output coordinate \(k\), let \(A_k=(p_{ij,k})_{i,j}\), a symmetric nonnegative matrix. Define \(U_1=\{A=A^T\ge0:\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}=m,\ \sum_{i,j\in\alpha}a_{ij}\le|\alpha|\ \forall\alpha\subseteq I\}\), where \(I=\{1,\ldots,m\}\). The sum equality is essential.

## Exact results and proof chain

**Theorem 2.1 (physical pp.2–4, necessary conditions).** If \(V\) is doubly stochastic, then for every \(k\),
\[
\sum_{i,j}p_{ij,k}=m,\quad
\sum_j p_{ij,k}\ge\tfrac12\ \ (\forall i),\quad
\sum_{i,j\in\alpha}p_{ij,k}\le|\alpha|\ \ (\forall\alpha\subseteq I).
\]
The proof evaluates \(V\) at the uniform vector to get the total mass; evaluates at vectors uniform on a chosen subset \(\alpha\) and uses the majorization relation to obtain the subset inequality; then combines the total and subset bounds to get the row lower bound. These are necessary slice-wise conditions, not the final necessary-and-sufficient criterion.

**Theorem 3.4 (physical pp.7–9; symmetrized stochastic matrix).** The paper considers \(A=\frac12(T+T^T)\), with \(T\) a row-stochastic matrix. Necessity of every subset inequality follows by summing entries on \(\alpha\times\alpha\). Potential statement-scope gap for independent review: the printed theorem lists symmetry, nonnegativity, and the subset inequalities, but does not display the total-mass equality. The immediately preceding definition of U_1 on physical p.4 includes that equality, and the sufficiency proof on pp.7–9 begins by assuming A∈U_1. If the theorem is read as a standalone assertion with only its displayed conditions, A=0 satisfies the subset bounds but cannot be the symmetrization of a row-stochastic matrix, since every such symmetrization has total entry sum m. Whether the U_1 restriction is intended as a standing domain hypothesis remains unresolved; do not label this an author error before R02 checks that scope. Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 4.3 state the normalization explicitly and are the safe formulations to use meanwhile.

The sufficiency proof intended for \(A\in U_1\) studies extreme points of the finite-dimensional polytope \(U_1\). It proves the extreme-point entry restriction (diagonal entries \(0\) or \(1\), off-diagonal entries \(0,1/2,1\)), handles the stochastic case directly, and otherwise uses a saturated \((m-1)\)-minor and induction on matrix order to construct \(T\). Convexity then extends the representation to all \(A\in U_1\).

**Theorem 3.6 (physical pp.10–11; checked from the rendered display).** For a symmetric nonnegative \(A\), the quadratic-form bound
\[
x_{[m]}\le(Ax,x)\le x_{[1]}\quad\forall x\in S^{m-1}
\]
holds iff \(\sum_{i,j\in\alpha}a_{ij}\le|\alpha|\) for every \(\alpha\subseteq I\) **and** equality holds for \(\alpha=I\), i.e. \(\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}=m\). Sufficiency uses the symmetrization representation \(A=\frac12(T+T^T)\) and the stochastic row bounds \(x_{[m]}\le\sum_jt_{ij}x_j\le x_{[1]}\); necessity tests the uniform vector on each subset, including \(I\). This gives an explicit normalized formulation without relying on whether \(U_1\) is an intended standing restriction for the preceding theorem.

**Theorem 3.8 (physical pp.11–13).** Let \(T_k\) consist of matrices \(T=(t_{ij})\) with \(0\le t_{ij}\le1\) and each row sum \(k\), for \(1\le k\le m\), and \(U_k=\{\frac12(T+T^T):T\in T_k\}\). For \(A\in U_k\) and \(x\in S^{m-1}\),
\[
\sum_{i=m-k+1}^m x_{[i]}\le(Ax,x)\le\sum_{i=1}^k x_{[i]}.
\]
The proof reorders coordinates and shifts coefficients \(\lambda_i\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_i\lambda_i=k\) to show their weighted sum lies between the \(k\) smallest and \(k\) largest coordinates. Each stochastic row of \(T\) then gives the claimed bound; the reverse ordering comes from \(T^T\) in the symmetrization.

**Corollary 4.3 (physical p.16; necessary and sufficient condition for a DSQO).** Writing \(V=(A_1|\cdots|A_m)\), \(V\) is doubly stochastic iff
\[
A_k\in U_1\quad(k=1,\ldots,m),\qquad \sum_{k=1}^m A_k=E,
\]
where \(E\) is the all-ones matrix. Theorem 4.1 (pp.14–15) proves sufficiency constructively: for a given \(A_1\in U_1\), choose \(A_2=\cdots=A_m=(E-A_1)/(m-1)\); represent \(A_1=\frac12(T_1+T_1^T)\) and \(T_2=\cdots=T_m=(E-T_1)/(m-1)\); entrywise bounds show the sum of any \(k\) of the \(T_i\)'s has entries in \([0,1]\) and row sums \(k\), hence the corresponding \(A_i\)'s satisfy the required subset conditions. Corollary 4.2 extends the completion argument. Corollary 4.4 concludes the set of DSQOs is a convex polytope.

Section 4 further treats extreme points: Theorem 4.5's displayed conclusion says the permuted operator lies in \(\operatorname{extr}U\), while its proof works by contradiction against membership in \(\operatorname{extr}B\); the statement/proof notation differs; keep this as a reviewer check and do not silently substitute one set for the other. Theorem 4.6 gives a sufficient extremality criterion; Theorem 4.8 treats \(m=3\), and Corollary 4.9 reports \(|\operatorname{extr}B|=222\). These finite-dimensional extremal claims are not needed for the basic majorization interface.

## W21 interface and limits

The usable methods are: translate \(Vx\prec x\) into coefficient-slice subset inequalities; certify a slice is the symmetrization of a stochastic matrix only with the full-mass equality; bound \((Ax,x)\) by sorted simplex coordinates; and prove quadratic-form order through a matrix representation. Theorem 3.4's printed statement may be missing a total-mass scope condition; its intended standing hypothesis remains for R02 review, as described above. No result here shows a general rank-based stationary-law operator is quadratic, doubly stochastic, or majorization-decreasing, and the paper's majorization property alone should not be upgraded to orbit convergence.
