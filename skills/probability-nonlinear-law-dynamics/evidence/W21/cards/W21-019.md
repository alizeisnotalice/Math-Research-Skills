## Operator and topology

A quadratic stochastic operator (QSO) on \(S^{m-1}\) has
\[
(Vx)_k=\sum_{i,j=1}^m P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,\quad
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1.
\]
It is Volterra when offspring type must equal one of the two parent types: \(P_{ij,k}=0\) if \(k\notin\{i,j\}\). The paper writes such a map as
\[
(Vx)_k=x_k\left(1+\sum_i a_{ki}x_i\right),
\]
where \(A=(a_{ki})\) is skew-symmetric and \(a_{ki}\in[-1,1]\). The topology is the usual Euclidean norm on the finite-dimensional simplex, \(\|x\|_2=(\sum_i x_i^2)^{1/2}\).

The source calls \(A\) transversal when all even-order “leading (principal)” minors are nonzero; it states the transversal Volterra operators form an open, everywhere-dense subset. On physical p.4 it explicitly says that from then on it considers transversal Volterra operators without repeating transversality, so this is the global standing assumption for the p.5 main theorem. For a transversal \(A\), every off-diagonal \(a_{ij}\ne0\), so it defines a tournament: edge \(i\to j\) iff \(a_{ij}<0\), otherwise \(j\to i\). A tournament is transitive if it has no directed 3-cycle.

## Definitions and theorem

Regularity means every orbit \(V^n x\) converges to a fixed point (the limit can depend on \(x\)). For \(x\in S^{m-1}\), \(\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{i:x_i\ne0\}\). The paper's Ergodic principle on the simplex means that for every \(x,y\in S^{m-1}\) with equal support,
\[
\|V^n x-V^n y\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]

**Main Theorem (physical p.5; unnumbered).** For a Volterra QSO \(V:S^{m-1}\to S^{m-1}\) in the source's standing scope, the following are equivalent:

1. \(V\) is regular.
2. \(V\)'s tournament is transitive.
3. \(V\) satisfies the Ergodic principle.
4. For every \(x\in S^{m-1}\), \(\|V^{n+1}x-V^n x\|_2\to0\).

The article states that transitivity implies regularity was already known in [3–4], and presents the converse plus the Ergodic-principle characterization as its main result. Those cited proofs are not included in this five-page source and were not independently audited here. **The five-page manuscript contains no proof of this equivalence theorem**; it ends the statement with acknowledgements and references. Therefore the card records the exact theorem and its cited context but does not supply or reconstruct a proof chain that is absent from this source.

The introduction labels a displayed QSO on the 2-simplex “not Volterra” and says its interior trajectories (except the center) and their Cesàro means do not converge, although it has no non-fixed periodic points. Directly factoring its displayed coordinates gives \(x'_1=x_1(1+x_2-x_3),\ x'_2=x_2(1+x_3-x_1),\ x'_3=x_3(1+x_1-x_2)\), so it is in fact Volterra. Its skew matrix has off-diagonal entries \(\pm1\), all three 2-by-2 principal minors equal \(1\), and a cyclic tournament; it is therefore transversal in the source's stated three-type sense. The introductory “not Volterra” classification conflicts with the displayed formula. The cyclic example is consistent with the main theorem's stated nonregular side, rather than a counterexample to that theorem. The manuscript cites other works for the trajectory/Cesàro facts and does not prove them here.

## W21 interface and limits

The theorem is a structural criterion for a *Volterra quadratic polynomial* with a tournament representation and the stated transversality convention. It does not imply convergence for a general simplex self-map, a rational stationary-law map, or a law update that has not been shown to be Volterra. No constants, convergence rates, or quantitative stability bounds are stated.
