## Proposition 2: continuity under a no-distance-tie condition

**Proposition 2 (statement, PDF p.7; proof, pp.8–9).** For `theta`-almost every fixed `s in S`, assume the random variable `d(s,Y)`, where `Y` has law `theta`, has a non-atomic distribution. Equivalently, for iid `Y_1,Y_2,Y_3 ~ theta`, the displayed no-distance-tie condition is

`P(d(Y_1,Y_2) = d(Y_1,Y_3)) = 0`.

Then for any `theta_n => theta`, `pi_{j,k}(theta_n) => pi_{j,k}(theta)`.

**Proof mechanism.** First prove finite-time weak convergence by induction, coupling initial points and fresh samples so the sampled configurations converge. The no-tie assumption makes the probability of a rank-switching near-tie event tend to zero. Then use the `k theta` domination/support bound from the shared Theorem 1 and its uniform-in-`theta` frozen-chain mixing tail to pass from finite-time laws to stationary laws. This yields continuity at the specified no-tie laws only; it is not global continuity. The same manuscript gives an example where ties prevent the kernel from being Feller.

## A121: finite-space conjecture, not theorem

**Conjecture 5 (PDF pp.14–15).** For every finite metric space with distinct nonzero pairwise distances and fixed `1 <= j <= k`, the outer iteration `theta[n+1] = pi_{j,k}(theta[n])` converges, for Lebesgue-almost every initial probability vector on the simplex, to a point mass `delta_s` or an equal two-point law `1/2(delta_{s_1}+delta_{s_2})`. The manuscript states this as a conjecture. Its preceding finite examples include numerically found nonuniform invariant laws, instability, and partial symmetry; none proves almost-everywhere convergence. The reference simplex measure is specified on PDF p.15.

The finite-state observation that transition entries are degree-`k` polynomials in `theta`, with stationary equations `pi K_theta=pi`, suggests exact rational elimination on strata. That is a proposed reduction, not a result proved in this manuscript; denominators, support faces, and boundary extensions must be checked independently.

## A122: interval question and density-only boundary

**§7.3 (PDF pp.21–22).** The manuscript asks whether any full-support invariant law exists on `[0,1]` for general `(j,k)` and conjectures no. Theorem 9 on PDF p.22 restates the companion's `k=2` classification; see the single shared proof card W21-001. The additional displayed criterion excludes invariant **densities** when `j=k` or when its stated multinomial expression is at most 2. The latter proof is omitted in this manuscript and its conclusion is density-specific. It does not rule out singular continuous or mixed full-support laws.

Section 7.4 (PDF pp.22–23) gives a uniform invariant law on the four-point set `{0,0.4,0.6,1}` for `pi_{3,4}`. This finite-support example is not a full-support interval law.

## Reading synthesis and scope

- §1 and §1.1 distinguish weak limits of outer iterates from fixedpoints when continuity is not known; full support is not weakly closed.
- §2 restates the shared inner-chain result and proves only the no-distance-tie continuity case above.
- §§3–6 present symmetry consequences, a two-state binomial formula, numerical finite examples, Conjecture 5, and the shared finite tree-space result; the conjecture and simulations are not proofs.
- §§7.1–7.2 and §§8–10 are interval/circle/high-dimensional simulations and heuristics. The final remarks explicitly say a suitable monotonicity proof has not been achieved.

RDE results apply only if the target map has a proved independent-copy representation; nonlinear Markov results for `mu -> mu K_mu` do not automatically govern `theta -> pi(K_theta)`. These operator boundaries are expanded in the skill method reference. This manuscript is a primary source for problem wording and conjectural/numerical status, not a proof of A121 or the general A122 question.
