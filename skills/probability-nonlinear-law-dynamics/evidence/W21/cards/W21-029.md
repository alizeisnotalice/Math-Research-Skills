## Centered affine RDE contraction

Let \(\mathcal M_2\) be the laws on \(\mathbb R\) with finite second moment, \(\ell_2\) the Mallows/Wasserstein-2 metric, and
\[
\mathcal M_2^0=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal M_2:\int x\,d\mu(x)=0\right\}.
\]
For a random coefficient family \((C,T_1,T_2,\ldots)\), define
\[
K(\mu)=\mathcal L\!\left(\sum_{i\ge1}T_iX_i+C\right),
\]
where \(X_i\) are iid with law \(\mu\) and independent of the whole coefficient family. The coefficients may be dependent among themselves; the proof does not assume independent \(T_i\).

**Lemma 1, contraction lemma (PDF p.6).** For centered \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal M_2\), with \(\sum_i\mathbb E T_i^2<\infty\),
\[
\ell_2(K\mu,K\nu)^2\le
\left(\sum_i\mathbb E T_i^2\right)\ell_2(\mu,\nu)^2.
\]
The source couples \(X_i=F_\mu^{-1}(U_i)\) and \(Y_i=F_\nu^{-1}(U_i)\) using independent uniforms \(U_i\), and uses the same independent coefficient family in both outputs. The common toll cancels. On expanding the squared sum, cross terms vanish because \(X_i-Y_i\) are independent centered variables and independent of the coefficient family. The diagonal terms give \(\sum_i\mathbb E T_i^2\,\mathbb E|X_i-Y_i|^2\); the quantile coupling realizes the one-dimensional \(\ell_2\) distance.

**Theorem 3 (PDF p.7).** If \(\mathbb E C^2<\infty\), \(\mathbb E C=0\), and
\[
\sum_{i\ge1}\mathbb E T_i^2<1,
\]
then \(K\) maps the complete space \((\mathcal M_2^0,\ell_2)\) into itself and is a strict contraction. For every \(\mu\in\mathcal M_2^0\), the iterates \(K^n(\mu)\) converge exponentially in \(\ell_2\) to the unique fixedpoint in \(\mathcal M_2^0\). The moment and centering assumptions are essential to this stated invariant class; the theorem does not classify fixedpoints outside the centered finite-variance class.

## How the paper uses this mechanism

The opening method separates four steps: normalize the algorithm from its expectation recursion; obtain a finite recursive law; identify a limiting independent-copy equation; then prove convergence of the finite sequence to that fixedpoint using an accompanying sequence and recursive error bounds. The theorem above supplies only the fixedpoint/contraction step; the finite-recursion approximation is a separate obligation.

The examples in §3 include Quicksort-type and tree algorithms. In §6, asymptotic-normality proofs use Zolotarev metrics because the limiting variance-preserving split operator has coefficient one in ordinary \(L_p\)-type metrics; the paper states that regularity order \(r>2\) is essential for the displayed proof. In §9 (PDF p.29), the Find-process law operator has a different state space: random functions on \([0,1]\), with the infimum-coupling \(L_p(\Omega\times[0,1])\) metric and the exact uniform-split recursion. The source prints the sufficient condition \(\|U^2\|_p+\|(1-U)^2\|_p<1\). For uniform \(U\), this left side is \(2/(2p+1)^{1/p}\); at \(p=3\), its cube is \(8/7>1\), so the printed condition fails there and should not be silently generalized. Separately, the two recursive branches have disjoint supports in the function argument. For coupled child processes \(X_i,Y_i\) and a common independent uniform split, direct integration gives \(\mathbb E\|KX-KY\|_p^p=[\mathbb E U^{p+1}+\mathbb E(1-U)^{p+1}]d^p=\frac{2}{p+2}d^p\), so this directly derived operator bound has factor \((2/(p+2))^{1/p}<1\) for every \(p>0\). This derivation is distinct from the source's displayed estimate; the exact p=3 comparison is saved in [the local check](../checks/W21-029-find-operator-contraction.py) and [its output](../checks/W21-029-find-operator-contraction.txt). The paper says additional regular-version arguments are needed to turn law convergence into a regular process version; its proof of that regular-version step is not supplied by the contraction calculation.

## Transfer boundary for W21

Use this as a theorem only after deriving the exact independent-copy affine RDE, its coefficient/toll law, the centered finite-variance domain, and a strict contraction in \(\ell_2\). It does not apply merely because a target is a map on probability measures. No recursive-copy representation has been derived for either \(\Phi(\theta)=\pi(K_\theta)\) or the A122 equation \(\theta K_\theta=\theta\); the Quicksort coefficient \(\sqrt{2/3}\) is not a constant for either target.
