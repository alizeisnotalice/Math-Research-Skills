## Equation and assumptions

Let $(N,A_1,A_2,\ldots)$, $N\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}$, have nonnegative weights, and assume $\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^NA_i=1$. On nonnegative finite-mean laws the smoothing transform is
\[
Z\overset d=\sum_{i=1}^NA_iZ_i,
\]
where, conditionally on weights, the $Z_i$ are iid copies of $Z$, independent of weights. The paper assumes a solution with $\mathbb EZ=1$. Put $Y_1=\sum_iA_i$, $\rho(s)=\mathbb E\sum_iA_i^s$, and $\rho'(1)=\mathbb E\sum_iA_i\log A_i$ when defined.

## Tail theorems

**Theorem 1.1 (physical p.5; printed p.4108).** Fix $\alpha>1$ with $\rho(\alpha)<1$ and $\rho(\alpha+\delta_0)<\infty$ for some $\delta_0>0$. For every slowly varying $\ell\in R_0$,
\[
\mathbb P(Y_1>x)\sim x^{-\alpha}\ell(x)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb P(Z>x)\sim(1-\rho(\alpha))^{-1}x^{-\alpha}\ell(x),
\qquad x\to\infty.
\]
Thus asymptotic multiplier is $(1-\rho(\alpha))^{-1}$. The hypotheses include a finite $(\alpha+\delta_0)$ weight moment; theorem concerns tails of a finite-mean smoothing-transform fixed point, not an arbitrary recursive law map.

**Theorem 1.2 (physical p.5; printed p.4108).** If $\rho(1+\delta_0)<\infty$ for some $\delta_0>0$ and $\mu:=-\rho'(1)\in(0,\infty)$, and $\ell\in R_0$ satisfies $\int_1^\infty\ell(x)\,dx/x<\infty$, define $\widetilde\ell(x)=\int_x^\infty\ell(t)\,dt/t$ for $x\ge1$. If
\[
\mathbb E[Y_1\mathbf1_{\{Y_1>x\}}]\sim\ell(x),
\]
then
\[
\mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_{\{Z>x\}}]\sim\mu^{-1}\widetilde\ell(x).
\]
This is one-way; Remark 1.1 says the converse is not established.

## Proof chain

Theorem 1.1 proof, Sections 2–6 (physical pp.7–25): Tauberian propositions relate tails to Laplace-transform remainders; Theorems 2.1–2.2 compare Laplace transforms of $Y_1$ and $Z$ through fixed-point identity; Section 3 controls derivatives/remainders, Sections 4–5 separate noninteger and integer $\alpha$, and summing weighted recursive terms produces geometric factor $1/(1-\rho(\alpha))$. Moment margin $\rho(\alpha+\delta_0)<\infty$ supports truncation and Potter-bound estimates and is not removable under the stated theorem. Theorem 1.2 proof (Section 7, physical pp.25–30) uses a size-biased/change-of-measure random walk, renewal sums, and tail-integral transform $\widetilde\ell$. Appendix (pp.30–33) proves an auxiliary proposition for the Tauberian comparison.
