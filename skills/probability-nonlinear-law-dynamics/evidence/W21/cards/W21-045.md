## Model and assumptions

For nonnegative random weights $T=(T_i)_{i\ge1}$, let $N=\#\{i:T_i>0\}$ and $m(\theta)=\mathbb E\sum_iT_i^\theta$. The functional equation is
\[
f(t)=\mathbb E\prod_{i\ge1}f(tT_i),
\tag{1.1}
\]
equivalent for Laplace transforms to $X\overset d=\sum_iT_iX_i$, and for survival functions to $X\overset d=\inf\{X_i/T_i:T_i>0\}$.

Assumptions: (A1) $\mathbb P(T\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N})<1$; (A2) $\mathbb EN>1$; (A3) characteristic exponent $\alpha>0$ has $m(\alpha)=1<m(\beta)$ for every $\beta\in[0,\alpha)$; and either (A4a)
\[
m'(\alpha)=\mathbb E\sum_iT_i^\alpha\log T_i\in(-\infty,0),\qquad
\mathbb E\!\left[\left(\sum_iT_i^\alpha\right)\log^+\!\left(\sum_iT_i^\alpha\right)\right]<\infty,
\]
or (A4b) $m(\theta)<\infty$ for some $\theta\in[0,\alpha)$. (A5) additionally assumes finite nonnull nonnegative $W$ solving $W\overset d=\sum_iT_i^\alpha W_i$. Proposition 2.1 shows A5 follows from A1–A3 plus A4a, or A1–A3 plus $\mathbb P(N<\infty)=1$. The A4a logarithm is applied to the **sum** $\sum_iT_i^\alpha$, as printed in the rendered source formula on physical p.4; it is not $\mathbb E\sum_iT_i^\alpha\log^+T_i^\alpha$.

Let $r>1$ be the lattice span when positive weights lie in $r^{\mathbb Z}$ a.s.; otherwise $r=1$ is nongeometric. $\mathcal H_r$ consists of positive multiplicatively $r$-periodic functions $h$ for which $h(t)t^\alpha$ is nondecreasing; for $r=1$, these are positive constants. $\mathcal P_r\subseteq\mathcal H_r$ imposes complete monotonicity of the derivative of $h(t)t^\alpha$.

## Classification and proof route

Theorem 2.2 (physical p.5/print p.2073): under (A1)–(A5), all nontrivial decreasing solutions in class $\mathcal M$ are
\[
f(t)=\mathbb E\exp[-Wh(t)t^\alpha]=\varphi(h(t)t^\alpha),
\qquad h\in\mathcal H_r.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Corollary 2.3 restricts Laplace-transform solutions to $0<\alpha\le1$ and $h\in\mathcal P_r$; Corollary 2.4 gives the left-continuous $h\in\mathcal H_r$ survival/min-type class. Theorem 8.3 (physical p.14/print p.2082) states that for any $f\in\mathcal S(\mathcal M)$, BRW disintegration satisfies $M(t)=e^{-Wh(t)t^\alpha}$ a.s. for some $h\in\mathcal H_r$, with $W$ endogenous for the modified transform.

The proof builds the multiplicative weighted branching model in Section 5, defines disintegration martingale $M_n(t)=\prod_{|v|=n}f(tL(v))$, and introduces an $\alpha$-tilted random walk and Hammersley–Kingman–Biggins stopping lines in Section 7. The regular-variation-at-zero theorem (Theorem 11.1, physical pp.26–27/print pp.2094–2095) identifies scaling of $1-f(t)$; Choquet–Deny then identifies the periodic factor. Section 12 (physical pp.32–34/print pp.2100–2102) proves Theorem 8.3 and endogeny by conditional expectation and stopping-line limits. The proof needs the weighted tree, branching independence, many-to-one tilt, and (A1)–(A5), not generic properties of law dynamics.
