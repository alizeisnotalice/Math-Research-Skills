## Wasserstein perturbation theorem

Let \(G\) be Polish and \(d\) a lower-semicontinuous metric on \(G\) (not necessarily the metric inducing its Polish topology). For Borel laws, let \(W\) be the coupling Wasserstein distance induced by \(d\), possibly infinite. For a Markov kernel \(P\), define
\[
\tau(P)=\sup_{x\ne y}\frac{W(\delta_xP,\delta_yP)}{d(x,y)}.
\]
It is submultiplicative, and \(W(\mu P,\nu P)\le\tau(P)W(\mu,\nu)\). Assumption 2.1 is the geometric Wasserstein-ergodicity bound
\[
\tau(P^n)\le C\rho^n,\qquad n\in\mathbb N,\quad C>0,\ \rho\in[0,1).
\]
**Zero-power constant scope (PDF p.5 and pp.6–7).** The source writes \(n\in\mathbb N\) in Assumption 2.1, then explicitly defines \(\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) at the start of §3, but does not separately define \(\mathbb N\). I read the displayed \(\mathbb N\) as positive integers, consistent with that distinction. Under this reading Assumption 2.1 allows \(C<1\); however, the Theorem 3.1 telescoping proof includes \(\tau(P^0)=1\) in its geometric sum and uses the same \(C\) for the zero-power term. The printed bound can fail for \(C<1\). The no-loss repair is to use \(C'=\max\{C,1\}\) throughout the theorem's bound. The same zero-power issue occurs in the \(\widetilde V\)-uniform-ergodicity constant in Theorem 3.2 because its telescoping proof has \(P^{n-i-1}\) including \(P^0\); use \(C'=\max\{C,1\}\) there as well. If the authors intended \(\mathbb N\) to include zero, these restrictions are instead implicit, but that convention is not stated. Exact two-state witnesses are saved in [the local check](../checks/W21-040-positive-n-constant.py) and [its output](../checks/W21-040-positive-n-constant.txt): for Theorem 3.1 the printed n=1 RHS is 1/10 while the true Wasserstein distance is 1; for Theorem 3.2 the printed RHS is approximately 0.0369465 while the true TV distance is 0.1. Replacing C by C'=max{C,1} makes these witnesses consistent with the bound. The exact kernels and rational parameters are in the script; the TV exponent evaluation uses Decimal arithmetic. This check isolates the scope issue and is not a replacement for the proof.

Compare an ideal chain \(P\), initial law \(p_0\), and perturbed chain \(\widetilde P\), initial law \(\widetilde p_0\). Theorem 3.1 is a finite-time perturbation bound and does not require a stationary law for this estimate. The perturbed kernel need not itself be uniformly ergodic in Theorem 3.1; it is required to have a measurable Lyapunov function \(\widetilde V:G\to[1,\infty)\) and constants \(\delta\in(0,1),L>0\) such that
\[
\widetilde P\widetilde V\le\delta\widetilde V+L.
\]
Set
\[
\gamma=\sup_x\frac{W(\delta_xP,\delta_x\widetilde P)}{\widetilde V(x)},\qquad
\kappa=\max\{\widetilde p_0(\widetilde V),\,L/(1-\delta)\}.
\]

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p.6; proof pp.6–7).**
\[
W(p_n,\widetilde p_n)\le
C\left(\rho^nW(p_0,\widetilde p_0)
 +(1-\rho^n)\frac{\gamma\kappa}{1-\rho}\right).
\]
The proof first telescopes
\[
\widetilde p_n-p_n=(\widetilde p_0-p_0)P^n+
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\widetilde p_i(\widetilde P-P)P^{n-i-1}.
\]
The drift inequality bounds every \(\widetilde p_i(\widetilde V)\) by \(\kappa\), hence each one-step perturbation in \(W\) is at most \(\gamma\kappa\). Contractivity of each remaining \(P\)-iterate multiplies it by \(\tau(P^{n-i-1})\); the geometric sum is at most \(C(1-\rho^n)/(1-\rho)\). If both kernels have stationary laws \(\pi,\widetilde\pi\) with finite \(W(\pi,\widetilde\pi)\), Corollary 3.1 yields
\[
W(\pi,\widetilde\pi)\le \frac{C\gamma L}{(1-\rho)(1-\delta)}.
\]
The corollary's printed cross-reference says “assumptions of Theorem 3.2”; its location and proof use Theorem 3.1, so retain this as a source cross-reference typo.

## A total-variation variant under geometric ergodicity

Theorem 3.2 (statement PDF pp.13–14; proof pp.14–15) handles geometrically ergodic \(P\) when only total-variation one-step error is available. It assumes \(P\) is \(\widetilde V\)-uniformly ergodic,
\[
\|P^n(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\widetilde V}
\le C\widetilde V(x)\rho^n,
\]
and the same measurable \(\widetilde V\ge1\) satisfies both
\[
\widetilde P\widetilde V\le\delta\widetilde V+L,\qquad
P\widetilde V\le\widetilde V+L.
\]
With \(\gamma=\sup_x\|P(x,\cdot)-\widetilde P(x,\cdot)\|_{\rm tv}/\widetilde V(x)\), \(\kappa=\max\{\widetilde p_0(\widetilde V),L/(1-\delta)\}\), and \(0<\gamma<e^{-1}\), its bound is
\[
\|p_n-\widetilde p_n\|_{\rm tv}
\le C\rho^n\|p_0-\widetilde p_0\|_{\widetilde V}
+\frac{\kappa e}{1-\rho}
[2C(L+1)]^{1/\log(\gamma^{-1})}\,
\gamma\log(\gamma^{-1}).
\]
The logarithmic exponent is \(1/\log(\gamma^{-1})\), confirmed against the rendered theorem and the proof's choice \(s=1/\log(\gamma^{-1})\). The stationary version is Remark 3.7, equation (3.19), PDF p.15, with \(\kappa\) replaced by \(L/(1-\delta)\).

## W21 transfer boundary

These estimates compare two specified kernels and their chain laws. For \(\Phi(\theta)=\pi(K_\theta)\), one must prove kernel sensitivity \(\sup_x W(\delta_xK_\theta,\delta_xK_\eta)/\widetilde V(x)\), common uniform ergodicity/drift constants on the relevant stratum, and finite-moment conditions. To infer contraction of \(\Phi\), the composed bound must have a constant below one. Mixing at fixed \(\theta\) alone supplies neither the cross-kernel perturbation nor that small constant. The theorem is not an automatic result for arbitrary law-valued nonlinear maps.
