## Operator, topology, theorem, and quantifiers

The main one-branch recurrence is \(Y_n\stackrel d=Y_{I_n}+b_n\), where \(I_n\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\), the index/toll input is independent of the recursive sequence, and \(P(I_n=n)<1\) eventually. Its normalized limiting equation is degenerate: the coefficient tends to 1, so the limit equation itself reads \(X\stackrel d=X\). The paper instead proves a normal limit using the scalar Zolotarev metric \(\zeta_3\) and the mean/variance asymptotics.

**Theorem 2.1.** Assume \(\|Y_n\|_3<\infty\) and
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E\ln((I_n\vee1)/n)<0,
\qquad \sup_{n\ge1}\|\ln((I_n\vee1)/n)\|_3<\infty.
\]
For real \(\alpha,\lambda,\kappa\) with \(0\le\lambda<2\alpha\), assume
\[
\|b_n-\mu_n+\mu_{I_n}\|_3=O((\ln n)^\kappa),\qquad
\sigma_n^2=C(\ln n)^{2\alpha}+O((\ln n)^\lambda),\quad C>0,
\]
where \(\mu_n=EY_n\), \(\sigma_n^2=\operatorname{Var}Y_n\). Define exactly
\[
\beta=\tfrac32\wedge3(\alpha-\kappa)\wedge3(\alpha-\lambda/2)\wedge(\alpha-\kappa+1).
\]
If \(\beta>1\), then
\[
(Y_n-EY_n)/(\sqrt C(\ln n)^\alpha)\Rightarrow N(0,1),\qquad
\zeta_3((Y_n-EY_n)/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}Y_n},N(0,1))=O((\ln n)^{-(\beta-1)}).
\]
Here \(\zeta_3\) is defined by twice differentiable test functions whose second derivative is 1-Lipschitz, and is finite for equal first two moments and finite third moments. The logarithmic drift and the uniform third moment bound on the log ratio are separate hypotheses; neither is just a consequence of the variance expansion.

## Proof chain actually read

1. Add \(L_\delta(n)=\ln(n\vee1)+\delta\mathbf1_{\{0,1\}}(n)\) to make logarithmic scaling defined at small indices; normalize by \(\sqrt C L_\delta(n)^\alpha\). The variance expansion gives normalized variance \(\tau_n^2\to1\).
2. Compare the normalized variable \(Z_n\) with the variance-matched normal \(N_n=\tau_nN\), by an accompanying sequence that uses the normal copy at the random index. Conditioning and the \((3,+)\)-ideal property of \(\zeta_3\) give a weighted recursion for \(d_n=\zeta_3(Z_n,N_n)\) with weight \((L_\delta(I_n)/L_\delta(n))^{3\alpha}\).
3. Taylor expansion, the third-moment bound on the toll, the variance error, and the log-index moment control bound the accompanying residual by \(r_n=O((\ln n)^{-\beta})\); the four terms in \(\beta\) come from these separate error sources.
4. Lemma 3.1 uses negative expected log drift to turn a recursion with \(r_n=O((\ln n)^{-\beta})\) into \(d_n=O((\ln n)^{-(\beta-1)})\), provided \(1<\beta<1+3\alpha\). The theorem’s stated \(\beta\) satisfies the needed upper bound in its application. This yields the claimed rate; the asymptotic variance then gives weak convergence under the alternate \(\sqrt C(\ln n)^\alpha\) normalization.

**Extension, Theorem 5.1 (proof is explicitly a sketch).** The paper gives a multi-branch version for \(Y_n\stackrel d=\sum_{r=1}^K Y^{(r)}_{I_r(n)}+b_n\): assume \(\limsup_n\mathbb E\ln(n^{-1}\prod_r(I_r(n)\vee1))<0\), \(\sup_n\|\ln((I_1(n)\vee1)/n)\|_3<\infty\), and \(\limsup_n\sum_rP(I_r(n)=n)<1\); toll L3 order \((\ln n)^\kappa\), variance \(C(\ln n)^{2\alpha}+O((\ln n)^\lambda)\), \(0\le\lambda<2\alpha\), and for some \(\xi\ge0\), \(\|\ln^\alpha(I_r(n)\vee1)\|_3=O((\ln n)^\xi)\) for every \(r=2,\ldots,K\). Its exponent is
\[
\beta=\tfrac32\wedge3(\alpha-\kappa)\wedge3(\alpha-\xi)\wedge3(\alpha-\lambda/2)\wedge(\alpha-\kappa+1)>1,
\]
with the same normal limit and \(\zeta_3\) rate. Because the proof is sketched, do not cite it as a fully expanded proof for a model outside these listed conditions.

## Proof limits and W21 interface

Applications include unsuccessful binary search, random-node depths, and broadcast. The method is tailored to a scale with logarithmic variance and a degenerate coefficient-one limit equation. It is useful where strict contraction fails but a negative log-size drift and quantitative mean/variance/toll estimates can be verified. It is not a global-attraction or uniqueness theorem for arbitrary fixed points; it does not by itself supply a probability metric contraction for the W21 law operator. Any transfer requires proving that the target recursion has this index/toll structure and the stated L3 estimates.
