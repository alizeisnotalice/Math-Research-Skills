## Model and differential inclusion

The paper uses the same finite state/action mean-field game as C011: \(S,A\) finite, \(m\in\mathcal P(S)\), action-dependent generator \(Q^a(m)\), reward \(r_{ia}(m)\), discounted objective with \(\beta\in(0,1)\), and Assumption A1 (all rate coordinates Lipschitz in \(m\), rewards continuous). For frozen \(m\), \(D(m)\) is the set of deterministic stationary discounted-optimal policies; Theorem 3.2 of the companion paper is recalled as the reason any stationary randomized best response lies in \(\operatorname{conv}D(m)\).

The myopic rule means agents optimize as if the present population state were frozen forever, then the aggregate population follows the current mixture of optimal policies. Define
\[
F(m)=\operatorname{conv}\{( \sum_i m_i Q^d_{ij}(m))_{j\in S}:d\in D(m)\}.
\]
A trajectory is an absolutely continuous \(m:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal P(S)\) with \(m(0)=m_0\) and \(\dot m(t)\in F(m(t))\) for a.e. \(t\). The construction and generator convention are part of the theorem; this is not a single-valued iteration of a stochastic matrix.

**Theorem 3.2 (physical p.7; Appendix A pp.20–22).** Under A1 the differential inclusion has a solution on \([0,\infty)\). The appendix verifies upper semicontinuity of \(F\), nonempty closed convex values, and a linear growth bound, then applies the cited existence theorem for differential inclusions. The result is existence, not uniqueness of trajectories. Remark 3.3 says stationary points of this inclusion are exactly stationary MFEs by definition.

## Local convergence

Lemma 4.1 (pp.9–10) says if \(D(\bar m)=\{d\}\), then there is \(\epsilon>0\) with the same unique optimizer throughout \(N_\epsilon(\bar m)\); it follows from continuity of policy values and the finite deterministic policy set.

**Theorem 4.2 (physical pp.10–12).** Let \((\bar m,d)\) be a stationary MFE with unique optimal deterministic strategy \(D(\bar m)=\{d\}\). If \(Q^d(m)\) is independent of \(m\) and is an irreducible generator, then some \(\delta>0\) exists so every solution starting at \(m_0\in\mathcal P(S)\cap N_\delta(\bar m)\) converges exponentially:
\[
\|m(t)-\bar m\|\le C_1e^{-C_2t}\quad(t\ge0)
\]
for constants \(C_1,C_2>0\). The proof first uses policy-value continuity to keep the inclusion equal to the one linear flow \(\dot m=(Q^d)^Tm\) in the unique-policy neighborhood. Irreducibility gives a simple zero eigenvalue with stationary vector \(\bar m\) and all other eigenvalues in the open left half-plane. The Jordan expansion bounds each transient mode by \(e^{\Re\lambda t}t^\ell/\ell!\); an explicit small-neighborhood choice then keeps the orbit in the region where \(d\) remains optimal. The rate depends on the nonzero spectrum and the Jordan-chain constants; the theorem states existence of constants, not a uniform rate over all games.

**Theorem 4.4 (physical p.12 and pp.12–15 proof).** For a unique-policy equilibrium \((\bar m,d)\), let \(O\supseteq\mathcal P(S)\) be open, \(Q^d:O\to\mathbb R^{S\times S}\) componentwise Lipschitz, \(Q^d(m)\) a transition-rate matrix for all \(m\in\mathcal P(S)\), and \(f^d(m)=(Q^d(m))^Tm\) continuously differentiable on \(O\). Assume \(Df^d(\bar m)\) has a zero eigenvalue with eigenvector \(\bar m\), and every other eigenvalue has strictly negative real part. Then some \(\delta,C_1,C_2>0\) give the same local exponential bound for every myopic-adjustment solution with \(m_0\in\mathcal P(S)\cap N_\delta(\bar m)\). The proof shifts coordinates by \(\bar m\), uses a Jordan-adapted basis and equivalent inner product to make the stable subspace dissipative, then controls the nonlinear remainder and preserves the unique-policy neighborhood. Remark 4.5 says this theorem's \(\delta\) is nonconstructive; Theorem 4.2's constant-generator argument gives an explicit neighborhood bound.

## Two-strategy global theorem

Section 5 assumes the set \(\mathcal U\) of deterministic policies that are uniquely optimal in some region contains exactly \(d_1,d_2\). It defines
\[
g(m)=(V^{d_2}(m)-V^{d_1}(m))\cdot\mathbf1,\qquad
V^d(m)=(\beta I-Q^d(m))^{-1}r^d(m).
\]
The section assumes \(Q^a_{ij}\) and \(r_{ia}\) are twice continuously differentiable on an open superset \(O\) of the simplex, so \(g\in C^2(O)\); the rate maps there define conservative generators on the simplex. Off \(g=0\), the inclusion uses \(d_1\) on \(g<0\) and \(d_2\) on \(g>0\); on \(g=0\), it allows their convex hull.

**Theorem 5.1 (physical pp.17–19).** Assume (a) \(\nabla g(m)\ne0\) for every \(m\in O\) with \(g(m)=0\); (b) each of the two nonlinear Markov chains with generator function \(Q^{d_1}(m)\), respectively \(Q^{d_2}(m)\), converges from every initial \(m_0\in\mathcal P(S)\) to some invariant distribution, which may depend on \(m_0\). Then:

1. If on \(g=0\), \(\langle (Q^{d_1}(m))^Tm,\nabla g(m)\rangle>0\) and \(\langle (Q^{d_2}(m))^Tm,\nabla(-g)(m)\rangle<0\), every myopic-adjustment process converges to a stationary MFE with a deterministic equilibrium strategy in \(\mathcal U\).
2. If those two inequalities are reversed (\(<0\) and \(>0\), respectively), the same conclusion holds.
3. If the two displayed quantities are both \(\ge0\), each process either converges to a deterministic stationary MFE with strategy in \(\mathcal U\), or there is \(T>0\) for which \(g(m(t))=0\) for all \(t>T\).

The source signs are retained as written; in particular the second inner product is with \(\nabla(-g)\), not \(\nabla g\). The proof first observes that a trajectory which eventually stays in a strict-sign region is a solution of the corresponding nonlinear Markov flow, hence converges by assumption. For cases (i)/(ii), the strict boundary derivative rules exclude remaining on the switching surface and force an eventual side. In case (iii), a first-exit derivative contradiction shows that after hitting \(g=0\) the trajectory remains there. The conclusion in (iii) deliberately leaves a boundary-following alternative; it does not assert convergence to a mixed equilibrium.

Example 5.2 (pp.19–20) uses a three-state, two-good-one-bad-state model. After Theorem 5.1 forces \(m_1=m_2\) for large time, the authors derive a scalar Riccati equation. They say numerical simulations suggest convergence to the mixed-equilibrium distribution is likely; this is explicitly not proved by the theorem.

## W21 interface and limits

This paper supplies a way to separate (i) existence of inclusion trajectories, (ii) local stability of an isolated unique-policy equilibrium, and (iii) global convergence under a two-policy switching geometry plus convergence of both nonlinear flows. To transfer any part, first identify the target operator with this precise controlled generator model and verify the applicable policy uniqueness, smooth switching-surface, irreducibility/spectral, or nonlinear-flow hypotheses. None of these results yields global attraction for an arbitrary finite-simplex map or establishes that stationary laws of a rank-based kernel form the inclusion \(F\).
