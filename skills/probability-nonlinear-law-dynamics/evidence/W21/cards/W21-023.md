## Operator equations and the two coefficient conventions

The paper specifies a symmetric two-parent kernel, but its parameter formulas do not define one consistent asymmetric endpoint-selection operator.

**Printed kernel (PDF p.3, Eq. (2.1)).** For `x<y` it sets `P(x,y,{x})=p` and `P(x,y,{y})=p`, while also requiring `p+q=1`. This is not a probability kernel unless `p=q=1/2`.

**Finite atomic calculation (PDF p.4, Eqs. (3.2)–(3.3)).** For `λ=αδ_a+(1−α)δ_b`, `a<b`, the paper obtains

`(Vλ)({a}) = α' = α[α+2p(1−α)]`.

This is the recurrence for a valid rule that selects the lower point with probability `p` and the upper point with probability `q`. Under this finite-section convention, `α_n→1` for `p>1/2` and `α_n→0` for `p<1/2`; the two-point law tends to its lower atom in the first case and its upper atom in the second.

**Continuous-law formulas (PDF pp.6–7, Eqs. (3.8)–(3.17)).** For `A=[a,b]`, the displayed interval formula is

`Vλ(A)=λ(A)[λ(A)+2p λ([0,a))+2q λ((b,1))]` (PDF p.6),

and the displayed first density and CDF recursion are

`f^(1)_λ(x)=2p λ([0,x))+2q λ((x,1))`,  `g^(n)_λ(x)=G^{∘n}(g_λ(x))`,

`G(u)=u(u+2q(1−u))`,  `g_λ(x)=λ([0,x))`,

`f^(n)_λ(x)=∏_{i=1}^n f(g^(i)_λ(x))`,  `f(u)=2pu+2q(1−u)` (PDF pp.6–7).

For a valid kernel that always outputs one of its two parents, the interval and density coefficients correspond instead to **lower-point probability `q`, upper-point probability `p`**. Thus the finite and continuous sections use opposite p/q conventions when `p≠q`. The discrepancy is not repaired by correcting Eq. (2.1) alone. The CDF iteration and density product can be used as algebraic consequences of the displayed continuous-section equations, explicitly under that section's lower-`q`/upper-`p` interpretation; they are not evidence that the finite and continuous sections describe the same asymmetric operator.

## Endpoint theorems as printed and their limits

**Theorems 4.4–4.5 (PDF p.9).** The paper claims `f^(n)_λ(x)≤(1/(2p))^n` on `[0,B_n]`, `B_n↑1`, for `p>1/2`, and the reflected `(1/(2q))^n` bound on `[A_n,1]`, `A_n↓0`, for `p<1/2`. The §4 argument applies Proposition 4.2's expanding-interval bound through Corollary 4.3 at the input `g_λ(x)`, but `x≤B_n` does not imply `g_λ(x)≤B_n` for an arbitrary continuous initial law. The stated quantifier includes laws for which this step fails. For example, under the continuous-section formulas, take `λ=Unif[1/4,3/4]` and `p=3/4`. At `x=3/4`, `g_λ(x)=1`, so the displayed density product gives `f^(n)_λ(x)=(2p)^n=(3/2)^n`, whereas for all sufficiently large `n`, `x≤B_n` and the theorem claims at most `(2/3)^n`. The reflected claim likewise fails at `x=1/4` for `p=1/4`, `q=3/4`.

**Theorem 4.6 (PDF p.9).** It states that every continuous initial law converges to `δ_0` for `p<1/2` or `δ_1` for `p>1/2`. This cannot hold at the weak level under the stated arbitrary-initial-law quantifier for any valid endpoint-selection correction: if `λ` is supported on `[1/4,3/4]` and each update outputs one of the sampled parents, every iterate remains supported there. In particular, `Unif[1/4,3/4]` cannot converge weakly to either endpoint atom (test the continuous function `x`); hence no usual stronger mode that implies weak convergence can give the claimed limit. No full-support or support-endpoint assumption appears in Theorem 4.6 as rendered. The section's use of the expanding-interval estimate at `g_λ(x)` also needs a condition not implied by `x≤B_n`; do not treat the endpoint claims as established for the stated class. This isolates a quantifier/proof gap and does not assert that the paper's other results are false.

## Remaining source-scope warnings

1. **State space.** Definition 1.3 on PDF p.3 gives `X=[0,1)`, whereas Theorems 4.5–4.7 use `[0,1]` and `δ_1`. As written, `δ_1` is outside `P([0,1))`.
2. **Convergence mode.** Definition 1.2 contrasts a “strong limit” with a weak limit but does not specify a topology or norm for strong convergence. Do not silently restate it as total-variation or setwise convergence. In any case, the endpoint claim above already fails at the weaker level of weak convergence for interior-supported initial laws.

## Transfer boundary

This is an explicit measure-valued QSO with a one-dimensional CDF recursion and an endpoint-selection bias. It is a useful contrast/example for law-map iteration, but it is not the nearest-order-statistic stationary-law map. It supplies no full-support fixed-law result for that target, and the source inconsistencies above must be handled before importing even its own stated theorem.
