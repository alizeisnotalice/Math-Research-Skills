## Exact nonlinear law map

On $\mathbb R^d$, for probability law $F$ and fixed noise law $G$, define
\[
Q_G(F)=\mathcal L\left(\frac{X_1+X_2}{2}+\eta\right),
\qquad X_1,X_2\stackrel{iid}{\sim}F,\quad\eta\sim G,
\]
with independence. Bilinear kernel is $Q_G(F_1,F_2)=\mathcal L((X_1+X_2)/2+\eta)$. Proposition 1 gives $Q_G(F_1,F_2)=\widetilde F_1*\widetilde F_2*G$, and diagonal characteristic-function identity
\[
\varphi_{Q_G(F)}(s)=\varphi_F(s/2)^2\varphi_G(s).
\tag{4}
\]
Proposition 2 iterates this factorization:
\[
Q_G^n(F)=F^{(n)}*G^{\{n\}},\quad
\varphi_{F^{(n)}}(s)=\varphi_F(s/2^n)^{2^n},\quad
\varphi_{G^{\{n\}}}(s)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\varphi_G(s/2^j)^{2^j}.
\tag{5–9}
\]
Equivalently, $F^{(n)}$ is a sample-mean law of $2^n$ iid $F$ variables, and $G^{\{n\}}$ is the independent sum of generation-wise means of $2^j$ iid noise variables.

## Main results and assumptions

Corollary 1 (physical p.8/print p.1820): $Q_G^n(F)$ has a weak limit iff $F^{(n)}*G^{\{n\}}$ does; if both factor limits exist, the limit is $F^{(\infty)}*G^{\{\infty\}}$.

Theorem 1 (physical p.9/print p.1821): if iterates converge weakly to $H$, continuity gives $H=Q_G(H)$ and
\[
\varphi_H(s)=\varphi_H(s/2)^2\varphi_G(s).
\tag{10}
\]
Conversely, any probability law satisfying this characteristic-function equation is a fixed point and the weak limit of its own iterates. For $G=\delta_0$, the fixed-point relation reduces to dyadic 1-stability, $\varphi_H(2s)=\varphi_H(s)^2$ (11).

Theorem 3 (p.11/print p.1823): if $G$ has finite second moments and mean zero, then $G^{\{n\}}\Rightarrow G^{\{\infty\}}$; limit has mean zero and covariance $2v_G$. Theorem 4 (p.11–12) gives the one-dimensional sufficient condition
\[
|\log\varphi_G(s)|\le C|s|^{1+\epsilon},
\quad |s|\le s_0,\quad C,s_0>0,\quad 0<\epsilon\le1,
\]
for existence of noise-factor limit. Theorem 5 (p.13/print p.1825) strengthens this to
\[
|\varphi_G(s)|\le\exp(-C|s|^{1+\epsilon})
\]
locally and concludes
\[
|\varphi_{G^{\{\infty\}}}(s)|\le\exp\!\left(-2^{-\epsilon}Cs_0^\epsilon|s|\right),\qquad |s|\ge s_0,
\]
hence absolute continuity, **whenever** $G^{\{\infty\}}:=\mathrm{w\!-\!lim}_{n\to\infty}G^{\{n\}}$ exists. Existence is an explicit premise in the theorem; the local modulus bound alone does not supply it.

**Negative example (verified).** Let $G=N(m,\sigma^2)$ with $m\ne0$ and $\sigma>0$. Its characteristic-function modulus is $e^{-\sigma^2s^2/2}$, so the local hypothesis holds with $\epsilon=1$, $C=\sigma^2/2$. But the exact factorization (5–9) gives
\[
G^{\{n\}}=N\!\left(nm,\ \sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^{-j}\right),\qquad
\sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^{-j}<2\sigma^2.
\]
For fixed $R$ and $n|m|>R$, Chebyshev gives $\mathbb P(|G^{\{n\}}|\le R)\le 2\sigma^2/(n|m|-R)^2\to0$. Thus these factors are not tight and have no weak limit. The checked exact geometric sums are in [the local calculation](../checks/W21-044-gaussian-noise-factor.txt).

## Reusable method and A121/A122 boundary

The continuity argument in Theorem 1 is reusable: for a weakly continuous self-map of probability laws, if iterates converge weakly, their limit is a fixed point. Here weak continuity follows from the characteristic-function update. Application to A121/A122 requires independent proof that $\theta\mapsto\pi(K_\theta)$ is weakly continuous (or, in the finite-state case, continuous in total variation) on the parameter region; this paper does not prove that.

The paper's factorization depends on parental averaging $(X_1+X_2)/2$ and independent additive noise. The rank-selection update has no demonstrated convolution/sample-mean factorization. Its QSO convergence results are adjacent model comparison, not direct evidence for A121/A122, and this source is **not counted** toward that quota.
