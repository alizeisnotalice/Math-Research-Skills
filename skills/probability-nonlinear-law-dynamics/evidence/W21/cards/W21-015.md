## Map and domain

On the simplex \(T=\{(x,y,z):x,y,z\ge0,\ x+y+z=1\}\), the paper studies
\[
V(x,y,z)=(x^2+2xy,\ y^2+2yz,\ z^2+2zx).
\]
This is a quadratic stochastic map preserving \(T\). The fixed points named are \(M_0=(1/3,1/3,1/3)\) and the three vertices \(M_1,M_2,M_3\). The article recalls earlier work that for interior \(a\ne M_0\) the orbit limit set is infinite and lies on the boundary; its own results below are the long-block and Cesàro nonconvergence statements.

Partition coordinate-order regions into
\[
G_1=\{x\ge y\ge z\},\,G_2=\{x\ge z\ge y\},\,
G_3=\{z\ge x\ge y\},\,G_4=\{z\ge y\ge x\},\,
G_5=\{y\ge z\ge x\},\,G_6=\{y\ge x\ge z\},
\]
and \(H_1=G_1\cup G_2,\ H_2=G_3\cup G_4,\ H_3=G_5\cup G_6\). Choose a neighborhood \(U_0\) of \(M_0\) inside the simplex interior so that \(U_i=H_i\setminus U_0\) are convex and satisfy the source's stated condition \(U_1\cap U_2\cap U_3=\varnothing\); the source does not assert pairwise disjointness. The theorem concerns \(U\) equal to one of these \(U_i\), not an arbitrary neighborhood.

## Theorems and proof chain

**Theorem 1 (physical p.1 statement and pp.1–2 proof).** Fix \(U\in\{U_1,U_2,U_3\}\), and an interior \(a\ne M_0\). Let integer sequences \((n_i,m_i)\) satisfy
\[
n_{i+1}>n_i+m_i,\quad V^{n_i}(a)\notin U,\quad
V^{n_i+k}(a)\in U\ (k=1,\ldots,m_i),\quad
V^{n_i+m_i+1}(a)\notin U.
\]
Then \(n_i\to\infty\), and for **every** \(c>0\), \(m_i>c\,n_i\) for all sufficiently large \(i\). This is the source's “faster than the whole previous trajectory” assertion, not merely one fixed positive linear lower bound.

The proof records the cyclic route through the order regions \(G_1\to G_2\to G_3\to G_4\to G_5\to G_6\to G_1\), and sets \(\varphi(x,y,z)=xyz\). Along the orbit,
\[
\varphi(Va)=\varphi(a)(1+y-z)(1+z-x)(1+x-y)<\varphi(a)
\]
away from the center; thus \(\varphi(V^n a)\to0\). The strict product inequality follows because the three positive factors have sum \(3\), so AM–GM gives product at most \(1\), with equality only when \(x=y=z\). The paper's lemma bounds the length \(n\) of a consecutive orbit segment in one \(U_i\) by \(n>A\log(B/\varphi(a_1))\), for absolute constants \(A,B>0\) and the first point \(a_1\) of that segment. Its proof compares coordinate growth along the cyclic order route, obtains a \(2^n\) upper bound for a coordinate ratio, and then a lower bound proportional to \(1/\varphi(a_1)\). The source next says that, for each fixed \(0<\rho<1\), one can choose a neighborhood \(U'\) of \(M_0\) so that \(\varphi(Va)<\rho\varphi(a)\) for \(a\in T\setminus U'\)—outside the neighborhood, not near \(M_0\) (the product ratio tends to \(1\) at the center). It then asserts that \(\varphi(V^n a)\to0\) implies the orbit eventually avoids \(U'\); this transition is not separately justified in the two-page note and is retained here as an unverified proof step. If that step is supplied, the displayed geometric descent outside \(U'\) and the lemma yield \(m_i>A'\log(B'/\rho^{n_i}\varphi(a))\), hence \(m_i>c n_i\) eventually for every fixed \(c>0\). The source prints this implication; this card does not treat its omitted transition as independently established.

**Theorem 2 (physical p.2).** For every \(a\in\operatorname{int}T\setminus\{M_0\}\), the source claims the Cesàro averages
\[
\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}V^k a
\]
have no limit. Its proof assumes convergence to \(\bar a\), chooses \(U_1\) at positive distance \(\delta\) from \(\bar a\), then invokes Theorem 1 for a block of length \(m_i>3n_i\) contained in \(U_1\). The average through \(n_i+m_i\) is a convex combination of a negligible initial segment and the block average in the convex set \(U_1\), contradicting convergence to \(\bar a\). Thus this proof depends on the Theorem 1 passage flagged above; the claimed nonconvergence is not independently proved here without that step. Note 1 says the Cesàro limit-point set consists of fixed points and contains all three vertices \(M_1,M_2,M_3\) for these initial conditions. This note is internally inconsistent: for a bounded Cesàro sequence, successive differences tend to zero, so its cluster set is connected, whereas a subset of the four isolated fixed points \(M_0,M_1,M_2,M_3\) containing three vertices is disconnected. Treat Note 1 as an unverified/contradictory source assertion, not as support for Theorem 2. Note 2 states the results also hold for the displayed \(V_\epsilon\) family with \(0<\epsilon\le1\); the note gives no separate full proof.



## W21 interface and limits

This is a concrete counterexample to “simplex invariance implies Cesàro convergence.” The map's exact cyclic quadratic structure, interior initial condition, and excluded center are essential. It provides a negative test for any proposed general convergence theorem, but does not show failure for the distinct W21 nonlinear stationary-law map unless that map is proved conjugate to or includes this \(V\). The earlier infinite omega-limit assertion is cited background; do not attribute its proof to this two-page note.
