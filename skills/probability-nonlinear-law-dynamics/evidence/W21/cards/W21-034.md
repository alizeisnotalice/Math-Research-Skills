## Operator and classification

For iid copies \(W_j\sim F\) and a fixed finite or countable family of real weights \(T_j\), the main operator is
\[
W\stackrel d=\inf_{j\in J} T_jW_j,
\]
with the corresponding supremum result obtained by sign reflection. In the positive-weight case,
\(\bar F(t)=\prod_{j\in J}\bar F(t/T_j)\), and the negative log-survival \(\nu_F(t)=-\ln\bar F(t)\) satisfies the multiplicative harmonic equation \(\nu_F=\nu_F\star\Lambda\), \(\Lambda=\sum_j\delta_{T_j}\). The paper uses the characteristic exponent \(-\alpha<0\) satisfying \(\sum_jT_j^{-\alpha}=1\), then invokes Choquet–Deny to classify positive solutions.

The opening of §1 (physical p.3) first handles the singleton case: if \(|J|=1\), there is no nontrivial fixed point unless \(T_1=1\); only in that case does the operator reduce to the identity and every probability law is fixed. The paper then imposes the standing assumption \(|J|\ge2\). Under this standing assumption, the all-unit-weight case is covered by A1 (at least two unit weights), whose positive fixed laws are only point masses; after the source’s sign reduction, the full fixed laws in the all-unit case are Dirac laws, not all probability laws. More generally, if \(\inf_jT_j<1\), there is no positive fixed law; when \(\inf_jT_j\ge1\) but not all weights equal 1, the full solution set is \(\{\delta_0\}\cup\mathcal F_T^+\). For A1/A2 (A1: at least two unit weights; A2: \(J=\mathbb N\), at most one unit weight, and \(\liminf_jT_j=1\)), positive fixed laws are only point masses. A3 (exactly one unit weight and remaining weights bounded strictly above 1) permits precisely the bounded-support laws satisfying \(0<\ell_F\le u_F<\infty\) and \(\inf_{j\in J^*}T_j\ge u_F/\ell_F\). A4 (\(J=\mathbb N\), \(\inf_jT_j>1\), and \(\liminf_{j\to\infty}T_j<\infty\)) has no positive fixed law. Sections 2–3 separate these simple cases from the harder A5/A6 positive regimes. A5 is finite \(J\) with \(\inf_jT_j>1\); A6 is \(J=\mathbb N\), \(\inf_jT_j>1\), and \(T_j\to\infty\). A characteristic exponent is assumed/extracted under the source’s A5/A6 conditions.

**Theorem 4.2.** Under those hypotheses and \(\sum_jT_j^{-\alpha}=1\):

- If \(G_\Lambda=\mathbb R_+\), all positive fixed laws are Weibull with survival \(\bar F(t)=\exp(-ct^\alpha)\), \(c>0\).
- If \(G_\Lambda=r^{\mathbb Z}\), \(r>1\), all positive fixed laws are \(r\)-Weibull, with \(\bar F(t)=\exp[-h(t)t^\alpha]\), where \(h\in\mathcal H(r,\alpha)\): multiplicatively r-periodic, left-continuous, and constrained so \(h(t)t^\alpha\) is nondecreasing. At \(\alpha=1\), the corresponding exponential forms are recovered.

The discrete formula is not an arbitrary periodic function: the monotonicity condition is required for a valid survival function. In the core infinite-countable case, Proposition 3.3 first establishes support/exponent properties through a branching-tree Markov-chain and normalized-hazard martingale argument; it is not omitted in the classification proof.

## Other cases and proof chain actually read

- Finite all-negative weights: Theorem 5.1 characterizes solutions via a separate min-max RDE and an auxiliary distributional identity. For \(J=\mathbb N\), only \(\delta_0\) is a fixed point. The authors explicitly caution that the finite negative characterization does not by itself answer whether nontrivial solutions exist.
- Mixed signs: the source reduces nontrivial fixed laws to laws supported on the relevant positive side and the positive-weight equation; negative terms cannot be carried over as if all coefficients were positive.
- Proof of the core theorem: normalize the characteristic exponent to \(-1\); use Lemma 3.2 or Proposition 3.3 to represent \(\nu_F\) as a continuous or lattice harmonic measure; evaluate that representation for \(\mathbb R_+\) versus \(r^{\mathbb Z}\), yielding Weibull or log-periodic Weibull. Conversely, substitute these survival functions into the product equation.

## W21 interface and limitations

This source directly treats deterministic multiplicative weights in an order-statistic RDE and demonstrates how multiplicative harmonicity plus Choquet–Deny can classify fixed laws. It provides no result for random weights, arbitrary nonlinear law maps, stationary Markov kernels, or uniqueness/global attraction outside the stated model. It cannot be used to infer that a candidate simplex fixed point must have a Weibull/log-periodic law without deriving the exact min/max operator and checking the support and weight hypotheses.
