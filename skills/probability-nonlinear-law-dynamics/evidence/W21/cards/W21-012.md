## Linear diffusion: quantitative reflection-coupling contractions

The base process solves `dX_t=b(X_t)dt+dB_t` on `R^d`. The assumptions used in the main geometric results are:

- **Generalized one-sided Lipschitz condition (2.1):** a continuous `κ:(0,∞)→[0,∞)` with `∫_0^1 rκ(r)dr<∞` satisfies `⟨x−y,b(x)−b(y)⟩≤κ(|x−y|)|x−y|²`.
- **Geometric Lyapunov drift (2.2):** a proper `C²` function `V` and `C,λ>0` satisfy `LV≤C−λV`.
- **Additive growth condition (2.10):** there are `α>0` and a bounded `S₂⊇S₁` such that, outside `S₂`,

  `V(x)+V(y) ≥ (4C/λ)(1+α∫₀ᴿ¹ ϕ(r)⁻¹dr Φ(|x−y|))`,

  where `S₁={(x,y):V(x)+V(y)≤4C/λ}`, `R₁=sup{|x−y|:(x,y)∈S₁}`, `R₂=sup{|x−y|:(x,y)∈S₂}`, `ϕ(r)=exp(−(1/2)∫₀ʳuκ(u)du)`, and `Φ(r)=∫₀ʳϕ(s)ds`.

For the additive result, the cost is

`ρ₁(x,y) = [f(|x−y|)+εV(x)+εV(y)] 1_(x≠y)`.

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF p.7; proof pp.26–28).** Under (2.1), (2.2), and (2.10), a concave bounded `f` and explicit `ε,c>0` give

`W_(ρ₁)(μp_t,νp_t) ≤ e^(−ct) W_(ρ₁)(μ,ν)`,  `c=min{β,α,λ}/2`,

where `β⁻¹=∫₀ᴿ² Φ(r)ϕ(r)⁻¹dr`; `ε⁻¹=4C∫₀ᴿ¹ϕ(r)⁻¹dr`. The proof applies Itô–Tanaka to the distance under reflection coupling, uses concavity and the curvature bound to control its second-order term, combines it with the two Lyapunov drift inequalities, and applies an exponential supermartingale/localization argument. The additive cost does not tend to zero as `x→y`; the finite-time coupling reaches the diagonal.

The paper also replaces the additive cost by a multiplicative semimetric

`ρ₂(x,y)=f(|x−y|)(1+εV(x)+εV(y))`.

It assumes in addition `sup_x |∇V(x)|/V(x)<∞`; define `Q(ε)=sup_x |∇V(x)|/max{V(x),1/ε}` and choose `ε` satisfying (2.25). **Theorem 2.2 (PDF p.11; proof pp.29–32)** yields `W_(ρ₂)(μp_t,νp_t)≤e^(−ct)W_(ρ₂)(μ,ν)` with

`c=min{β,λ,4Cελ}/2`,  `β⁻¹=∫₀ᴿ² Φ(r)ϕ(r)⁻¹dr`,

and `ϕ(r)=exp(−(1/2)∫₀ʳuκ(u)du−2Q(ε)r)`. The PDF statement prints “Assumptions 2.2, 2.2, and 2.4”; its setup and proof use assumptions 2.1, 2.2, and 2.4. In the proof, the cross-variation of `f(|X−Y|)` and the Lyapunov factor is bounded by `4Q(ε)f'G`, which cancels the matching negative term from the distance differential inequality. The multiplicative cost tends to zero on the diagonal and is a semimetric, not necessarily a metric.

## Nonlinear McKean–Vlasov diffusions

For `dX_t=b(X_t)dt+τ∫ϑ(X_t,y)μ_t(dy)dt+dB_t`, `μ_t=Law(X_t)`, the interaction kernel `ϑ` is globally Lipschitz in both coordinates. Theorem 2.3 assumes strict contractivity of the confining drift at infinity and gives explicit constants with

`W_(ρ₀)(μ_t,ν_t) ≤ exp((|τ|K−c)t) W_(ρ₀)(μ₀,ν₀)`.

Thus exponential contraction follows when `|τ|K<c`; the proof blends reflection and synchronous couplings at a small distance cutoff, controls the interaction by `L(|X−Y|+W₁(μ_t,ν_t))`, and sends the cutoff to zero. This is a law-flow estimate for that SDE.

**Theorem 2.4 (statement PDF p.15; proof pp.35–39).** Under (2.1), global Lipschitz interaction, and quadratic dissipativity `⟨x,b(x)⟩≤−λ|x|²` outside a ball, take `V(x)=1+|x|²`. For each initial moment bound `R`, there is a sufficiently small interaction range `|τ|≤τ₀(R)` such that

`W_(ρ₂)(μ_t,ν_t)≤e^(−ct)W_(ρ₂)(μ₀,ν₀)`,  `W₁(μ_t,ν_t)≤K₀e^(−ct)W_(ρ₂)(μ₀,ν₀)`

when `μ₀(V),ν₀(V)≤R`. For arbitrary finite-second-moment initial laws, a small interaction range and `K₁` give the distinct estimate

`W_(ρ₂)(μ_t,ν_t)≤e^(−ct)(W_(ρ₂)(μ₀,ν₀)+K₁[εμ₀(V)+εν₀(V)]²)`.

The proof first establishes a uniform quadratic Lyapunov estimate; the blended coupling yields a differential inequality for the multiplicative cost, with nonlinear terms bounded using the moment estimates and the Lipschitz interaction. Integrating and letting the cutoff vanish gives local contraction, and an integrable transient term yields the all-initial-law estimate. The authors say the smallness threshold is not claimed sharp and note that large interaction can produce multiple stationary solutions.

Section 2.4 also treats subgeometric drift: `LV≤C−η(V)` with increasing concave `η`, plus a growth condition, yields a coupling-time tail bound of the form `||p_t(x,·)−p_t(y,·)||_TV≤ρ₁(x,y)/H⁻¹(ct)`, where `H(u)=∫_l^u ds/η(s)`.

## Transfer boundary

These are diffusion-semigroup contractions under specified drift, Lyapunov, geometry, and coupling assumptions. The McKean–Vlasov theorems control the evolution of its law through time; they do not control the nested discrete map `θ↦π(K_θ)`. To use them for A121/A122 would require an exact diffusion representation and verification of the metric and dissipativity assumptions, neither of which has been established for the finite-state rank kernel.
