## Model, state space, and equilibrium

The state space \(S\) and action space \(A\) are finite. A population state is \(m\in\mathcal P(S)\). At state \(i\), action \(a\) gives reward \(r_{ia}(m)\) and row-generator rates \(Q^a_{ij}(m)\); a stationary randomized strategy has probabilities \(\pi_{ia}\), with
\[
Q^\pi_{ij}(m)=\sum_{a\in A}\pi_{ia}Q^a_{ij}(m).
\]
The discounted reward criterion uses \(\beta\in(0,1)\) as stated in this paper. Assumption A1 requires every \(m\mapsto Q^a_{ij}(m)\) to be Lipschitz on \(\mathcal P(S)\), and every \(m\mapsto r_{ia}(m)\) to be continuous. Finite \(S,A\) and compactness of \(\mathcal P(S)\) then give bounded rewards. The paper's stationary MFE is a pair \((m,\pi)\): \(m^TQ^\pi(m)=0\), and \(\pi\) is optimal against the frozen \(m\) among all strategies, including time-dependent ones, for every initial distribution \(x_0\). The pair matters because a reducible generator may have several stationary distributions for the same strategy.

## Exact results and proof dependencies

**Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2 (physical pp.10–12).** Fix \(m\). The controlled problem is a finite-state continuous-time Markov decision process with rates \(Q^a(m)\), rewards \(r_{ia}(m)\), and discount \(\beta\). Under A1, a stationary optimum exists. Write \(V^*(m)\) for the optimal value vector and
\[
O_i(m)=\arg\max_{a\in A}\{r_{ia}(m)+\sum_j Q^a_{ij}(m)V^*_j(m)\},\qquad
D(m)=\{d:S\to A:d(i)\in O_i(m)\ \forall i\}.
\]
Then \(D(m)\ne\varnothing\), and the optimal stationary strategies are exactly the convex hull of the deterministic stationary strategies in \(D(m)\). The proof freezes \(m\), uses continuous-time uniformization with \(\alpha=\|Q(m)\|/(\beta+\|Q(m)\|)\), then invokes the finite-state discounted-MDP optimality and policy-evaluation results (Puterman). The converse uses positive probability on a non-maximizing state-action choice to force a strict value loss. This is a statement about stationary randomized policies in the frozen finite MDP, not arbitrary distributions over transition kernels.

**Theorem 4.1 (physical pp.14–15).** Under A1, a stationary MFE exists. Define
\[
\phi(m)=\{x\in\mathcal P(S):\exists\pi\in\operatorname{conv}(D(m)),\ x^TQ^\pi(m)=0\}.
\]
For every \(m\), \(\phi(m)\) is nonempty (a finite-state CTMC has a stationary law for any admissible \(\pi\)); it is convex; and its graph is closed. The proof of closedness takes convergent \((m_n,x_n)\), extracts a convergent subsequence of compact stationary-strategy vectors, then uses that only finitely many support rectangles \(A_1\times\cdots\times A_{|S|}\) can occur and continuity of \(V^d\) and \(Q^a\) to retain optimality and balance in the limit. Kakutani's fixed-point theorem gives \(m\in\phi(m)\); Lemma 3.1/Theorem 3.2 make the corresponding pair an MFE.

**Theorem 5.1, cut criterion (physical p.16).** For any stationary strategy \(\pi\), any \(T\subseteq S\), and any stationary population distribution \(m\),
\[
\sum_{j\in T}\sum_{i\in S\setminus T}m_iQ^\pi_{ij}(m)
=\sum_{j\in T}\sum_{i\in S\setminus T}m_jQ^\pi_{ji}(m).
\]
This is an exact reformulation of the coordinate balance equations plus generator conservativity: sum the stationarity identity over \(j\in T\) and cancel internal \(T\)-flows. It does not add a separate assumption.

**Theorem 5.2 (physical pp.17–21).** Fix \(m\) and assume \(Q^d(m)\) is irreducible for **every deterministic stationary strategy** \(d\in D^s\), not merely for optimal strategies. Let \(D(m)=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}\) be the deterministic optimal strategies. Each has a unique stationary distribution \(x^{d_k}(m)\), and
\[
\phi(m)=\operatorname{conv}\{x^{d_1}(m),\ldots,x^{d_n}(m)\}.
\]
The paper gives a closed form: for irreducible conservative \(Q^\pi\), replace the last row of \((Q^\pi)^T\) by \((1,\ldots,1)\), obtaining \(\widetilde Q^\pi\); then \(x^\pi=(\widetilde Q^\pi)^{-1}e_S\). Lemma 5.3 proves the deleted minor has eigenvalues with negative real part, hence full rank and determinant sign \((-1)^{|S|+1}\); Lemma 5.5 applies Cramer's rule. Lemma 5.6 uses continuity in \(\pi\), connectedness of the strategy simplex, and nonvanishing determinant to show the denominator sign is uniform. Multilinearity of the determinant in the generator columns then writes \(x^\pi\) as a convex combination of deterministic-policy stationary laws; the coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. For the reverse inclusion, mix the occupation arrays of optimal deterministic policies: set \(z_{ia}=\sum_k\lambda_k x_i^{d_k}\mathbf1_{\{a=d_k(i)\}}\) and \(x_i=\sum_k\lambda_kx_i^{d_k}\). For \(x_i>0\), define \(\pi_{ia}=z_{ia}/x_i\); in zero-mass rows choose any optimal action. The balance equations mix linearly, so \(x^TQ^\pi=0\), and every action used with positive mass is optimal. This is the occupation-measure convexity mechanism of Theorem 4.1; a convex mixture of policies alone does not imply that its stationary law is the mixture of their stationary laws.

**Theorem 5.7 (physical pp.21–22).** On a set \(T\subseteq\mathcal P(S)\) where every \(Q^\pi(m)\) is irreducible, all MFE distributions in \(T\) are obtained by partitioning deterministic actions into rectangles \(A_1\times\cdots\times A_{|S|}\), taking fixed points of the convex hull of the deterministic stationary-law maps for policies in that rectangle, and intersecting with the corresponding optimality region \(\mathrm{Opt}(A_1\times\cdots\times A_{|S|})\). For constant dynamics, Corollary 5.8 replaces each stationary-law map by \((\widetilde Q^d)^{-1}e_S\). This is a characterization procedure under the stated irreducibility condition, not a uniqueness result.

The complete paper also reads the two-state consumer-choice example (pp.22–26), the three-state corruption example (pp.27–29), and Appendix A (pp.29–31). In the consumer-choice example the paper finds one to five stationary distributions as parameters and best-response thresholds vary. The corruption example uses the cut criterion for a three-state model and notes omitted cases. These examples remain model-specific.

## W21 interface and limits

Useful interfaces are finite-state stationary balance, best-response set-valued fixed points, cut-flow identities, and the determinant/convex-hull formula for irreducible policy generators. A target law map \(\Phi\) must first be shown to arise from a finite-state MFG of this kind, with continuous/Lipschitz rates and rewards and the stated optimality semantics. The result does not establish that an arbitrary rank-based Markov kernel is such a generator, that its stationary-law map is continuous across reducible strata, or that iterating \(\Phi\) converges.
