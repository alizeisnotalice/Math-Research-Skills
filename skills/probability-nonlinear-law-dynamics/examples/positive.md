# Positive pattern: separate the inner stationary law from the outer map

**Evidence used:** W21-001, complete 15-page Aldous–Feng paper; card and section coverage linked from `references/sources.md`.

For a fixed compact metric space and fixed parameter law `theta`, W21-001 Theorem 1 proves that the order-statistic kernel `K_{theta,j,k}` has a unique stationary law `pi_{j,k}(theta)`. The proof couples two chains using shared fresh samples; a partition convexity bound gives a per-two-step coalescence probability at least `k^{-(k-1)}`, and the displayed total-variation bound is

`D(2t) <= (1 - k^{-(k-1)})^t` for integers `t >= 1`.

A precise use is: for each fixed `theta`, denote the unique inner invariant law by `pi(theta)` and use the theorem's bound to control convergence of the frozen chain. Then separately define `Phi(theta)=pi(theta)` and prove the requested continuity or orbit property of `Phi` by an outer-map argument.

The same source proves that, on `[0,1]`, for `k=2` the only laws invariant for `pi_{2,2}` (and, by Theorem 3, `pi_{1,2}`) are point masses and equal two-point laws (Theorem 4, PDF pp.10–12). This exact parameter scope is useful when the user asks the `k=2` classification; it does not settle other `k`.

## Positive pattern: choose a theorem after matching the operator

- **Continuous-time nonlinear Markov flow:** W21-003 Theorem 3.1 gives an invariant law for a continuous generator map `Q(m)` by applying Kakutani to its stationary-law correspondence; Theorem 3.2 adds irreducibility and a nonsingular reduced Jacobian for uniqueness. In two states, a continuous scalar vector field with a unique equilibrium gives convergence by a sign argument. This is a valid route when the target really is the ODE `m˙=mQ(m)` and those hypotheses hold.
- **Group-indexed QSO:** W21-018 proves that the distance to the uniform law on the current support coset tends to zero after the exact coefficients `p_ij,k=μ_(k−i−j)` are established. The coset can move periodically; only when the support is all of `G` does this directly give convergence to one fixed law, the center. Its proof's quadratic error recurrence implies a double-exponential bound, but Theorem 3.11's printed `log log ||error||` expression is undefined near zero and should not be repeated as a valid rate theorem. The support algebra is the proof mechanism, so verifying the coefficient identity comes first.
- **Poisson RDE:** W21-036 proves a unique fixedpoint and alternating shifted limits for its Poisson d-th-minimum transform. The complementary-CDF operator, integrability domain, and shift normalization are all specified before using the convergence theorem. This is a positive global-iteration example whose shift qualification must be retained.
- **Nonlinear Markov update:** W21-004 Theorem 2.2 applies to the exact update `N(μ)=μP_μ` when the source cross-state/law bound is `sup d_TV(P_μ(x,·),P_ν(y,·))≤2(1−α)`, which guarantees common transition mass at least `α` (not `2α`), and the same-state law sensitivity is at most `λ≤α`. It proves a unique invariant law and gives geometric convergence for `λ<α` or a `2/(λn)` bound at the threshold. This is an A122 method candidate only after the target's two Dobrushin bounds are proved; it is not an A121 outer-map result.
- **Finite-state MFG fixedpoint and stability:** W21-013 Theorem 4.1 gives a Kakutani existence proof for the stated discounted finite-state/action stationary-MFE correspondence under A1. W21-014 then supplies local-stability criteria for its myopic differential inclusion, and a two-policy global alternative only under the switching-surface and nonlinear-flow assumptions. Match the game and continuous-time inclusion before using either theorem.
- **Doubly stochastic QSO structure:** W21-021 Corollary 4.3 gives a useful finite-dimensional certificate once the target is already proved to be a QSO and each symmetric coefficient slice satisfies the normalized `U_1` conditions and the slices sum to the all-ones matrix. This certifies majorization structure; it does not by itself show that iterates converge.
- **Stationary-regime passage:** W21-007 Theorem 1 applies when stochastic systems `Y^N` satisfy Feller and fixed-time conditional convergence to a deterministic measurable process. Each weak limit point of invariant laws is then invariant for that process. The corollary's Dirac limit additionally requires tightness and a continuous semiflow with a common global attractor. This transfers invariant probabilities across `N`, not iterations of one fixed operator.
- **Diffusion contraction:** W21-012 gives explicit reflection-coupling contractions for diffusion semigroups and for specified McKean–Vlasov law flows. Its Lyapunov, one-sided curvature, interaction Lipschitz, and small-interaction hypotheses are part of the theorem; retain whether the cost is additive or multiplicative.
- **Weighted-sum smoothing RDE:** W21-025 classifies nontrivial fixedpoints of `T(μ)=Law(Σ_i A_iZ_i)` through the root `p(α)=1`, weight moments, and lattice profile. Its iteration theorem requires matching small-argument Laplace asymptotics. First derive that independent-copy weighted-sum identity for the target before applying it.
- **Centered recursive-cost contraction:** W21-027 proves `W₂(SF,SG)≤√(2/3)W₂(F,G)` for a centered Quicksort cost recursion. The exact coupling uses two independent copies and a common uniform pivot; the factor is specific to the `τ²+(1−τ)²` recursion.

- **Generic affine RDE (W21-029):** if the actual transform is \(Law(\sum_iT_iX_i+C)\) with iid children independent of the coefficient family, finite second moments, zero toll mean, and \(\sum_iET_i^2<1\), Theorem 3 gives a unique centered fixedpoint and geometric W2 iteration. The finite recursion approximating that RDE must still be proved separately. The distinct §9 Find-process operator uses a function-valued \(L_p(\Omega\times[0,1])\) metric: its source-printed sufficient coefficient \(2/(2p+1)^{1/p}\) exceeds 1 at \(p=3\), while direct integration over the two disjoint support intervals gives the separately derived contraction factor \((2/(p+2))^{1/p}\). Keep those calculations separate and retain the source's regular-version gap.
- **Finite-chain stationary sensitivity (W21-038):** if every base and perturbed matrix has one irreducible class and the base matrices have a uniform scrambling margin, the source bounds stationary-law changes by the row-matrix perturbation divided by \(1-	au_1(P)\). The parameter-to-kernel bound is an additional lemma.
- **Wasserstein stationary sensitivity (W21-040):** under the source geometric Wasserstein contraction and perturbed-kernel Lyapunov drift, Theorem 3.1 gives finite-time bounds and a stationary perturbation corollary. Under the source's `n∈N` notation read as positive integers, use `C'=max(C,1)` in both Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 because their proofs include a zero-power term. For a family of kernels, verify common constants and the weighted one-step error before deriving any outer-map Lipschitz bound.

The detailed cards and physical-page coverage are linked from [the source map](../references/sources.md).


## Positive recursive-law examples with distinct hypotheses

- **Vector/scalar transfer (W21-030):** apply its Zolotarev theorem only after deriving the independent-copy recursion, L_s coefficient/toll convergence, strict contraction, and control of small/self indices. Theorem 4.1 is multivariate and Theorem 5.1 univariate; neither is an arbitrary simplex-map result.
- **Degenerate recursion (W21-031):** a coefficient tending to one can still yield a normal limit under negative expected log-index drift and the stated third-moment, mean, variance, and toll rates. This does not supply a strict contraction. The multi-branch extension’s proof is a sketch.
- **Deterministic weighted RDEs (W21-032/034):** the countable weighted-sum classification depends on infinite divisibility and the multiplicative group generated by fixed weights; weighted minima yield Weibull or constrained log-periodic Weibull laws under their own support/exponent assumptions. Keep the two operators and their hypotheses separate.
- **Urn RDE systems (W21-035):** the source derives affine systems and contraction constants from a balanced-urn tree split and specified moment classes. For cyclic urns, retain the phase rotation and asymptotic periodicity instead of reporting convergence to a single fixed real law.

The complete source proofs and physical-page coverage are linked from [the source map](../references/sources.md).


## Positive method pattern: compose only after matching stationary-law hypotheses

W21-039 Theorem 3.1/Corollary 3.1 gives a quantitative perturbation template for `π(P)` when the base kernel is uniformly ergodic and the perturbed kernel has an invariant law. Its finite-state rank-kernel use first proves `||K_θ−K_η||op≤k||θ−η||` by maximal product coupling, then composes with the source bound only under the exact base-chain hypotheses. Uniform outer regularity requires uniform mixing constants; no outer orbit conclusion follows from this card. The theorem and coupling derivation are in [the W21-039 card](../evidence/W21/cards/W21-039.md).

## Positive method pattern: exact RDE/QSO classes

W21-041/045 give distinct full-text proof templates for weighted smoothing transforms under their branching, function-class and integrability assumptions; W21-042 adds a finite-mean weighted-sum tail theorem. W21-043 supports the stated elementary BRW criterion, elementary-class uniqueness, and separately conditioned moment/contraction tools; its arXiv-v1 full regular-variation and all-fixedpoint claims are isolated after neutral review. W21-044 handles the exact centered parental-average-plus-independent-noise QSO; its Theorem 5 requires that the noise-factor weak limit already exists. These sources are provisionally eligible at the tool-method level; none has a proved A121/A122 representation.
