# Finite unit-distance constraints to plane coloring

Source: OpenAI, *The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable*, SHA-256 \`607c2601b5ae655c7c116272840e260f71f637aacf611453186e0a1c65111d36\`, physical p. 26, proof of Theorem 1.3. Pinned snapshot: https://github.com/openai/math/tree/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6

The source's reverse implication first obtains a conull partition into strictly unit-independent density-point classes.  Any fixed finite plane configuration has a translate wholly in that conull set, so its unit-distance graph admits the corresponding proper \(k\)-coloring.  In the compact product \(\{1,\ldots,k\}^{\mathbb R^2}\), each unit-edge condition is closed and depends on two coordinates.  Since every finite family is satisfiable, the finite intersection property and product compactness produce a coloring satisfying every unit-edge condition.

This transfer needs finite satisfiability of every unit-distance constraint set and the stated product compactness setting.  It does not make a finite coloring test decide measurability or any stronger regularity of a plane coloring.
