# Projector metric and support-complex reduction

Source: OpenAI, *A nine-dimensional counterexample to Borsuk's covering assertion*, SHA-256 \`e7ffa6f812d97395aa7794f4dc0f394ea36f24b00ced0acad29157c4c3bcca87\`, physical pp. 3-7, Section 2 and Lemmas 2.1-2.4.

For a line \(x=[u]\), write \(P_x=uu^\mathsf T\).  The source fixes
\[
\|P_x-P_y\|_F^2=2-2\langle u,v\rangle^2,
\]
so the diameter of \(X_k=\{P_x\}\) is \(\sqrt2\), attained exactly by orthogonal lines.  A strict-diameter cover is enlarged by a gap and converted with a smooth partition of unity into an admissible map: coordinates are nonnegative, sum to one, and supports at orthogonal lines are disjoint.

The resulting finite object is the support complex \(I\in K\) iff \(I\subseteq S(x)\) for some line.  The source then extends the map to positive semidefinite matrices through a rotational average; its coordinate sum is \(\operatorname{tr}Q\), and positive coordinates are exactly those used on \(\mathbb P(\operatorname{ran}Q)\).  This records the stated reduction, not a generic Borsuk-cover solver.
