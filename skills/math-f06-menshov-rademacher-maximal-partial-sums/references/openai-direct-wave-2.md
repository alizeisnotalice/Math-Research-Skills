# Finite block oscillation and Hilbert van der Corput

Sources: *Pointwise convergence of triple ergodic averages for mixing transformations*, family 154, SHA-256 `e76fd7732f10b2501961f4c0e1febe00c3ed29ec553aefd4fb97b31c5473091d`, [official PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Pointwise-convergence-of-triple-ergodic-averages-for-mixing-transformations-October-4-2026/pointwise-triple-ergodic-averages-mixing-transformations.pdf), physical pp. 5--6, Lemmas 2.3--2.4.

For uniformly bounded measurable \(Z_k\), an oscillation estimate over every deterministic list of \(w\) disjoint index blocks with total \(L^1\) oscillation at most \(C\sqrt w\) implies almost-everywhere convergence. The finite contradiction chooses deterministic terminal indices from monotone finite maxima on a positive-measure non-Cauchy set.

For a bounded Hilbert sequence \(u_n\), use the finite window identity with a fixed width \(H\), expand the squared norm, then take \(N\) to infinity before \(H\) to obtain the van der Corput criterion from correlations \(\gamma_h\to0\).

Boundary: the oscillation hypothesis must hold for every deterministic block list; convergence of selected subsequences or a single block partition is insufficient. Mixing is an application-specific way to produce correlations, not part of the finite Hilbert inequality.
