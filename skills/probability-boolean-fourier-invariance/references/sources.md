## Read primary results

- **M0112, Kahn–Kalai–Linial (1988):** [author-hosted PDF](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~kalai/kkl.pdf), conference DOI 10.1109/SFCS.1988.21923. Partial online reading; local attempted arXiv copy was actually BKS and was removed.
- **BKS1999, Benjamini–Kalai–Schramm:** [primary journal PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1999__90__5_0.pdf). Full 46-page local text read. Theorem 1.9’s asymptotic criterion sums positive levels 1≤|S|≤k, excluding the constant coefficient; its fixed-size-noise transfer also needs high-level tail control. Influence is forced-flip probability. BKS noise itself flips each bit independently at rate ε; a separate operation that resamples a uniform bit at rate ε induces an actual flip only at ε/2. Theorem 1.6 is \(II(f)\le C\Lambda(f)^2(1-\log\Lambda(f))\log n\).
- **MOO/M0235, Mossel–O'Donnell–Oleszkiewicz (2010):** [Annals PDF](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v171-n1-p05-p.pdf). Full 50-page local text read. Theorems 3.18–3.20 give conditional invariance and smoothing interfaces; Theorem 4.4 is Majority Is Stablest under low-degree-influence assumptions, and Theorem 4.9 transfers the reveal/prediction result to product spaces. Physical pages 21, 26–27, 34, and 40 were rendered for formula checks. Under Hypothesis 1, Theorem 3.18’s error is \( (2B/r!)d\eta^{-rd}\tau^{r/2-1}\) (no extra \(r^{r/2-1}\)); Theorem 3.19 has separate errors, including \(d\eta^{-rd}\tau^{r/2-1}\) under Hypothesis 1 and \(10^d\tau\) under Hypothesis 4, without \(B\). Theorem 3.20 assumes \(\mathbb E[Q^2]\le1\) and Hypothesis 1, 3, or 4 only.
- **M0118, de Wolf (2008):** [Theory of Computing article](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/gs001/). Full 18-page CWI author copy read by L08 and hash/page count rechecked. The uniform-cube character/Parseval framework, Bonami–Beckner statement, KKL Fourier-weight corollary, Talagrand influence proof, sensitivity/degree consequences, and literature guide are covered in the canonical card. In §4.2, for R=2^n/|A| and k-subset parity bias β_S, the average square bias is O((ln R/n)^k) when 1≤k≤n and k≤2 ln R; the optimizer is δ=k/(2 ln R), and the implicit constant is inside the kth power. The paper does not impose a separate k≤n/2 condition. The survey defers the difficult one-bit hypercontractivity proof; the old `booleansurvey.pdf` remains a rejected, unrelated physics artifact.
- **KKL2014, Bourgain–Kahn–Kalai:** [arXiv:1409.3033](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3033). Full 21-page arXiv v1 read by L08. The random-clause Theorems 1.1–1.2 and Theorem 1.3’s projection result concern large coalitions (up to just under half the coordinates) under density assumptions; they do not yield B162’s (O(\log n)) coalition under a small individual-influence upper bound. The exact projection/existential-completion definitions and proof dependencies are in the source card. One local normalization issue is isolated there: Eq. (16), physical p.13, omits a factor 4 relative to standard normalized Walsh coefficients for the paper’s L1 flip influence; the proof uses only an absolute threshold constant, so this factor can be absorbed and does not invalidate the existence/scale theorem.
- **FRIEDGUT2004, Friedgut (2004):** [Cambridge DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548303005832). Conjectures 2.2 and 2.6 read in the author-uploaded text; fixed-ε discrete statement remains a conjecture in that source.
- **BGP2012, Benjamini–Gurel-Gurevich–Peled:** [arXiv:1201.3261](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3261). Full text read; Theorem 16 settles only the two-level majority estimate. Section 5, Question 4 asks for (K_2) of iterated majority of threes and supplies no solution (A019).
- **KANE2011, Kane:** [arXiv:1012.1614](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.1614). Full 10-page local text read; Gaussian-marginal k-independence PTF upper bound, not optimal.
- **M0113, Kushilevitz–Mansour (1993):** [SIAM record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0222080). Full 22-page author manuscript read; local D02 PDF has reversed physical-page order. Its recursive prefix-energy estimator uses membership queries at correlated inputs with a shared fixed suffix; this is not passive iid labeled sampling. Fourier-sparse learning and parity-decision-tree bounds are background only, not an A165 Fourier-L0 lower bound or an A166 input-support theorem.
- **M0114, Nisan–Szegedy (1994):** [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01263419). Full 13-page paper read; Theorem 1.3 gives \(\deg(f)\le D(f)\le16\deg(f)^8\), while Theorem 1.5 gives \(\widetilde{\deg}(f)\le\deg(f)\le D(f)\le c\widetilde{\deg}(f)^8\) for uniform error 1/3. These degree and decision-tree comparisons do not lower-bound Fourier support.
- **M0122, Gopalan–O’Donnell–Servedio–Shpilka–Wimmer (2011):** full 25-page author manuscript read by W10. Theorems 3.3–3.4 use squared Fourier-ℓ₂ tail to recover sparse Boolean spectra; Theorems 4.1 and 5.3 test Fourier sparsity and Fourier dimension. The paper explicitly separates support cardinality, span dimension, algebraic degree, and input support; its bounds do not prove A165’s lower bound.
- **M0128, Sanyal (ECCC 2014/088 revision 1):** full 9-page report read by L08. Theorem 1.2 gives an upper bound on Fourier dimension in terms of Fourier sparsity, so the direction is opposite to A165’s desired sparsity lower bound.
- **M0129, Shpilka–Tal–Volk (arXiv:1304.0371v2):** full 26-page manuscript, including appendices, read by L08. Its headline Boolean-cube theorems are printed for 0/1-valued f, while the proofs use the sign encoding 1−2f; §2 (physical p.6) describes the full spectral norms as roughly comparable up to a factor of 2; exactly, nonconstant coefficients scale by −2, the constant coefficient becomes 1−2 f̂(∅), and sparsity can change only at the constant term (by at most 1). Do not transfer exact A² codimension/tree bounds across encodings while keeping the same numerical A. The results assume small Fourier ℓ₁ norm, and some parity-tree bounds also assume at most s nonzero coefficients; neither premise is supplied by A165.
- **M0130, Sanders (arXiv:1804.04050v3):** full 11-page author manuscript read by W10 using the arXiv text corresponding to the local Oxford accepted copy. Theorem 1.1 represents integer-valued functions on \(\mathbb F_2^n\) with Fourier A-norm at most M as signed sums of at most \(\exp(M^{3+o(1)})\) subspace indicators; Proposition 2.3 and Lemma 3.1 retain linear dependence on p in their codimension bounds, respectively \(O(p\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon))\) and \(O(p\epsilon^{-2})\). This is an ℓ₁ structural theorem, not an ℓ₀ bound.
- **M0132, Yaroslavtsev–Zhou (arXiv:1910.05686v1):** full 19-page paper read by L08. It gives nonadaptive oracle algorithms for Fourier-sparsity testing/distance estimation; its distance parameter is squared Fourier-ℓ₂ error to real-valued sparse spectra, not Hamming distance or an unconditional structural sparsity lower bound.
- **M0134, Friedgut–Kalai–Naor (2002):** full 12-page canonical FKN2002 article read by W10. Theorem 1.1 assumes small squared Fourier mass above level 1 and gives constant/dictator stability; for its 0/1-valued comparands, squared L² distance equals normalized Hamming distance, while unsquared L² is its square root. The edge-boundary corollary uses the paper’s normalized influence identity. The social-choice Theorem 1.3 is cited, not proved in this paper.
- **M0137, Gutin–Yeo (arXiv:1106.1049v3):** full 10-page manuscript read by L08. Hypercontractive moment bounds require bounded Fourier width; preserve the width hypothesis and distinguish the resulting coefficient norms from Fourier ℓ₀.
- **M0138, Ada–Fawzi–Hatami (arXiv:1205.5282v1):** full 17-page manuscript read by L08. Its spectral-norm results are restricted to symmetric Boolean functions and concern Fourier ℓ₁, not general Fourier support cardinality.
- **M0140, Haviv–Regev (CCC 2015):** full 14-page paper read by W10. The list-size bound counts k-sparse Boolean functions within an integer number d of input errors; for a general real center, its proof compares two Boolean candidates g and g₀, whose difference is three-valued with input support at most 2d and Fourier support at most 2k. The paper also gives exact-learning and nonadaptive one-sided Booleanity-testing bounds. The lower bound is not for adaptive or two-sided testers.
- **M0141, Defant–Mastyło–Pérez (arXiv:1706.03670v1):** full 25-page manuscript read by W10. Theorem 1.1 controls the Fourier coefficient \(\ell_{2d/(d+1)}\) norm for bounded Fourier degree d by \(C^{\sqrt{d\log d}}\|f\|_\infty\) (asymptotic form for d≥2); this is not an ℓ₀ or ℓ₁ support theorem.
- **M0142, Mande–Sanyal (arXiv:2008.00266v1):** full 23-page manuscript and appendices read by W10. The unconditional parity-decision-tree depth is O(\sqrt{k}) for Fourier sparsity k; the improved folding conclusion is conditional, and the XOR communication conjecture is not proved.
- **DKN-PTF2:** [arXiv:0911.3389](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3389). Full 36-page local text read. Theorem 1.1 gives a \(\widetilde{\Omega}(\epsilon^{-9})\)-wise Boolean-cube bound (polylog factors suppressed) for degree-2 PTFs; Theorem D.1 gives a separate Ω(ε⁻⁸) Gaussian-coordinate result, and the paper treats intersections in both models.
- **BEL-PTF:** [arXiv:0911.3473](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3473). Full 30-page local text read. The approximation lower bound uses many influential input coordinates; the k-wise fooling theorem assumes the PTF factors through a bounded number of normalized linear forms. The first page’s “September 9, 2021” date is retained as a source-print discrepancy with its 2009 arXiv history.
- **HKM-PTF:** [Theory of Computing](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v010a001/). Full 20-page local text read. Theorems 1.3–1.6 give Boolean/Gaussian noise-sensitivity and average-sensitivity bounds; Theorem 8.2 is \(\mathrm{AS}(f)\le3n^{1-2^{-d}}\), with statement/proof on physical PDF pp. 14–15 of the 20-page arXiv author version (printed footer pp. 13–14). Preserve the stated Gaussian PTF class and distinguish this local version from the journal’s 26-page pagination.
- **M0123, O’Donnell–Wright–Zhou (2011):** [ICALP/Springer chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-22006-7_28). Full 13-page IAS author manuscript read by L08. FEI is proved for symmetric Boolean functions (constant 12.04), product-action d-part-symmetric functions (12.04+log₂d), and recursively read-once decision trees (4.88). The read-once definition requires disjoint variable sets for the two branches recursively; pathwise no-repeat alone is insufficient. The 4.88 analytic supremum is numerically checked in the paper.
- **DKN-HS, Diakonikolas–Gopalan–Jaiswal–Servedio–Viola (2010):** [SIAM J. Comput. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/100783030). Full 27-page author manuscript read by L08. For arbitrary real halfspaces on the uniform Boolean cube, k-wise independence fools to error ε when k≥C ε⁻² log²(1/ε); the comparison distribution and real threshold/weight scope are part of the result. Regularity is normalized by ‖w‖₂=1 and maxᵢ|wᵢ|≤ε; do not transfer this halfspace theorem directly to degree-d PTFs.
- **MZ-PTF, Meka–Zuckerman (2010/2013):** [SIAM J. Comput. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/100811623). Full 28-page arXiv v5 manuscript read by L08. The regular-halfspace generator theorem uses α-almost pairwise independence and a δ-almost 4-wise source with δ=ε²/(4n⁵); the regular degree-d PTF theorem instead assumes 4d-wise independence and yields error O(d ε^{2/(4d+1)}). Preserve these distinct hypotheses and the explicit/implicit-generator distinction.
- **M0136, Kolountzakis et al. (2009):** [Combinatorica DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-009-2310-z). Full 18-page author manuscript read by L08. For symmetric Boolean functions, excluding constants and parity/complement, τ(k)≤4k/log k for large k. This is a symmetric-class Fourier-support result with imported number-theoretic inputs, not a lower bound for arbitrary Boolean Fourier support.
- **M0124, Tsang–Wong–Xie–Zhang (2013):** [FOCS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/focs.2013.76). Full 25-page arXiv manuscript read by L08. It proves Fourier sparsity/spectral-norm bounds for XOR communication and parity decision trees; Theorem 11 assumes discarded squared Fourier ℓ₂ mass at most μ², not Hamming distance. Its log-rank conclusion is for XOR matrices only.
- **M0110, Simons open-problem compilation:** [PDF](https://simons.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/openprobsmerged.pdf). Selected pages read; its B162 notation is less precise than Friedgut's original conjecture.
- **CG2026, Chattopadhyay–Gurumukhani:** [arXiv:2609.16401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16401). Full 18-page arXiv v2 read by L08. Theorem 3.1 gives a coalition of size (O_\epsilon(n/\sqrt{\log n})) that can force an existentially selected output with probability at least (1-\epsilon) on finite alphabets; the continuous-cube transfer is stated for monotone or Borel functions. The proof imports Hatami’s p-biased pseudo-junta theorem. It does not settle B162’s discrete (O(\log n)) small-influence claim. Distinct from the separate FRIEDGUT2026 paper.
- **FRIEDGUT2026:** [arXiv:2610.03086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03086). Full six pages; simplified continuous-cube proof.
- **M0145, O'Donnell:** [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139814782). Local 419-page book; only selected passages, not full chapters, read.
- **M0131, Huang (2019):** [Annals DOI](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2019.190.3.6). Full seven-page paper read; sensitivity/degree boundary, not a Fourier-L0 theorem.
