# Product-basis influence identities and multiscale threshold selection

Sources:

- *A Sharp Threshold Bound for Monotone Graph Properties*, family 186, SHA-256 `0e6a660e755ef2b6a30755fa40c18256a4ad7f54fe1040f79e13458c1ecc290a`, [official PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/A-Sharp-Threshold-Bound-for-Monotone-Graph-Properties-September-25-2026/paper.pdf), physical pp. 3--5 and 9, equations (2.1)--(2.3), Lemma 2.1.
- *Integral and fractional expectation thresholds are equivalent*, family 175, SHA-256 `0c02b128253cb6e07f23931992bf377e8dd74c032572143878b1821cdcd32f39`, [official PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Integral-and-fractional-expectation-thresholds-are-equivalent-September-23-2026/paper.pdf), physical pp. 4--8, Theorem 2.1.

For a \(p\)-biased product space, use \(\chi_i=(x_i-p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)}\). Parseval identifies coordinate Fourier mass with \(p(1-p)\) times influence and identifies total degree-weighted mass with total influence. Record the bias and normalization before using either identity.

For a non-\(p\)-small finite set family with distributions supported on its members, the second source gives a multiscale independent-color selector when the displayed weighted mass budget is at most one half. Preserve the common-member event and the stated probability target rather than treating it as an arbitrary monotone-threshold theorem.

Boundary: low-degree capture and monotone differentiation are separate later inputs; the selector does not itself prove a sharp threshold.
