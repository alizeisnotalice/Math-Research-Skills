## Definitions and principal statements

The domain is real-valued $f:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb R$, under the uniform cube inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=\mathbb E_x[f(x)g(x)]$. Fourier coefficients are $\widehat f(\alpha)=\mathbb E_x[f(x)\chi_\alpha(x)]$, and Parseval is $\|f\|_2^2=\sum_\alpha\widehat f(\alpha)^2$. Let $\mathcal F_s$ be all real-valued functions with at most $s$ nonzero Fourier coefficients. The paper measures $\operatorname{dist}_2(f,\mathcal F_s)=\min_{g\in\mathcal F_s}\|f-g\|_2$, but its main estimator estimates the **squared** distance $\operatorname{dist}_2^2$. The theorem is stated for the scale-free case $\|f\|_2=1$.

- **Theorem 1.1 (physical p.3):** for any $s\ge1,\epsilon>0$, with non-adaptive query access to a real function $f:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb R$ of unit $L^2$ norm, an algorithm uses at most $O((s/\epsilon^4)\log(1/\epsilon)\log(1/\delta))$ queries and outputs an estimate of $\operatorname{dist}_2^2(f,\mathcal F_s)$ with additive error at most $\epsilon$, with probability at least $1-\delta$. Its running time is $\widetilde O((s/\epsilon^4)\log(1/\delta))$; the tilde suppresses polylogarithmic factors in $s,1/\epsilon$.
- **Theorem 1.2 (physical p.4):** for $s\le2^{n-1}$, any non-adaptive query algorithm given $f$ with $\|f\|_2^2=1\pm\epsilon$ that distinguishes $s$-sparse from functions $1/3$-far from $\mathcal F_s$ in the paper’s squared-$\ell_2$ distance, with success at least $2/3$, needs at least $c\sqrt{s}$ queries for a universal constant $c>0$. This is for real-valued functions, not only Boolean $f$.

## Algorithm and proof chain for Theorem 1.1

For fixed $f$, its closest $s$-sparse Fourier approximation is the projection onto its $s$ largest-magnitude Fourier coefficients; by Parseval, the squared distance is total energy minus the sum of those $s$ squared coefficients. The algorithm estimates that top-$s$ energy.

1. **Algorithm 1 (physical p.7)** draws a uniformly random codimension-$d$ subspace $H\le\mathbb F_2^n$ with $d=\log_2(2s/\epsilon^4)$. It partitions Fourier indices into cosets $a+H$, $a\in H^\perp$. For each of $\ell=O(\log(1/\epsilon))$ repetitions, it samples $\gamma=O(s\|f\|_2^2/\epsilon^4)$ pairs $x\sim U(\mathbb F_2^n)$, $z\sim U(H^\perp)$ and uses $\chi_a(z)f(x)f(x+z)$ to estimate the energy of bucket $a+H$. It returns the maximum sum over $s$ buckets of the coordinatewise medians.
2. **Proposition 2.7 (physical p.6), Fourier hashing:** averaging $\chi_a(z)f(x)f(x+z)$ over $x,z$ returns exactly $\sum_{\alpha\in a+H}\widehat f(\alpha)^2$. This is the bridge from query samples to bucket energy.
3. **Hashing error.** Definition 3.3 and Lemmas 3.4–3.5 (physical pp.8–9) bound, for random $H$, collision bias and variance when top Fourier coefficients share a bucket. Corollary 3.6 (p.10) sets $2^d=2s/\epsilon^4$ and controls the top-$s$ bucket-energy error by $O(\epsilon^2\|f\|_2^2)$ with constant probability. The proofs use pairwise collision probabilities for random subspaces and Parseval.
4. **Sampling error.** Fact 3.7 and Lemma 3.8 (physical pp.10–11) bound the variance of empirical bucket estimates. Lemma 3.9 (p.11) applies median amplification; Lemma 3.10 (p.12) controls aggregate squared error across the selected $s$ buckets by $\epsilon^2\|f\|_2^2$ in expectation.
5. **Theorem 1.1 proof (physical pp.12–14):** combine hashing and sampling errors, compare the estimated largest-$s$ bucket energy with the true energy of the largest-$s$ Fourier coefficients, and use that this coefficient set defines the best $s$-sparse $L^2$ projection. The paper tracks constants in the proof and absorbs them by parameter rescaling to obtain additive error $\epsilon$ and confidence $1-\delta$.

## Lower bound and A165 limit

Theorem 1.2’s proof (physical pp.15–16) uses a Yao distributional construction: under $D_{YES}$, choose an $s$-sparse random Fourier support with Gaussian coefficients; under $D_{NO}$, use all Fourier characters with Gaussian coefficients and a different variance. For a small fixed query set, compare the induced Gaussian vectors through their covariance matrices and bound total variation; below $c\sqrt{s}$ queries the cases cannot be distinguished at the stated constant success. This is a non-adaptive query lower bound for the testing problem.

**A165 interface:** estimating how far a real function is from Fourier-sparse functions with oracle queries neither gives a structural lower bound on the support of a Boolean function nor uses A165’s “all singleton coefficients nonzero” hypothesis. The result is auxiliary computational context; no $\Omega(n^2)$ conclusion follows. Keep squared $L^2$ distance, Boolean Hamming distance, and Fourier $L^0$ support distinct.
