## Definitions and theorem

On finite products, for $f:\Sigma^n\to\{0,1\}$, a coalition S is allowed to choose the S-coordinates as a function of the random outside input. The paper writes $P_S^b(f,\sigma)$ for the probability that there exists a completion on S that forces output b under the product distribution $\sigma^n$. Its coalition influence is $J_S^b=P_S^b-\Pr[f=b]$, and $J^b(f)=\sum_iJ_i^b(f)$. On the p-biased Boolean cube, resampling influence is $I_i^p(f)=\Pr_{x,y\sim\mu_p}[f(x)\ne f(x^{(i\gets y_i)})]$, with total $I^p=\sum_iI_i^p$. Use the source’s $(P,J,I^p)$ definitions; these are not the discrete Boolean coordinate-flip influences $\operatorname{Inf}_i$ used in B162.

**Theorem 3.1 (physical PDF p.8).** For every fixed $\epsilon>0$, there is $C=C(\epsilon)>0$ such that for all $r,n\in\mathbb N$, every $f:[r]^n\to\{0,1\}$ admits $b\in\{0,1\}$ and $B\subseteq[n]$ with
$$
|B|\le C\,n/\sqrt{\log n},\qquad P_B^b(f)\ge1-\epsilon.
$$
The paper separately states the same bound for monotone or Borel-measurable Boolean functions on $[0,1]^n$ with product-uniform measure. The selected target $b$ is existential: this is not a theorem forcing a target chosen in advance. The finite-alphabet result has no individual-influence upper-bound hypothesis, but its coalition scale is $O_\epsilon(n/\sqrt{\log n})$, not $O(\log n)$.

Corollary 3.2, p.8, transfers to the continuous cube by monotonization and discretization (with $r$ chosen on the order of $n/\epsilon$). Its continuous hypothesis is monotone or Borel measurable; the proof explicitly attends to Borel measurability. Do not silently extend it to arbitrary Lebesgue-measurable functions, since existential projections of Borel sets are the measurable events used in the argument.

## Proof dependency and exact chain

### Direction-switching lemma (Lemma 3.3)

For fixed $\epsilon\in(0,1)$, there is $C(\epsilon)\ge1$ so that for every $r,n$, $\tau>0$, and monotone $f:[r]^n\to\{0,1\}$ with $\mathbb Ef\ge\epsilon$ and $J^0(f)\le\tau$, some $T\subseteq[n]$ has $ |T|\le \exp(C\tau^2)$ and $P_T^1(f)\ge1-\epsilon$. Locator: Lemma 3.3 p.8, proof p.10. This switches from small aggregate influence toward 0 to a small coalition forcing 1; the direction and density hypotheses are essential.

Proof uses two inputs:

1. **Theorem 3.4 p.9**, explicitly attributed as a simplified Corollary 2.10 of Hatami [Hat12], not proved here: for monotone $g:\{0,1\}^m\to\{0,1\}$ on $\mu_p$, $0<p\le1/2$, $\mathbb E_{\mu_p}g\ge\epsilon$, there is $S$ with $ |S|\le\exp(C(\epsilon)(I^p(g))^2)$ and $P_S^1(g,\mu_p)\ge1-\epsilon$.
2. **Lemma 3.5 pp.9–15**, proved by an encoding: map each $r$-ary coordinate to a block of bits via a monotone max-selector $\phi_a$, chosen so every nonconstant threshold event becomes an OR of a subset of that block. For a monotone $f:[r]^n\to\{0,1\}$, the encoded $g:\{0,1\}^{m}\to\{0,1\}$ is monotone, satisfies $ |\mathbb E_{\mu_p}g-\mathbb E_{U_n}f|\le prn$, and $I^p(g)\le2J^0(f)+4prn^2$. If a bit coalition $S$ forces $g=1$ with probability at least $1-\epsilon$, the set T of original coordinate blocks met by S has $ |T|\le|S|$ and forces $f=1$ with probability at least $1-\epsilon-prn$.

The parameter choice in the proof of Lemma 3.3 is
$$
p=\min\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2rn},\frac{\tau}{2rn^2}\right),
$$
so $prn\le\epsilon/2$ and $4prn^2\le2\tau$. Apply Lemma 3.5 with error $\epsilon/2$, obtaining encoded mean at least $\epsilon/2$ and $I^p(g)\le4\tau$; then Theorem 3.4 yields coalition size $\exp(C(\epsilon)\tau^2)$ after absorbing fixed factors in the constant. This carefully inherits Hatami’s theorem as an external dependency.

### Theorem 3.1 iteration

After Claim 2.2 reduces to monotone $f$, set $\delta=(100C_{\mathrm{Lemma,3.3}})^{-1}\sqrt{\log n}/n$ (with the paper’s stated constant). Begin with no fixed coordinates. If $\Pr[f_i=0]\ge1-\epsilon$, return target 0. Otherwise, if some remaining j has $J_j^0(f_i)\ge\delta$, fix that coordinate to the minimum alphabet value 1 and continue; each such step raises $\Pr[f_i=0]$ by at least $\delta$, so there are at most $1/\delta=O(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ steps. If instead all remaining $J_j^0<\delta$, then $J^0(f_i)\le n\delta$; apply Lemma 3.3 with $\tau=n\delta$, giving a 1-forcing coalition of size $\exp(C(n\delta)^2)\le\sqrt n$ after choosing the small constant. Monotonicity lets the original coalition and the coordinates already fixed to 1 be combined without worsening the forcing probability. This establishes the stated $O_\epsilon(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ bound.

### Monotonization/discretization and probability lemmas

- Claim 2.2, pp.6–7: replace each one-coordinate slice by an upper interval of equal measure (and complementary lower interval for output 0). This preserves mean and does not increase either coalition-completion probability. The proof checks Borel measurability using Fubini–Tonelli and a Borel description of the slice threshold.
- Claim 2.3, p.7: discretize a monotone function on $[0,1]^n$ to $[r]^n$ on the regular grid; expectation and each coalition probability change by at most $n/r$, by a union bound on coordinate grid errors.
- Claim 2.5, p.7: data processing for total variation controls expectations/probabilities of events; used to transfer distributional approximation through encodings.

## Relation to B162 and limits

B162 asks for a monotone Boolean function sequence with each discrete coordinate influence at most $c\log n/n$, and a coalition of size $O_c(\log n)$ fixed to all 1 or all 0 making a conditional mean tend to an endpoint; its source quantifiers still require clarification. This paper proves neither that statement nor its small-influence premise. Theorem 3.1 applies to arbitrary finite alphabets (hence Boolean cube) and gives a much larger $O_\epsilon(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ coalition with an existential target. The result is therefore a useful boundary/contrast source, not a resolution of the discrete logarithmic coalition question. Its conclusion notes that a separate work by Friedgut [Fri26] improves the general large-alphabet size to $O_\epsilon(n/\log n)$; that result belongs to the distinct `FRIEDGUT2026` source and is not proved here.

## Proof gaps to preserve

1. The key pseudo-junta theorem (Theorem 3.4) is imported from Hatami; this paper supplies only the encoding transfer needed to invoke it.
2. The conclusion’s improved $O_\epsilon(n/\log n)$ is attributed to another paper, not this theorem.
3. Monotone/Borel measurable is the stated continuous-cube domain. No claim for arbitrary Lebesgue-measurable input is established.
4. The output bit $b$ is chosen by the theorem; do not replace it with a prescribed target.
5. Coalition forcing $P_S^b$, total influence toward zero $J^0$, and p-biased resampling influence $I^p$ are different quantities. None should be replaced without proving the required interface.
