# M0131 — Induced subgraphs of hypercubes and a proof of the Sensitivity Conjecture

**Source.** Hao Huang, *Annals of Mathematics* 190 (2019), 949–955, DOI [10.4007/annals.2019.190.3.6](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2019.190.3.6). The verified local PDF has seven physical pages; SHA-256 is recorded in sources.json. Title, author, pagination, and the main induced-subgraph theorem were cross-checked against the Annals record.

## Exact statements and proof chain

- **Theorem 1.1 (physical PDF p. 1; printed p. 949).** Every induced subgraph \(H\subseteq Q_n\) with \(2^{n-1}+1\) vertices has maximum degree at least \(\sqrt n\). The bound is tight when \(n\) is a square.
- Define the signed adjacency matrices recursively by
  \[
  A_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  A_n=\begin{pmatrix}A_{n-1}&I\\I&-A_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  **Lemma 2.2 (physical p. 4; printed p. 952)** gives \(A_n^2=nI\); trace zero gives eigenvalues \(+\sqrt n\) and \(-\sqrt n\), each with multiplicity \(2^{n-1}\). **Lemma 2.3 (physical p. 4; printed p. 952)** states that if a symmetric matrix \(A\in\{-1,0,1\}^{V(H)\times V(H)}\) is supported on the edges of \(H\), then \(\Delta(H)\ge\lambda_1(A)\). Apply this to the principal submatrix of \(A_n\) on \(H\); Cauchy interlacing yields \(\lambda_1(A_H)\ge\lambda_{2^{n-1}}(A_n)=\sqrt n\). The final proof steps continue on physical p. 5.
- **Theorem 1.3 (physical p. 3; printed p. 951; Gotsman–Linial equivalence).** For every **monotone** \(h:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\), the following are equivalent:
  1. for every induced subgraph \(H\subseteq Q_n\) with \(|V(H)|\ne2^{n-1}\),
     \[
     \Gamma(H):=\max\{\Delta(H),\Delta(Q_n-H)\}\ge h(n);
     \]
  2. every Boolean function \(f\) satisfies \(s(f)\ge h(\deg(f))\).
  
  The graph quantity is the maximum of the degrees on both sides of the cut; it is not just the degree on whichever side has more vertices.
- **Theorems 1.4–1.5 (physical p. 3; printed p. 951).** Theorem 1.1 gives \(s(f)\ge\sqrt{\deg(f)}\). Combining this with the polynomial relation between block sensitivity and degree yields \(bs(f)\le s(f)^4\), the Sensitivity Conjecture.

## Transfer boundary for Fourier work

This is a signed-graph spectral/interlacing theorem and its Gotsman–Linial equivalence. It relates pointwise sensitivity to real multilinear polynomial degree. It gives no lower bound on Fourier \(L_0\) support from nonzero singleton coefficients and does not identify maximum sensitivity with total influence. In particular, it does not settle A165.
