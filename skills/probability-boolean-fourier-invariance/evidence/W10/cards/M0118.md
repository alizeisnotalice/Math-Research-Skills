## Scope and core framework

The paper works on the uniform cube $\{0,1\}^n$, identifying subsets of $[n]$ with bit strings. In the sign convention $\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}$, the characters form an orthonormal basis for the normalized uniform inner product. Thus $f=\sum_S\widehat f(S)\chi_S$ and Parseval gives $\mathbb E f^2=\sum_S\widehat f(S)^2$. Definitions and proofs of orthogonality, Fourier inversion, Parseval and the convolution/product identity are in §§2.1–2.3 (PDF pp.1–4). The article then illustrates sparse parity correlation, Hadamard list decoding and uniform-distribution learning (§§3.1–3.3, pp.4–6); these are examples, not hypotheses or conclusions about coalition forcing.

## Theorem/result cards

### Bonami–Beckner hypercontractivity and the KKL Fourier-weight inequality

**Source locator.** Theorem 1, §4.1, physical PDF p.6; Corollary 1, p.7. Formula and parameter range visually checked against rendered pages p.6–7.

Let $T_\rho f=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\rho^{|S|}\widehat f(S)\chi_S$. For $1\le p\le q$ and $0\le\rho\le\sqrt{(p-1)/(q-1)}$, the paper states
$$
\|T_\rho f\|_q\le \|f\|_p.
$$
The proof is only sketched as induction on $n$; the difficult one-bit base case is deferred to O’Donnell’s Lecture 16 notes, with Janson cited for a general account. Do not cite this survey as containing a complete proof of the inequality.

For $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{-1,0,1\}$ and $0\le\delta\le1$, Corollary 1 states
$$
\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\delta^{|S|}\widehat f(S)^2\le \Pr[f\ne0]^{2/(1+\delta)}.
$$
The derivation is substitution in hypercontractivity with $p=1+\delta$, $q=2$, $\rho=\sqrt\delta$: because $f^2=1_{\{f\ne0\}}$, $\|f\|_{1+\delta}^2=\Pr[f\ne0]^{2/(1+\delta)}$. This requires the stated three-valued range; it is not directly asserted here for arbitrary real-valued functions.

### Random parity bias bound

**Source locator.** §4.2, physical PDF pp.7–8.

For a nonempty set $A\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$, write $R=2^n/|A|$ and $\beta_S=\mathbb E_{x\sim A}[\chi_S(x)]$. Applying the weighted KKL inequality to $1_A$ gives, for $0<\delta\le1$,
$$
\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}k}\beta_S^2\le \delta^{-k}R^{2\delta}.
$$
For integers $1\le k\le n$ with $k\le2\ln R$, the right side is minimized at $\delta=k/(2\ln R)$. Dividing by $\binom nk$ yields the source's Equation (3),
$$
\mathbb E_{S\sim\binom{[n]}k}[\beta_S^2]=O\!\left(\left(\frac{\ln R}{n}\right)^k\right).
$$
The implicit constant is inside the $k$th power; directly from the preceding bound and $\binom nk\ge(n/k)^k$, one may take $(2e\ln R/n)^k$. The optimizer condition is $k\le2\ln R$; the source does not add a separate $k\le n/2$ restriction. This controls the average squared bias over $k$-subsets, not every parity individually. For $A=0^c\times\{0,1\}^{n-c}$, the example has exactly $\mathbb E_S[\beta_S^2]=\binom ck/\binom nk$ (zero if $k>c$), illustrating the scale in the stated range.

### Talagrand influence inequality (proof supplied in this exposition)

**Source locator.** §4.3, physical PDF pp.8–10; Lemma 1 and displayed equation (4), visually checked on pp.9–10.

For Boolean $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, define $\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)=\Pr[f(x)\ne f(x\oplus e_i)]$ under uniform $x$. If all relevant coordinate influences lie strictly between 0 and 1 (zero coordinates are omitted; an influence-one coordinate can be factored out), then
$$
\sum_i\frac{\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)}{\log(1/\operatorname{Inf}_i(f))}=\Omega(\operatorname{Var} f).
$$
The proof first establishes for $g$ with unequal $L_{3/2}$ and $L_2$ norms
$$
\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\frac{\widehat g(S)^2}{|S|}
\le \frac{2.5\|g\|_2^2}{\log(\|g\|_2/\|g\|_{3/2})}.
$$
It splits Fourier levels at the largest $m$ satisfying $2^m\|g\|_{3/2}^2\le\|g\|_2^2$, uses BB to bound the low levels and $\sum_{k=1}^m2^k/k\le4\cdot2^m/(m+1)$ for the high levels. Set $g=f-f(\cdot\oplus e_i)$; then $\|g\|_2^2=\|g\|_{3/2}^{3/2}=\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)$, and $\widehat g(S)=2\widehat f(S)$ for $i\in S$, zero otherwise. Summing over coordinates and using Parseval yields the displayed $\Omega(\operatorname{Var}f)$ statement. Logarithm base affects only the universal constant.

### Sensitivity, polynomial approximation and degree

**Source locator.** §4.4, physical PDF pp.10–11.

The total influence equals average sensitivity $s(f)=\sum_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)$. If a degree-⁠$d$ polynomial $p:\{0,1\}^n\to[0,1]$ approximates Boolean $f$ pointwise within $\epsilon<1/2$, the paper derives $ (1-2\epsilon)^2s(f)\le d$ from Parseval and $\sum_S|S|\widehat q(S)^2\le d\|q\|_2^2$ for $q=2p-1$. It also recalls the Nisan–Szegedy consequence $\deg(f)\ge\log n-O(\log\log n)$ when $f$ depends on every coordinate, with a derivative/influence argument. The latter is a citation-level consequence in this survey rather than a new full derivation of all background results.

## Interface to W10 / B162

Use this source for uniform-cube Fourier conventions, the hypercontractive estimate, and the proof mechanism behind the KKL influence inequality. It does **not** prove a short-coalition conditional-bias conclusion from $\max_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\le c\log n/n$. The individual-coordinate KKL lower bound and an upper bound on each influence are logically different inputs. Transfer to biased cubes, non-Boolean alphabets, coalition resampling probabilities, or an $O(\log n)$ coalition requires additional definitions and lemmas. In particular, do not infer B162’s conjectured conclusion from hypercontractivity or Talagrand alone.

## Proof/transfer gaps

1. The BB theorem’s full proof is deferred; a proof-dependent application should cite/read an original proof or the referenced lecture notes.
2. The parity estimate is an average over coordinate sets. Its stated range is integer $1\le k\le n$ with $k\le2\ln R$, so $\delta=k/(2\ln R)\in(0,1]$; the paper imposes no extra $k\le n/2$ restriction.
3. Talagrand’s displayed denominator is meaningful under the stated nondegeneracy treatment; zero- and unit-influence coordinates must be handled separately as above.
4. This expository paper does not supply the B162 coalition theorem, biased/nonuniform variants, or a resolution of the open-problem quantifiers.
