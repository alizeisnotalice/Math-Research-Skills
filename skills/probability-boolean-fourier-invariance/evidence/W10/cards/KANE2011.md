# KANE2011 — k-independent Gaussians fool polynomial threshold functions

**Source.** Daniel M. Kane, “k-Independent Gaussians Fool Polynomial Threshold Functions,” arXiv:1012.1614v3 (2011 version; first submitted 2010). The verified local PDF has ten physical pages, matching title and author; the arXiv record confirms the version and explicit bound. SHA-256 is recorded in `sources.json`.

## Theorem and parameters

**Theorem 1 (physical p. 1; proof physical pp. 9–10).** For integer degree (d>0) and accuracy parameter \(\epsilon>0\), there is a sufficient independence order
\[
k=O_d\!\left(\epsilon^{-2^{O(d)}}\right)
\]
such that for every degree-(d) polynomial (p), every (k)-wise independent family (X) of standard Gaussian coordinates, and a fully independent standard Gaussian vector (Y),
\[
\left|\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}p(X)]-\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}p(Y)]\right|=O(\epsilon).
\]
The display in the theorem gives the nested-exponent form. The paper's explicit sufficient estimate is derived later, on **physical PDF p. 8**: (k=O_d(\epsilon^{-4d\,7^d})). These are two presentations of the same upper bound, not two separate theorem numbers. Keep the (O(\epsilon)) expectation error. The input coordinates are Gaussian with standard Gaussian one-dimensional marginals; this is not a Boolean-cube (k)-wise-independence statement.

## Proof route

The paper first proves Proposition 2 for multilinear polynomials, then reduces general degree-(d) polynomials to that case in §VIII. For the multilinear step, §III (physical pp. 2–3) establishes degree-dependent moment bounds for Gaussian multilinear forms, including decoupling; §IV begins on physical p. 3 and supplies a structured decomposition; §V begins on physical p. 4 and applies Fourier-transform mollification to smooth the threshold; and §VI transfers the smoothed expectation using Taylor expansion and moment matching. Section VII bounds the approximation errors, using Gaussian polynomial anti-concentration. Its Theorem 13 (physical p. 8) states for normalized (p) with \(\mathbb E[p(Y)^2]=1\) that
\[
\Pr(|p(Y)|<t)=O(d\,t^{1/d}).
\]
Theorem 13 is a Carbery–Wright consequence and is used to control the threshold boundary layer; the authors do not infer sign agreement from moment matching alone. Section VIII lifts from multilinear to general polynomials, and the proof of Theorem 1 on physical pp. 9–10 preserves the (O(\epsilon)) error. The conclusion explicitly says the bound is far from tight and conjectures substantially better dependence.
