## Notation and main theorem

Let (G=\mathbb F_2^n), Fourier transform be normalized against uniform measure, and (\|f\|_A=\sum_{r\in G}|\widehat f(r)|) be the spectral (Fourier (\ell_1)) norm. **Theorem 1.1 (physical p.1 / printed p.1)** states: if (f:G\to\mathbb Z) is integer-valued and (\|f\|_A\le M), then there are subspaces (V_1,\ldots,V_L\le G) and signs (\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}) such that

\[
f=\sum_{i=1}^L\varepsilon_i1_{V_i},\qquad L\le \exp(M^{3+o(1)}).
\]

The theorem is dimension-independent and applies to integer-valued (f), not only Boolean functions. It is about Fourier (\ell_1), not support size (\ell_0). The paper explicitly says it does not characterize which such signed sums are Boolean. Theorem 1.2 is a distinct, stronger-in-small-groups Boolean result: if (f) is Boolean and (\|f\|_A\le M), then (f) is a signed sum of at most (\exp(O(M(M+\log\log|G|)))) subspace indicators.

## Proof chain

1. **Proposition 2.1 (pp.6–7):** if (f) is (\epsilon)-almost integer-valued, (\|f\|_A\le M), and (\epsilon\le e^{-CM}), a large subset of (\operatorname{supp} f_{\mathbb Z}) has small doubling: ( |A+A|\le e^{O(M\log M)}|A|), while ( |A|\ge e^{-O(M\log M)}|\operatorname{supp}f_{\mathbb Z}|). The proof uses convolution powers, Chebyshev polynomials, arithmetic connectivity, and Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers.
2. **Proposition 2.2 (pp.8–10):** if ( |A+A|\le K|A|), there is a subspace (V\le G) with ( |V|\ge e^{-\log^{3+o(1)}K}|A|) and ( |A\cap V|\ge e^{-\log^{1+o(1)}K}|V|). Its proof combines Plünnecke, Croot–Sisask almost-periodicity, a covering lemma, and Chang’s large-spectrum lemma; these additive-combinatorics inputs are cited rather than reproved in full.
3. **Proposition 2.3 / Lemma 3.1 (pp.3,5–6):** for (V\le G), (\|f\|_A\le M), (0<\epsilon\le1), (p\ge2), there is (U\le V) with (\operatorname{codim}_V U=O(p\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon))) and (\|f-f*\mu_U\|_{L_p(\mu_W)}\le\epsilon M) for every coset (W\in G/U). Lemma 3.1 gives the corresponding codimension (O(p\epsilon^{-2})). The proof first approximates (f) by an average of characters using Croot–Laba–Sisask, then iterates subspace refinements and dyadic spectral-mass drops. Retain the linear dependence on (p) in both bounds.
4. **Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 2.5 (pp.3–4):** combining the three ingredients, convolve over a subspace so the integer-rounding ( (f*\mu_V)_{\mathbb Z}) is nonzero and becomes increasingly close to integer-valued. Each subtraction (f_{i+1}=f_i-f_i*\mu_{V_{i+1}}) lowers spectral norm by at least (1/2), so iteration stops in at most (2M) stages. Each rounded convolution is an integer-valued function constant on cosets and is expanded as a signed sum of subspace cosets; summing stage costs gives (e^{M^{3+o(1)}}).

## Routing boundary

This is a Fourier (\ell_1)-structure theorem for functions on (\mathbb F_2^n). It does not bound Fourier (\ell_0) from nonzero singleton coefficients, nor does it prove an influence, noise-sensitivity, or invariance result. Keep the ambient group and integer-valued assumption explicit when reusing it.
