## Definitions and main distinctions

The paper works with Boolean (f:mathbb F_2^n\to\{-1,1\}), normalized uniform-cube Walsh coefficients, and (widehat f(\alpha)=\mathbb E[f(x)(-1)^{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}]). It separates:

- Fourier sparsity (\operatorname{spar}(f)=|\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|);
- Fourier dimension (\operatorname{dim}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{supp}\widehat f));
- (\mathbb F_2)-algebraic degree and real/Fourier degree;
- Boolean input support (\{x:f(x)\ne0\}), which is a different object again.

The exact implications are (\deg_2(f)\le\log_2\operatorname{spar}(f)\le\operatorname{dim}(f)); the first inequality can be strict already at degree one. A (k)-dimensional function is (2^k)-Fourier-sparse, but a sparse support can span many dimensions. The paper supplies property testers/learning algorithms for the first two spectral parameters; it does not prove a general lower bound on Fourier (\ell_0) from nonzero singleton coefficients.

## Results and proof mechanisms

- **Fact 2.3 / Fact 2.5 (physical pp.5–6):** Fourier projection onto a coset of a subspace is a conditional average against a character; bucket weight is estimated by a two-query correlation. These identities justify the hashing tester’s access model.
- **Theorem 3.3 (pp.8–9):** if (f) is (\mu)-close to an (s)-sparse spectrum in the sense that discarded **squared Fourier (\ell_2) mass** is at most (\mu^2), then each of the (s) retained large coefficients is within (\mu/\sqrt s) of a (\lceil\log_2s\rceil)-granular value. A random (t=\lceil\log_2s\rceil+1) coset hash isolates a selected coefficient from the other large coefficients with positive probability; tail energy in that bucket is at most (\mu^2/s). The resulting projection is an average of (2^t) Boolean values and hence granular.
- **Theorem 3.4 (pp.9–10):** if additionally (\mu\le1/(20s^2)), (f) is within Hamming distance at most (\mu^2/2) of an (s)-Fourier-sparse Boolean function. Round the retained coefficients and let (F) be the resulting sparse polynomial. From (f^2=1), Parseval, and the coefficient error bound, each Fourier coefficient of (F^2) is within less than (1/(5s^2)) of an integer. Since (F^2) has (2\lceil\log s\rceil)-granular coefficients, the noninteger gap is at least (2^{-2\lceil\log s\rceil}\ge1/(4s^2)); thus (F^2=1) and (F) is Boolean. Disagreement probability is (\mathbb E(f-F)^2/4\le\mu^2/2).
- **Theorem 4.1 (pp.10–12):** the nonadaptive Test-Sparsity tester uses (O(s^6\log s/\epsilon^2+s^{14}\log s)) queries. It randomizes coset hashing, estimates bucket weights, and rejects if at least (s+1) buckets are heavy. Completeness follows from exact support size; soundness uses pairwise-independent hashing to force many heavy buckets if the tail exceeds (\mu^2), then Theorem 3.4.
- **Theorem 5.3 (pp.12–14):** (k)-Fourier-dimensionality is testable with (O(k2^{2k}/\epsilon)) queries. The identity (\operatorname{Inv}(f)=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{supp}\widehat f)^\perp) turns invariance into a codimension test. Large coefficients are collected in a set (B); random hyperplane hashing controls the remaining squared spectral mass. Passing implies the span of (B) has dimension at most (k) and tail mass at most (\epsilon); thresholding the truncated polynomial gives an (\epsilon)-close (k)-dimensional Boolean function.
- **Theorem 6.12 (pp.15–19):** for a fixed class of (k)-variable functions that each depend on every one of their (k) inputs, implicit truth-table reconstruction gives a tester using (\operatorname{poly}(2^k,1/\epsilon)) queries. The all-input-variables-dependence condition is used to identify the character span.
- **Theorem 7.1 (pp.19–21):** for fixed (\tau>0), distributions on (k)-dimensional functions and functions conditioned to be ((1/2-\tau))-far from all degree-(k) functions require (\Omega(2^{k/2})) adaptive queries to distinguish. The proof uses a volume bound for random functions near the low-degree class and collision bounds for coset-hash query paths; corollaries give (\Omega(2^{k/2})) for dimension testing and (\Omega(\sqrt{s})) for sparsity testing.
- **Theorem 8.1 and Corollary (pp.21–23):** a Boolean function with Fourier sparsity (s>1) has coefficient granularity (\lfloor\log_2s\rfloor-1); (s=1) is 0-granular. The induction uses restriction/dimensional reduction. In particular, Boolean Fourier sparsity can only be 1, 4, or at least 8.
- **Theorems 8.2 and 8.4 (pp.23–24):** unique decoding rounds coefficients under Hamming error (<2^{-(d+1)}) for Fourier-sparse degree-(d) functions; a separate dimension decoder assumes error (<2^{-(k+1)}-\epsilon). These are exact-learning guarantees under the stated small-error thresholds, not general noise robustness.

## Scope and limits

The hashing, tester, and decoder guarantees depend on uniform-cube membership queries and the paper’s stated squared Fourier-tail or very-small Hamming-error conditions. They do not identify Fourier sparsity with Fourier dimension, algebraic degree, or the number of nonzero input points. The paper’s results are useful for naming those parameters and for algorithmic context; they do not settle A165’s (\Omega(n^2)) conjecture.
