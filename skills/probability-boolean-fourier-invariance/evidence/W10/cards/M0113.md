## Results and proof route

The learner has **membership-query access**: it may request \(f(x)\) at any chosen input, while its final prediction error is measured under the uniform cube. Their decision-tree model allows each internal node to query a GF(2)-linear test (a parity of a subset of input bits), so the conclusion is about parity decision trees, not only ordinary coordinate decision trees.

- **Sparse-spectrum learning (§§2–3; printed pp. 4–12, physical pp. 11–19).** For a prefix \(\alpha\), Lemma 3.2 gives \(f_\alpha(x)=\mathbb E_y[f(yx)\chi_\alpha(y)]\). To estimate its energy, the algorithm samples an outer suffix \(x_i\), then repeatedly queries \(f(y_{i,j}x_i)\) while holding that suffix fixed, squares the inner estimate, and averages over suffixes. Thus the prefix-energy estimator uses **correlated membership-query inputs** within each fixed-suffix batch; it is not a passive stream of independent uniformly labeled examples. A concentration bound controls which prefixes have enough spectral mass to recurse. This finds large coefficients and produces an (L_2) approximation when the target is (L_2)-approximable by a polynomially sparse Fourier polynomial. The running time is polynomial in the dimension, sparsity/accuracy parameters, and sampling precision.
- **Polynomial spectral norm (§4; printed pp. 13–16, physical pp. 7–10).** If the Fourier (L_1) norm (sum_S|\widehat f(S)|) is polynomially bounded, thresholding small coefficients gives a polynomially sparse approximation: the discarded squared mass is controlled by the threshold times the (L_1) norm. The source then gives a deterministic learning procedure for this class.
- **Decision-tree transfer (§5; printed pp. 17–19, physical pp. 4–6).** Lemma 5.1 proves that a parity decision tree with (m) nodes has Fourier (L_1) norm at most (m), by decomposing the function over its leaves. Therefore these trees fall within the preceding learnable class. The paper also gives exact identification of depth-(d) trees in time polynomial in (2^d) and (n).
- **Extensions (§6; printed pp. 20–22, physical pp. 1–3).** The discussion covers extensions/open directions and references; it does not assert a lower bound on Fourier support from nonzero first-level coefficients.

## Boundary for A165/A166

This is a learning theorem for Fourier-sparse approximants and functions with bounded Fourier (L_1) norm. It does not prove a lower bound on Fourier (L_0) support for Boolean functions whose singleton coefficients are all nonzero (A165). A165's premise is stronger than mere dependence on every coordinate, but neither the (L_1)-learning guarantee nor parity-tree learning implies its quadratic (L_0) bound.

For A166, the source is only a spectral-background pointer: its sparsity is **Fourier support**, whereas A166's (m) counts nonzero **input points** of a real-valued function. The source does not prove the small-coordinate subcube conditional-expectation statement. A useful exact interface is
\[
\mathbb E[f(X)\mid X_S=y]=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\widehat f(T)\chi_T(y)
\]
under the uniform cube and the standard \(\{-1,1\}\) character normalization; a dimension-free lower bound for this quantity under only (m)-point input support remains unproved here.
