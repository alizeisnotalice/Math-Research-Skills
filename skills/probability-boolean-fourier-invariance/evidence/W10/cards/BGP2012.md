## Model and polynomial duality

For \(A(n,k,p)\), the paper considers all laws \(Q\) on \(n\) bits with common marginal \(p\) and \(k\)-wise independent coordinates. For an event \(f\), \(\varepsilon_f(k,p)\) is the spread of \(Q(f=1)\) over those laws. \(K_1\) is the asymptotic independence threshold for off-critical sharp-threshold behavior; \(K_2\) is the threshold for matching the fully independent output probability at the critical point.

**Proposition 4 (physical p. 4, printed p. 3).** Linear-programming duality identifies the maximum and minimum of \(Q(f=1)\) with the minimum/maximum product-measure expectations of degree-at-most-\(k\) real polynomial majorants/minorants of \(f\) on the Boolean cube. Thus small spread is equivalent to a small expected gap between pointwise sandwiching polynomials. This is stronger than ordinary \(L_1\) approximation; the paper leaves open its relation to \(L_2\) approximation.

## Moment bound and normalization

**Theorem 7 (physical p. 5, printed p. 4) and equations (5)–(6).** The truncated moment problem bounds the atom/threshold probability of any real law matching the first \(k\) moments of the reference binomial variable. Equation (6) is not standardized: it uses \(\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2)\), with center \(n/2\). In the notation of (6),
\[
\rho_m(n/2)\le \frac{2}{\sqrt m},
\]
for even \(n,m\) in the stated range. In the Majority application the theorem uses the even moment index \(m=k/2\), so this estimate gives \(2\sqrt2/\sqrt{k}\). Do not rewrite the source bound as a normalized Gaussian/binomial statement or replace \(m\) by \(k\).

**Theorem 10 (physical p. 6, printed p. 5).** For even \(2\le k<n\),
\[
\frac{C}{\sqrt{k\log k}}\le
\max_{Q\in A(n,k,1/2)}
\left|Q(\mathrm{Maj}_n=1)-\frac12\right|
\le \frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{k}}.
\]
The upper bound follows from Theorem 7 and (6); the lower bound is a construction. For the separate XOR0 example, the sign of the Majority bias depends on parity: the proof of Theorem 16 explicitly assumes \((n+1)/2\) is even. For the other parity use XOR1 (or state the corresponding subsequence) so that the block bias is positive. The absolute-value conclusion of Theorem 10 itself is unaffected.

## Two-level versus iterated ternary majority

Let \(m\) be odd, \(n=m^2\), partition the bits into \(m\) blocks of size \(m\), and take the majority inside each block and then of the \(m\) outputs. **Theorem 16 (physical p. 7, printed p. 6; proof physical p. 8, printed p. 7)** states
\[
\sqrt n\le K_2(\mathrm{Maj2})\le\omega(\sqrt n).
\]
For the upper bound, the block outputs are \(k/m\)-wise independent and the one-level Majority result suffices when \(k/m\to\infty\). For the lower bound, independently place an XOR0/XOR1 extremal law in each block with parity chosen so each block output has positive bias at least \(1/(3\sqrt m)\). The independent block outputs then have a nonzero limiting normalized shift, so the outer majority remains bounded away from \(1/2\) at \(k=m-1\). This is a two-level result and does not iterate automatically.

**A019 source interface (physical p. 13, printed p. 12).** Section 5, Question 4 asks for \(K_2\) of iterated majority of threes, separately from the two-level majority-of-majorities question. It supplies the target model and open interface, not a solution: determine the growth of the smallest \(k\) that forces the iterated ternary-majority output to match its fully independent critical-point behavior as depth/input size grows. A valid proof needs a recursive construction or bound that respects the full joint laws of the three-ary subtrees; the one-block extremizer and Theorem 16 do not provide such a recursion.
