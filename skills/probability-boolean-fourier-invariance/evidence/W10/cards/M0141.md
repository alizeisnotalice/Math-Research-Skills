## Main theorem and norm distinction

For (f:\{\pm1\}^n\to\mathbb R) of Fourier-Walsh degree at most (d\), normalized under the uniform cube, define (BH_{\{\pm1\}}^{\le d}) as the least constant in the coefficient inequality. **Theorem 1.1 (physical p.3)** proves, for (d\ge2),

\[
\left(\sum_{S\subseteq[n],\,|S|\le d}|\widehat f(S)|^{2d/(d+1)}\right)^{(d+1)/(2d)}
\le C^{\sqrt{d\log d}}\,\|f\|_\infty,
\]

with absolute (C), uniformly in (n). This is a dimension-free Fourier coefficient (\ell_{2d/(d+1)}) estimate for bounded-degree functions. The (d=1) case is treated as a finite elementary base case; the displayed asymptotic (C^{\sqrt{d\log d}}) form is stated for (d\ge2). This norm is neither Fourier support cardinality (\ell_0) nor spectral norm (\ell_1).

The source also states **Theorem 4.2 (physical p.23)**, a Lorentz refinement: with (D(n,d)=\sum_{m=0}^d\binom nm) and (\widehat f^*(k)) the decreasing rearrangement of the coefficients with (|S|\le d),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{D(n,d)}\widehat f^*(k)\,k^{-(d-1)/(2d)}\le L^d\|f\|_\infty
\]
for an absolute (L\ge1). The paper does not claim the same subexponential constant for this Lorentz estimate.

## Proof architecture

- **§1, pp.1–7:** defines Fourier-Walsh degree and (d)-homogeneity; compares Boolean-cube Bohnenblust–Hille constants with those on the polytorus and the real cube. Lemma 1.3 records the polynomial norm comparisons, including the sharp (1+\sqrt2) exponential factor for real polynomials on ([-1,1]^n); this warns against transferring the complex argument directly.
- **§2, pp.7–12, homogeneous case:** symmetrize a (d)-homogeneous multilinear form and apply Blei’s mixed-norm inequality (Proposition 2.1), hypercontractivity (Bonami–Gross), and a real polarization estimate (Proposition 2.2, based on Markov/Chebyshev coefficients). This yields a recurrence reducing the homogeneous degree to a smaller (k); iteration from the finite degree-2 base gives (BH_{=d}\le C^{\sqrt{d\log d}}\).
- **§3.1–3.2, pp.13–17:** represent a general cube function as a tetrahedral (separately affine) polynomial with the same sup norm. Since real polynomial norm comparison incurs exponential loss, introduce the associated multiaffine form (L_Q) and prove Proposition 3.1: for (0\le m\le d/2), (\sup_{x,y\in[-1,1]^n}|L_Q(x^m,y^{d-m})|\le2^{dm}\|Q\|_\infty). Proposition 3.2 derives its coefficient bounds by interpolation in the Bernstein basis using Chebyshev extrema.
- **§3.3, pp.18–21:** apply Blei’s inequality to zero-augmented index maps, use the coefficient multiplicity ratio (46), hypercontractivity, and Proposition 3.1 to obtain
  \[
  BH_{\{\pm1\}}^{\le d}\le BH_{\{\pm1\}}^{\le m}\exp\!\left(2\left(\frac d{m+1}+m\log d\right)\right).
  \]
  Choosing (m=\lfloor\sqrt{d/\log d}\rfloor) gives a one-step (\exp(O(\sqrt{d\log d}))) reduction; iteration proves Theorem 1.1. Hypercontractivity is quoted, while the paper proves the specific coefficient/interpolation/polarization reductions.
- **§4, pp.21–24:** discusses the Aaronson–Ambainis influence conjecture, a known degree-(2d) substitute, quantum-information applications, Lorentz norms, and an open question on norm growth of homogeneous parts. **Conjecture 4.1** is conjectural in this source, not a theorem proved here.
- **References, pp.24–25.**

## Scope boundary

The input is a uniformly bounded function of low Fourier degree, and the output controls an intermediate coefficient norm. Do not infer an (\ell_0) support lower bound, a junta theorem, or a low-influence invariance principle from it. The paper’s Aaronson–Ambainis discussion is a degree-bounded norm consequence and does not close that conjecture.
