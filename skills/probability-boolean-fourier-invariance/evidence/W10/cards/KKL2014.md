## Definitions

On the uniform cube $\Omega([n])=\{0,1\}^n$, for Boolean $f$, let $\mathbb Ef=\Pr[f=1]$. For $S\subseteq[n]$, the positive coalition influence is
$$
I_S^+(f)=\Pr_{u\sim\Omega([n]\setminus S)}[\exists v\in\{0,1\}^S:f(u,v)=1]-\mathbb Ef,
$$
and $I_S^-$ is defined analogously for output 0; $I_S=I_S^++I_S^-$. The completion probability is $J_S^+(f)=I_S^+(f)+\mathbb Ef$, the probability that a random assignment outside $S$ can be completed on $S$ to make $f=1$. Thus $J_S^+(f)=\Pr_u[\exists v:f(u,v)=1]$. These are existential forcing probabilities, not the conditional expectation given that coordinates in S are fixed to a prescribed value.

## Theorems and proof chains

### Theorem 1.1: balanced-density random-clause counterexample

**Locator:** Theorem 1.1 physical PDF p.2; proof §3 pp.7–9; Lemma 3.1 p.7 and Lemma 3.2 pp.8–9.

For fixed $\alpha,\delta\in(0,1)$, there is a Boolean $f$ with $\mathbb Ef=\alpha$ such that every $S$ of size $(1/2-\delta)n$ satisfies $J_S^+(f)<1-n^{-C}$ for some constant $C=C(\delta)$. The construction is $f=\bigwedge_{i=1}^m C_i$, where the $C_i$ are independent random $k$-clauses (each a disjunction of $k$ literals on distinct variables). Choose $k=C\log_2n$ with $C$ large enough and $m=2^k\ln(1/\alpha)$.

Lemma 3.1: if $k=o(\sqrt n)$ and $(1/2+\delta)^k m=\omega(n)$, then with high probability, uniformly over all $S$ of size $(1/2-\delta)n$, the number $m_S$ of clauses whose variable supports miss $S$ satisfies $m_S\sim(1/2+\delta)^k m$. The proof is Chernoff concentration for each S with exponentially small tail followed by a union bound over S. It uses $\binom{n-|S|}{k}/\binom nk\sim(1/2+\delta)^k$.

Lemma 3.2: under the paper’s displayed conditions $e^{-\xi^2 n}=o((1-2^{-k})^m)$ and $ ((1+2\xi)/4)^k=o(1/m)$, the random formula has $\mathbb Ef\sim(1-2^{-k})^m$ with high probability. The proof is second moment: classify pairs by Hamming distance, separate atypically low-weight points, condition on the all-zero input, and bound the conditional satisfaction probability. With the parameter choice above, all S retain at least one clause whose support misses S, so $J_S^+\le1-2^{-k}=1-n^{-C}$. The expectation is asymptotically $\alpha$; shrinking the satisfying set to make its measure exactly $\alpha$ can only lower $J_S^+$.

### Theorem 1.2: near-full measure still need not give a strong half-size coalition

**Locator:** Theorem 1.2 physical PDF p.3; proof §3 pp.9–12, including Claims A–E.

For fixed $\epsilon,\delta>0$, there are $\alpha>0$ and Boolean $f$ such that $\mathbb Ef>(1-\epsilon)^n$, while no $S$ of size $(1/2-\delta)n$ has $I_S^+(f)>\exp(-n^\alpha)$. The authors note a strengthening to $\mathbb Ef>\exp(-n^{1-c})$ for a fixed $c=c_\delta>0$.

Proof outline/chain: again use a random k-CNF, now with $\xi=\delta/3$, fixed $\epsilon_0\in(0,\xi^2)$, $k=(1+\delta)\log_2 n$, and $m=\epsilon_0 2^k n$. Lemma 3.1 makes every S leave about $\epsilon_0(1+2\delta)^k n$ clauses. For fixed S the residual formula $f_S$ is a conjunction of those clauses in the smaller coordinate universe. Claim A bounds the chance its expectation exceeds $e^{-n^\delta}$; union bound over S yields uniform small $I_S^+$. Claim B controls how much one random clause can retain the measure of a set; its proof uses the incidence graph between cube points and codimension-k subcubes (Claim C), then Hamming-ball/binomial-tail bounds (Claims D–E). A Chernoff bound shows sufficiently many clauses contract measure, giving the claimed expectation and influence estimates. The constants and little-o hypotheses in Claims A–E are part of this proof; do not reuse only the conclusion with altered $k,m,\delta$.

### Theorem 1.3: polynomial density implies a large, near-half coalition

**Locator:** Theorem 1.3 physical PDF p.3; complete proof §4 pp.13–19; intermediate projection and Fourier arguments visually inspected on rendered pp.13–19.

For each $C>0$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that if $\mathbb Ef>n^{-C}$, there is an S of size at most $(1/2-\delta)n$ for which the projection of $A=f^{-1}(1)$ onto $S^c$ has measure $>.9$. Equivalently, $J_S^+(f)>.9$. The fixed numerical threshold can be replaced by any prescribed $\rho<1$, with constants adjusted.

Proof chain: in §4.1 (physical p.13), the source defines $I_j(f)=\|f|_{\varepsilon_j=1}-f|_{\varepsilon_j=0}\|_1$ for $0/1$-valued f and writes Eq. (16) as $\sum_j I_j(f)=\sum_S |S|\widehat f(S)^2$. With the standard normalized Walsh convention $\widehat f(S)=\mathbb E[f\chi_S]$ used in this card, the direct identity is instead $\sum_j I_j(f)=4\sum_S |S|\widehat f(S)^2$; thus Eq. (16) has a factor-4 normalization discrepancy as printed. The proof uses it to compare total influence against a fixed constant times $\log n\,\mathbb Ef$ and select a coordinate. Absorbing the factor into that absolute constant preserves the existence/scale argument; this local discrepancy is not grounds to reject the full theorem. Keep the source's printed equation and the normalized-convention identity distinct. If the total influence exceeds the threshold, some coordinate has large influence relative to mass; projecting existentially along it increases the measure, and iteration either obtains mass $>.9$ using a small B0 or reaches the low-total-influence regime. In the latter case, choose a random block $A$ of size $\delta n$ and a Fourier cutoff $ |S\cap A|\le10^{-10}\log n$; averaging ensures most Fourier mass lies under cutoff. Split into fibers over $A^c$; hypercontractivity gives a substantial set of fibers with a lower mass bound. Iteratively project coordinates in A. High-influence fibers are handled by direct mass increase. For low-influence fibers, the truncated Fourier/hypercontractive estimate supplies a coordinate with a quantitative influence increment; the number of selected coordinates stays below a small fraction of $|A|$. Finally partition fibers by the selected-coordinate pattern, use pigeonholing and Sauer–Shelah shattering to find an $A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A^c$ whose trace projection is full while discarding only a controlled number of coordinates. Together with B0 and the iteration set, the resulting coalition has size strictly below $(1/2-\delta)n$ and projection measure $>.9$.

This is a technically specific projection theorem. Its output is not a set of $O(\log n)$ variables controlled by the individual-influence upper bound in B162, and its density assumption is $\mathbb Ef>n^{-C}$, not merely the condition in that dossier.

## W10 / B162 interface and limits

The dossier’s target is a monotone Boolean sequence with $\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\le c\log n/n$ for every coordinate and a coalition $J$ of size $O_c(\log n)$ fixed all 1 or all 0 so that conditional mean approaches 1 or 0; the original epsilon quantifier still needs resolution. Theorem 1.1–1.2 provide counterexamples to optimistic half-size-coalition statements, and Theorem 1.3 gives a complementary scale-below-$n/2$ projection result. None assumes the B162 small-max-influence hypothesis and none yields its logarithmic size. The source also identifies Question 2.6/2.7 as open questions, but a 2014 statement cannot certify current open status. Keep B162 resolution status unverified until the cited Friedgut formulation and current literature are checked.

Do not conflate:

- $I_S^+$: gain above the baseline $\mathbb Ef$ in the probability of output 1 when S may choose its values;
- $J_S^+$: probability an outside assignment admits some completing assignment on S;
- conditional expectation after fixing S to a specific assignment (which is a different quantity).
