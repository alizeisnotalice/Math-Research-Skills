## Exact degree results and proof chain

- **Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 (physical p. 3; printed p. 303).** For a Boolean function (f), (deg(f)) is the degree of its unique multilinear real polynomial representation. If (f) depends on all (n) variables, then \(\deg(f)>\log_2 n-O(\log\log n)\).
- **Proof of Theorem 2.1 (physical pp. 5–7; printed pp. 305–307).** In the ({-1,1}) encoding, Parseval and the Fourier influence identity yield \(\sum_i\mathrm{Inf}_i(f)\le\deg(f)\). For each coordinate, the discrete derivative (f_i) is a nonzero multilinear polynomial because (f) depends on that coordinate. The Schwartz zero-count lemma (Lemma 2.6, physical p. 6) gives \(\Pr[f_i\ne0]>2^{-d}\), where (d=\deg(f)), hence each influence exceeds (2^{-d}). Summing gives (n2^{-d}<d), which yields the logarithmic degree bound.
- **Theorem 1.3 (physical p. 4; printed p. 304).** For every Boolean function,
  \[
  \deg(f)\le D(f)\le 16\deg(f)^8,
  \]
  where \(D(f)\) is deterministic decision-tree complexity. Both bounds are non-strict, including for constant functions.
- **Definition 1.4 and Theorem 1.5 (physical p. 4; printed p. 304).** The uniform approximate degree \(\widetilde{\deg}(f)\) is the minimum degree of a real polynomial \(p\) satisfying \(|p(x)-f(x)|\le 1/3\) for every Boolean input; the paper notes that any fixed error in \((0,1/2)\) gives the same polynomial relations. The theorem gives
  \[
  \widetilde{\deg}(f)\le\deg(f)\le D(f)\le c\,\widetilde{\deg}(f)^8
  \]
  for an absolute constant \(c\). The proof route in §3 uses symmetrization, Ehlich–Zeller/Rivlin–Cheney approximation bounds, and block sensitivity.

The authors explicitly note that real polynomial degree is invariant under changing the two real encodings of Boolean inputs, while coefficient sparsity can change. The spectral sparsity question is therefore not settled by these degree relations.

## Boundary for A165

A165 assumes **every singleton Fourier coefficient is nonzero** and asks for a quadratic lower bound on the number of nonzero Fourier coefficients. Theorem 1.2 instead assumes dependence on every coordinate and lower-bounds real multilinear degree. Neither the premise nor the conclusion is the A165 one; the paper supplies a useful distinction among dependence, degree, and support, not a proof of the (L_0) claim.
