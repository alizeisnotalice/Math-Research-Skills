## Notation and principal results

Functions on $\mathbb F_2^n$ are Boolean; Fourier coefficients are normalized under the uniform cube and logarithms are base 2. $\deg_2(f)$ is algebraic degree over $\mathbb F_2$; $D_\oplus(f)$ is parity decision-tree depth; $\|\hat f\|_0$ is Fourier sparsity, $\|\hat f\|_1$ spectral norm, and for XOR communication matrix $M_{f\circ\oplus}$ its rank equals $\|\hat f\|_0$.

The abstract (physical p.1) gives the constant-degree bound $D_{CC}(f\circ\oplus)=O(2^{d^2/2}\log^{d-2}\|\hat f\|_1)$ for Boolean f of 2-degree d (with low-degree cases understood separately in the body), and the all-degree bound $D_{CC}(f\circ\oplus)=O(d\|\hat f\|_1)=O(\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}(M)}\log\operatorname{rank}(M))$. The latter is specific to XOR functions and is unconditional.

- **Lemma 4 (physical p.5):** for Boolean f of 2-degree d, $\operatorname{rank}(f)=O(2^{d^2/2}\log^{d-2}\|\hat f\|_1)$, with the paper’s low-degree cases treated in the proof/corollaries. For constant d this implies the log-rank conclusion for XOR functions.
- **Theorem 9 (p.6):** for every Boolean $f:\{0,1\}^m\times\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, $D_{CC}(f)\le2D_\oplus(f)=O(\deg_2(f)\|\hat f\|_1)$.
- **Theorem 10 (p.6):** for every Boolean f on the cube, $D_{CC}(f\circ\oplus)=O(\deg_2(f)\|\hat f\|_1)=O(\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}(M_{f\circ\oplus})}\log\operatorname{rank}(M_{f\circ\oplus}))$.
- **Theorem 11 (physical p.6):** if $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{+1,-1\}$ is $\mu$-close in Fourier $\ell_2$ to an s-sparse spectrum, with $\mu\le \log^{O(1)}\|\hat f\|_0/\sqrt{\|\hat f\|_0}$ and $s\le\log^{O(1)}\|\hat f\|_0$, then $D_\oplus(f)\le\log^{O(1)}\|\hat f\|_0$. “$\mu$-close” here means the discarded squared Fourier mass is at most $\mu^2$; it is not Hamming distance.

## Proof chains

### Main parity-decision-tree reduction

**Theorem 22 (physical p.12)** gives the Main PDT algorithm: if $\operatorname{rank}(f)\le M(f)$ for a rank bound M that does not increase under restrictions, then $D_\oplus(f)\le\deg_2(f)M(f)$; the XOR communication protocol costs at most $2\log\|\hat f\|_0\,M(f)$. The algorithm queries parity linear forms in a polynomial-rank decomposition $f=\ell_1f_1+\cdots+\ell_rf_r+f_0$, reducing 2-degree each round. This is the interface used to turn rank/spectral estimates into communication bounds.

### Low 2-degree (Lemma 26)

**Lemma 26 (physical pp.13–16)** states, for nonconstant Boolean f of 2-degree d,
$$
\operatorname{rank}(f)\le C_{\oplus,\min}(f)\le D_\oplus(f)
\le O\!\left(2^{d^2/2}\bigl(\log^{d-2}\|\hat f\|_1+1\bigr)\right).
$$
The proof is induction in d. Degrees 1–3 are handled directly: d=2 by the cited Dickson structure theorem; d=3 by the paper’s proposition based on Haramaty–Shpilka. For d≥4 choose a direction t with nonconstant derivative $\Delta_t f$. Restrict to an affine subspace on which the derivative is constant (using the induction hypothesis); split f into the half-sum/half-difference pair over the two cosets. Their Fourier supports pair disjointly, and each restricted function has spectral norm bounded by the original norm through the restriction lemma. Repeatedly applying such restrictions reduces spectral norm by a constant factor until the induction bounds apply; the recurrence in the paper is $B_d(m)=B_{d-1}(m^2)\log m+B_{d-1}(m)+1$, giving the stated quasi-polynomial factor. The base-case and affine-restriction hypotheses are essential; this is not a generic degree reduction for real polynomials.

### Small spectral norm and light tails

**Lemma 30 (physical pp.17–18)** gives $C_{\oplus,\min}(f)=O(\|\hat f\|_1)$ for Boolean f, via repeated affine restrictions to make f constant. Greedily fold a pair of Fourier coefficients of the same sign. When the largest coefficient is at most 1/2, Parseval forces a definite increase in that coefficient; after it exceeds 1/2, the Boolean convolution identity supplies same-sign pairs whose folding decreases spectral norm by more than 1. Thus only O($\|\hat f\|_1$) restrictions are needed. Corollary 8 and Theorems 9–10 combine this with the PDT simulation and rank identities.

Section 6 (physical pp.18–20) treats light Fourier tails. Lemma 31 is Chang’s dimension bound for large coefficients; Lemma 32 gives coefficient granularity for Boolean functions; Lemmas 33–34 control low-density functions and rounding, with cited results from Gopalan et al. Theorem 11 combines them with the product/convolution identity. Appendix B (pp.24–25) proves the needed low-density statement using the Bonami–Beckner hypercontractive inequality (Theorem 36); that hypercontractive theorem itself is quoted from Bonami/Beckner, not proved here.

## Limits and conjectures

- The log-rank conclusion is for XOR matrices $M_{f\circ\oplus}$; it does not establish the general log-rank conjecture for arbitrary communication matrices.
- The paper’s conjectures in §§1 and 7 remain conjectures within this source, not lemmas. No claim here about their current resolution is made.
- Theorem 11’s premise is small Fourier tail in squared $\ell_2$ mass and its parameter inequalities; a small Hamming error or merely sparse approximation without the stated bounds does not suffice.
- Several ingredients are cited (Dickson, Chang, hypercontractivity, cubic structure/rounding). The source proves the reductions and applications, not every imported theorem.
