## Setting, parameters, main theorem

Use uniform Fourier coefficients for $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$. Symmetric means $f(x)$ depends only on Hamming weight $|x|=\sum_i x_i$. Spectral norm is $\|\widehat f\|_1=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}|\widehat f(S)|$, not Fourier sparsity. Let $r_0,r_1$ be the smallest integers less than $n/2$ such that one of $f$ or $f\cdot\mathrm{PARITY}$ is constant on all inputs whose weights lie in $[r_0,n-r_1]$; set $r(f)=\max(r_0,r_1)$.

**Theorem 1.1 (Main Theorem, physical p.3; printed p.3):** for every symmetric Boolean $f$, if $r(f)>1$, then
$$
\log\|\widehat f\|_1=\Theta\!\left(r(f)\log\frac{n}{r(f)}\right).
$$
If $r(f)\le1$, then $\|\widehat f\|_1=\Theta(1)$. The asymptotic constants are universal. **Corollary 1.2 (p.4)** gives parity decision-tree size $2^{\Theta(r(f)\log(n/r(f)))}$. **Corollary 1.3 (p.4)**, using Shi–Zhang’s protocol, gives public-coin constant-error randomized communication complexity $O(\log^2\|\widehat F\|_1)$ for the symmetric XOR function $F(x,y)=f(x\oplus y)$.

## Proof structure and dependencies

1. **Upper bound, Lemma 3.1 (physical p.6):** by definition, $f$ or its parity product is constant on the middle Hamming-weight interval, so the nonconstant Fourier contribution can be bounded by the binomial tails. Symmetry makes each Fourier level uniform; entropy/binomial estimates give $\log\|\widehat f\|_1\le2r\log(n/r)+3$.
2. For the lower bound away from $r\approx n/2$, define $f_{ij}(x)=f(x+e_i+e_j)-f(x)$ and $R(f)=\sum_S|S|(n-|S|)\widehat f(S)^2$. The exact identity on the cube is $\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb E f_{ij}^2=8R(f)$. **Lemma 3.2 (physical p.6)** lower-bounds the relevant derivative energy from $r_0,r_1$; **Lemma 3.3 (pp.7–10)** combines this with a bound on nonconstant Fourier mass to force sufficient $L^2$ mass on middle levels. Since a symmetric function has equal-magnitude coefficients within each level, many such coefficients give the required spectral-$L^1$ lower bound. The proof treats small endpoint values separately.
3. For $r\approx n/2$, **Lemma 3.4 (physical p.11)** imports the noise-sensitivity estimate of O’Donnell–Wright–Zhou for symmetric functions and proves the needed analogue for the two-coordinate derivatives $f_{ij}$. For $\rho=1-c/n$, $c\in[1,n]$, its displayed estimate is
$$
\sum_S\widehat f_{ij}(S)^2\rho^{|S|}\le \frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi c}}\sum_S\widehat f_{ij}(S)^2.
$$
The proof invokes their interleaving/unimodality lemma; this is imported, while the derivative reduction is shown here. **Lemma 3.5 (physical pp.11–13)** chooses $c=10^4$ and $\beta=10^{-4}\ln2$, combines the estimate for $f$ and $f\cdot\mathrm{PARITY}$, and obtains $\log\|\widehat f\|_1=\Omega(n)$ when $r(f)\ge\gamma n$ for an absolute $\gamma<1/2$. This closes the near-middle regime.
4. For Corollary 1.2, physical pp.13–14: the $L^1$ norm lower-bounds parity decision-tree leaves because a leaf indicator has Fourier $L^1$ norm 1 and $f$ is a signed sum of leaf indicators. The upper bound prunes the full decision tree using middle-interval constancy; Corollary 1.3 applies a separately cited communication protocol.

Section 5, physical pp.14–15, states approximate-spectral-norm and related applications as conjectures. They are not proved by Theorem 1.1. References occupy physical pp.16–17.

## A165 applicability boundary

The main theorem is restricted to **symmetric** functions and controls Fourier $L^1$ norm using a Hamming-tail parameter $r(f)$. A165 concerns general Boolean functions, assumes only all singleton Fourier coefficients are nonzero, and asks for a lower bound on Fourier $L^0$ support. Neither symmetry nor a relation converting this $L^1$ characterization into the conjectured $L^0\ge c n^2$ conclusion is provided. This paper is auxiliary special-class context, not a proof of A165.
