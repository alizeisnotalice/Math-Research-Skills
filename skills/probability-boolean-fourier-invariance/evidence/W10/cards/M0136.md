## Parameter and main result

For a symmetric Boolean $f:\{0,1\}^k\to\{0,1\}$, call f t-null if all nonempty Fourier coefficients of levels 1 through t vanish. Constants and parity (including its complement) are excluded when defining $\tau(k)$: it is the least t such that every other symmetric Boolean function has a nonzero Fourier coefficient at some level 1 through t.

**Theorem 2.2 (physical p.3).** There is an absolute $k_0$ such that, for $k\ge k_0$,
$$
\tau(k)\le \frac{4k}{\log k}.
$$
This is 4k divided by log k. The printed/scanned fraction is visually confirmed; OCR that renders it as $4k\log k$ is wrong. The logarithm base only changes the absolute constant.

**Theorem 2.1 (p.3).** For fixed integers $1\le s\le\ell$ with $\tau(\ell)\le s$, there exists $k_0(s,\ell)$ so that for every $k\ge k_0$, $\tau(k)\le\frac{s+1}{\ell+1}k+o(k)$. This self-similarity estimate is conditional on the stated small-block hypothesis. The authors note that a computer search observed $\tau(30)=2$, which yields the corresponding $3k/31$ consequence; this computational observation is not needed for Theorem 2.2.

## Proof chain for Theorem 2.2

Section 4 (physical pp.4–6) converts t-nullness into a Diophantine system. Symmetry makes the Fourier constraints depend only on Hamming-weight values $f_0,\ldots,f_k\in\{0,1\}$. Vanishing low-order Fourier coefficients is equivalent to matching the uniform moments through the specified degree, giving binomial-coefficient linear relations among the $f_i$.

Section 6 (pp.9–16) assumes for contradiction that the first $\epsilon k$ Fourier levels vanish, with $\epsilon=4/\log k$, and sets $N=k-\epsilon k$. Subtracting adjacent binomial equations yields a recurrence for $D_j=f_{j+1}-f_j$:
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{N}\binom Nj D_{\nu+j}=0
$$
through the valid index range. Its characteristic root $-1$ has multiplicity N, so $D_j=(-1)^jP(j)$ for a real/integer-valued polynomial P of degree at most $N-1$; Booleanity gives $P(j)\in\{-1,0,1\}$ on the relevant integer points.

The rest is a structured arithmetic argument, not a generic recurrence bound:

1. **Lemma 6.2 (pp.10–11)** uses primes and the Siegel–Walfisz prime-distribution theorem (stated as Theorem A) to prove 2-periodicity of P on a long terminal interval. Congruence modulo a prime becomes equality because the values lie in $\{-1,0,1\}$.
2. **Lemma 6.3 (p.11)** uses the alternating-difference constraint and Boolean values to show P is constant on that interval. **Lemma 6.4 (pp.11–12)** uses Lucas’ theorem modulo a suitable prime to propagate constancy from the interval into a central region.
3. **Theorem 6.5 (pp.12–13)** states that vanishing Fourier levels 1 through r for a nonnegative weight f on the cube forces all coordinate-product moments through degree r under the f-weighted measure to agree with the uniform cube. Corollary 6.6 applies this to the sum $S=\sum_iX_i$.
4. **Lemma 6.7 (pp.13–14)** rules out P=0 on the central interval: under the f-weighted law, support then forces a variance of order $\epsilon^2k^2$, while the matched moments imply the uniform variance k; for $\epsilon=4/\log k$ this is a contradiction for large k.
5. Therefore P is nonzero (±1) in the central interval. **Lemma 6.8 (pp.14–16)** repeatedly uses prime/Lucas interval transfers, for dyadic m up to $\frac12\log k$, to cover all indices and show P is constant everywhere. Then the sequence f is constant or parity, contradicting the choice of f. This proves Theorem 2.2.

The self-similarity Theorem 2.1 is proved separately in §5 (pp.6–9), first by a prime-modulus graph argument (Theorem 5.3, Lemma 5.4), then by block recursion and Lucas congruences. It should not be folded into the proof of the sharper logarithmic bound.

## Algorithmic consequence and limits

**Theorem 7.1 (physical p.17).** Symmetric k-juntas can be learned exactly under the uniform distribution, with confidence $1-\delta$, in time $n^{O(k/\log k)}\operatorname{poly}(2^k,n,\log(1/\delta))$. Section 7 uses the support bound to enumerate/learn symmetric functions. The theorem is about symmetric juntas and uniform PAC learning, not arbitrary Boolean junta learning.

The number-theoretic prime estimate (Siegel–Walfisz) and cited integer-valued-polynomial facts are imported. The paper proves how they combine with the Fourier/moment recurrence, but does not reprove those analytic number-theory inputs. The conclusion does not imply that every symmetric function has a nonzero coefficient at level 1, or eliminate constants/parity; those are explicitly the excluded cases.
