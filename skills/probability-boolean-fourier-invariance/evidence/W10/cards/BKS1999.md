# BKS1999 — noise sensitivity and percolation

**Source.** Itai Benjamini, Gil Kalai, and Oded Schramm, “Noise Sensitivity of Boolean Functions and Applications to Percolation,” *Publications Mathématiques de l’IHÉS* 90 (1999), 5–43 (published 2001), DOI 10.1007/BF02698830. ## Model and principal results

Work on the uniform cube Ωⁿ={−1,1}ⁿ. The coordinate influence \(I_i(g)\) is the probability that **forcing a flip** of coordinate \(i\) changes \(g\), equivalently \(\Pr[g(x)\ne g(x^{\oplus i})]\); it is not the probability under an independent resampling operation. If a uniform bit is independently resampled with probability \(\epsilon\), its effective flip probability is \(\epsilon/2\), since the replacement agrees with the old bit half the time. Set \(II(A)=\sum_i I_i(1_A)^2\). The paper’s noise parameter $\epsilon$ flips each coordinate independently with probability $\epsilon$ (bit-flip coupling), and defines its sensitivity gauge from that coupling; the asymptotic statements concern sequences of events and are not finite-\(n\) bounds without a rate.

- **Theorems 1.3–1.5 (§1, §2).** Vanishing squared-influence sum implies asymptotic noise sensitivity; monotone events with squared-influence sum bounded away from zero do not have that property. In Theorem 1.5, if \(II(A)\le n^{-a}\) for \(a\in(0,1/2]\), then for \(0<\epsilon<1/4\) the noise-sensitivity gauge satisfies \(\phi(A,\epsilon)\le c_1n^{-c_2\epsilon}\), with constants depending on \(a\).
- **Theorem 1.6 (§3, physical p. 7).** For monotone \(f:\Omega_n\to[0,1]\),
  \[ 
  II(f)\le C\,\Lambda(f)^2\bigl(1-\log\Lambda(f)\bigr)\log n,
  \qquad \Lambda(f)=\max_{K\subseteq[n]}\left|\mathbb E[fM_K]\right|,
  \]
  where \(M_K\) is the paper’s unweighted-majority family and \(C\) is an absolute constant. Theorem 1.7 gives the equivalent asymptotic criterion using maximum weighted-majority correlation.
- **Theorem 1.8 (§3).** Generalized weighted-majority threshold events have a uniform-stability bound; the paper later notes the sharper exponent obtained in subsequent work, so do not cite the original displayed exponent as optimal.
- **Theorem 1.9 (§1, physical p. 9).** For a sequence (A_m\subseteq\Omega^{n_m}), noise sensitivity is equivalent to vanishing positive low-degree Fourier mass: for every fixed (k\ge1),
  \[
  \sum_{S:\,1\le |S|\le k}\widehat{1_{A_m}}(S)^2\longrightarrow0.
  \]
  The constant coefficient (S=\varnothing) is excluded. Uniform stability instead corresponds to uniform tightness of the high-degree tail as (k\to\infty).
- **Theorem 2.6 (§2).** For monotone events, each fixed positive Fourier level admits a quantitative bound in terms of (II(A)) and a logarithmic factor.
- **Percolation application (§4).** The crossing-event argument combines exploration coupling, radial connection estimates/RSW, and the majority-correlation bound to show (II(C_m)\to0); Theorem 1.3 then gives asymptotic noise sensitivity.
- **Fixed-size noise (§9, Theorem 9.1).** Transfer to a fixed-size slice requires control of both low and high Fourier levels; parity shows why low-level control alone is insufficient.

## Scope checks

Noise sensitivity, uniform stability, squared total influence, maximum coordinate influence, and low-level Fourier weight are related but distinct notions. Theorem 1.9 is a qualitative sequence criterion, not a statement that any individual nonconstant function has a prescribed support size. In particular it does not prove A165’s quadratic Fourier-ℓ0 lower bound. Keep the positive-level condition (1\le|S|\le k); including (S=\varnothing) would incorrectly impose a condition on event density.
