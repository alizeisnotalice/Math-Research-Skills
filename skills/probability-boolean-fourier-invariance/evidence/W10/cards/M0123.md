## Definitions and scope

The paper uses Boolean functions $f:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$, uniform-cube Fourier coefficients, total influence $I[f]=\sum_S |S|\hat f(S)^2$, and spectral entropy $H[\hat f^2]=\sum_S \hat f(S)^2\log_2(1/\hat f(S)^2)$. The Fourier Entropy–Influence (FEI) conjecture asks for a universal $C$ with $H[\hat f^2]\le C I[f]$ for every Boolean f. This paper proves it only for the classes below.

## Theorems and proof chains

### Symmetric and part-symmetric cases

**Theorem 1 (physical p.3).** For symmetric Boolean f, $H[\hat f^2]\le12.04 I[f]$.

**Theorem 2 (physical p.4).** For a function invariant under a product action $S_{n_1}\times\cdots\times S_{n_d}$ (the paper’s $d$-part-symmetric class), $H[\hat f^2]\le(12.04+\log_2 d)I[f]$.

For symmetric f, write $W_k=\sum_{|S|=k}\hat f(S)^2$. Splitting entropy by Fourier level gives a within-level contribution bounded by $\sum_k W_k\log_2\binom nk$ and a level-distribution entropy. The proof controls the former by noise stability of the discrete derivative. **Theorem 6 (physical p.7)** states that if $g$ is a discrete derivative of a symmetric $f:\{-1,1\}^{n+1}\to\{-1,1\}$, then for real $1\le c\le n$,
$$
\operatorname{Stab}_{1-c/n}[g]\le \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi c}}\,\mathbb E[g^2].
$$
Its proof couples correlated strings and conditions on Hamming weight: the weight transition is a sum of independent binomials and is unimodal; the derivative’s positive and negative sets interlace, so the relevant probability is bounded by the largest point mass. A binomial/Stirling estimate gives the displayed $c^{-1/2}$ bound. Summing this over levels gives **Theorem 4 (p.6–8)**, the within-level entropy estimate. **Theorem 5 (p.8–9)** bounds spectral-level entropy by $3I[f]$, using $H(K)\le\mathbb E K$ and the edge-isoperimetric inequality; together these yield Theorem 1 and the numerical constant 12.04.

For the product-symmetry extension, the Fourier level is a vector of block degrees. The proof repeats the derivative/noise-stability argument on each block and controls the entropy of the degree vector using **Proposition 3 (p.9)**, $H[K]\le(1+\log_2 d)\mathbb E|K|$. This yields the additive $\log_2 d$ in Theorem 2. Do not read “constant d” as an unrestricted symmetry statement with a dimension-independent constant.

### Read-once decision trees

**Theorem 3 (physical p.4; proof §4, pp.10–12).** If f is computable by a read-once decision tree, then $H[\hat f^2]\le4.88 I[f]$. The source defines read-once recursively: at every node, the two branch functions depend on disjoint coordinate sets, and each branch is itself read-once. Thus a variable cannot occur in two different branches anywhere in the tree; merely forbidding a repeated query along each root-to-leaf path is weaker and is not the theorem's hypothesis.

The proof inducts on tree depth using the source's global branch-disjointness condition (formal definition on physical p.6). At the root, the two subfunctions use disjoint variable sets, so they are independent under the product cube and $\mathbb E[f_0f_1]=\mathbb E[f_0]\mathbb E[f_1]$. Parseval/influence identities express the total influence through the child influences and root query, while the spectral entropy decomposes into a child-entropy term plus a binary mixing term determined by the child means. **Lemma 3 (physical p.10)** reduces the induction to a two-parameter analytic inequality; its proof takes the supremum of an explicit one-variable function. The authors state that numerical checking verifies the supremum is at most 4.88 (p.10–11), so the constant is supported there by numerical analysis rather than a symbolic certificate reproduced in the paper. Preserve this proof limitation.

## Limits and transfer cautions

- This is not a proof of FEI for arbitrary Boolean functions; all three conclusions are class-restricted.
- The proposed recursively read-once extension in the closing remarks (physical p.12) is only a remark: the authors say details will appear elsewhere. Do not promote it to a theorem.
- The exact symmetry group and recursive global branch-disjointness in the read-once condition are structural hypotheses, not consequences of low degree or small influence. A tree that only avoids repeated variables on individual paths may still repeat a variable across separate branches and is not covered.
- The 4.88 proof depends on the paper’s numerical supremum check. If a downstream use requires a fully symbolic constant proof, that remains an external verification task.
