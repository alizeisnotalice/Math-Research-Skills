# M0110 — Simons open-problem collection (selected Boolean-function items)

**Source.** Yuval Filmus, Hamed Hatami, Steven Heilman, Elchanan Mossel, Ryan O’Donnell, Sushant Sachdeva, Andrew Wan, Karl Wimmer, *Real Analysis in Computer Science: A collection of Open Problems* (2014), 35-page PDF. Local file: `evidence/fulltexts/M0110.pdf`; SHA-256 is in `sources.json`. Front page matches the title and author list. This is an open-problem compilation, so it identifies questions and cited prior work; it is not itself a proof source for the questions.

## Read locations and exact statements

- **PDF p. 6, printed p. 6 — “Largest influence and the degree.”** For balanced (f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}) with Fourier coefficients zero for every (|S|>d), ask for the smallest possible \(\max_i \operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\). The source records a lower bound \(\Omega(d^{-4})\), obtained by combining degree-to-decision-tree depth (O(d^4)) with OSSS. This is degree-versus-maximum-influence, not a bound on Fourier support.
- **PDF p. 6, printed p. 6 — “Small influences and influential tribes.”** The Simons compilation displays a fixed \(\epsilon\) in the hypothesis, but the conclusion is \(1-o(1)\)/\(o(1)\) and does not quantify that error. Record this as a statement mismatch in this secondary source; do not silently replace it with the stronger original statement.
- **PDF p. 10, printed p. 10 — “Almost-resiliency of monotone functions.”** Among monotone functions that depend on all (n) coordinates, maximize (d(n)) such that all Fourier coefficients with (2\le |S|\le d(n)) vanish. Monotonicity and dependence imply every first-level coefficient is nonzero. This differs from the next problem because dependence on every coordinate is weaker than nonzero *every* singleton coefficient for an arbitrary Boolean function.
- **PDF p. 10, printed p. 10 — “L0 sparsity of spectrum.”** If each singleton coefficient \(\hat f(\{i\})\) is nonzero for every \(1\le i\le n\), prove that the number of nonzero Fourier coefficients is \(\Omega(n^2)\). The adjacent address function has \(n=2^r+r\) inputs: \(2^r\) data variables \(x_j\) and \(r\) selector variables \(y_i\). The source asserts nonzero singleton coefficients only for the \(2^r\) data variables. Under the uniform cube, for each fixed selector string \(y\), averaging over the independent data block gives \(\mathbb E_x f(x,y)=1/2\); hence each selector singleton coefficient is zero. Thus this example does not meet the all-\(n\)-singletons premise and is not an upper-bound construction for that exact class. Exact enumeration for \(r=1,2,3\) is a finite check only; the general selector-zero statement follows from the conditional average, not extrapolation.
- **PDF p. 13, printed p. 13 — “k-wise Independence for PTFs.”** For fixed (d\in\mathbb N,\epsilon\in(0,1)), determine least (k(d,\epsilon)) such that every degree-(d) polynomial (p:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R) and every (X\in\mathbb R^n) with standard Gaussian marginals and (k)-wise independent coordinates satisfy
  \[
  |\Pr[p(X)\ge0]-\Pr[p(Z)\ge0]|\le\epsilon,
  \]
  where (Z\sim N(0,I_n)). This is the Gaussian-marginal finite-independence problem, not Boolean-sign k-wise independence. The source’s 2014 remarks list historical upper/lower bounds; they are not asserted here as the best 2026 bounds.

## Transfer boundaries

The source itself keeps separate (i) exact dependence on every coordinate, (ii) nonzero first-level Fourier coefficients, (iii) degree, (iv) maximum individual influence, and (v) Fourier (L_0) support. Do not substitute one for another. For B162, the original source is Ehud Friedgut, “Influences in Product Spaces: KKL and BKKKL Revisited” (2004), Conjecture 2.6. It states: for every c>0 and fixed ε>0 there is b=b(c,ε)>0 such that if Boolean f on the uniform cube has Inf_i(f)≤c log(n)/n for every coordinate, then some J with |J|≤b log n has either E[f | X_J=0^J]≤ε or E[f | X_J=1^J]≥1−ε. In §1 Friedgut says the paper restricts to monotone functions but that KKL shifting causes no loss for its purposes; Conjecture 2.6's displayed line itself does not restate monotonicity. Keep that standing context visible. A dossier variant allowing arbitrary coalition assignments should be recorded separately unless its reduction is shown. This remains a conjecture in the 2004 source, not a theorem.
Do not conflate this discrete Boolean-cube conjecture with Friedgut’s Conjecture 2.2 on the continuous cube [0,1]^n: that asks for a coalition of size o(n) forcing one output with probability 1−o(1). Chattopadhyay–Gurumukhani (2026, arXiv:2609.16401v2) prove O(n/√log n) for the finite-alphabet and continuous-cube versions; Friedgut’s 2026 write-up gives O(n/(ε log n)) for the monotone continuous-cube version. These results do not prove the discrete fixed-ε O(log n) conclusion of Conjecture 2.6.
For B170, the Gaussian marginals and degree of the *threshold polynomial* are part of the problem definition; results for Boolean signs or for polynomial approximation do not settle its optimal k(d,ε).
