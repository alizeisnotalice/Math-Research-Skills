## Definitions and exact inequalities

The paper uses uniform product measure on $\{-1,1\}^n$ and real-valued pseudo-Boolean functions $f(x)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal F}\widehat f(I)\chi_I(x)$, with all coefficients in $\mathcal F$ nonzero and $m=|\mathcal F|$ (including the empty support if its coefficient is nonzero). For nonzero $f$, $m\ge1$; the zero function is trivial and the displayed $2\rho/m$ expression is not used. The degree is $\max_{I\in\mathcal F}|I|$. Fourier width is
$$
\rho=\max_{i\in[n]}|\{I\in\mathcal F:i\in I\}|,
$$
the maximum number of nonzero Fourier monomials containing one coordinate. Norms are $\|f\|_p=(\mathbb E|f(x)|^p)^{1/p}$.

- **Theorem 1 (physical p.3; proof continues p.4):** for width $\rho\ge0$,
$$
\|f\|_4\le\left(2\rho+1-\frac{2\rho}{m}\right)^{1/4}\|f\|_2.
$$
For $\rho=0$, $f$ is constant. The paper gives sharp examples: $1+\sum_i x_i$ and the sum of all parity characters. The theorem improves the coarser factor $(2\rho+1)^{1/4}$ by the $2\rho/m$ term.
- **Theorem 2 (physical pp.4–6):** for $\rho\ge1$ and every positive integer $r$,
$$
\|f\|_{2r}\le\big((2r)!\,\rho^{r-1}\big)^{1/(2r)}\|f\|_2.
$$
- **Corollary 1 (physical p.7):** for $q>p\ge2$, with $r=\lceil q/2\rceil$,
$$
\|f\|_q\le\big((2r)!\,\rho^{r-1}\big)^{1/(2r)}\|f\|_p.
$$
The corollary follows by monotonicity of $L^p$ norms from Theorem 2 and $2r\ge q$; do not state the even-moment theorem itself for arbitrary real $q$.

## Proof mechanism

For Theorem 1, expand $\mathbb E[f^4]$ into quadruples of Fourier terms. A product has nonzero expectation only if every variable occurs an even number of times. Pair-balanced quadruples contribute $\sum_{p,s}w_p^2w_s^2$, where $w_j=\widehat f(I_j)$. For the remaining quadruples, fixing an ordered pair $(p,q)$ leaves at most $2\rho$ choices for $(s,t)$: a coordinate in the symmetric difference of $I_p,I_q$ must occur in exactly one of $I_s,I_t$, with at most $\rho$ choices for that support and two role choices. Symmetrizing with $(s,t,p,q)$, then using $w_pw_qw_sw_t\le(w_p^2w_q^2+w_s^2w_t^2)/2$, gives
$$
\mathbb E[f^4]\le(2\rho+1)\left(\sum_jw_j^2\right)^2-2\rho\sum_jw_j^4.
$$
Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sum_jw_j^4/(\sum_jw_j^2)^2\ge1/m$; Parseval yields the sharpened theorem.

For Theorem 2, expand the $2r$-th moment and discard terms where a variable occurs an odd number of times. For each surviving product, greedily choose minimally-even multisets containing a currently largest-weight factor. Width bounds the number of choices in each of the first $r-1$ stages; the proof bounds the number of compatible decompositions by $r!\rho^{r-1}$. The multinomial coefficient is at most $(2r)!/r!$. Grouping terms and applying Parseval gives $\mathbb E[f^{2r}]\le(2r)!\rho^{r-1}(\mathbb E[f^2])^r$, hence Theorem 2. The paper notes it does not optimize this constant; the stronger conjectured $\sqrt{r\rho}$ growth is only Conjecture 1 on physical p.6, not a result.

## A165 transfer boundary

A165’s nonzero first-level coefficients imply each coordinate appears in at least one support, but do not supply a useful upper bound on Fourier width $\rho$; $\rho$ can be large. Even when bounded width is known, these are upper bounds on $L^q$ in terms of $L^2$, not lower bounds on the number of nonzero coefficients. They do not establish A165’s Fourier-$L^0$ lower bound.
