## Definitions and theorem

For Boolean $f:\mathbb F_2^n\to\{+1,-1\}$, use the uniform-cube Fourier expansion $f(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb F_2^n}\widehat f(\gamma)\chi_\gamma(x)$. The Fourier support is $\operatorname{supp}(f)=\{\gamma:\widehat f(\gamma)\ne0\}$, Fourier sparsity is $s=|\operatorname{supp}(f)|$, and Fourier dimension is $\dim(f)=\dim\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{supp}(f))$. The elementary bounds are $\log_2s\le\dim(f)\le s$.

**Theorem 1.2 (physical p.2).** Every Boolean $f$ with Fourier sparsity $s$ satisfies
$$
\dim(f)=O(\sqrt{s}\log s).
$$
The abstract gives the address function as a near-tight example: sparsity $s$ and dimension at least $\sqrt{s}$, so the remaining gap is a logarithmic factor.

## Proof chain and exact dependencies

1. **Proposition 2.7 (physical p.5):** the non-adaptive parity-decision-tree complexity $\mathrm{NADT}_\oplus(f)$ equals $\dim(f)$. Querying a basis of the span of the Fourier support determines every supported character and hence $f$. Conversely, after an invertible linear change of coordinates, any $t$ parities determining $f$ make the transformed function a junta on $t$ coordinates, so its Fourier-support span has dimension at most $t$.
2. **Theorem 2.5 (physical p.5), imported from Tsang–Wong–Xie–Zhang, Lemma 28:** if $\|\widehat f\|_1=A$, there is an affine subspace of codimension $O(A)$ on which $f$ is constant. **Claim 2.3 (p.4)** and Parseval give $A\le\sqrt{s}$; hence **Lemma 1.3 (p.2)** gives codimension $O(\sqrt{s})$. This is a cited input, not reproved here.
3. The paper organizes restrictions of $f$ into parity-query leaves and makes the parity choices common to all leaves, producing a non-adaptive query set. At iteration $i$, let $l(i)$ be the number of occupied cosets of the queried span in the original support, and let $\Delta s(i)$ be the sparsity of the selected leaf. **Proposition 3.2 (p.6)** identifies the union size of the restricted supports with $l(i)$.
4. **Lemma 3.3 (p.7):** some leaf has sparsity at least $l(i)^2/s$. For each occupied coset the corresponding nonzero polynomial is nonzero on a random affine restriction with probability at least the reciprocal of its sparsity, by the uncertainty principle **Theorem 2.4 (p.4)**; averaging and $\sum_j k_j=s$ yield the claim.
5. **Lemma 3.4 (p.7):** with threshold $\tau\ge\sqrt{2s}$, choosing a largest-sparsity leaf and querying the parities on which it becomes constant reduces the union-support size by $\Delta l(i)\ge\Delta s(i)/4$. The constant restriction forces every nonconstant character in that leaf to be paired with another support element in each assignment; Lemma 3.3 ensures $\Delta s(i)\ge l(i-1)^2/s\ge2$, which yields the factor $1/4$.
6. By Lemma 1.3, the $i$-th batch uses $O(\sqrt{\Delta s(i)})$ parity queries. The preceding two inequalities telescope to $O(\sqrt{s}\sum_i\Delta l(i)/l(i-1))=O(\sqrt{s}\log s)$; the terminal support has size $O(\sqrt{s})$ and can be queried directly. Proposition 2.7 converts this non-adaptive query count into the theorem’s Fourier-dimension bound.

## Scope and A165 interface

This is an **upper bound on Fourier dimension in terms of Fourier $L^0$ support size**. It does not give a lower bound on Fourier sparsity from nonzero singleton coefficients. A165 asks for $L^0\ge c n^2$ for general Boolean functions with every first-level coefficient nonzero; no implication from this theorem supplies that conclusion. Preserve $\dim(\operatorname{span}\operatorname{supp}(f))$ as distinct from $s=|\operatorname{supp}(f)|$. The codimension lemma cited from M0124/TWXZ is a separate spectral-$L^1$-norm result with its own hypotheses.
