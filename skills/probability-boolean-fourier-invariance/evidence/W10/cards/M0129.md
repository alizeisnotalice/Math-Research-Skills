## Notation and Boolean-cube results

The headline Boolean-cube theorems on physical pp.3–4 are stated for $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ with $A=\|\widehat f\|_1$ in that displayed encoding and $s=|\{\alpha:\widehat f(\alpha)\ne0\}|$ when Fourier sparsity is assumed. Section 2 (physical p.6) says the proofs work with the sign encoding $F=1-2f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{\pm1\}$. For nonempty $S$, $\widehat F(S)=-2\widehat f(S)$, while $\widehat F(\emptyset)=1-2\widehat f(\emptyset)$; hence nonconstant Fourier mass scales by exactly 2 and Fourier sparsity changes only at the constant coefficient (by at most 1). The source describes the full spectral norms as roughly comparable up to a factor of 2, but the constant coefficient is not simply doubled. Do not carry the same numerical $A$ across encodings when quoting the exact $A^2$ codimension/tree bounds; re-express the parameter under the proof’s sign convention first. Fourier coefficients use the uniform cube. A parity decision tree ($\oplus$-DT) may query arbitrary linear forms.

- **Theorem 1.1 (physical p.3; printed p.2):** there is an affine subspace of codimension at most $A^2$ on which $f$ is constant. Section 1.4 (physical p.5) notes that Tsang et al. later proved a stronger $O(A)$ codimension result; this manuscript’s own theorem states $A^2$.
- **Theorem 1.2 (physical p.3; printed p.2):** $f$ has a parity decision tree of size at most $2^{A^2}n^{2A}$. The exponent is $2A$, not $2^A$; the induction formula is visible on physical p.11 (printed p.10) and matches the [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0371).
- **Theorem 1.3 (physical p.3; printed p.2):** if also $s$ is the Fourier sparsity, the parity decision tree has depth at most $A^2\log s$. Section 1.4 records the later $O(A\log s)$ improvement by Tsang et al.; keep it attributed as a cited later result.
- **Theorem 1.4 (physical p.4; printed p.3):** for each $\delta,\epsilon>0$, a randomized oracle algorithm succeeds with probability at least $1-\delta$ and outputs a parity decision tree of depth $O(A^2+\log(1/\epsilon))$, size $2^{O(A^2)}\min\{1/\epsilon^2,\ O(\log(1/\epsilon))^{2A}\}$, computing a Boolean $g_\epsilon$ with uniform Hamming distance $\Pr_x[f(x)\ne g_\epsilon(x)]\le\epsilon$. Runtime is polynomial in $n,\exp(A^2),1/\epsilon,\log(1/\delta)$. This is Hamming approximation, not Fourier-$L^2$ approximation.

## Proof mechanism

The Boolean identity $f^2=1$ gives **Lemma 3.2 (physical p.9; printed p.8)**: for every nonzero $\alpha$, $\sum_\gamma\widehat f(\gamma)\widehat f(\alpha+\gamma)=0$. Restricting to the hyperplane $\chi_{\alpha+\beta}=\pm1$ folds paired Fourier coefficients into their sum or difference. **Lemma 3.1 (physical pp.8–9; printed pp.7–8)** uses this cancellation: for the largest and second-largest coefficients $\widehat f(\alpha),\widehat f(\beta)$, the two restrictions lose at least the two magnitudes (the assignment is swapped when the signs differ). **Lemma 3.3 (physical p.10; printed p.9)** gives
$$
|\widehat f(\alpha)|\ge 1/A,\qquad |\widehat f(\beta)|>(1-|\widehat f(\alpha)|^2)/A.
$$
Thus **Corollary 3.4 (physical p.10)** finds a restriction of spectral norm at most $A-1/A$ whenever $A>1$, while **Lemma 3.5 (physical p.10)** says a Boolean function with norm $1$ is a signed character. Iteration proves Theorem 1.1; the paper remarks its basic proof gives a constant subspace after at most $A^2$ restrictions, while the cited TWXZ lemma improves the order to $O(A)$.

For Theorem 1.2, induction on $n$ and $A$ uses the first query $\chi_{\alpha+\beta}$. If $|\widehat f(\alpha)|\ge1/2$, the two restricted norms are at most $A-1/2$ and $A$; otherwise they are at most $A-1/A$ and $A-3/(4A)$. This yields the displayed $2^{A^2}n^{2A}$ bound. For Theorem 1.3, first fix $A^2$ parities so $f$ is constant on that affine subspace. Every nonzero Fourier coefficient must then have a partner in its coset under the span of these parities; after any assignment each restricted Fourier support has at most half the previous size. Repeating for at most $\log_2s$ rounds gives depth $A^2\log s$.

Theorem 1.4 follows from **Lemma 3.6 (physical p.12; printed p.11)**, a functional parity tree whose random input reaches a high-bias leaf with probability at least $1-\epsilon$, then replaces the corresponding leaves by constants. The construction stops at $K=\max\{10A^2,2\log(1/\epsilon)\}$; binomial-tail control bounds low-bias paths. **Lemma 3.7 (physical pp.13–14)** supplies the coefficient-learning step (Goldreich–Levin/Kushilevitz–Mansour framework). These algorithmic inputs are used as cited tools rather than reproved in full.

## $\mathbb Z_p^n$ extension and limits

Section 4 (physical pp.14–22) treats Boolean functions on $\mathbb Z_p^n$, $p>2$, with complex Fourier coefficients. **Lemma 4.1 (physical p.15)** gives three restriction-loss alternatives, with $c_0$ absolute and $c_1(p)=O(1/p^2)$; the resulting analogues include codimension $O(A^2)$ (Theorem 4.7, physical p.20), $p$-ary tree-size bounds with $p$-dependent constants (Theorem 4.8, p.20), and $O(A^2\log s)$ depth under the same extra sparsity condition (Theorem 4.10, p.21). The complex-vector geometry is Lemma 4.3 (p.16), with loss factor $C(\theta)=(1-\cos\theta)/2$. These are not needed for A165’s Boolean-cube statement.

**A165 interface:** the results depend on small Fourier $L^1$ norm $A$, and Theorem 1.3 additionally assumes a bound on Fourier $L^0$ sparsity $s$. A165 supplies only nonzero singleton coefficients and asks for a lower bound on $L^0$ support; it supplies neither small $A$ nor a sparsity upper bound. These results therefore do not prove $\Omega(n^2)$. Do not replace Fourier $L^0$ by the $L^1$ spectral norm or call the Hamming approximation theorem an $L^2$-Fourier result.
