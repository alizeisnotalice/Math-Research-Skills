# M0112 — Kahn–Kalai–Linial influence theorem (1988)

**Source and identity.** Jeff Kahn, Gil Kalai, and Nathan Linial, “The Influence of Variables on Boolean Functions” (FOCS 1988 extended abstract), DOI 10.1109/SFCS.1988.21923. The author's PDF at `https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/~kalai/kkl.pdf` has the matching title and authors. ## Verified use and boundary

The paper introduces coordinate and coalition influence in the uniform Boolean cube and uses harmonic analysis to prove influential-variable/coalition results. In Fourier coordinates, for Boolean (f), the standard coordinate influence is (mathrm{Inf}_i(f)=\sum_{S\ni i}\hat f(S)^2). In its balanced setting, KKL yields a coordinate with influence at least on the order of ((\log n)/n), with constants depending on the normalization and the formulation of the theorem.

This is a maximum-coordinate-influence statement. It does not say all singleton coefficients are nonzero, does not imply quadratic Fourier support, and does not by itself establish Friedgut's fixed-error small-tribe conjecture. For exact assumptions and later variants consult the full source before using a sharp constant or a nonbalanced version.
