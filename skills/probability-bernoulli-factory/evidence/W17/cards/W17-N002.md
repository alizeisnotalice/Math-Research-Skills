## Factory construction and assumptions

The stated Keane–O’Brien condition is that f:[0,1]→[0,1] is continuous and either identically 0 or 1, or for some integer n≥1,
min{f(p),1−f(p)}≥min{p,1−p}^n for 0≤p≤1.
The paper calls this polynomial boundedness. Its construction defines f1=f and, for each k, chooses a finite integer η(f,k), independent of p, large enough that
`r_k(p)=f_k(p)−(1/4)P{f_k(X̄_{η(f,k)}(p))≥1/2}∈[0,3/4]`, and `f_{k+1}(p)=(4/3)r_k(p)∈[0,1]`,
where X̄ is the mean of that many independent p-coins. The range `[0,3/4]` is for the **unscaled residual** `r_k`. Algorithm 1 line 3 literally prints `BinCount/L`, whereas adjacent Eq. (2) and prose use `BinCount/η(f,L)`; the card follows the latter sample-mean construction and records the pseudocode discrepancy. Keane–O’Brien guarantees such a choice and that each `f_k` remains polynomially bounded (physical p. 2).

Draw L~Geom(1/4) on 1,2,…, independently of the p-coins; draw η(f,L) p-coins and return 1{f_L(X̄)≥1/2}. The geometric mixture expansion
f(p)=Σ_{k≥1}(3/4)^(k−1)(1/4) P{f_k(X̄_{η(f,k)})≥1/2}
converges uniformly. Crucially, the number of p-coins is independent of their outcomes, but the paper gives no growth bound for η(f,k); moment claims about η(f,L) therefore do not follow (p. 2).

## Main theorems and proof dependencies

- **Theorem 1 (physical pp. 2–3).** For a continuous, polynomially bounded, Lipschitz f:[0,1]→[0,1], the finite Wright–Fisher allele-frequency process with selection mixture f^(N)(p)=(1−σ/N)p+(σ/N)f(p) converges weakly in Skorokhod topology to dY_t=σ(f(Y_t)−Y_t)dt+√(Y_t(1−Y_t))dW_t. The proof expands h∈C² with bounded third derivative, shows generator convergence NL_Nh→σ(f−y)h′+½y(1−y)h″, then applies Kallenberg’s pseudo-Poisson approximation/Feller argument. The paper notes a possible extension to certain non-Lipschitz drifts via separate Feller-semigroup conditions.
- **Theorem 2 (physical pp. 3–4).** The ancestral-lineage limit has generator
  Gh(n)=binom(n,2)[h(n−1)−h(n)]+σnΣ_{k≥1}(3/4)^(k−1)(1/4)[h(n+η(f,k)−1)−h(n)].
  Per generation, a pair coalesces with probability 1/N+o(1/N); triple/multiple mergers, multiple branching, and merger-plus-branching events are o(1/N). One lineage branches into η(f,k) potential ancestors with probability (σ/N)(3/4)^(k−1)/4. Thus only isolated binary mergers and single branchings survive at the N-time scale.
- **Theorem 3 (physical pp. 3–4).** The Wright–Fisher diffusion and Bernstein-coefficient process are dual under H(y,v)=⟨B_{dim(v)−1}(y),v⟩, for every t≥0, y∈[0,1], n∈N, and v∈R^(n+1). The coalescence/diffusion identity is standard; for the selection term, the proof differentiates the Bernstein duality function and uses the hypergeometric repartition identity (10), equating separate independent coin groups with a uniformly allocated pooled sample. The coefficient process branches at rate nσP(L=k) and coalesces at rate binom(n,2).
- **Theorem 4 (physical pp. 4–5; statement/proof mismatch).** The printed theorem says the reflected branching Brownian representation solves Eq. (11), whose displayed PDE is ∂ₜu−Δu=λf(u). Its proof instead decomposes at the first branching time and derives ∂ₜq−Δq=λ[f(q)−q] (the reaction term also agrees with the voting interpretation of Eq. (12)). Thus the printed Eq. (11)/Theorem 4 statement and the proof do not match: the proof supports the PDE with −q, not the printed right-hand side λf(q). Do not silently treat this as a source convention or claim that the printed Theorem 4 proves Eq. (11) as displayed. Under the proof-derived equation, the initial and reflecting/Neumann boundary conditions are inherited from the leaves and reflected Brownian motion; this remains a model-specific representation, not a general boundary-condition theorem.

## Relevance boundary

This source establishes model-specific diffusion limits, ancestral generators, duality and a branching representation, while using a factory whose input count is outcome-independent. It does not give a stopping-time moment bound or solve general scalar factory cost questions: η(f,k) is finite but difficult to compute and may grow at an unbounded rate. Do not turn the genetics constructions into a general cost theorem.
