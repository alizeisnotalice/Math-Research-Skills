# Source card — Nacu–Peres, “Fast simulation of new coins from old” (2005)

**Identity and reading evidence.** Full 23-page article read; local source PDF SHA256 and exact version are in `sources.json`. The paper is Annals of Applied Probability 15(1A), 93–115 (2005), arXiv math/0309222v5. Complex polynomial statements and proof pages 4–5 were rendered and visually checked.

## Model and main exact statements

The input is an iid sequence of `p`-coin tosses, with optional independent auxiliary random bits in algorithms where specified. The output is exactly Bernoulli with success probability `f(p)`. `N` is the input stopping time. “Fast” means an exponential tail for each fixed `p` (constants may depend on `p`); uniform exponential tails on compact parameter sets are separately established under a closed-interval analyticity condition.

**Proposition 3 / Bernstein-envelope characterization (PDF pp.3–6).** A scheme induces lower/upper degree-`n` Bernstein polynomials

`g_n(p)=Σ_k binom(n,k)a(n,k)p^k(1−p)^(n−k)`,
`h_n(p)=Σ_k binom(n,k)b(n,k)p^k(1−p)^(n−k)`,

with `0≤a≤b≤1`, `binom(n,k)a,b` integers, `g_n≤f≤h_n`, lower and upper consistency under Bernstein degree elevation, and the exact identity `P_p(N>n)=h_n(p)−g_n(p)`. Conversely, nested envelopes converging to `f` construct a valid factory with precisely that tail. Proof uses the sets of finite input words where the algorithm has already output 1 versus where it either outputs 1 or has not yet stopped; consistency follows because accepted prefixes remain accepted after any extension. This is a full stopping-time criterion, not a criterion in terms of moments alone.

**Theorem 2 (PDF p.2).** For a function into `(0,1)`, real analyticity on a closed interval `I⊂(0,1)` (understood on an open neighborhood for the forward direction) yields a factory with an exponential tail uniform over `p∈I`; conversely an exponential-tail factory on an open subset implies real analyticity there. The constants/domain qualifications matter: “fast” on open sets is not automatically a uniform global bound.

**Smoothness/moment consequences (PDF pp.17–19).** The paper’s table and Propositions 22–23 distinguish tail hypotheses carefully. Finite expected input count with a uniform bound on a compact interval gives a Lipschitz conclusion. For `k` derivatives, the stated sufficient condition is uniform vanishing of the `k`-th moment tails `E_p[N^k 1_{N>n}]` on an open parameter set; under this uniform-integrability condition, `f∈C^k`. Pointwise finiteness `E_p[N^k]<∞` for every `p` is not asserted to imply that uniform tail condition.

## Proof and construction map

- §1, PDF pp.1–3: stopping-time model, fast rate, and relation to prior observability results.
- §2, pp.3–6: nested Bernstein lower/upper bounds and exact tail formula; word-tree proof.
- §3, pp.6–12: doubling construction for a clipped linear example, with geometric/hypergeometric tail estimates; this is a method example, not a universal linear-factory theorem.
- §4, pp.13–17: closure operations, positive power-series construction, and analytic examples.
- §5, pp.17–19: necessity of analyticity and moment-tail regularity conditions; retain the uniform tail assumption.
- §6, pp.20–21: parameter transformations and constructions.
- §7, pp.21–22: continuous functions and a Pólya-based terminating construction.
- §8, pp.22–23: open questions, including moment/smoothness questions; these are not resolved by the paper’s earlier sufficient conditions.

## Use for C204 and limits

This paper gives the key exact representation framework and useful sufficient regularity results. It does **not** give a complete necessary-and-sufficient characterization for pointwise finite `r`-th stopping moments at every admissible parameter. Its “finite kth moment” differentiability statement needs uniform decay of moment tails; replacing that by merely pointwise finiteness would strengthen the theorem without proof.
