## Theorem 2: bounded unbiased debiasing

Input: a known f:[0,1]→(0,1) and i.i.d. unknown-x coins Xᵢ~Ber(x). For noninteger ρ, C^ρ[0,1] means floor(ρ) continuous derivatives and a ρ−floor(ρ) Hölder last derivative. Theorem 1 (rephrasing Nacu–Peres, physical p. 3) gives Bernstein coefficient arrays 0≤a(n,k)≤b(n,k)≤1, compatible under hypergeometric thinning, with lower/upper polynomials gₙ and hₙ converging to f and uniform gap hₙ−gₙ≤Cn^(−ρ/2).

Let L be independent of the coins, with P(L=n)=n^(−λ)/ζ(λ,k₀) for n≥k₀, where ζ(λ,k₀)=Σ_{j≥0}(j+k₀)^(−λ). For S_L=Σ_{i=1}^L Xᵢ, define H_L(m,S_L) by the hypergeometric average of the size-m lower coefficients. **Theorem 2 (physical pp. 4–7):** if f∈C^ρ, ρ>3, choose 1<λ<(ρ−1)/2 and any sufficiently large start k₀; then

ψ(L,S_L)=H_L(k₀−1,S_L)+Σ_{n=k₀}^L [H_L(n,S_L)−H_L(n−1,S_L)]/P(L≥n)

is unbiased for f(x) and lies in [0,1] almost surely. The theorem’s k₀ is chosen large enough from the envelope constants; it is not an arbitrary start for the boundedness conclusion.

**Proof chain.** (i) Conditional hypergeometric averaging yields E[H_L(n,S_L)|L]=gₙ(x); reciprocal-survival weighting cancels the event L≥n, so expectations telescope to g_{k₀−1}+Σ_{n≥k₀}(gₙ−gₙ₋₁)=f. (ii) Compatibility of lower coefficients makes each increment nonnegative; the combinatorial cancellation identity (10) proves this termwise. (iii) For the upper bound, the paper applies Stirling’s lower bound to a central binomial denominator, obtaining (14), then sums against the power-law survival tail. Convergence is possible when λ<(ρ−1)/2; the upper envelope hₙ and f([0,1])⊂(0,1) give a fixed distance from 1, and sufficiently large k₀ makes the residual sum smaller than that distance (eqs. 15–17; p. 7). Formula (14) was rendered and visually checked.

Corollary 1 drops the upper-bound assumptions and retains an unbiased a.s.-nonnegative estimator whenever the lower coefficients converge, for any λ>1 and any start; its upper-only analogue is bounded by one but need not be nonnegative. Corollary 2 under Theorem 2 gives Var(ψ)≤1/4 because ψ∈[0,1]. The authors say ρ>2 appears enough for convergence of the variance series but could not verify the additional condition needed for finite variance in all such cases (physical p. 8).

## Cost implication (derived, not a paper theorem)

The p-coin count is exactly L and is independent of the p-coin outcomes; an independent auxiliary uniform can turn the [0,1]-valued ψ into an f(x)-coin. From the stated mass P(L=n)∝n^(−λ), E[L^r]<∞ iff λ>r+1. Combining this series test with the theorem’s allowed 1<λ<(ρ−1)/2, a choice with finite r-th input-count moment exists when ρ>2r+3. This is only a sufficient smoothness/cost implication of the paper’s construction, not an iff for arbitrary factories. For r=1, finite mean cost by this route requires ρ>5; the theorem’s basic ρ>3 guarantee alone does not imply finite mean L.

The implementability section (physical p. 8) notes that constructing Nacu–Peres coefficient arrays can be difficult; affine functions are a simple exception. Open problems include optimal tails and endpoint behavior. The theorem excludes f(0),f(1)∈{0,1}, and does not characterize all functions with finite stopping moments.
