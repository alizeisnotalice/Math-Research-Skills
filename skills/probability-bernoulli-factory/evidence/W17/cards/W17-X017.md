## Tree model and theorem-level results

The input is an iid sequence of Bernoulli(`p`) tosses, with `p` unknown to the algorithm; a tree has internal continuation nodes and leaves labelled by fair outputs. The tree retains the complete ordered history rather than only the current head/tail counts. `E(A;p)` denotes expected input tosses for the specified tree algorithm; “optimal” refers to this expected-input criterion.

**Theorem 1 (printed p.216; physical PDF p.6).** There is a tree `T` with `E(T;p)≤E(Q₃;p)` for every `0<p<1`, with equality only at `p=1/2`. The proof swaps two subtrees of `Q₃` while preserving the 0–1 probability balance. The expectation change is `p⁹q⁴(q−p)+p⁴q⁹(p−q)=p⁴q⁴(q−p)(p⁵−q⁵)`, which is negative for `p≠1/2` and zero at `p=1/2`: since `p⁵−q⁵` has the sign of `p−q`, the two factors have opposite signs on either side of `1/2`. This is a uniform improvement of `Q₃`, not the paper’s no-E-optimality result.

**Theorem 2 and corollary (printed p.217; physical PDF p.7).** Theorem 2 says there is no algorithm whose only continuation sequences are `{Rⁿ,Lⁿ:n≥1}`. Its following corollary states `E(A;p)>1/(pq)−1` for any algorithm `A` and probability `p`; keep the theorem’s restricted continuation-sequence assertion distinct from the corollary’s broader stated scope. The adjacent Lemma 1 proves endpoint lower bounds by using Theorem 2 to force an additional continuation sequence and evaluating its probability.

**Theorem 3 (printed pp.217–218; physical pp.7–8).** There is no E-optimal algorithm. Its proof combines Lemma 1’s alternative near-endpoint lower bounds with a mirrored algorithm and an explicit tree `V`. Lemma 2 (physical p.8) verifies that `V` outputs 0 and 1 with probability `1/2` and that `E(V;p)≤1/(pq)−1+O(q²)` as `q↓0`; the continuation-node sum also establishes almost-sure termination. The theorem’s conclusion is global nonattainment of an E-minimum, while the displayed construction is a boundary-asymptotic comparison.

**Theorem 4 (printed p.220; physical PDF p.10).** For the authors’ SW procedure producing one of `N` equiprobable outcomes from iid draws of a fixed discrete source with parameter vector `p`, `E(SW;p;N)` is of order `log N` for fixed source probabilities. The upper bound uses `L=⌈−log_p N⌉` and a binomial-tail estimate for the event that a length-`L` outcome block has not yet identified its class. The lower comparison `E≥log₂N` follows from the information/entropy constraint. Theorem 5 (printed p.221; physical p.11) gives the corresponding `O(log N)` extension for a fixed nonbinary discrete distribution. Constants and source model are not uniformized here over all `p` or all distributions.

This is auxiliary random-bit extraction/expected-cost literature, not a general Bernoulli-factory theorem. Its expected flips for one unbiased bit or `N`-way output do not give moments of the stopping time for an arbitrary factory. Conversion of auxiliary randomness to p-only cost remains a separate interface that needs proof.
