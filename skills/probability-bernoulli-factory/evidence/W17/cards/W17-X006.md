## Finite-automaton and pushdown boundary

The paper distinguishes automata with finite control from pushdown automata, not unrestricted random stopping algorithms.

- **Theorem 1.1 (§1, PDF pp. 2–3).** For `D⊂(0,1)` and `f:D→(0,1)`, an `f(p)` coin can be simulated by a finite automaton for every `p∈D` iff `f` is a rational function over `Q` on `D` (equivalently, agrees there with a rational function whose numerator/denominator satisfy the required strict range condition on `(0,1)`). The paper’s proof plan is made explicit in Theorem 2.2.
- **Theorem 2.2 (§2, pp. 5–9).** Rational functions, block procedures, and finite automata are equivalent. For finite automaton ⇒ rationality, Proposition 2.3 writes success probabilities as the unique bounded solution to a finite linear harmonic system; Cramer’s rule makes that solution rational. For rationality ⇒ finite automaton, Proposition 2.5 identifies the block procedure with integer coefficient bounds, and Lemma 2.7 clears denominators and raises degree until Pólya’s positivity theorem applies to the numerator, denominator, and their difference. This constructs admissible blocks. Theorem 2.9/Corollary 2.10 extend the result to multi-sided coins.
- **Theorem 1.2 (§3, pp. 9–12).** If a function is simulable by a pushdown automaton, then it is algebraic over `Q`. This is a necessary condition only; the paper does not prove every algebraic probability function admits such an automaton. A bounded hitting-probability function solves a finite system of polynomial fixed-point equations induced by state/stack transitions. The finite positive solution is semialgebraic, hence algebraic by Hillar’s Appendix Theorem A.1 and the real-algebraic arguments in Appendix A (PDF pp. 16–18).
- **Nonrational example (§3.2, pp. 12–13).** A pushdown automaton implements a random walk with a stack; its bounded hitting probability solves a quadratic equation. The rendered example yields a square-root coin, so pushdown automata can exceed finite automata’s rational class.
- **§4 (pp. 13–15) and refs (pp. 15, 19).** Related context and open problems; Appendix A (pp. 16–18) proves the algebraic-function lemma. The complete PDF has references at both p. 15 and final p. 19 due the appended author proof material.
