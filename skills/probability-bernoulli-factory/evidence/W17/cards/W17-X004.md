## Exact event algorithm and hypotheses

The setting is an event of unknown probability `s∈(0,1)` whose approximations can be computed with finite effort. In §2 the random lower/upper bounds satisfy, almost surely, `L_n≤U_n`, take values in `[0,1]`, and obey `E L_n=l_n↑s`, `E U_n=u_n↓s` (conditions (1)–(3), physical PDF p.5). The paper explicitly says it does **not** assume `L_n≤s` or `U_n≥s` pointwise; the bounds are stochastic approximants, not pathwise bounds on the constant `s`. Algorithm 3 separately assumes a.s. monotonicity `L_{n−1}≤L_n`, `U_{n−1}≥U_n` (condition (4)).

**Theorem 2.5 (printed article p.5; physical PDF p.6).** For Algorithm 4, the paper assumes (1), (2), (3), and the reverse-time conditional relations (6)–(7):

`E(L_{n−1}|F_{n,∞}) = E(L_{n−1}|F_n) ≤ L_n`,
`E(U_{n−1}|F_{n,∞}) = E(U_{n−1}|F_n) ≥ U_n` a.s.

With `\tilde L_n,\tilde U_n` defined by (8)–(9), the output is an `s`-coin and `P(N>n iterations)=u_n−l_n`. The proof establishes that the recursive transformed bounds are ordered and monotone a.s., preserve their expectations, and hence have gap expectation `u_n−l_n`; comparing one uniform random variable against the nested interval gives the tail identity and exact output law.

**Cost boundary.** The theorem's `N` is the number of algorithmic iterations. In the Bernoulli-factory specialization of Proposition 3.1 (statement: article p.6 / physical PDF p.7; proof: article p.7 / physical PDF p.8), iteration `n` corresponds to extending the p-coin prefix to length `n`, so its input-toss cost is explicitly tied to `n`. For a general event estimator, one iteration may require a variable or nontrivial amount of work to obtain `L_n,U_n`; tail bounds on iteration count imply input-toss moments only after bounding that per-stage input cost.

## Unbiased-estimator extension and limits

**Theorem 2.7 (physical PDF p.7; printed article p.6).** If `L_n≤U_n` a.s.; both sequences lie in one deterministic interval `[-M,M]` a.s.; their expectations satisfy `E L_n↑s` and `E U_n↓s`; and the same reverse-time conditional equalities/inequalities as above hold, then an unbiased estimator of `s∈R` can be constructed. The proof rescales by the affine map from `[-M,M]` to `[0,1]`, applies Algorithm 4, and transforms the Bernoulli output back. It is a **uniform boundedness** assumption, not a variance condition.

**Proposition 3.1 (statement physical PDF p.7 / printed article p.6; proof physical PDF p.8 / printed article p.7).** In the paper's weak-simulation setting with auxiliary `U(0,1)` randomness, an `f(p)` factory on `P⊆(0,1)` exists iff there are nested Bernstein polynomial envelopes with coefficients in `[0,1]`, pointwise limits equal to `f`, and the stated hypergeometric degree-elevation inequalities. The proof realizes `L_n=a(n,H_n)` and `U_n=b(n,H_n)` from p-coin head counts, then verifies the reverse-time conditional inequalities by conditioning on the hypergeometric law of earlier heads given later heads. This is an existence/construction criterion under its stated parameter domain, not a general higher-moment classification.
