## Usable result: extracting a fair bit from one biased coin

Assume an infinite supply of independent tosses of one Bernoulli(`p`) coin, with `0<p<1`. Read tosses in pairs. Discard a pair `HH` or `TT`; for `HT` output 1, and for `TH` output 0. The source describes this on printed scan p. 768 (physical PDF p. 1).

The exact proof chain is the two-pair symmetry:

1. `P(HT)=p(1-p)=P(TH)`, so the probability a pair is accepted is `2p(1-p)>0`.
2. Conditional on acceptance, `P(output=1)=p(1-p)/(2p(1-p))=1/2`.
3. Independent disjoint input pairs make successive accepted outputs independent. The number of pair trials to the next accepted pair is geometric with parameter `2p(1-p)`, hence the expected toss count per fair bit is `2/[2p(1-p)]=1/[p(1-p)]`.

Thus it is an exact fair-bit extractor, with almost-sure termination for each fixed interior `p`. At `p=0` or `p=1`, every pair is equal and the method never terminates. Its expected toss count is not uniform as `p` approaches either endpoint. These endpoint and cost statements follow directly from the acceptance probability; the paper does not give a general stopping-time theorem.

## Scope in the paper

The first page contrasts physical randomness with arithmetic recipes and introduces this pair method. The remainder discusses pseudo-random digit generation by iterating the quadratic map, warns that deterministic dependence and finite precision matter, and presents inverse-transform/rejection-style methods for prescribed real distributions. On printed p. 769 (physical p. 2), rejection sampling accepts `X` with probability proportional to a target density `f(X)`; the accepted sample has density `f` after normalization, while efficiency depends on the mean-to-maximum density ratio. The angle rejection example and a decreasing-uniform/retrial construction for an exponential law continue on printed p. 770 (physical p. 3). These are algorithmic examples, not modern arbitrary-`f(p)` Bernoulli-factory characterizations.

## Transfer limits

- This yields external fair randomness only when the input coin is an independent Bernoulli(`p`) source with fixed `0<p<1`; it does not itself supply a fair bit uniformly over `p∈[0,1]`.
- A downstream construction may use the fair bit for independent uniform choices, but must state that the random-bit source satisfies the interior-coin assumption (or that fair randomness is separately available).
- It does not transform a `p`-coin into an arbitrary scalar `f(p)`-coin, and it gives no general expected-cost bound uniform in `p`.
- The historical inverse-transform and rejection examples use uniform/physical randomness and target-density calculations. They are not evidence for vector-polytope Bernoulli factories or endpoint guarantees.
