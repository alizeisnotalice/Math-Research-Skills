# W17-N004 — Scalable Bernoulli Factory MCMC for Intractable Marginalised Posteriors

**Authors/version.** Timothée Stumpf-Fétizon and Flávio B. Gonçalves, arXiv:2505.05438v3, revised 29 Aug 2026; complete 42-physical-page preprint. SHA-256 2a9f1106b9793ba7708e5323c8f514e2baad7935a528bda9a994ea26c77e90a6.

## Scope and exact transition

The paper targets marginal Bayesian MCMC when the likelihood is intractable but has an appropriate factorization and simulable likelihood-ratio coins. It constructs a Barker-within-Gibbs update using a recursive cascade of 2-coin factories (C2C). It is an application-specific exact MCMC algorithm, not a general theorem about every scalar f(p) factory or every likelihood model.

For proposal ϑ from θ and posterior π(θ|x), the Barker acceptance probability is π(ϑ|x)/[π(ϑ|x)+π(θ|x)]. Factor the odds into n terms hᵢ=dᵢqᵢ/[dᵢᵣₑᵥqᵢᵣₑᵥ], with tractable positive dᵢ and simulable probability coins qᵢ; conditional independence of the factor coins is Assumption 1 (physical p. 8). Internal C2C nodes recursively merge child coins; the node probability has odds equal to the product of child odds. Proposition 1 is proved by induction on tree height (pp. 9–11).

For node probability rⱼ and child probabilities rⱼ₁,rⱼ₂, the 2-coin merger has

rⱼ = rⱼ₁rⱼ₂/[rⱼ₁rⱼ₂+(1−rⱼ₁)(1−rⱼ₂)],  τⱼ = rⱼ/(rⱼ₁rⱼ₂) = 1/[rⱼ₁rⱼ₂+(1−rⱼ₁)(1−rⱼ₂)],

where τⱼ is its expected number of merge loops (printed eq. 15, physical p. 11; rendered). If leaf coin simulation cost is ρⱼ, Proposition 2 gives

ρ₀=Σ_{j₁…jℓ∈{1,2}^ℓ} ρ_{j₁…jℓ} τ₀ Π_{k=1}^{ℓ−1} τ_{j₁…jₖ}

(eq. 16, p. 11). This follows by the exact recursion ρ_node=τ_node(ρ_left+ρ_right), with independent node costs.

The “portkey” modification adds a state-symmetric escape/rejection mass bⱼ(θ,ϑ)=bⱼ(ϑ,θ). Proposition 3 recursively preserves the ratio of directional accept/reject masses, so the root update remains reversible and has acceptance probability

κ(θ|ϑ)π(ϑ,x)/[b₀(θ,ϑ)+κ(θ|ϑ)π(ϑ,x)+κ(ϑ|θ)π(θ,x)].

The escape term can control per-attempt effort, but does not make an arbitrary unknown likelihood factor efficiently simulable (pp. 12–13, 34–35).

## Complexity theorem and cost model

**Theorem 1, merge cost (physical pp. 14–16).** Under the paper’s correctly specified iid latent-variable DGP (Assumption 2), locally twice differentiable log likelihood with zero-mean score and uniform local MGF bounds on score/Hessian (Assumption 3), and a Bernstein–von Mises posterior/latent-posterior concentration plus O(n^(−1/2)) uniform proposal with limiting constant acceptance (Assumption 4), the merge overhead obeys 4^(−ℓ)ωₙ=O_{Gₙ}(1) as n,ℓ→∞ with ℓ scaled with n. The proof replaces the stationary law by its BvM limit in total variation, bounds expected factor likelihood ratios by 1+O(1/n) using Taylor expansion and MGF assumptions, then uses the random-partition merge formula and Markov’s inequality. This is stochastic boundedness only; the authors explicitly warn ωₙ need not be integrable without stronger tail assumptions (p. 15; proof pp. 37–38).

For ordinary bounded-likelihood coins, leaf loop counts grow exponentially in the number n/2^ℓ of factors assigned to each leaf; choose 2^ℓ∼n to hold leaf overhead constant, at merge overhead O(n²). If a likelihood-ratio coin has the local lower bound needed in Proposition 6 (conditional log-ratio derivative MGF, eq. 29), then 2^ℓ log τ_leaf/√n=O_{Gₙ}(1); setting 4^ℓ∼n controls leaf overhead and gives merge overhead O(n) (pp. 16–18). Total operation cost still depends on how expensive each leaf coin is:

- independent per-factor estimators without batching: leaf iteration cost O(√n), hence total O(n^(3/2)) in the ratio-estimator setting;
- Poisson/russian-roulette ratio estimators with the stated “batching property”: leaf coin costs shrink as batch size grows, allowing O(1) leaf iteration cost and total O(n);
- without the ratio bound: the displayed vanilla regime is O(n²) total under the listed cost model.

These are conditional complexity regimes, not a blanket polynomial-time guarantee for every intractable posterior. The application sections study exact diffusion-likelihood and Cox-process constructions and report empirical computation; those experiments do not establish the asymptotic theorem. Technical appendices A–C include implementation details, proofs of Propositions 1–6 and Theorem 1, and supporting likelihood-ratio lemmas. The remaining Propositions 7–8 give exponential cost/tail bounds under their own positive-mean/log-MGF assumptions; they do not remove the model assumptions.

**Appendix C cost lower bound and tail refinement (physical pp. 41–42).** Proposition 7 fixes the measure with density π(y₁:n|θ₀)π(z₁:n|y₁:n,θₙ), where θ₀ is true and θₙ can be any state-space point; with rᵢ(θ)=−log pᵢ(θ), Sₙ=Σᵢrᵢ(θ), and C(n)=(Πᵢpᵢ(θ))⁻¹ as a lower bound on expected number of loops, the condition E[rᵢ(θ)]≥ε>0 for every i implies E[C(n)]≥e^{εn} by Jensen. This direct Proposition 7 argument remains separate.

Proposition 8 (physical pp. 41–42) defines `Fᵢ=σ(p₁(θ),…,pᵢ(θ))`, `Mₙ=Sₙ−ΣᵢE[rᵢ(θ)|Fᵢ₋₁]`, and assumes a conditional sub-Gaussian increment MGF bound. It then prints the Bernstein conclusion (86) centered at the **unconditional** `E[Sₙ]` and uses it in (87) to claim an exponential tail for `Πᵢpᵢ(θ)`. The proof interface is not certified under the stated assumptions: a martingale Bernstein inequality for `Mₙ` controls deviations from its random predictable compensator `Aₙ=ΣᵢE[rᵢ(θ)|Fᵢ₋₁]`, while the stated positive-mean condition only bounds unconditional `E[rᵢ]`; it does not bound `Aₙ−E[Sₙ]` pathwise or with a tail estimate. Thus (86)–(87) and the consequent `O(ρⁿ)` product bound are isolated as source claims, not used as proved under these assumptions. This does not affect Proposition 7's Jensen bound or the paper's main IID Theorem 1 result.
