## Theorem and input/output model (§§1–2, physical pp. 1–7)

The flow-based polytope is described on a directed edge set E by edge coordinates `x_e∈[0,1]` and integer node demands `d_v`, with net outflow equal to `d_v` at each node. The network incidence matrix is totally unimodular, so its vertices are integral flows. The input is an independent Bernoulli(`p_e`) coin for each edge, with `p` in the relative polytope and every coordinate strictly between 0 and 1. The output is an integral flow vertex f with `P(e∈f)=p_e` for every edge. Theorem 1 (physical p. 4) requires the undirected graph on E to be connected; Theorem 3 (physical p. 8) includes the nonempty interior condition `P∩(0,1)^E≠∅`. These are vector marginals/output constraints, not a scalar `f(p)` construction.

## Algorithm 1 and proof chain (§3, physical pp. 8–15)

Algorithm 1 first flips each edge coin and restarts unless the 0/1 vector is a feasible flow f. Given f, it selects uniformly a directed tree T from the edge set such that reversing precisely the tree edges with `f_e=1` produces an arborescence rooted at a fixed node. It then flips the selected tree-edge coins again and restarts if any second outcome equals its original `f_e`; otherwise it outputs f. Uniform arborescence sampling is reduced to counting directed arborescences with the Matrix–Tree theorem and self-reduction. **Algorithm-level correction:** choosing uniformly among the `N_f` eligible trees contributes a flow-dependent `1/N_f` to the successful-round weight. R02's exact admissible example gives first-edge marginal `179/875`, rather than the input `1/5`; the literal Algorithm 1 is therefore not certified as an exact sampler. See `evidence/shared/reviews/R02/w17_x045_adjudication.md`.

The proof turns the acceptance/output probability into a Bernstein polynomial (Theorem 3, physical p. 8):

`P_{f,r}(x) = [∏_{e∈E} x_e^{f_e}(1−x_e)^{1−f_e}] · [Σ_{T: Flip_f(T)∈Arb_r} ∏_{e∈T}x_e^{1−f_e}(1−x_e)^{f_e}]`.

Theorem 3 verifies two polynomial conditions. First, for all `x∈P`, `Σ_f(f−x)P_{f,r}(x)=0`. Second, for all interior x, `Σ_f P_{f,r}(x)>0`. These conditions support a **separate** Bernoulli race with weights `P_{f,r}(x)`, which has mean x and terminates almost surely pointwise. They do not establish that literal Algorithm 1 implements those weights: its conditional tree sampling normalizes by `N_f`. Theorem 3's polynomial identities are not refuted by the finite counterexample.

For root independence (§3.1, physical pp. 9–10), define the reversed-edge weight `M_f(x)_e=x_e(1−f_e)+(1−x_e)f_e`. Since `x−f` is a circulation (subtract the identical flow-balance equations for x and f), reversing edges selected by f transports this difference to a circulation on the flipped graph. The arborescence sum in `P_{f,r}` is a Laplacian minor by the directed Matrix–Tree theorem. The transformed Laplacian has zero row and column sums, so its cofactors agree; thus the polynomial is independent of the root when x∈P.

For the marginal identity (§3.2, physical pp. 10–14), fix an edge `η=(s,t)`. It is enough to prove `(1−x_η)Σ_{f: f_η=1}P_f(x)=x_ηΣ_{f:f_η=0}P_f(x)`. The proof roots the arborescences at t and s on the two sides. Because η cannot lie in an arborescence pointing toward t when its flipped orientation points away, the remaining tree path plus η defines a cycle. Adding/subtracting this circulation bijects the admissible flow/tree terms, and the monomial factors agree after the η-coordinate is removed. This establishes the η marginal, hence the vector identity.

For positivity (§3.3, physical pp. 14–15), the interior point gives positive chance for each variable edge to be present and absent across feasible flows. After flipping orientations relative to a fixed vertex f, every edge of the transformed graph lies in a circulation. Lemma 11 shows that a connected underlying graph with this property is strongly connected; Lemma 10 then supplies an arborescence rooted at the chosen node. Thus at least one `P_{f,r}(x)` is positive for each interior x. Section 3.4 (physical p. 15) explains how to eliminate coordinates identically 0/1 and split disconnected edge sets into connected components.

## Scope and limitations

- The construction needs independent edge coins, integral flow-polytope structure, connectedness (or decomposition into connected components), and a point with all variable parameters in `(0,1)`.
- Keep the literal conditional-tree Algorithm 1 separate from the common-weight polynomial race. The exact counterexample in the R02 adjudication shows the former's claimed first-edge marginal fails on that instance; it does not refute Theorem 3's standalone identities.
- It outputs a 0/1 flow vector whose coordinate marginals match p; it does not produce a scalar Bernoulli(`g(p)`) from one p-coin.
- The algorithm uses external random choices, including uniform arborescence selection. This source does not derive those choices from the biased input coins.
- The positivity argument gives pointwise almost-sure termination on the interior. It supplies no uniform expected number of coin tosses or general moment/tail bound as p approaches the boundary.
- Integer demands and total unimodularity explain integrality of vertices; arbitrary polytopes with similar-looking constraints are outside this result.
