## Model and exact scope

Input is an iid sequence of unknown-bias `Bernoulli(p)` coins, with auxiliary randomness; `T` is the number of input p-coin flips. For constants `C>1` and known `ε>0`, target `f(p)=Cp` on `p∈[0,(1−ε)/C]` (so `Cp≤1−ε`). Theorem 1 (PDF p. 4) proves the Section 2 factory is exact and

`sup_{p∈[0,(1−ε)/C]} E_p[T] ≤ 9.5 C ε^(−1)`.

**Theorem 2, literal source statement (physical p. 4).** The page prints

`sup_{p∈[0,(1−ε)/C]} E_p[T] ≥ (3/16)e^(−2) ln(7) C ε^(−1)(1−ε^(−1/2))²(1−(1−ε)/C)`.

Keep this exact display as source evidence, not as a lower bound verified by the proof below. The abstract separately reports `0.004 C/ε` for `ε≤1/2`; the displayed proof does not support that summary as written.

**Lemma 11 / Theorem 2 lower-bound route is not certified (physical pp. 16–17).** Lemma 11 claims that, after drawing `W~Bern(Cp)` until four successes and setting `A` to the total number of trials, `4/(CA)` is an `(√ε,1/4)` approximation to `p` for `p∈[0,1/2]`. This precision claim is false under the stated domain. For `C=2`, `p=1/4`, `ε=1/100`, the source conditions hold strictly (`Cp=1/2<99/100` and `p< (1−ε)/C`). Here `A~NegBin(4,1/2)` and the required interval is `[9/40,11/40]`; the only admissible integer is `A=8`, so success probability is `P(A=8)=35/256<3/4`. The exact check is `evidence/W17/experiments/x010_precision_counterexample.py/.out`; the independent R02 adjudication is `evidence/shared/reviews/R02/w17_x010_adjudication.md`.

The separate cost identity `E[T_B]=4E[T]/(Cp)` for fresh factory calls is valid when the call has finite mean cost, but it cannot repair the false precision premise. If one **conditionally assumed** Lemma 11's precision assertion, the displayed Lemma 10 substitution would algebraically give

`E_p[T] ≥ (3 ln(7)/(64e²)) (C/ε)(1−√ε)²(1−p)`.

That coefficient calculation is recorded in `evidence/W17/experiments/review_point_fixes.py/.out`; it is conditional algebra only, not a verified lower bound. The source's Theorem 2 display on p. 4 instead has coefficient `3 ln(7)/(16e²)` and factor `(1−ε^(−1/2))²`, so both coefficient and epsilon factor differ from the proof route. Preserve the literal display and the invalid proof premise separately. This finding does not disprove the actual lower-bound problem and does not affect Theorem 1's exact factory or `9.5C/ε` upper bound.

## Construction and proof chain

- **Factorization (algorithm intuition, §2.1).** Use `p·1+(1−p)·((C−1)p/(1−p))=Cp`. The second probability is realized through a geometric number of Bernoulli draws; the algorithm adjusts the linear factor in stages.
- **Martingale correctness (§2.2–2.3, physical pp. 5–9).** The evolving candidate mean is built so its conditional expectation equals the preceding candidate mean. Lemma 1 stops a bounded martingale on `{0,1}` and uses optional stopping to obtain an exact Bernoulli output. Lemma 2 verifies the transition kernels preserve the martingale. Thus the algorithm’s limit is Bernoulli(`Cp`); the proof separately establishes almost-sure termination.
- **Expected-cost bound (§3, pp. 9–15).** Lemma 3 controls the probability a stage reaches its upper threshold via the stopped martingale; Lemma 4 bounds first-stage flips. Lemmas 5–9 control later stage costs, the probability each stage is reached, and the geometric series in the total cost. Theorem 3 gives the parameter-dependent bound; selecting parameters yields Theorem 1’s `9.5C/ε` bound.
- **Attempted lower bound (§4, pp. 15–17).** The proof reduces a low-cost factory to a relative-accuracy estimator and invokes a sequential information/variance lower bound. Lemma 11's claimed precision guarantee has the admissible counterexample above, so this route does not certify Theorem 2's lower bound. The theorem's true lower-bound status is not decided here; it is not a general bound for arbitrary factory targets.

The comparison table on pp. 14–15 reports 10,000 source-run simulations; these are the authors’ reported experiments, not local numerical evidence.
