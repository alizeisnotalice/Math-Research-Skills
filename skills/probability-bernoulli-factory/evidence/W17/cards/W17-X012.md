## Main mechanism: perfect sampling via a factory

For a general-state-space geometrically ergodic Markov chain, the paper combines a split-chain minorization with a Bernoulli factory inside a rejection sampler. A one-step minorization `P(x,A)≥s(x)Q(A)` yields an accessible regeneration atom and a mixture representation of the stationary distribution. Drift and minorization imply a geometric bound on the regeneration-time tail; the rejection proposal is chosen to dominate that tail. When the acceptance probability requires a coin with mean `ap>1·p`, a Bernoulli factory is used.

The raw target `ap` on its full natural interval may fail the Keane–O’Brien boundary condition. To obtain a smooth valid target, choose `0<δ<ω<1` and define the extension equal to `ap` on `[0,(1−ω)/a]`; on the remaining interval use `(1−ω)+F(p−(1−ω)/a)`, where `F(x)=δ∫_0^(ax/δ)e^(−t²)dt`. This is twice differentiable and concave, with

`|f″(p)|≤C := a²√2/(δ√e)`.

Set `a(n,k)=f(k/n)` and `b(n,k)=a(n,k)+C/(2n)`. The algorithm applies the recursive hypergeometric degree-elevation construction of Łatuszyński et al. The concavity of `f` gives the lower envelope inequality; the curvature bound and Nacu–Peres Lemma 6 give the upper envelope inequality. Appendix A verifies these hypotheses and invokes Łatuszyński et al., Proposition 3.1 / Theorem 2.5.

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 10; printed p. 19).** If `0<a<∞`, `0<δ<ω<1`, `ap∈[0,1−ω]`, and `W_i` are iid `Bernoulli(p)`, Algorithm I outputs an exact `Bernoulli(ap)` coin. Its literal statement is `P(N>n iterations)=a²/(nδ√(2e))` and Remark 5 repeats that reading. Keep this as the source’s printed assertion, with an indexing caveat: Algorithm I initializes `n=2^m`, uses the prefix sum `H_n=Σ_{i=1}^n W_i`, and on continuation sets `n←2n`; Appendix A derives the tail from the coefficient/envelope gap at prefix index `n`. Thus its operational `n` is a doubling input-prefix length, while the theorem calls `n` an iteration count. If `k` counts completed continuations from the initial prefix, the current prefix is `n_k=2^{m+k}`. The article does not reconcile the two indices. Until that is resolved, do not use the displayed expression as a verified round-count tail or assert an exact stage-to-toss moment conversion. The exact-output theorem and the envelope construction remain usable with this source-scope limitation.

## Exact-sampling application and reported examples

Sections 2 and 4 give the mixture/rejection sampler and its cost setup. Drift/minorization are worked through for a Metropolis–Hastings example, a Gibbs example, and a Bayesian one-way random-effects model (Theorem 2, balanced-design condition). The tables, plots, and styrene-data timings in §§3.2, 5, and 6 are source-reported experiments, not locally reproduced results. The appendices supply the factory theorem proof and the model-specific drift/minorization calculations.
