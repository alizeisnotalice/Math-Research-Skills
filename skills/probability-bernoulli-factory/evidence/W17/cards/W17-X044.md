## Model and general race lemma (§2, physical pp. 2–8)

The input is an independent vector of Bernoulli coins with unknown parameters `x∈P∩(0,1)^n`, where `P⊂[0,1]^n` is a polytope. The output is a random vertex `V` of `P` with prescribed coordinate means `E[V]=x`. This is a *vector-valued polytope factory*, not the scalar problem of returning a Bernoulli coin of bias `f(p)`.

The decision-tree / Bernstein-polynomial route is explicit. A finite leaf probability is a Bernstein monomial `M(x)=∏_i x_i^{a_i}(1−x_i)^{b_i}` and can be implemented by flipping each input coin `a_i+b_i` times and testing the required pattern (Lemma 2.4, physical p. 6). A nonnegative Bernstein polynomial with coefficients scaled to have sum at most one is implementable by externally sampling a coefficient label and running its monomial test (Lemma 2.4, physical p. 6). If each vertex v is assigned a nonzero Bernstein polynomial `P_v`, the race selects v with probability `P_v(x)/Σ_w P_w(x)`. Theorem 2.8 (physical p. 8) shows that the correct mean is equivalent to the vector identity `Σ_v P_v(x)(v−x)=0`; if also `Σ_v P_v(x)>0` throughout the target domain, the race terminates almost surely there. The positivity condition on the *whole polytope* is what upgrades the interior factory to a strong one. External randomization is used for coefficient/index choices.

## Geometry and classification (§§3–6, physical pp. 8–27)

For perfect matchings (the Birkhoff–von Neumann polytope), Section 3 assigns a polynomial to each permutation. It is built from the product of matching-edge probabilities and a sum over rooted arborescences of the complete directed graph. The directed matrix-tree theorem writes the arborescence sum as a Laplacian cofactor. On doubly stochastic inputs, the relevant Laplacian has zero row and column sums; equality of cofactors makes the polynomial independent of the chosen root. A combinatorial bijection between permutation–tree pairs and rooted bi-trees then yields the coordinate marginal identity in Theorem 3.4. This gives a genuine multivariate exact sampling construction with external combinatorial random choices.

The necessary condition (Theorem 4.1, physical pp. 14–15; full proof in Appendix C, pp. 34–36) states that if `P∩(0,1)^n` is nonempty and a factory exists, then `P` must be the intersection of the cube with an affine subspace. The strong version has the analogous necessary condition (Theorem 4.2). For generic affine sections `H={x:Wx=b}` of codimension k, each relevant vertex has a set S of exactly k fractional coordinates and `W_S` is invertible. The polynomial assigned to a partition `π=(A,S,B)` is

`P_π(x)=|det W_S| ∏_{i∈A}(1−x_i) ∏_{i∈B}x_i ∏_{i∈S}x_i(1−x_i)`.

For a fixed input x in the polytope, at least one valid partition contributes positively: write x as a convex combination of vertices and choose a vertex with positive weight; its zero/one coordinates force the compatible A/B/S inclusions, and each fractional factor is positive. For the mean identity, split the sum coordinatewise according to whether the coordinate lies in A, B or S. Cramer’s rule converts the fractional-coordinate correction into determinant ratios. The remaining signed sum is identified with a decomposition of a zonotope into half-open parallelotopes; the two sign classes tile the same zonotope and their signed volume sum cancels. Theorem 5.1 (physical p. 16) therefore gives a strong factory for generic affine sections.

For a non-generic affine section, Theorem 6.1 (physical pp. 24–25) perturbs the affine right-hand side through generic sections. There are only finitely many partition sets, so a subsequence has a fixed partition family; taking its polynomial limit preserves the vector identity. The resulting race is positive and terminates for `x∈P∩(0,1)^n`. It need not terminate at boundary points where some coordinates are deterministic. The perturbation is part of the construction; applying the generic formula directly to the unperturbed partition family is explicitly warned to fail in examples.

Section 6.1 (physical pp. 25–27) recovers Sampford sampling for the k-subset polytope. For noninteger `α`, the vertices have `⌊α⌋` coordinates 1 and one fractional coordinate; for integer k, limits from `k−ε` and `k+ε` produce two different Sampford variants. These are interior factories and do not terminate at k-subset vertices. Appendix E (physical pp. 38–42) proves a narrower impossibility statement: if `1<k<n−1`, no *strong* k-subset factory with the source's uniform exponential-convergence property exists. The definition on physical p. 38 requires one `c<1` working uniformly over every depth and every `x∈S`; Theorem E.1 uses this uniform notion. The chain is Lemma E.4 (each finite-depth output polynomial contains the vertex-support product), Lemma E.5/Corollary E.6 (derivatives at other vertices vanish), Lemma E.7 (differentiable strong factories are impossible by differentiating the mean identity at a vertex), and Theorem E.10 (uniform exponential convergence implies differentiability using a multivariate polynomial derivative bound). This does not rule out pointwise strong factories with parameter-dependent exponential-tail constants, nor prove every strong factory impossible.

## Transfer limits

- The output is a vertex-valued vector with mean x; it is neither a scalar `f(p)`-coin nor a general random variable with arbitrary target law.
- For generic sections, Theorem 5.1 gives the source's uniform strong-factory property. For non-generic sections, Theorem 6.1 guarantees only pointwise a.s. termination on the open-coordinate domain `P∩(0,1)^n`; do not transfer the generic uniform tail property to it.
- The Sampford result excludes deterministic endpoints; the exponential-tail impossibility is restricted to `1<k<n−1` and does not exclude all strong procedures.
- The race/decision-tree construction uses external randomness for coefficient selection and finite combinatorial choices. A biased input coin cannot be silently assumed to supply endpoint-uniform fair bits.
- For each fixed interior `x`, the finite polynomial race has positive success probability and a bounded maximum input-toss count per round. The resulting pointwise geometric tail gives all moments for that construction. On a compact interior subset where its continuous success probability is positive, the minimum is positive and the same argument gives a derived uniform geometric bound. These consequences do not cover external-randomness costs or arbitrary factories; keep the uniform-strong and pointwise-only theorem scopes above distinct.
