## Construction and bounds

For a power-series function `f(p)=1−Σ_{k≥1}c_k(1−p)^k`, with `c_k≥0`, `Σc_k=1`, define

`d_k = c_k/(1−Σ_{j<k} c_j)`.

At stage `k`, use a fresh `p`-coin `X_k` and independent uniform `U_k`; let `V_k=1[U_k<d_k]`. Stop and output `X_k` if either is 1, otherwise continue. Proposition 1 (PDF pp.3–4) verifies `c_k=d_k Π_{j<k}(1−d_j)`; Theorems 1–2 (pp.4–5) give exact output `Ber(f(p))`, `E_p N=f(p)/p`, and an exponential tail. For `f(p)=p^a`, `0<a<1`, this gives `d_k=a/k` and `E_pN=p^{a−1}`.

**Theorem 3 (PDF p.6; proof §7 pp.13–15).** For a differentiable `f:S→(0,1)` on an open set `S⊂(0,1)`, any possibly randomized Bernoulli factory that is **fast in the paper’s exponential-tail sense** satisfies

`E_p[N] ≥ (f'(p))² p(1−p)/(f(p)(1−f(p)))`.

The argument views the output as a sequential estimator, represents randomized input by `Z_i=X_i+U_i`, computes the Fisher information per observation as `1/[p(1−p)]`, and applies Wolfowitz’s sequential Cramér–Rao inequality under the theorem’s sequential-estimator regularity conditions. The stopping-time count is the number of input observations as defined by the paper.

**Theorem 4 (PDF p.7; proof §7.7, pp.17–18).** Let `S⊆(0,1)` be open with `0` as a limit point, and let differentiable `f:S→(0,1)` satisfy condition (12), namely `f'(p)=Ω(f(p)/p)` as `p↓0`. Any possibly randomized Bernoulli factory for `f` that is fast in the paper's sense (condition (1), inherited from Nacu–Peres) satisfies `E_p[N]=Ω(f(p)/p)` as `p↓0`. The theorem explicitly says condition (11), `f(p)/p→∞`, is **not** required. The proof inserts the derivative lower bound into Theorem 3's sequential Cramér–Rao inequality (10), obtaining `E[N]≥C² f(p)(1−p)/(p(1−f(p)))`, then bounds `(1−p)/(1−f(p))` below by a positive constant for sufficiently small `p` to conclude the `Ω(f(p)/p)` bound. It also notes that without (11) the conclusion may be uninformative; this does not add (11) to the theorem's hypotheses.

**Corollary 1 (PDF p.7).** For functions representable by (2)–(4) satisfying both (11) and (12), Algorithm 1 is order-optimal up to constants among fast factories. This corollary's asymptotic-optimality comparison uses (11) and (12); Theorem 4 itself uses only (12). “Order-optimal” does not assert that the ratio to the optimal expected cost tends to 1. Neither result covers arbitrary a.s.-terminating factories or classifies finite-`r` moments.

## Full paper map and limits

- §§1–2, PDF pp.1–5: model, algorithm, coefficient relation, exactness, expected count and exponential tail.
- §3, pp.5–8: continuity of expected runtime and lower bounds; Theorems 3–4.
- §4, pp.8–9: a nonrandomized fair-bit implementation and its cost.
- §§5–6, pp.9–10: power-series transformations and conclusions/open problems.
- §7, pp.10–21: full proofs, including continuity, sequential Cramér–Rao, and comparison to the construction.

**C204 boundary.** This yields a one-sided expected-input lower bound in a specified class under fastness. It neither proves nor disproves a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for pointwise finite higher moments. Do not drop the fastness or asymptotic hypotheses.
