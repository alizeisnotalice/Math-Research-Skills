## Model, notation, and theorem

The model permits tosses of both an unknown-bias `p`-coin and a fair coin, with a random stopping time. The paper's rate index is the paired input stage: when the `p`-coin has been tossed `n` times, it also uses `n` fair-coin tosses (PDF pp.2–4). At the open-problem statement (PDF p.28), `N` is described as the number of tosses of both kinds of coins. Do not silently relabel this as a p-coin-only count; if a task counts primitive tosses or only unknown-p tosses, state the convention and convert using the implementation's per-stage cost.

Let

`Δ_n(x) = max{sqrt{x(1−x)/n}, 1/n}`.

For noninteger `α>0`, `C^α[0,1]` means `r=⌊α⌋` derivatives with the `r`th derivative Hölder of order `α−r` (Definition 5, PDF p.5). The square root in `Δ_n` was confirmed visually on rendered PDF pp.2 and 5; the extracted text drops it.

**Theorem 8 (PDF p.6).** For `f:[0,1]→(0,1)` and noninteger `α>0`, `f∈C^α[0,1]` iff it can be simulated on all of `[0,1]` at the rate `P_p(N>n)=O((Δ_n(p))^α)`, uniformly in `p`, with the Bernstein-envelope conditions from Result 3. Because this paper adds a fair coin at each input stage, the integrality condition is Result 3(ii′): `binom(n,k)a(n,k)` and `binom(n,k)b(n,k)` must be integer multiples of `2^{-n}` (PDF p.4). This is distinct from the pure p-coin condition (ii), which requires integers. The theorem is not stated for integer `α`.

The rate is boundary-aware. At each fixed interior `p∈(0,1)`, `Δ_n(p) ≍ n^{-1/2}`, so the tail is `O(n^{-α/2})`; at an endpoint, `Δ_n=1/n`, giving the faster scale `O(n^{-α})`. The constant in the theorem is uniform over the parameter.

## Exact proof chain

1. **Reduction to consistent Bernstein-positive approximants (§2, pp.2–6).** Result 3 (inherited from Nacu–Peres) equates an exact factory to nested lower/upper Bernstein polynomials with coefficient constraints and `h_n−g_n=P_p(N>n)`. Lemma 9 fills gaps between `b`-adic degrees by multiplying by `(x+(1−x))^{n−n'}`; the Bernstein coefficients become convex combinations, preserving bounds and consistency.
2. **Necessity (§3, pp.7–10).** From the consistent lower approximants, form increments `G_n=g_{2^{n+1}}−g_{2^n}`. Then `G_n` is Bernstein-positive and `f=ΣG_n`. Lorentz’s Bernstein-positive derivative inequality (Result 11, p.7–8), under its doubling assumption on the majorant, bounds derivatives by `|(G_n)^{(j)}(x)|≤C(Δ_{2^n}(x))^{α−j}`. Split the derivative difference at scale `N` satisfying `Δ_{2^{N+1}}(x)<|x−y|≤Δ_{2^N}(x)`; low scales use the next derivative and high scales use direct derivative bounds. Geometric summation gives the Hölder modulus for `f^{(r)}`. At integer order this argument yields only the Zygmund second-difference necessity in Theorem 13 (pp.9–10), not sufficiency.
3. **Sufficiency machinery (§4, pp.10–18).** Lorentz operators `Q_{n,r}` reproduce polynomials through degree `r` (Lemma 14); their correction coefficients `τ_j` obey recurrence (29), and derivative bounds for Bernstein basis functions and `τ_j` (Lemmas 17–18) feed simultaneous approximation (Lemma 19) and derivative bounds (Lemmas 20–22). These locate the technical constants and boundary control; the proof is not merely the classical unconstrained Jackson approximation theorem.
4. **Iterative consistent construction (§5, pp.18–24).** Choose a geometric subsequence `n∈Λ={b^m n_0}` and set `f_{n_0}=Q_{n_0,r}f`, `f_n=f_{n/b}+Q_{n,r}(f−f_{n/b})`. Steps 1–4 (pp.20–22) control derivatives, the `C^α` norm, approximation error, and Bernstein coefficients strictly inside `(0,1)`. Then add/subtract a positive Bernstein majorant `D B_n φ_n`, with `φ_n=θ_α n^{−α}+[x(1−x)/n]^{α/2}` (equation (47), p.22). Lemma 25 and coefficient comparison (pp.22–24) yield ordered lower/upper approximants with gap `O(Δ_n^α)`; Lemma 9 fills all degrees. This proves sufficiency.
5. **Counterexample and integer boundary (§6–7, pp.24–28).** The authors construct a positive-coefficient approximation counterexample to Lorentz’s claimed converse using Gaussian convolutions/Poisson summation (pp.24–27); mere positive approximation is not enough—the consistency/series structure is essential. For integer `α`, Theorem 13 proves a Zygmund-type necessary condition, while Conjecture 34 is explicitly conjectural (p.28).

## C204: a printed tail identity caveat

On PDF p.28, the authors pose the finite-`α`-moment problem and print `E_p[N^α] (= Σ_{n≥1} n^α P_p(N>n)) < ∞`. The equality in parentheses is not the standard tail-sum identity as printed. For integer-valued `N≥0`, the standard identity is `E[N^α]=Σ_{n≥0}((n+1)^α−n^α)P(N>n)` (with the appropriate endpoint convention); for example, if `N=2` surely and `α=1`, the left side is 2 while the printed weighted sum is 1. This exact local calculation is recorded in `experiments/exact_checks.out`. The intended moment-finiteness question remains meaningful, but this skill does not propagate the printed identity as an exact formula. Any condition based on the alternate weighted sum must be treated as a different tail-moment condition and checked with the authors/reviewer.

For fixed interior `p`, Theorem 8 gives a uniform-in-parameter tail of order `n^{−α/2}`, so it implies finite `r`th moments when `r<α/2` by the standard tail formula. At `p=0,1`, the bound uses the faster endpoint scale, but the worst-case interior governs a uniform-in-`p` moment claim. It does not settle the endpoint moment exponent or characterize all functions with pointwise finite `r`th moments. Thus it is strong partial evidence for C204, not a complete answer.
