## Exact Barker and portkey factories (§§1–4, physical pp.1–10)

For current/proposed states `x,y`, Barker’s acceptance probability is

`α_B(x,y)=π(y)q(y,x)/[π(x)q(x,y)+π(y)q(y,x)]`.

Assume local factorizations `π(x)q(x,y)=c_x p_x` and `π(y)q(y,x)=c_y p_y`, with known positive scaling bounds `c_x,c_y` (possibly only known up to the same normalizing constant) and accessible coins `p_x,p_y∈(0,1)`. Algorithm 2 (p.5) chooses a known branch with probability `c_y/(c_x+c_y)`, flips `p_y` and accepts on heads; on the other branch it flips `p_x` and rejects on heads. Tails restart. Theorem 1 (p.5) states that the modified acceptance probability

`α(x,y)=π(y)q(y,x)/[π(x)q(x,y)+π(y)q(y,x)+d(x,y)]`

is π-reversible exactly when `d(x,y)=d(y,x)`. The two-coin loop count is geometric with success probability `(c_xp_x+c_yp_y)/(c_x+c_y)` and mean `(c_x+c_y)/(c_xp_x+c_yp_y)`; loose local upper bounds can make it expensive.

For any fixed user-selected `0<β≤1`, Algorithm 3 (p.7) adds an independent known `Bern(β)` “portkey” gate; its failure immediately rejects. Theorem 2 (p.6, proof on p.6) verifies exactly the acceptance probability

`α_(β)(x,y)=π(y)q(y,x)/[π(x)q(x,y)+π(y)q(y,x)+(1−β)(c_x+c_y)/β]`.

For each fixed `β<1`, the number of iterations is geometric with success probability `s_β=(1−β)+β(c_xp_x+c_yp_y)/(c_x+c_y)≥1−β`. Thus this loop count has all finite moments, uniformly over local biases for that fixed β. Each iteration uses a bounded number of coin opportunities, so that opportunity count also has a geometric tail. This does not bound the primitive cost of generating `p_x`/`p_y` coins if they are themselves factories; charge those nested costs separately. At `β=1`, the construction reverts to the original two-coin loop and loses the bias-uniform geometric bound.

Theorem 3 (p.8) proves `α_(β)≤β α_B`; Peskun ordering and the cited asymptotic-variance ordering give the stated variance comparison. Theorem 4 (p.8) provides a reverse bound only if both input biases have a common lower bound `p_x,p_y>δ>0`; it must not be used without this condition. Section 4 flips the two-coin construction using lower bounds expressed as `π(x)^{-1}q(x,y)^{-1}=\tilde c_x\tilde p_x`; Theorem 5 (p.10) gives the exact flipped acceptance probability and Theorem 6 the corresponding variance comparisons. Rendered p.10 confirms the denominator and reciprocal-bias factors.

## Applications and limits (§5, physical pp.10–19; end matter pp.20–22)

The examples use model-specific local bounds in a Weibull-mixture posterior, a constrained correlation-matrix problem, and a Wright–Fisher diffusion. They illustrate when an upper- or lower-bound coin can be generated efficiently. The empirical run-time and autocorrelation plots are demonstrations, not theorem-level uniform complexity claims. The remaining pages give acknowledgments and references; the 22-page file has no omitted proof appendix.

- The output is a Barker/modified-Barker accept event inside a specified MCMC transition, not an arbitrary scalar `f(p)` output.
- For fixed `β<1`, the portkey loop/opportunity clock has a geometric tail and all moments; primitive costs of simulating local coins remain separate and may depend on their biases.
- The reverse comparison in Theorem 4 requires both `p_x,p_y>δ`; Theorem 3 does not.
- Mixing/asymptotic-variance improvements and computational cost are different criteria; the paper explicitly trades acceptance probability for cheaper factory loops.
