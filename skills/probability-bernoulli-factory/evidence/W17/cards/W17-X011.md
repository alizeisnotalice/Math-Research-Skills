## Exact statements and cost convention

The input consists of unknown-bias p-coin flips plus auxiliary randomness; costs count input coin flips. For `C>0` known and `r=Cp≤M<1/2`, Theorem 1 (PDF p. 5) produces an exact `Cp` coin with expected flips at most

`C/((1−2M)(1+Cp)) + Cp · [15.2 C/(1−2M+Cp)]`.

The multivariate Theorem 2 (same page) treats `r=Σ_i C_i p_i≤M<1/2`, with `C=Σ_i C_i`, and counts total flips from among the `k` input-coin types. **Its literal second term contains the undefined symbol `Cp`**, even though no scalar `p` is defined in this theorem. The source’s §5 construction independently generates Poisson processes at rates `C_i`, so its stated route requires `C_i≥0` and `C=Σ_i C_i>0`. With `p_eff=r/C∈[0,1]`, the rate mixture is the single-coin `Cp_eff=r` construction. Applying Theorem 1 to that mixture gives the derived bound

`C/((1−2M)(1+r)) + r · [15.2 C/(1−2M+r)]`.

This is the proof-supported interpretation under the nonnegative-rate assumptions, not the formula literally typeset in Theorem 2. Preserve the source artifact; do not silently report that the printed formula has `r` in this term. Do not apply either bound outside the stated known upper bound `M<1/2`.

For larger `Cp`, the paper’s recursive algorithm gives an upper bound of `7.57 C ε^(−1)` when `Cp≤1−ε` is known. The abstract describes the small-mean cost as roughly `C` and says the first-order lower-bound optimality is **conjectured**, not proved for random stopping factories.

## Construction and proof chain

- **Small means (§2, physical pp. 6–9).** Superpose a rate-`C` Poisson process generated by thinning p-coin-driven events with an independent rate-one process. The first event type yields a logistic Bernoulli coin with mean `r/(1+r)`. Lemmas 1–3 establish the logistic output law, its expected input-flip count, and the recursive small-`r` factory.
- **Large means (§3, pp. 9–18).** A gambler’s-ruin walk creates a coin with mean `r(1−r^(m−1))/(1−r^m)` and cost at most `C(m−1)` (Lemmas 4–5). A high-power logistic construction (Lemma 6) and recursive `p′` coin (Lemma 7) feed the linear factory. The algorithm chooses `m=ceil(4.5/ε)+1` and `β=1+1/(m−1)` (p. 14). Lemma 8 bounds expected recursive calls by `1.4`, using a `2/7` generation contraction; Lemma 9 proves exactness by truncating recursion and letting the truncation grow. Lemma 10’s heading says `7.67 C/ε`, but its proof sums to `469/62≤7.57 C/ε`; the abstract and introduction also state `7.57`. Preserve this internal numerical typo transparently and use the proof’s final sum as the supported constant.
- **Lower-bound discussion (§4, pp. 18–19).** A deterministic-sample unbiased estimator variance argument yields `n≥C(1−p)/(1−Cp)` in its fixed-n model. The authors explicitly state that this does not prove the random-stopping Bernoulli-factory lower bound; it is evidence for their conjecture.
- **Multivariate extension (§5, pp. 19–20).** Poisson thinning among the k coin types yields the stated linear combination target, with total flip cost. References occupy p. 21.

## Use and limits for C204

This is an explicit expected-cost algorithm for linear `Cp` and a stated multivariate extension. It is auxiliary evidence about the `r=1` cost scale; it is neither a function-class characterization nor a theorem that the conjectured `C` lower bound holds for arbitrary random stopping factories.
