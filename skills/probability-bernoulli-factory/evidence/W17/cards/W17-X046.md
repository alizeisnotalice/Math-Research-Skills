## Main result and proof mechanism

The paper concerns approximation of a nonnegative smooth function by Bernstein-type polynomials with nonnegative coefficients; it is not a random algorithm. For a scalar coordinate, let `δ_n(x)=sqrt(x(1−x)/n)` and `Δ_n(x)=max{1/n,δ_n(x)}`. Lemma 1 (physical p.3) improves the centered absolute binomial moment estimate to

`T̄*_{n,s}(x) ≤ A_s δ_n(x)^{2∧s} Δ_n(x)^{(s−2)∨0}`,

with listed constants `A_0=A_1=A_2=1`, `A_3=2`, `A_4=4`; equality holds for `s=0,2`. The zero factor at `x=0,1` for `s≥1` is the boundary improvement over the earlier Lorentz estimate.

Theorem 2(i) (p.3; proof pp.4–5) gives, for `f∈C^(r)[0,1]^d`, `r=0,1`, the multivariate Bernstein error bound

`|f(x)−B_n^f(x)| ≤ (d+1) ω^(r)(max_j δ_{n_j}(x_j)) (Σ_j δ_{n_j}(x_j))^r`.

Theorem 2(ii) (statement p.3; proof pp.4–6) fixes `r≥0`, a positive lower bound `m>0`, and derivative bounds `M_i`; for `f∈Λ_r^(d)(m,M_r)` it constructs positive-coefficient polynomials `P_n` of degree vector `n` satisfying

`|f(x)−P_n(x)| ≤ C_{r,d} ω^(r)(D_n(x)) D_n(x)^(r−2) [Σ_j δ_{n_j}(x_j)]²`,

where `D_n(x)=max_j Δ_{n_j}(x_j)` and `C_{r,d}` depends on the stated class parameters and dimension. The class requires `m≤f≤M_0` and continuous partial derivatives through order `r` with the specified uniform derivative bounds.

The proof starts with a Taylor correction `Q^f_{n,r}` to the product Bernstein operator (equations (5)–(9), pp.4–6). Taylor’s remainder is controlled by the modulus of continuity of the order-`r` derivatives and products of centered binomial moments; Lemma 1 supplies the boundary-sensitive `δ² Δ^(s−2)` factors. An induction on `r` controls the corrected approximation error. The strict lower bound `f≥m>0` then allows degree elevation by `r` to ensure all coefficients are positive; the approximation estimate transfers to the elevated polynomial. The theorem is an existence/result for positive **real** coefficients and an error estimate, not a finite-depth coin-event criterion.

## Relevance boundary

This paper is auxiliary background for A202’s positive-polynomial theme. Its coefficients are neither required to be integers nor shown to satisfy the finite-event unnormalized Bernstein inequalities `0≤a_{N,j}≤binom(N,j)`. It gives no exact Bernoulli factory, no least event depth, and no bound for A202’s maximum degree. Do not turn approximation error or positive real coefficients into an admissible finite coin-flipping event without a separate exact coefficient conversion.
