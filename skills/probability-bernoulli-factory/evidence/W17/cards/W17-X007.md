## Model and construction ( §§1–3.4, physical pp.1–14)

The paper states the scalar setting for `p∈(0,1)` and extends it to rational probability maps on the open simplex. Theorem 3.1 (physical p. 9) is stated for every rational map `f:Δ_m→Δ_v`. Its finite ladder definition (Definition 2.2, p. 5), however, requires a common polynomial denominator with no zero on the **closed** simplex, and the proof (Appendix B, pp. 28–30) uses a fixed finite aggregation of ladder states. The R02 source-level adjudication found that the open-simplex theorem hypotheses do not ensure this representation: for `f(p)=(p₀/(p₀+p₁),p₁/(p₀+p₁))` on open `Δ₂`, two interior paths to `(0,0,1)` give first-coordinate limits `1/2` and `1/3`; a finite aggregate of closed-nonvanishing ladder coordinates would extend continuously. It also found the Appendix B Pólya lemma's open-simplex positivity hypothesis insufficient for its coefficient-positivity conclusion. Therefore use this conversion only for a verified closed-positive-denominator/nonnegative-representation subclass. This bounded finding does not disprove rational-factory existence (the displayed ratio has a simple geometric rejection factory), nor does it invalidate chain/CFTP results once a valid ladder is supplied. See `evidence/shared/reviews/R02/w17_x007_adjudication.md`.

A multivariate ladder has states whose probabilities are positive-coefficient monomials over a common denominator, `π_i(p)=R_i p^{n_i}/C(p)`, with a common total degree and no denominator roots on the closed simplex. For a target where the required positivity/decomposition and closed-denominator conditions have been checked, the proof route of Theorem 3.1 is: (1) write each rational coordinate over homogeneous numerator/denominator polynomials; (2) take a common denominator and expand same-degree nonnegative monomials; (3) make one state per monomial and group states by a fixed partition; and (4) augment and thin (Proposition 2.8) until the ladder is fine and connected. Aggregation recovers `f(p)`. The argument must not use positivity only on the open simplex as a substitute for the stronger coefficient/closed-boundary conditions.

Given a fine connected ladder, the paper builds a reversible chain with transition entries `P_ij=V_ij W_ij` off the diagonal, where `W_ij=p_b` when die face `b` permits the move and zero otherwise. The iterative `V` construction caps outgoing probabilities while preserving the ladder ratios; Proposition 3.4 proves row stochasticity and detailed balance, and Proposition 3.5 proves Peskun maximality among reversible chains with the same adjacency and stationary law. Proposition 3.6 defines an update map using a die-face draw `B~p` and an independent `U~Unif(0,1)`; CFTP then gives an exact stationary draw. For a univariate ladder the chain is nearest-neighbor and Corollary 3.7 gives a monotone update, so only the bottom and top chains need to be tracked.

## Exact-sampling cost and its clock/randomness boundary ( §§3.5, 4–5, physical pp.14–24; Appendix A, pp.24–27)

Theorem 3.9 (p.15; proof pp.32–35) states that every finite-support integer-valued `W` becomes strictly log-concave after adding an independent `Bin(n,1/2)` for some finite `n`. The proof factors the probability generating polynomial into real linear and irreducible quadratic factors, proves eventual coefficient positivity/log-concavity after binomial smoothing, and combines factors using preservation of log-concavity. It gives existence of `n(W)`, not a useful uniform or explicit degree bound.

Proposition 3.10 (pp.15–16; proof pp.36–37) bounds the number `N` of CFTP iterations/input-die draws for a multivariate ladder after degree elevation, under its nonempty-face set `E`; its displayed bound depends on the ladder constant `a` and the face probabilities `p_b`. For `m=1`, Corollary 3.11 (p.16) gives an explicit ladder disaggregation with

`E[N] ≤ min{(((a p)^(-2(k−1))−1)/(1−a p)), (((a(1−p))^(-2(k−1))−1)/(1−a(1−p)))} ≤ (((a/2)^(-2(k−1))−1)/(1−a/2)`,

where `a=min_i{R_{i−1}/(R_{i−1}∨R_i) ∧ R_i/(R_{i−1}∨R_i)}∈(0,1]`. This is the source's uniform-in-`p` expected count for the specified CFTP input-toss/iteration convention, not a uniform exponential-tail claim. Proposition 3.12 (p.16; proof pp.37–38) gives `P(N≥n)≤(k−1)ρ(p)^n` and `E[N]≤(k−1)ρ(p)/(1−ρ(p))` for a strictly log-concave univariate ladder, with `ρ(p)∈(0,1)` computed from adjacent transition probabilities. This geometric-tail rate is parameter-dependent; do not call it uniform in `p`.

There is a further implementation boundary. The update map uses an independent uniform variate. Appendix A.1 (pp.24–26) shows how to synthesize fair bits and a categorical uniform draw from the input die: compare two iid die outcomes, discard ties, use the comparison as a fair bit, then refine a binary expansion until the target CDF interval is resolved. The CFTP bounds above count `N` input-die updates; an implementation that charges the die draws used to create each auxiliary uniform must add that cost. For a binary `p`-coin, the comparison subroutine uses two input tosses per pair and succeeds with probability `2p(1−p)`, so its mean input-toss cost per fair bit is `1/[p(1−p)]`; this diverges near 0 or 1. Thus the paper’s stated uniform bound on `N` alone does not establish a uniform bound for the composed die-only implementation when all auxiliary-randomness tosses are charged. Algorithm 4’s uniform-refinement loop has `P(L>l)≤k2^(−l)` for a `k`-outcome known categorical law, but the cost of its fair bits still needs conversion.

Examples 3–6 (pp.18–23) relate the construction to polynomial targets, faster ladders, the logistic factory `Cp/(1+Cp)` (Example 5 gives its expected `p`-coin toss count `C/(1+Cp)`), and the multivariate Bernoulli race. These are algorithm-specific examples, not a general moment theorem. The independent-coin Bernoulli-race example transforms `m` input coins into an `(m+1)`-face die by a random index selection; it remains a multivariate access model.

## Proof coverage and transfer limits

Appendix B proves connectedization (Proposition 2.8, p.27), the positive-coefficient denominator lemma and Theorem 3.1 (pp.28–30), the chain and Peskun statements (Propositions 3.4–3.5, pp.30–31), the update-map/coin case and log-concavity results (pp.32–35), the CFTP cost bound (Proposition 3.10, pp.36–37), and the coupling/log-concavity tail arguments (Proposition 3.12 and Lemma 3.14, pp.37–38). The appendices and references finish on pp.38–40.

- Theorem 3.1's literal open-simplex rational-map statement is broader than the closed-denominator ladder representation justified by the displayed definition/proof route. Until reconciled, restrict this source's rational-map conversion to verified closed-positive-denominator and nonnegative-representation cases. This does not refute existence of factories for the excluded boundary-singular maps.
- The per-parameter geometric tail in Proposition 3.12 does not give a uniform tail or higher-moment bound. Corollary 3.11 is a separate expected-count estimate for a degree-elevated scalar ladder.
- Distinguish CFTP update/coin counts from the extra die cost needed to generate internal uniforms in a die-only implementation.
- Theorem 3.9 is qualitative and does not supply an effective sharp augmentation degree.
