## Machine model, exactness and strong realizability (§1, physical pp.1–4)

A Buffon machine is a finite-control/register computation whose random input is an iid stream from a Bernoulli generator `ΓB(λ)` with unknown parameter `λ`; it outputs a Bernoulli variable with the prescribed parameter `φ(λ)`. Definition 2 (physical p. 3) calls a function **realizable** when the machine's coin count has finite expectation and **strongly realizable** when that count has an exponential tail, with constants allowed to depend on `λ`. Strong realizability implies finite expectation pointwise, but the definitions do not assert uniform constants over `λ`.

Theorem 1.1 (p.4) states: (i) every rational-coefficient polynomial mapping `(0,1)` into `(0,1)` is strongly realizable by a finite graph; (ii) every rational-coefficient rational function mapping `(0,1)` into `(0,1)` is strongly realizable by a finite graph. Its proof specifies **unbounded-precision arithmetic**. The preceding computationally-unrestricted-device phrase belongs to cited computable-Lipschitz/analytic results, not this finite-graph theorem. The theorem does not provide an optimal stopping-tail exponent.

## Discrete distributions and Buffon-number constructions (§§2–4, physical pp.4–11)

Section 2 defines the von Neumann scheme based on a class of permutations and the exponential generating function of the class. Theorem 2.1 (pp.5–6) gives the exact law of the random integer `N` returned by the scheme and analyzes three permutation classes: all permutations lead to a geometric law, sorted permutations to a Poisson law, and cyclic permutations to a logarithmic-series law. Its cost statement distinguishes the random value `N`, the number of iterations, and `C`, the scheme’s flip count excluding calls to the external `λ`-Bernoulli generator. For the analyzed scheme, `C` has the displayed probability generating function and exponential tails. Do not count this as an exponential tail for total input `λ`-coin tosses unless the excluded generator calls are also bounded. The proof sketch conditions on the order type of a vector of iid uniforms and derives the probability generating function for `C`.

Section 3 builds machine constructions for special numbers using composition and integration identities, including Buffon machines for `π`-related constants and classical series. Theorem 4.1 (p.9) states that if `φ(λ)` is realizable, then its integral `Φ(λ)=λ^{-1}∫_0^λ φ(w)dw` is realizable by adding a geometric bag; integration from zero also preserves realizability. Theorem 4.2 (p.9) gives strong realizability of `log(1+x)`, `arctan(x)`, `½ arcsin(x)`, and `∫_0^x e^{-w²/2}dw` for `0≤x<1`, with different bag/stack/register needs. The constructions use composition, complement, geometric randomization and exact integration; the constants/number machines are examples, not a new general cost theorem. The appendix gives a source listing for a `π/4` generator, and the final pages discuss limitations and open directions.

## Transfer limits

- The theorem’s input is a `λ`-coin stream and its output is a Bernoulli event. Buffon-number algorithms may call submachines repeatedly; distinguish the outer input-flip count from internal generated values.
- “Strongly realizable” is pointwise in `λ` under the paper’s definition; it does not assert uniform tail constants or uniform moments over `(0,1)`.
- The machine’s polynomial/rational existence theorem is not a finite fixed-depth event theorem and does not provide the sharp A202 degree maximum.
- Do not infer finite `r`-th stopping moments from a pointwise exponential-tail statement without keeping the source’s parameter dependence and exact cost counter explicit.
