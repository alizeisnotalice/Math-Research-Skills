## Quantitative Pólya certificate (Theorem 1, physical pp. 1–4)

Let `f` be a homogeneous form of degree `d` in `n` variables, written in multinomial-normalized form
`f(X)=Σ_{|α|=d} c(α)b_α X^α`, where `c(α)=d!/α!`. Put `L=max_{|α|=d}|b_α|` and `λ=min_{x∈Δ_n}f(x)`, with `Δ_n={x_i≥0, Σx_i=1}`. If `λ>0` and the integer `N` satisfies

`N > d(d−1)L/(2λ) − d`,

then every coefficient of `(X_1+…+X_n)^N f(X)` is strictly positive.

The proof connects each coefficient to a perturbed evaluation of `f` on the simplex. For a coefficient indexed by `β` with `|β|=N+d`, normalize by the positive multinomial factor and set `y=β/(N+d)`, `t=1/(N+d)`. The normalized coefficient is the falling-factorial perturbation `f_t(y)`. The multinomial Vandermonde–Chu identity collapses the full-degree normalization to the common factor `∏_{j=0}^{d-1}(1−jt)`. Comparing each falling-factorial product with its ordinary monomial gives a uniform perturbation bounded by the coefficient scale `L` and the degree loss `d(d−1)t/2`. The displayed strict bound on `N` makes that error smaller than `λ`; hence `f_t(y)>0` at every lattice point indexing a coefficient, proving positivity of all coefficients.

The paper’s example `f(x,y)=x²−(2−δ)xy+y²` has simplex minimum `λ=δ/4` and `L=1` for `0<δ≤2`. In this range it is strictly positive on the simplex; the theorem gives the sufficient condition `N>4/δ−2`. The authors also give a sharper exact coefficient criterion for this example; the sufficient theorem bound should not be described as sharp.

## Extension to polynomials positive on polyhedra (Section 3, physical pp. 4–7)

The paper applies the simplex result to a polynomial strictly positive on a compact polyhedron described by affine inequalities. Theorem 2 (physical p. 4) assumes that the polyhedron has nonempty interior; retain this hypothesis with the representation statement. It expresses such a polynomial as a finite sum of products of the defining nonnegative affine forms with nonnegative coefficients. The proof route is: map the polyhedron into a simplex, homogenize the target polynomial, use the affine-kernel relations to add a sum-of-squares correction that makes the lift strictly positive on the simplex, apply the quantitative simplex certificate, and pull the resulting nonnegative-coefficient identity back through the affine map. The perturbation vanishes on the original affine slice, so the identity restricts to the desired polyhedral representation. The algorithmic discussion uses Gröbner-basis/kernel computation and an optimization step to select the correction; those are computational inputs, not a universal complexity bound for arbitrary polyhedra.

## Correct use and limits

- This is an algebraic positive-coefficient certificate. It can support a proof that a polynomial admits a Bernstein-style positive expansion when strict positivity and the relevant compact simplex/polyhedron hypotheses hold.
- It is not a randomized algorithm and does not provide Bernoulli-coin access, a sampling procedure, a stopping-time bound, or an endpoint extension.
- The theorem requires a positive minimum `λ>0`; a polynomial that vanishes on the boundary is outside the stated strict-positivity hypothesis.
- The 2000 author manuscript is the exact local version read; the journal pagination is recorded only as bibliographic correspondence.
