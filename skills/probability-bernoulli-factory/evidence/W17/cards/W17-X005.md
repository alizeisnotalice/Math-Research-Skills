## Relevant definitions and exact results

Let a finite event be an event whose occurrence is determined by the first `N` coin tosses. Its least such `N` is its event degree. A “coin-flipping polynomial” is the probability of a finite event; its coin-flipping degree is the least event degree realizing it.

**Lemma 4.1 (PDF pp.16–17).** A function is the probability of a finite event of degree at most `N` iff

`f(x) = Σ_{i=0}^N a_i x^i(1−x)^(N−i)`, with integers `0 ≤ a_i ≤ binom(N,i)`.

The coefficients count the accepting length-`N` strings with exactly `i` heads. Conversely, selecting `a_i` such strings for each `i` constructs the event. The degree-`N` unnormalized Bernstein coefficients are uniquely determined by `f` (Lemma 4.3, pp.17–18), so this is also an exact finite-`N` test.

**Theorem 4.5 (PDF pp.18–20).** A function is the probability of some finite event iff it is an integer-coefficient polynomial that is identically 0, identically 1, or maps `(0,1)` into `(0,1)`. Proof: elevate a degree-`d` integer polynomial to degree `N`; its unnormalized Bernstein coefficient is

`a_{N,j} = Σ_{i=0}^d a_{d,i} binom(N−d,j−i)`.

If for arbitrarily large `N` some coefficient is negative or exceeds `binom(N,j)`, take a subsequence with `j/N → ξ`. For interior `ξ`, the normalized coefficient tends to `f(ξ)` (equations (14)–(17)); for endpoint `ξ=0` or `1`, the first nonzero endpoint coefficient gives a nearby interior value outside `[0,1]`. Replacing `f` by `1−f` handles the upper violation. Thus mapping the open interval strictly into itself forces all degree-elevated coefficients into the admissible range for some finite `N`.

**Theorem 4.6 (PDF pp.20–21).** For each fixed polynomial degree `d`, only finitely many coin-flipping polynomials have degree at most `d`. The coefficient vector lies in the unit ball of the norm `||(a_0,…,a_d)|| = max_{x∈[0,1]} |Σ a_i x^i|`; this ball is compact and contains finitely many integer lattice points. Consequently the maximal coin-flipping degree `N(d)` is finite for each fixed `d`.

## Numerical claim versus theorem

The manuscript says it “seems” that `N(3)=46`, and reports `N(4)≥735` from the polynomial `f(x)=13x(1−x)^3−21x²(1−x)^2+14x³(1−x)`. These are computationally phrased claims, not the content of Theorem 4.6. The local exact-arithmetic check in `experiments/exact_checks.py` degree-elevates the unnormalized degree-4 coefficient vector `(0,13,−21,14,0)` and finds the first admissible degree is 735: degree 734 fails and 735 passes. This verifies this example’s lower bound; it does not find or prove the sharp value of `N(4)`, nor any sharp general upper bound. Output is in `experiments/exact_checks.out`.

## Scope for A202 and limits

This is direct evidence that A202’s fixed-degree maximum is finite, and it gives a computable exact test for a proposed polynomial and degree. It does not provide a sharp closed formula or a practical sharp upper bound for general `N(d)`. The results concern **finite deterministic-depth events**, whereas the general Bernoulli-factory problem allows a random stopping time that is merely almost surely finite. Do not identify these two models.
