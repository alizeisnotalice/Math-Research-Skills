## Stability condition and main entropy theorem

For a distribution Sigma on permutations of n elements, Definition 1.3 (PDF p. 3) calls Sigma epsilon-stable if there is one ordering x1,...,xn such that for every i<j,

\[
\Pr_{\pi\leftarrow\Sigma}[\pi(x_i)<\pi(x_j)]\ge \frac12+\epsilon.
\]

The consistency of all pairwise biases with this same ordering is an essential hypothesis. Theorem 1.4 states that the maximum-entropy distribution Sigma* satisfying these pairwise margin constraints obeys

\[
H_2(\Sigma^*)\le (1-C\epsilon^4)n\log_2 n,
\]

for an absolute constant C (PDF p. 3). The displayed theorem has no additive -O(n) term. In the poset application noted by the authors, the uniform distribution on linear extensions must first be shown to satisfy the same epsilon-stability condition; only then does the entropy bound imply e(P) at most (n!)^(1-C epsilon^4).

## Proof structure: local tests to global entropy

The paper encodes a distribution as a vector over permutations and organizes pairwise marginal constraints into offline tests (Sections 3–4, PDF pp. 5–11). For disjoint sets S,T in the ordered domain, the test counts inversions across S,T and passes when the count is at most (1-epsilon/2)|S||T|. By the stable marginal bounds and linearity of expectation, Lemma 4.3 gives each fixed test probability Omega(epsilon) of passing.

For epsilon=2^(-ell), the proof recursively splits the domain into halves U,V and the range into 2^(ell+1) blocks. It measures how strongly the preimage of each range block is biased toward U or V. Lemma 4.5 lower-bounds cross-inversions in terms of this bias; Claim 4.6 shows that a passed test forces at least one range block to be nontrivially biased. The tests are chosen offline so that later tests depend only on the collapsed relative orders inside U and V, not on earlier test outcomes.

The test outcomes form a recursive vector r(pi), with potential phi=c(n)^T r. Theorem 4.10 bounds the number of permutations with a given outcome vector in terms of this potential. Lemma 4.12 uses the Omega(epsilon) pass probability to show that a nontrivial fraction of Sigma's mass is on high-potential (“good”) permutations. A union bound over outcome vectors then yields Theorem 4.13 and the main (1-C epsilon^4)n log n entropy reduction. This argument controls the maximum-entropy law under the stated marginals; it does not bound every distribution lacking the consistent ordering.

Claim 4.14 is a separate lower-bound construction, but its printed statement and proof do not match. The claim heading says H_epsilon >= (1-epsilon)n log n-O(n). Its proof mixes a uniform permutation with the identity using weights 1-2epsilon and 2epsilon, which gives the stated pairwise bias 1/2+epsilon but only proves H(Sigma) >= (1-2epsilon) log(n!) = (1-2epsilon)n log n-O(n). That calculation does not establish the stronger printed coefficient 1-epsilon. Record the construction and this weaker bound; do not repeat the stronger coefficient as proved. Neither lower-bound expression is an extra term in Theorem 1.4.

## Transfer limit

This is an entropy theorem for distributions on permutations under globally consistent pairwise comparison margins. It gives a poset #LE bound only when the uniform linear-extension law is proved epsilon-stable in that precise sense. It provides no such stability proof for free-boundary square labelings conditioned on exactly two peaks.
