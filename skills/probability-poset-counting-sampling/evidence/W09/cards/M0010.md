## Results and locations

- Theorem 1, General Path Coupling (PDF p. 3), treats a finite one-coordinate-update chain on the full product space `C^V`, with a unique stationary distribution. It couples chains along Hamming paths and bounds their expected distance from the one-step bounds for pairs differing at one coordinate. If the resulting contraction factor is below 1, total variation distance is bounded by the initial Hamming diameter times that factor to the power `t`.
- Theorem 2, Metropolis Path Coupling (PDF pp. 3–5), extends the argument to a constrained state space `R ⊆ C^V` for Metropolis updates, using minimum transition distance. Its nonexpanding case additionally requires a positive lower bound on the probability that the coupled distance changes; the proof derives the corresponding diameter-based polynomial bound.
- Applications occupy PDF pp. 5–8. They are analyses of the specific colouring, orientation, SAT, and independent-set chains stated there, not a theorem about arbitrary constrained subsets.

## Proof chain and assumptions

For Theorem 1, join arbitrary endpoints by a Hamming path whose successive states differ at one site. Use maximal couplings for the single-site update laws on each adjacent pair. The expected endpoint distance is bounded by summing the adjacent-pair bounds along the path; the coupling lemma converts contraction to a total-variation mixing bound. The chain must have the stated product state space, update structure, and stationary law. Theorem 2 changes both the state-space and metric hypotheses and cannot be invoked from Theorem 1 alone.

## Use and transfer limit

This supplies a proof method for a proposed local-move chain only after its target stationary law, legal state space, coupling, and contraction are established. It proves no mixing result on the exact-two-peak square-labeling space.
