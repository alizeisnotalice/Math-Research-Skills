## Result and location

The paper defines a peak at `v` when `deg_G(v)≥2` and `ℓ(v)>ℓ(w)` for every neighbor `w` (PDF p. 1). Its Theorem 2.6 (PDF p. 4) says `GraphPeakSetAlgorithm(G,S,L,P)` outputs the set `P(S,G,L)`, where `V_{<2}(G)⊆L⊆V(G)` and `P(S,G,L)` consists of labelings whose peak set `S'` satisfies

`S ⊆ S' ⊆ S ∪ (L \ N_G(S))`.

To enumerate exactly `P(S;G)`, initialize `L=V_{<2}(G)`. Then the only allowed peak set is `S` itself, so `P(S,G,L)=P(S;G)` (Definition 2.5, PDF p. 4). The theorem is a recursive construction of the actual labeling set; counting that output gives the exact count.

## Proof chain and assumptions

At each call the current largest label `|V(G)|` is assigned either to a required peak `v∈S` or to a permitted candidate `v∈L\N_G(S)`. The algorithm removes `v`, assigns the top label, and updates `L` to `(L∪N_G(v))∩(V(G)\{v})`; if `v∈S`, it also removes `v` from `S` (Algorithm 1, PDF p. 4). Lemmas 2.7–2.8 prove the two inclusions by induction on the number of vertices (PDF pp. 5–7): deleting a chosen vertex can create peaks only among the tracked allowance set, and reinserting its largest label restores precisely the permitted peak-set range. The update to `L` is part of the state: deletion changes vertex degrees and can create new leaves or isolated vertices, so the recursive `L` cannot be dropped from the theorem or implementation.

For the exact-set call use `L=V_{<2}(G)` at the initial graph and retain the recursive update. On a free-boundary `n×n` square with `n≥2`, every initial vertex has degree at least two, hence initial `L=∅`; later recursive calls still update `L` as vertices are removed. The paper reports that its implementation is prohibitively slow on dense graphs above about ten vertices (PDF p. 9); it does not provide a polynomial-time bound for the general construction.

## Use and transfer limit

This gives the exact graph-neighborhood predicate and an exact finite enumeration method that can check small free-boundary square instances. It proves no asymptotic peak-count law, concentration result, or large-square sampling guarantee. Keep the square's actual boundary graph and dynamically updated recursion state when using it for A022.
