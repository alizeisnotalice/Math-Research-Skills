# M0016: A random polynomial-time algorithm for approximating the volume of convex bodies

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Authors: Martin Dyer, Alan Frieze, Ravi Kannan; 1991.

Evidence: read the complete 17-page local scan and a complete searchable copy, PDF pp. 1–17. The scan and public copy agree on the title, authors, and pagination.

## Theorem 1: grid-walk mixing, not the volume algorithm

Theorem 1 (PDF p. 5) is a bound for the technical grid walk. For its finite, irreducible, aperiodic, symmetric transition matrix $P$, whose stationary distribution is uniform on its states, it states for every pair of states $i,j$ and every integer $t\ge 0$:

\[
\left|p_{ij}^{(t)}-\pi_j\right|\le\left(1-\frac{1}{10^{17}n^{19}}\right)^t.
\]

The states are grid cubes that *weakly intersect* a slight enlargement $K(\alpha)$ of a well-rounded convex body. The technical walk uses self-loops and symmetric facet transitions (attempt each of the $2n$ facets with probability $1/(4n)$, with an additional probability $1/2$ of making no move) so the transition matrix is symmetric and has a uniform stationary law. The bound is a coordinatewise convergence statement for this chain. It is not the volume-approximation theorem and does not, by itself, state an algorithm for a discrete poset event.

## The volume approximation guarantee

The randomized polynomial-time relative-error volume guarantee appears in the abstract and Introduction (PDF pp. 1–2), and is established by the algorithm and analysis in Sections 2–3 (PDF pp. 3–7), not by Theorem 1. In the paper's oracle model, the input is a closed, bounded, full-dimensional convex body with a well-guaranteed membership oracle: the input includes an outer ball containing the body and an inner ball of positive radius contained in it, as well as membership queries. The paper uses the oracle framework of Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver to obtain a weak separation oracle and an affine transformation that rounds the body to contain the unit ball and lie in a concentric ball of radius $r=(n+1)\sqrt n$. For dimension $n\ge3$ and requested relative error $0<\varepsilon<1$, it gives an estimate within relative error $\varepsilon$ with probability at least $3/4$, in time polynomial in $n$ and $1/\varepsilon$, counting oracle calls as unit time. Independent repetitions and a median reduce the failure probability.

The proof has distinct links. First, the affine rounding bounds the aspect ratio. Next, the algorithm estimates a product of volume ratios along nested dilates/intersections between a known-volume ball and the target body. Each trial runs the technical walk on the preceding body, then chooses a point uniformly from a finite fine grid inside the selected cube and tests membership in the preceding and next bodies. The isoperimetric argument in Section 4 (PDF pp. 8–10) bounds conductance: a geometric boundary estimate for the smoothed convex body is transferred to grid edges, first for the natural walk and then for the technically enlarged state space. Theorem 2 (Sinclair–Jerrum; PDF p. 8) turns that conductance bound into rapid mixing. Finally, the Section 3 sampling estimates and concentration inequalities control each ratio estimate and their product. Theorem 1 supplies the mixing input to this chain of arguments.

## Relevance and transfer limit

The Introduction explicitly notes that volume approximation also gives an approximation to the number of linear extensions through order-polytope volume (PDF p. 2; see Lovász, cited there). That is a continuous-volume approximation route under the paper's oracle and rounding framework. It does not directly provide an exact sampler for labelings of a finite free-boundary square conditioned on having a specified peak set: the encoding, oracle, approximation-to-counting reduction, and conditioning step each require their own proof. In particular, this result supplies no torus or Eden-model conclusion for A022.
