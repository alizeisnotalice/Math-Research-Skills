# M0012: Fast Perfect Sampling from Linear Extensions

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Author: Mark Huber; 2006.

## Reading evidence

Read all 9 pages of the journal article. Bounding-chain construction and complete coupling: PDF pp. 3–5; coalescence estimate: Theorem 5, pp. 6–7; general CFTP correctness: Theorem 8, pp. 7–8; expected running-time bounds: Lemma 10, PDF p. 8.

## Results and locations

For a fixed finite poset, the Karzanov–Khachiyan adjacent-swap chain has the uniform linear-extension law as its stationary distribution. Theorem 3 constructs a bounding chain on inverse-permutation positions; its state invariants certify which linear extensions remain possible. The complete coupling is non-Markovian because the update coin for the original chain depends on the current bounding state, although the bounding process itself evolves Markovianly.

Theorem 5 bounds the chance that the bounding chain has not collapsed after a specified number of chain steps; in particular, its coalescence time is `O(n³(log n+log(1/δ)))` for failure probability `δ`. Theorem 8 is a general correctness result: if some finite horizon has a positive probability of making the coupled map constant at every larger horizon, recursive non-Markovian CFTP terminates almost surely and returns an exact stationary sample. It is not the paper's expected-runtime theorem. For the linear-extension algorithm, Lemma 10 gives expected running time at most `4.3 n³ ln n` for recursive horizon doubling; it also gives a fixed-horizon variant with expected time at most `1.8 n³(ln n+1)`. These bounds count transitions of the specified chain.

## Proof chain and assumptions

The bounding chain tracks upper bounds on inverse positions and preserves injectivity and poset-order invariants. A sine-potential estimate for the motion of holes gives Theorem 5's collapse tail. The CFTP procedure reuses the same random updates when horizons are enlarged; the non-Markovian theorem proves exactness under its positive-constant-map condition. The expected-time claim then follows by integrating the tail estimate in Theorem 5, as done in Lemma 10.

## Use and transfer limit

The target is uniform sampling from one fixed poset's linear extensions. A set of square labelings with exactly two graph peaks is not thereby a linear-extension space, and this result supplies no sampler or running-time bound for that target.
