# W09-X007: A Scalable Scheme for Counting Linear Extensions

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Authors: Topi Talvitie, Kustaa Kangas, Teppo Niinimäki, Mikko Koivisto; IJCAI 2018.

## Reading evidence

Read the complete 7-page IJCAI paper. The relaxation Tootsie Pop counting result is Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3); the continuous chain and stationarity result are Theorem 2 (PDF p. 4); monotonicity and exact perfect sampling are Lemma 1 and Theorem 3 (PDF p. 5). Experiments and scope limits are on pp. 5–6.

## Results and locations

The Relaxation Tootsie Pop algorithm gives an `(ε,δ)`-approximation to `ℓ(P)` using calls to a generalized-order-constraint sampling subroutine (Theorem 1). Its sampling component is an **exact** sampler on the continuous set

`Ω = {x∈[0,1]^n : x_i−x_j≤s_ij for all i<j}`, with `s_ij≥0`.

The continuous-relocation chain resamples one coordinate uniformly in `[0,1]` and rejects a proposed point outside `Ω`; Theorem 2 proves the uniform distribution on `Ω` is stationary. Lemma 1 proves coordinatewise monotonicity of the update. Theorem 3 then proves the monotone CFTP sampler returns an exact uniform point of `Ω` in finite expected time. The paper does not give a useful general polynomial upper bound on that expected time.

Thus the **count estimate is approximate**, while the auxiliary continuous sample is **exact**. The sampler is not described as approximate sampling from the discrete set of linear extensions.

## Proof chain and assumptions

The chain's proposal is symmetric; rejecting invalid coordinate updates preserves uniform measure on `Ω`. The all-zero and all-one states are extremal for coordinatewise order. A shared sequence of coordinate/time updates preserves this order, and the CFTP endpoints coalesce; a prescribed finite update pattern has positive probability, proving finite expected termination. The Tootsie Pop count guarantee separately depends on the stated nested-set and sampling-subroutine conditions.

## Use and transfer limit

This paper provides a pattern for exact sampling from a continuous auxiliary relaxation combined with approximate counting. Its theorem does not sample uniformly from arbitrary peak-conditioned graph labelings, nor does it provide an exact count of linear extensions.

## Revision response (R05)

Corrected the former claim that the paper samples approximately from the discrete linear-extension set. Separated approximate counting (Theorem 1) from exact uniform sampling on the continuous constraint region (Theorems 2–3 and Lemma 1), and replaced the inaccurate pop/unpop proof sketch with the continuous-relocation/CFTP construction.
