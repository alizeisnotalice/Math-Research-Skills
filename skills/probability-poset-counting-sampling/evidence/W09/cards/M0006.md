# M0006: Two poset polytopes

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Author: Richard P. Stanley; *Discrete & Computational Geometry* 1 (1986), 9–23.

## Reading evidence

Read the complete 15-page article. The order and chain polytope definitions are in §§1–2 (PDF pp. 1–6); the transfer map is Theorem 3.2 (p. 6); the Ehrhart argument is Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 (pp. 7–8); the separate triangulation appears in §5 (pp. 9–10). Sections 6 and the references are on pp. 10–15.

## Result and location

For a finite `n`-element poset `P`, the order polytope `O(P)` consists of order-preserving functions `f:P→[0,1]`; the chain polytope `C(P)` consists of `g(x)≥0` with `Σ_{x∈C}g(x)≤1` for every chain `C` (Definitions 1.1 and 2.1, PDF pp. 2 and 4). Theorem 3.2 (p. 6) gives a continuous piecewise-linear bijection `φ:O(P)→C(P)` and shows that `mf(x)` is integral for every `x` iff `mφ(f)(x)` is integral for every `x`.

Theorem 4.1 (PDF p. 7) proves the Ehrhart identity

`i(O(P),m)=i(C(P),m)=Ω(P,m+1)`,

where `Ω(P,m+1)` counts order-preserving maps into `{0,1,...,m}`. Since this degree-`n` polynomial has leading coefficient `e(P)/n!`, comparing leading coefficients gives Corollary 4.2 (PDF p. 7):

`vol(O(P))=vol(C(P))=e(P)/n!`.

Here `e(P)` is the number of linear extensions of `P`.

## Proof chain and assumptions

The proof of Theorem 4.1 first identifies lattice points in `mO(P)` with order-preserving maps into `{0,...,m}`. The transfer-map integrality equivalence in Theorem 3.2(b) then gives the same lattice-point count for `mC(P)`. Ehrhart theory says that the leading coefficient of each full-dimensional `n`-polytope's Ehrhart polynomial is its volume; the leading coefficient of the order polynomial is `e(P)/n!` (Theorem 4.1 and its preceding paragraph, PDF p. 7). This is the article's route to Corollary 4.2.

There is a distinct geometric proof for `O(P)` in §5 (PDF pp. 9–10): its canonical triangulation has one maximal simplex for each linear extension, and each simplex has volume `1/n!`; hence `vol(O(P))=e(P)/n!` directly. The transfer map sends this triangulation to one of `C(P)`, but the article's Corollary 4.2 proof for equality of the two volumes is the Ehrhart/transfer-integrality argument above; do not describe the map as globally volume-preserving.

## Use and transfer limit

This converts a fixed poset's linear-extension count to an exact polytope volume and applies to conjunctions of order constraints. An exact-two-peaks graph event is a disjunction of local inequalities; it is not one order polytope without a disjoint decomposition or additional state accounting.
