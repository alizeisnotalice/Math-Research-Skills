## Exact blocking expansion

For a partition $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ with two rows, $P_\lambda$ is its cell poset; its linear extensions are standard Young tableaux of shape $\lambda$. For incomparable cells $\alpha,\beta$, define $e(P_\lambda;\alpha<\beta)$ as the number of extensions placing $\alpha$ before $\beta$. Theorem 2.9 characterizes blocking ideals $T$: $T$ is an ideal avoiding $\alpha,\beta$ such that $T\cup\{\alpha\}$ is an ideal, equivalently it contains the fixed part $A_P(\alpha)=\Lambda_P(\alpha)\setminus\{\alpha\}$ and is contained as an ideal in $D_P(\alpha,\beta)=P\setminus(V_P(\alpha)\cup V_P(\beta))$. Equivalently, $T=A_P(\alpha)\cup W$ with $W$ an order ideal in the variable part $G_P(\alpha,\beta)=D_P(\alpha,\beta)\setminus A_P(\alpha)$ (PDF pp. 6–7).

Corollary 2.7 gives the exact decomposition

\[
e(P;\alpha<\beta)=\sum_{T\in BI_P(\alpha<\beta)}e(T)\,e\bigl(P\setminus(T\cup\{\alpha\})\bigr).
\]

For the two-row case, Theorem 4.2 lists the blocking ideals and resulting skew-tableau sums (PDF pp. 14–15). If $\alpha=(1,a)$ and $\beta=(2,b)$ with $1\le b<a\le\lambda_1$, then $BI_P(\alpha<\beta)=\{(a-1,t):0\le t\le b-1\}$ and

\[
e(P_\lambda;\alpha<\beta)=\sum_{t=0}^{b-1}f^{(a-1,t)}f^{\lambda/(a,t)}.
\]

If $\alpha=(2,a)$ and $\beta=(1,b)$ with $1\le a<b\le\lambda_1$, then $BI_P(\alpha<\beta)=\{(t,a-1):a\le t\le b-1\}$ and $e(P_\lambda;\alpha<\beta)=\sum_{t=a}^{b-1}f^{(t,a-1)}f^{\lambda/(t,a)}$. Here $f^\mu$ and $f^{\lambda/\mu}$ count standard tableaux of straight and skew shape, respectively; hook-length and Jacobi–Trudi–Aitken formulas make these sums explicit.

## Limit theorem and its scope

Theorem 4.7 is only for the specific pair $\alpha=(1,a)$, $\beta=(2,1)$ with fixed $a\ge2$ (PDF p. 18). If $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\to\infty$ while $\lambda_1-\lambda_2$ stays bounded, then

\[
\Pr_{P_\lambda}(\alpha<\beta)\longrightarrow \frac{a+1}{2^a}.
\]

In the different regime with $\lambda_2$ fixed and $\lambda_1\to\infty$, the same probability tends to $1$. These are distinct shape limits; the paper explicitly notes that other growth directions can give different limits.

## Transfer limit

These are pairwise-order probabilities for uniformly random linear extensions of two-row partition posets, derived through standard-tableau counts. They are not local-peak probabilities for uniformly labeled free-boundary squares. Neither the blocking expansion nor the stated limit yields the exact-two-peak count or conditional sampler for A022.

Stated the blocking-ideal definition/structure and exact expansion with assumptions, and gave the two distinct two-row cases in Theorem 4.2. Corrected Theorem 4.7's locator to physical PDF p. 18 (p. 21 is Theorem 4.11), restricted it to the actual fixed pair $(1,a),(2,1)$, and recorded both shape regimes and the exact bounded-difference limit $(a+1)/2^a$.
