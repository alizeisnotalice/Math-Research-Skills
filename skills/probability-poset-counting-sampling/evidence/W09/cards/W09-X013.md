## Representation and input class

The paper counts linear extensions of a finite N-free poset P, meaning its diagram contains no copy of the four-element N configuration (PDF pp. 2–3). Theorem 1.1 says this is equivalent to the diagram being the line digraph of a directed acyclic graph. In the resulting arc diagram A(P), vertices of P are represented by arcs of the DAG; consecutive arcs encode a cover relation. The arc diagram is treated both as a DAG and, after taking transitive closure, as a poset. The paper assumes that P and A(P) are available. When the representation is required to have a unique source and sink, that normalization makes it unique (PDF p. 3).

## Exact active-set dynamic program

Fix a linear extension L0=(x1,...,xn) of P. Let Pi be the induced subposet on x1,...,xi, and let the arc representing xj be (v_j^-,v_j^+). The active set at the cut after xi is

\[
A_i=\{v:\exists j'\le i<j''\text{ with }v_{j'}^+=v=v_{j''}^-\}.
\]

Thus an active DAG vertex has a processed arc entering it and an unprocessed arc leaving it. For each map f from A_i to [i], z_i(f) counts the linear extensions L of Pi in which, for every active vertex v, the last processed element whose arc ends at v occupies position f(v) (PDF p. 7). This boundary information suffices because the predecessors of the next arc xi are exactly the arcs entering v_i^-.

To extend a state f at level i-1, insert xi after t old elements. The prose states the legal range as f(v_i^-)\le t<i: all predecessors must remain before xi, and the insertion position is otherwise unrestricted. The update f'=Gamma_i(f,t) shifts positions after the insertion and records xi as the last arc entering v_i^+:

\[
f'(v)=
\begin{cases}
\max(f(v),t)+1,&v=v_i^+,\\
f(v),&v\ne v_i^+\text{ and }f(v)\le t,\\
f(v)+1,&v\ne v_i^+\text{ and }f(v)>t.
\end{cases}
\]

Initialize z_1(f_0)=1 with f_0(v_1^+)=1, and all other states zero. For each legal insertion, add z_{i-1}(f) to z_i(Gamma_i(f,t)). The paper describes updating the active set by removing v_i^- once all its incident elements are processed and adding v_i^+ when xi is its first processed incident element. The recurrence is exact: deleting xi from an extension of Pi gives an extension of P_{i-1} and a unique insertion slot t; the state map preserves the last-hit positions needed at the next cut. At i=n the active set is empty and z_n(empty)=e(P).

Two indexing/domain details need explicit handling in an implementation. With t defined as the number of old elements before xi, the p. 7 prose has t<i, while the final loop line prints t<=i; the former is consistent with the old extension having i-1 elements. Also, when a tail has no processed predecessors or a head is newly added to the active set, the corresponding threshold or last position is not an old f-value. These cases use the vacuous lower bound t>=0 and the new head position t+1, respectively. These are boundary cases of the recurrence, not extra theorem hypotheses.

## Complexity and the width/spread theorem

If all active sets for the chosen L0 have size at most alpha, the direct state bound gives at most n^alpha maps per level and at most n insertion slots, hence O(n^(alpha+2)). The authors improve this by observing that each relevant state with a nonempty active set has some active v with f(v)=i, reducing the number of relevant maps per level to at most alpha*n^(alpha-1), and obtain O(n^(alpha+1)) time (PDF pp. 8–9). An empty active set contributes its single empty map. Proposition 3.2 says bounded-activity counting is efficient if a linear extension L attaining the minimum activity

\[
\alpha(P)=\min_L\alpha(L)
\]

is supplied. The authors explicitly say they do not know how to compute alpha(P) (PDF p. 8).

For a constructive bound, define the spread of a DAG as the maximum difference in lengths of two internally vertex-disjoint directed paths with the same endpoints, with spread zero when no such pair exists (PDF pp. 8–9). Lemma 3.3 orders DAG vertices topologically by nondecreasing rank (longest-path distance from the source), then orders the corresponding outgoing arcs in blocks. It shows that the resulting linear extension L_T has

\[
\alpha(L_T)\le \operatorname{width}(A(P))\bigl(\operatorname{spread}(A(P))+2\bigr).
\]

The proof partitions DAG vertices into rank antichains S_j, each of size at most the DAG width. For an active vertex of rank k at a cut whose current arc starts at rank j, the construction gives a second path to it of length at most j+1. Clipping both paths at their last common vertex gives internally disjoint paths whose length difference is at least k-j-1. Thus active vertices can occur only in S_j,...,S_{j+s+1}, at most width(A(P))*(s+2) vertices. Theorem 3.4 then yields

\[
e(P)\text{ is computable in }O\!\left(n^{k(s+2)+1}\right)
\]

when P is N-free and its arc diagram has width at most k and spread at most s (PDF pp. 9–10). For fixed k and s this is polynomial time; since they occur in the exponent, the displayed bound is XP-style rather than an FPT bound when k and s vary with the input. Width alone is insufficient: the paper gives width-one examples with activity growing linearly (PDF p. 8).

## Transfer limit

This is an exact counting algorithm for the restricted N-free class, with a supplied-order activity bound or the joint arc-diagram width/spread bound. It does not give an algorithm for arbitrary posets, and does not establish that the free-boundary square peak-constrained labelings form an N-free poset with bounded width and spread. No A022 count or sampler follows without proving such a representation and its parameter bounds.
