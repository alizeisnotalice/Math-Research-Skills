## Result and locations

Shearer’s inequality is **Lemma 2.8**, not a theorem (physical PDF p. 7; printed p. 5). If `F` is a family of subsets of `[n]` (repetitions allowed) and every coordinate belongs to at least `t` members, then for any random vector `(X₁,...,Xₙ)`,

`H(X₁,...,Xₙ) ≤ (1/t) Σ_{F∈𝓕} H(X_F)`.

The combinatorial version is Lemma 2.9 (physical PDF p. 8; printed p. 6): for any family `𝓐` of subsets of `[n]`, `|𝓐| ≤ ∏_{F∈𝓕}|trace_F(𝓐)|^(1/t)`. The first conditional version, Lemma 2.15 (physical PDF p. 11; printed p. 9), conditions both sides on an auxiliary random variable `Z`; Lemma 2.16 (physical PDF p. 12) gives Kahn’s stronger conditional inequality with the specified partial order and conditioning on coordinates below each `F`.

## Proof chain and assumptions

Fix the coordinate order `1,...,n`. For each `F={i₁<...<i_k}`, the chain rule gives `H(X_F)=Σ_j H(X_{i_j}|X_{i_ℓ}, ℓ<j)`. Dropping conditioning yields `H(X_F)≥Σ_j H(X_{i_j}|X₁,...,X_{i_j−1})`. Summing over `F`, each coordinate’s chain-rule term occurs at least `t` times, so `Σ_F H(X_F)≥t H(X₁,...,Xₙ)`; divide by `t` (proof in §2.8, physical PDF p. 10; printed p. 8). Repetitions in `𝓕` count as distinct members in this multiplicity condition.

For Lemma 2.9, choose `X` uniformly from `𝓐`, use `H(X)=log|𝓐|` and `H(X_F)≤log|trace_F(𝓐)|`, apply Lemma 2.8, and exponentiate (physical PDF p. 8; printed p. 6). Later examples include Bregman’s permanent bound and coloring/homomorphism bounds; these are applications of the entropy inequalities, not peak-distance results.

## Use and transfer limit

Shearer supplies upper bounds on global entropy or family size from local marginals/traces. A peak-distance concentration claim still needs matching lower-count information and a strict entropy gap for the separated configurations; this tutorial does not establish that for the free-boundary square.
