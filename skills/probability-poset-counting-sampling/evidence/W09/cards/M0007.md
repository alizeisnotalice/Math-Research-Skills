## Results and locations

- Theorem 1 (print p. 230) proves that exact Linear Extension Count is #P-complete.
- Theorem 2 (print p. 230) states an FPRAS, explicitly credited to Dyer, Frieze, and Kannan's earlier convex-body-volume result. It is background, not a new result of this paper.
- Theorem 3 (print pp. 230–231) restates the Karzanov–Khachiyan almost-uniform sampler. Theorem 4 (print p. 232) estimates a comparison probability under its stated lower-bound condition. Theorem 5 (print pp. 232–233) gives the paper's explicit FPRAS by telescoping comparison probabilities along a sorting sequence.

## Proof chain and assumptions

For Theorem 1, the authors reduce #3SAT counting to an oracle for #LE using posets built from variables, clause gadgets, and auxiliary prime-sized antichains. Modular counts over selected primes recover the number of satisfying assignments; this is a Turing reduction, not a parsimonious reduction. Theorem 5 uses a sequence of posets formed by adding comparisons, estimates each conditional comparison probability with the sampler in Theorem 3, and multiplies the reciprocal estimates. Theorem 2's FPRAS attribution must remain with the earlier Dyer–Frieze–Kannan result.

## Use and transfer limit

This establishes the fixed-poset #LE complexity baseline and distinguishes exact hardness from randomized approximate counting. It supplies neither an exact count for the free-boundary two-peak square nor a sampler for that conditioned labeling space; a non-overcounting reduction to poset extensions would still be needed.
