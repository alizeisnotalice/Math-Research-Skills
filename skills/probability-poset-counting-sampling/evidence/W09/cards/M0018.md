## Stationary crater–permutation correspondence

For the stationary meteor process on a finite graph, Proposition 3.8(v) states that a vertex $v$ has zero mass at time 0 exactly when its last meteor time $T^v$ on $(-\infty,0]$ is the largest among $T^x$ for $x\in U(v)$, where $U(v)$ consists of $v$ and its neighbors (PDF p. 20). The independent rate-one Poisson clocks give i.i.d. exponential backward waiting times $-T^v$. Their ranks are a uniform permutation of the graph's labeled vertices. On the cycle $C_k$, a crater is therefore a cyclic local peak: $a_{j-1}<a_j>a_{j+1}$, with indices interpreted cyclically (PDF pp. 22–23). This identifies the stationary crater set in distribution; it is a model-specific cycle statement.

## Exact run probabilities and mound location

Theorem 4.1 (PDF pp. 22–23) gives two different noncrater-run events, which should not be conflated. In the stationary regime, $F_0^k=\{M_2=0\}$ has probability $1/3$ for $k\ge3$. For $n\ge1$ and $k\ge n+4$, the *maximal* run event

\[
F_n^k=\{M_i>0\ (3\le i\le n+2),\ M_2=M_{n+3}=0\}
\]

has probability $n(n+3)2^{n+1}/(n+4)!$. A second event, requiring only that $M_i>0$ for $3\le i\le n+2$ (without requiring the run to be maximal at its ends), has probability $2^{n+1}/(n+2)!$ for $n\ge1$, $k\ge n+3$.

Proposition 4.8(ii) (PDF pp. 28–30) conditions on the first, maximal-run event. There is a unique mound between the endpoint craters at 2 and $n+3$; if $R$ is its position, then for each $\varepsilon>0$ there are constants $c_1,c_2>0$ such that

\[
\Pr\!\left(\left|R/n-1/2\right|>\varepsilon\mid F_n^k\right)<c_1e^{-c_2n}
\]

for all $n\ge1$ and $k\ge n+4$. The proof counts the two-peak/one-valley permutation patterns and applies Stirling bounds to the binomial coefficient; the exponential rate is for fixed $\varepsilon$, with constants depending on it.

## Two other cycle results

Theorem 4.6 (PDF p. 28) fixes a finite family of local patterns $\mathcal A$ and counts its occurrences $N$ among the $k$ cycle sites. It asserts constants $\mu,\sigma>0$ for which $(N-k\mu)/(\sigma\sqrt{k})$ converges in distribution to the standard normal as $k\to\infty$. In the proof, indicators are functions of i.i.d. exponential ranks in a window whose length is bounded by the longest pattern; indicators at separation greater than that bound are independent. The finite-range process satisfies the cited mixing CLT, and deleting the bounded cyclic boundary changes the count by at most that window size.

Theorem 4.9 (PDF pp. 30–32) is a fixed-count, large-cycle separation result. For any fixed $n\ge2$, any $p<1$, and any $r>2$, for all sufficiently large $k$ the conditional probability exceeds $p$ that the largest consecutive-crater gap divided by the smallest is at most $r$, given exactly $n$ craters. The analogous statement holds for consecutive crater–mound gaps given exactly $n$ craters and mounds. Its proof uses Proposition 4.8(ii) to rule out a mound far from the midpoint of a long gap, then sums over pairs of sites. It does not assert a Poisson limit or independence of crater locations.

## Transfer limit

These are exact or asymptotic results for a stationary meteor-driven mass process on the periodic one-dimensional cycle. The rank representation can provide a cyclic peak-statistics comparison, but none of these results gives peak-set probabilities for a uniformly random labeling of the free-boundary square in A022. In particular, do not transfer the cycle topology, meteor dynamics, fixed-count gap separation, or CLT to the square without a new coupling/counting argument.
