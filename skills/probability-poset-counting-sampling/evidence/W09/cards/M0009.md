## Result and location

Theorem 1 (PDF p. 5) states that CFTP returns almost surely and its output has the unique stationary law for a finite ergodic chain, under the paper's random-map construction (PDF pp. 3–5). For a chain with transition probabilities `p_{ij}`, each time step uses a random map `f_t`; in the construction for Theorem 1, `f_t(i)` is generated by a separate call to the one-step transition routine for each state `i`, and maps at distinct times are independent. Compose maps from the past toward time zero and stop when the composition is constant.

Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 5–6) permits a general update `f_t(i)=φ(i,U_t)` with the correct one-state transition marginals, but explicitly **assumes almost-sure eventual coalescence** of the random-map composition; Theorem 3 further permits time-dependent updates preserving `π` and has the same coalescence premise. These are not guarantees that every coupling of an ergodic chain coalesces.

## Proof chain and assumptions

For Theorem 1, finite irreducibility and aperiodicity give an `L` for which every transition probability `P^L(i,j)` is positive. With the independently generated statewise random maps used there, a block of `L` maps has positive probability of sending every initial state to the same state, hence of being constant. Disjoint blocks are independent, so some past block is constant almost surely. The output law is stationary: the limiting output at time zero is one transition of the corresponding limiting output at time `−1`, and the two have the same law by time-shift invariance; uniqueness identifies this law with `π` (proof, PDF p. 5).

An arbitrary common-randomness coupling need not have this coalescence property. For example, if each update map is a random permutation of the state space, every composition remains bijective and can never be constant. A different coupling is valid only if its own almost-sure coalescence is established, as required by Theorems 2–3.

In monotone CFTP (PDF pp. 7–8), if all update maps preserve a partial order with bottom and top states, their trajectories bound every trajectory, so coalescence of those two certifies a constant composition. The algorithm doubles the lookback horizon and **reuses exactly the same random map at each already assigned time** whenever the horizon is extended; resampling old maps changes the joint construction and can bias the result (PDF pp. 5–8). The order-ideal application is uniform sampling from `J(P)`, whose elements correspond bijectively to antichains (PDF pp. 10–11).

## Use and transfer limit

This supplies exact stationary sampling once the target chain, update maps, and coalescence condition are proved. It does not by itself supply a chain or exact sampler for labelings conditioned on exactly two peaks, nor a useful runtime bound for the A022 square target.

## Revision response (R05)

Re-read the complete searchable 27-page article and corrected the overbroad implication that ergodicity plus any shared random-map coupling guarantees termination. Theorem 1's proof relies on the independently generated random maps from the paper's construction, which give a positive chance of a constant block; Theorems 2–3 require almost-sure coalescence as an explicit hypothesis. Added the random-permutation counterexample, stated the past-map reuse rule, and separated correctness/termination from monotone coupling and runtime results.
