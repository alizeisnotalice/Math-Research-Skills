## Result and location

The asymptotic regime fixes a finite connected simple graph `G` with positive edge lengths `ℓ(e)`, hence one compact real metric graph `S`; each edge `e` is replaced in `G_n` by `⌈nℓ(e)⌉` edges of length `ℓ(e)/⌈nℓ(e)⌉` (Section 3.2, PDF p. 6). The graph is not allowed to grow with the subdivision parameter `n`.

A flooding `f` grows by finitely many linear-rate arms until all edge length `ζ=Σ_eℓ(e)` is absorbed (Definition 3.1, PDF pp. 6–8). On an interval `[t_{k-1},t_k]`, arm speeds `z_j^k≥0` satisfy `Σ_j z_j^k=1`. With `0 log 0=0`, its entropy is

Writing `Δt_k=t_k-t_{k-1}`, equations (3.3)–(3.4) give the two equivalent forms

`β(f) = Σ_k [Δt_k log Δt_k − Σ_{j=1}^{m_k} z_j^k Δt_k log(z_j^k Δt_k)]`

`= −Σ_k Δt_k Σ_{j=1}^{m_k} z_j^k log z_j^k`,

using `Σ_j z_j^k=1` and `0 log 0=0` (PDF p. 8). The `Δt_k log Δt_k` term in (3.3) is essential when the first form is written using `log(z_j^k Δt_k)`; omitting it makes the value depend on how a constant-speed interval is partitioned.

`F_M` is the family with exactly `M` distinct source points `f(0)`; `F_{≤M}=⋃_{1≤K≤M}F_K`. The paper defines `β*(S,M)=sup_{f∈F_{≤M}}β(f)` and `F*_M={f∈F_{≤M}:β(f)=β*(S,M)}` (PDF pp. 10–11). Floodings are metrized by `dist(f,g)=sup_{t∈[0,ζ]} d_H(f(t),g(t))`, where `d_H` is Hausdorff distance between compact subsets of the metric graph with its shortest-path metric. The paper proves `F_{≤M}` compact and `β` continuous, so this supremum is attained.

For a labeling `L` of `G_n` with exactly `M` local maxima, set `R=L^{-1}` and `C_k=R({N_n,N_n-1,...,N_n-k})`, where `N_n=|V_n|`. The associated flooding `f_L` records the continuously growing subgraph occupied by this descending-label prefix: at the listed event times, include the peak locations and subdivided edges whose endpoints are both in `C_k`, and interpolate linearly between events (PDF pp. 10–11). Theorem 3.3 (PDF pp. 11–14) states that for every `ε>0`, for all sufficiently large `n`, under the uniform labeling law `P_M^n` conditioned on exactly `M` local maxima,

`P_M^n{ β(f_L) ≤ β*(S,M)-ε } < exp(-nε + 2(log n)^2)`.  (3.6)

This is a quantitative **entropy-value** lower-tail bound. The theorem does not give a metric-distance rate from `f_L` to the maximizer set. Its second assertion (3.7) controls a separate symmetric-difference discrepancy between the occupied vertex set and the descending-label prefix.

## Proof chain and assumptions

For a proposed flooding shape, each interval contributes multinomially many assignments of a consecutive block of descending labels among its growing arms. Stirling's formula gives log-count `nβ(f)+O(log n)`. The proof bounds the number of discrete arm/profile descriptions by a polynomial in `n`, giving an upper bound for the count with entropy at most `β*−ε`; a family approximating an entropy maximizer supplies a denominator lower bound of order `exp(nβ*+O(log n))`. The comparison yields (3.6), including the displayed `2(log n)^2` remainder (PDF pp. 12–14).

Here a peak is any vertex that is a strict local maximum relative to its neighbors (Section 2.1, PDF p. 2); unlike the convention in M0002, the definition does not exclude degree-one vertices. Theorem 4.6 (PDF pp. 19–20) concerns a maximizer with exactly `M` sources: for every `t<ζ` its flooded set has exactly `M` disjoint components, no degree-one vertices, and no loops; its sources are centroids of the tree metric components, with the stated arm-speed ratios. These are properties of maximizers on a fixed metric graph.

## Use and transfer limit

This supplies a fixed-metric-graph subdivision and entropy-counting framework. It does not apply directly to a sequence of growing free-boundary `n×n` square graphs: that changes the underlying graph and geometry rather than refining a fixed finite metric graph. Its metric-topology compactness, optimizer structure, and exponent do not prove A022's peak-distance law or any Eden-model claim.
