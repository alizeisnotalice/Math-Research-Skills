## Results and locations

For the free-boundary grid `G_{n,n}=P_n □ P_n`, Theorem 4.2 concerns **exactly one peak**. Put `f(n)=(log n)^(-1/2) log log n`; let `C_k` be the first `k` vertices in descending label order and `∂C_k` the exterior vertex boundary. The event `F` is that at least `f(n)n^2` indices `k∈[n^2]` satisfy

`|∂C_k| ≤ n^(2-f(n))`.

For all sufficiently large `n`, `P_1(F) ≤ exp(-½ n^2 (log log n)^2)` (PDF p. 6). The exponent on the right-hand side of the boundary threshold is `2-f(n)`.

For a `(d+1)`-regular rooted tree of depth `k`, Theorem 7.2 concerns the locations `K_1,K_2` of the largest and second-largest local maxima (PDF pp. 17–18):

- (a) If `d≥10` and `k≥2`, the joint location mass `P_2(K_1=y_1,K_2=y_2)` is maximized only when `y_1` is the root `v*`.
- (b) If `d≥3`, `k≥2`, and `m≥√(8k)`, then `P_2(min_i dist(K_i,v*)≥m) ≤ 8 exp(-(m/2) log(d-1))`.
- (c) For each `d≥3` there is `c_1>0` such that for every `k≥2` and `y_1,y_2` with `dist(y_i,v*)≥2`, `P_2(K_1=v*,K_2=y_1) ≥ c_1 P_2(K_1=v*,K_2=y_2)`.
- (d) For each `d≥3` there are `c,c'>0` such that for `k≥2` and `r≥2√(8k)`, `P_2(|dist(K_1,K_2)-k|≥r) ≤ 8 exp(-(r/4)log(d-1)) + c exp(-(r/2)log d) ≤ c' exp(-(√k/64)log d)`.

## Proof chain and assumptions

For Theorem 4.2, Step 1 constructs a large denominator for each fixed one-peak location. It fixes high labels on a sparse scaffold consisting of coordinate lines through the peak and lines spaced by `ℓ=⌊log n⌋`; each remaining horizontal segment is assigned monotonically toward the scaffold, preventing additional peaks. The resulting log-count is at least `2n² log n - O(n² log log n)` (PDF pp. 6–7). Step 2 counts possible connected growth sequences with many indices having boundary at most `n^(2-f(n))`: at each such index there are at most `n^(2-f(n))` next-vertex choices, while the other indices have at most `n²`; summing over index sets gives log-count at most `2n² log n - n²(log log n)² + O(n²)`. Dividing by the denominator yields the displayed probability estimate. The boundary threshold is `n^(2-f(n))`, not `n²-f(n)`.

The regular-tree result is a separate family and its dimension/range conditions are part of each conclusion: part (a) needs `d≥10`; parts (b)–(d) need `d≥3`, with the additional `k,m` (or `r`) conditions above. The paper's introduction poses a two-peak question on a two-dimensional torus; Theorem 4.2 is instead a one-peak theorem on a free-boundary square.

## Use and transfer limit

The free-square result proves a single-peak level-boundary estimate. The tree results concern a fixed-depth regular tree. Neither theorem establishes the distribution of the two peak locations on a growing free-boundary square in A022. The torus motivation and tree estimates cannot be transplanted to that graph family.
