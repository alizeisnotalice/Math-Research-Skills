## Result and location

For a fixed set of indices `S={i_1<...<i_s}` that is admissible (there exists a permutation with this peak set), Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2) gives

`|P(S;n)| = p(S;n) 2^(n-|S|-1)`,

where `p(S;n)` is an integer-valued polynomial of degree `max(S)-1` for nonempty `S` (degree 0 for `S=∅`). For nonempty fixed `S`, the formula is stated for `n>max(S)`; the minimum `n` for which `S` is admissible is `max(S)+1`. Peak positions are indices `2,...,n-1`; the endpoints are not peaks.

Theorem 2.2's proof (PDF pp. 3–4) gives the recursive construction. Write `m=max(S)`, `k=m-1`, `S_1=S\{m}`, and `S_2=S_1∪{m-1}`. With `p_j(n)=p(S_j;n)` and `p_2=0` when `S_2` is inadmissible, Corollary 2.3 states

`p(S;n) = p_1(m-1) binom(n,m-1) - 2p_1(n) - p_2(n)`.

For example, the paper gives `p({2,5};n)=n(n-5)(n-2)(n-1)/12` (PDF p. 5).

## Proof chain and assumptions

The proof first establishes `|P(∅;n)|=2^(n-1)` by placing the minimum element and noting that, for no peaks, the part to its left must decrease and the part to its right must increase (Proposition 2.1, PDF p. 3). For nonempty `S`, it partitions by **positions** at `k=m-1`: `Π` consists of permutations whose prefix of length `k` has peak set `S_1` and whose suffix has no peaks. Choose which `k` labels occur in the prefix and arrange the two parts; this gives `|Π|=binom(n,k)|P(S_1;k)||P(∅;n-k)|`. Then `Π` is the disjoint union of `P(S_1;n)`, `P(S_2;n)`, and `P(S;n)`. Subtract the first two counts and factor out `2^(n-|S|-1)` to obtain the recurrence. The leading binomial term has degree `k`, while the subtracted peak polynomials have smaller degree, yielding degree `m-1`.

This split is at the **index** `m-1`, not at the position of the maximum label or by splitting the permutation around its maximum value. The input is a fixed admissible index set and permutations of `[n]`; endpoint indices are excluded by the peak definition.

## Use and transfer limit

This is an exact one-dimensional path baseline and a finite-count check for path instances. Its index-neighbor predicate agrees with degree-at-least-two peaks on a path, but its recurrence does not count the free-boundary square-lattice event in A022; the square graph has a different neighborhood predicate and boundary geometry.
