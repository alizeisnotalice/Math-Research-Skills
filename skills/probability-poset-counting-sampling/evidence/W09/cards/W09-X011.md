# W09-X011: An efficient data structure for counting all linear extensions of a poset, calculating its jump number, and the likes

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Author: Marcel Wild; arXiv preprint 1704.07708v1 (2017; manuscript dated 2018).

Evidence: complete 9-page preprint read; the wildcard representation, recurrence, and reported numerics were checked against PDF pp. 1–4 and 8–9.

## Ideal-coal-mine representation

The article represents the ideal lattice $\operatorname{Id}(P)$ by multivalued rows with wildcards. A row compactly describes many ideals using fixed 0/1 entries, free entries, and implications between designated entries. The row's cardinality-generating polynomial gives the number of represented ideals at each size (PDF pp. 1–2). This compresses the *representation* of the ideal lattice; the linear-extension calculation still visits the individual ideals at each cardinality.

For an ideal $X$, let $e(X)$ count the linear extensions of the induced subposet on $X$. The exact recurrence is

\[
e(\varnothing)=1,\qquad e(X)=\sum_{a:\,X\setminus\{a\}\in\operatorname{Id}(P)} e(X\setminus\{a\}).
\]

The eligible predecessors are the lower covers of $X$ in the ideal lattice. The paper gives the test $X\setminus\{a\}$ is an ideal iff $X\cap\operatorname{uc}(a)=\varnothing$, where $\operatorname{uc}(a)$ is the set of upper covers of $a$ in $P$. Process ideals by size: maintain the sorted list of pairs $(X,e(X))$ at level $k$, enumerate each level-$k+1$ ideal from the compressed rows, find its lower covers, and look up their counts (PDF pp. 3–4). The same framework recursively computes average ranks, rank probabilities, and weighted jump numbers, with separate recurrences in §§4–6.

## Evidence and complexity limit

The author explicitly declines to state a formal complexity theorem, noting that the underlying exact tasks are #P-hard and presenting numerical evidence instead (PDF p. 1). Section 8 reports timings for selected examples: a “thin” 180-element poset with 3,396,086 ideals compressed into 6,970 rows, and a truncated Boolean lattice with 7,828,352 ideals compressed into 24,871 rows (PDF p. 8). These examples show observed compression on those inputs; they do not prove an output-polynomial or worst-case runtime bound. The counting recurrence itself remains exact but can still require processing exponentially many ideals.

## Transfer limit

This data structure and recurrence concern all ideals and all linear extensions of a fixed poset. They do not encode the disjunctive exact-two-peak event on a square, nor supply its exact count or a conditional sampler. Any transfer needs a separate lossless encoding and complexity analysis.

## Revision response (R05)

Clarified that wildcard rows compress the representation of the ideal lattice, while the LE dynamic program still scans individual ideals level by level. Added the precise lower-cover recurrence and test, separated the related rank/jump recurrences, and recorded the author's explicit “no formal theorem” limitation. The reported 180-element and truncated Boolean-lattice timings are now labeled as examples rather than complexity guarantees.
