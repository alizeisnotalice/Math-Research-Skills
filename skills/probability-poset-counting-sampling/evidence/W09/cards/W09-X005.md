# W09-X005: Sorting under Partial Information (without the Ellipsoid Algorithm)

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Authors: Jean Cardinal, Samuel Fiorini, Gwenaël Joret, Raphaël M. Jungers, J. Ian Munro; 2013.

Evidence: complete 29-page paper read; result table and the merge/entropy arguments were rechecked against PDF pp. 1–4 and 13–25.

## Problem and stated bounds

The input is a poset $P$ encoding known pairwise comparison outcomes; the algorithm must discover an unknown linear extension using comparisons. The worst-case information lower bound is $\log_2 e(P)$. The paper's Table 1 (PDF p. 3) gives three algorithms with different tradeoffs:

| Algorithm | Global complexity | Number of comparisons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $O(n^2)$ | $O(\log n\,\log e(P))$ |
| 2 | $O(n^{2.5})$ | at most $(1+\varepsilon)\log e(P)+O_\varepsilon(n)$, for any $\varepsilon>0$ |
| 3 | $O(n^{2.5})$ | at most $15.09\log e(P)$ |

All logarithms in the paper are binary. The preprocessing can contain the expensive computations; after preprocessing, the sorting phase can be implemented in $O(q)+O(n)$ time for $q$ comparisons (PDF pp. 1–3, 25–27). This latter bound is the sorting-phase time, not the total global complexity.

## Entropy and proof route

Write $H(\bar P)$ for the graph entropy of the incomparability graph of $P$. Theorem 2 (PDF p. 7) proves the tight relation $nH(\bar P)\le2\log_2 e(P)$. Algorithms 1–3 turn this information measure into comparison bounds without repeatedly solving a general convex program. Algorithm 1 inserts elements using an entropy-guided chain structure. Algorithm 2 extracts a greedy chain decomposition and merges its chains; Theorem 3 bounds its comparisons by $(1+\varepsilon)\log_2 e(P)+O_\varepsilon(n)$, while the decomposition cost gives the $O(n^{2.5})$ global bound. For small $\log e(P)$, Algorithm 3 preserves the relations to a longest chain and reduces to merging under partial information. Theorem 4 composes a $c_3\log e(P')$ merge routine into a $(9.09+c_3)\log e(P)$ sorter; Theorem 6 supplies a merge algorithm for a poset covered by two chains using at most $6\log e(P)$ comparisons. Together these give the stated $15.09\log e(P)$ bound (PDF §§6–8, pp. 16–27).

## Transfer limit

These are adaptive comparison algorithms for discovering one unknown linear extension and bounds on comparisons/computational work. They are not algorithms for counting extensions or sampling an extension uniformly. In particular, the entropy inequalities do not count the subset satisfying an exact-two-peak constraint and do not give a conditional sampler for A022.
