# M0011: Faster Random Generation of Linear Extensions

Reader: W09. Reading status: fulltext_read.

Authors: Russ Bubley, Martin Dyer; 1999.

## Reading evidence

Read the complete 8-page journal PDF. Main-chain construction and mixing derivation are on PDF pp. 2–5; the appendix's Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 are on pp. 7–8.

## Results and locations

The paper's mixing result is derived in Section 3, rather than stated as its Appendix Theorem 1. On PDF pp. 2–5, the authors define a chain on the linear extensions of a fixed poset: choose an adjacent-position index `i` according to a concave distribution `f` on `{1,…,n−1}`, flip a fair coin, and attempt that adjacent swap if it remains a linear extension. For `F(i)=i(n−i)/K`, where `K=(n³−n)/6`, the transposition-distance coupling contracts in expectation by a factor at most `1−1/K`. Since the diameter is at most `⌊n²/4⌋`, the variation distance is at most `D(1−1/K)^t`, giving a mixing bound `K log(D/ε)=O(n³ log(n/ε))`.

Appendix A Theorem 1 (PDF p. 7) is a different statement: for two linear extensions farther than one transposition apart, it finds an intermediate linear extension splitting their Spearman-footrule distance additively. Corollary 1 (PDF pp. 7–8) identifies this footrule metric with the paper's transposition distance. These geometric facts support the path construction used earlier.

## Proof chain and assumptions

The stationary distribution of the adjacent-swap chain is uniform by transition symmetry. The coupling compares a shortest transposition path between two extensions; concavity of `f` makes the expected distance nonincreasing, and the quadratic choice gives the one-step contraction above. The result concerns the stated fixed-poset chain. In particular, the quadratic position proposal is not the uniform adjacent-index proposal; taking `f` uniform recovers the Karzanov–Khachiyan chain and does not give this paper's `O(n³ log n)` proof.

## Use and transfer limit

This is an approximate sampler for the extensions of one fixed poset. A peak-conditioned graph-labeling space needs a separate state graph, invariant-law proof, and contraction analysis.
