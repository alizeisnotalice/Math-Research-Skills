# Mahler Part I: exponential/logarithm--Liouville independence

The source proves the following two interfaces.  If
\(\vartheta_1,\ldots,\vartheta_N\) are algebraic and linearly independent
over \(\mathbb Q\), and \(\lambda\) is a Liouville number, then

\[
 e^{\vartheta_1},\ldots,e^{\vartheta_N},\lambda
\]

are algebraically independent over the algebraic numbers.  If \(z\) is the
real logarithm of a positive rational number different from one, or if
\(z=\pi\), and \(\lambda\) is Liouville, then \(z,\lambda\) are
algebraically independent over the algebraic numbers.

These are qualitative theorems.  Do not use them as an effective lower-bound
routine, and do not substitute an arbitrary logarithm, a merely
transcendental number, or a number that has only numerical evidence of being
Liouville.  The theorem has no direct Euler--Mascheroni-constant input.
