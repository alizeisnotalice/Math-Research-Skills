# Method workflow and finite-state derivations

This workflow combines scoped source interfaces with elementary finite-dimensional derivations. The source register says which theorem was checked for each interface; a displayed derivation below is not additional source or reading credit.

## 1. Fix the model and the spectral quantity

For a finite state space \(\Omega\), write whether vectors are columns or rows, whether a kernel acts on functions or measures, and whether the chain is discrete-time or generated by a continuous-time generator. Record the full-support stationary law \(\pi\), parameter domain, irreducibility, reversibility, and regularity.

For a reversible kernel \(P\) on \(|\Omega|\ge2\), define the Poincare gap by
\[
\gamma_P=\inf_{\pi f=0,\ f\ne0}\frac{\langle f,(I-P)f\rangle_\pi}{\|f\|_\pi^2}=1-\lambda_2(P),
\]
where eigenvalues are ordered decreasingly with multiplicity. This gap is zero if \(P\) is reducible. The absolute gap is
\[
\gamma_{\rm abs}=1-\max\{|\lambda_2(P)|,|\lambda_{|\Omega|}(P)|\};
\]
it is a different quantity and controls discrete-time convergence of powers only with its own hypotheses.

For a finite reversible generator \(Q\), define
\[
\gamma_Q=\inf_{\pi f=0,\ f\ne0}\frac{-\langle f,Qf\rangle_\pi}{\|f\|_\pi^2}.
\]
It is zero if \(Q\) is reducible. For an irreducible chain with \(|\Omega|\ge2\), it equals the first strictly positive eigenvalue of \(-Q\); this equality need not hold for reducible generators, whose mean-zero Rayleigh infimum can be zero even though \(-Q\) has positive eigenvalues. If \(Q=c(P-I)\) with \(c>0\), then \(\gamma_Q=c\gamma_P\); call this the first positive eigenvalue only in the irreducible case. State the clock multiplier. Aperiodicity is not needed for the Poincare Rayleigh identity, but it matters for convergence of powers or a Neumann series.

## 2. Dirichlet form, Rayleigh quotient, and comparison

Suppose \(P\) is row-stochastic and reversible for a full-support probability \(\pi\). In \(L^2(\pi)\), \(P\) is self-adjoint. From stationarity and the row sums,

\[
\mathcal E_P(f,f)=\langle f,(I-P)f\rangle_\pi
=\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi(x)P(x,y)(f(x)-f(y))^2.
\]

On the \(\pi\)-mean-zero subspace, expand \(f\) in an orthonormal eigenbasis. Then \(\mathcal E_P(f,f)/\|f\|_\pi^2\) is a convex combination of \(1-\lambda_i(P)\), \(i\ge2\), so its infimum is \(1-\lambda_2(P)\). This identifies the Poincaré gap, not the absolute gap. For example, the two-state flip has Poincaré gap 2 and absolute gap 0.

For a finite reversible continuous-time generator with off-diagonal rates \(q(x,y)\ge0\), row sums zero, and full-support invariant law \(\pi\), use \(\mathcal E_Q(f,f)=-\langle f,Qf\rangle_\pi\). On the mean-zero subspace its Rayleigh infimum is the Poincare gap \(\gamma_Q\); it equals the first strictly positive eigenvalue of \(-Q\) when the chain is irreducible and \( |\Omega|\ge2 \), and it is zero when the chain is reducible. The exact counterexample in the cases（本机附件：`references/cases.md`） has spectrum \(\{0,0,2,2\}\) but mean-zero energy zero. If each update operator is conditional expectation in \(L^2(\pi)\), it is an orthogonal projection, hence a convex average of heat-bath updates has nonnegative spectrum. Do not use that conclusion for general Metropolis or non-lazy kernels without a separate lower-edge argument.

A precise form comparison illustrates where stationary-measure factors enter. If two finite irreducible reversible kernels \(P,\widetilde P\) act on the same state space, \(\widetilde\pi(x)\ge a\pi(x)>0\), and a path comparison proves \(\mathcal E_{\widetilde P}(f,f)\le A\mathcal E_P(f,f)\) for every \(f\), then

\[
\operatorname{gap}(P)\ge (a/A)\operatorname{gap}(\widetilde P).
\]

Indeed, \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(f)\le a^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_{\widetilde\pi}(f)\); combine this with the two Rayleigh bounds. The accepted Diaconis–Saloff-Coste use gives the path load, \(A\), and pointwise measure factor \(a\) on original printed pp.698–699. Omitting \(a\) changes the claim.

## 3. Conjugate a reversible family to a fixed Euclidean space

Let \(D_\theta=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_\theta)\), with \(\pi_\theta(x)>0\), and suppose detailed balance holds:

\[
D_\theta P_\theta=P_\theta^{\mathsf T}D_\theta.
\]

Then

\[
S_\theta=D_\theta^{1/2}P_\theta D_\theta^{-1/2}
\]

is real symmetric and similar to \(P_\theta\); the similarity preserves eigenvalues and multiplicities. Put \(W=D^{1/2}\) and \(K=W'W^{-1}=\tfrac12D^{-1}D'\). Direct product differentiation gives the full derivative

\[
S'=WP'W^{-1}+KS-SK.
\]

Dropping either derivative of \(D^{1/2}\) gives the wrong matrix derivative. For a differentiable normalized simple branch \(Su=\lambda u\), \(u^{\mathsf T}u=1\), differentiating and multiplying by \(u^{\mathsf T}\) yields \(\lambda'=u^{\mathsf T}S'u\). The commutator term cancels because

\[
u^{\mathsf T}(KS-SK)u=\lambda u^{\mathsf T}Ku-\lambda u^{\mathsf T}Ku=0.
\]

Equivalently, with \(u=D^{1/2}f\) and \(\langle f,f\rangle_\pi=1\), \(\lambda'=\langle f,P'f\rangle_\pi\). The derivative of the normalization is what cancels the measure and eigenvector terms in the direct weighted calculation. This scalar formula requires a differentiable simple eigenbranch; ordering an eigenvalue alone does not provide one.

At an isolated eigenvalue of multiplicity \(r\), let \(P_0\) be its orthogonal spectral projection in the fixed Euclidean space. For a one-parameter self-adjoint first-order expansion \(S(t)=S(0)+tS'(0)+o(t)\), the first-order slopes of the cluster are the eigenvalues of \(P_0S'(0)P_0\) restricted to \(\operatorname{Ran}P_0\). An ordered eigenvalue can have a corner at the crossing; state the direction and whether the result is one-sided. This is a cluster statement and does not produce a global eigenvector labeling.

## 4. Separate stationary-law response from the nontrivial spectrum

For an irreducible finite kernel, set \(\Pi=\mathbf 1\pi\). Since \(\Pi P=P\Pi=\Pi\), the reduced inverse

\[
Z=(I-P+\Pi)^{-1}-\Pi
\]

satisfies \((I-P)Z=Z(I-P)=I-\Pi\), \(Z\mathbf1=0\), and \(\pi Z=0\). The full matrix \(I-P\) is singular; use the reduced inverse or an explicitly defined zero-mean inverse. If \(P_\theta\) is differentiable and \(\pi_\theta P_\theta=\pi_\theta\), differentiating gives \(\pi'(I-P)=\pi P'\), \(\pi'\mathbf1=0\), and therefore \(\pi'=\pi P'Z\). This is a stationary-law response, not a formula for \(\lambda_2'\).

The algebraic inverse exists for a finite irreducible chain even when it is periodic. The series \(\sum_{k\ge0}(P^k-\Pi)\) needs convergence on the mean-zero subspace; an irreducible periodic chain can have oscillating partial sums although \(Z\) exists. Quantitative bounds require a stated norm and a certified inverse bound.

## 5. Turn theorem bounds into an indexed certificate

For a self-adjoint finite matrix, list the exact ordered eigenvalues and the exact meaning of each interval. If all indices satisfy \(\lambda_i\in[l_i,u_i]\) and \(u_i<l_{i+1}\), then the ordered eigenvalues are separated by at least \(l_{i+1}-u_i>0\). A theorem that only finds a spectral point nearest a residual, or encloses an invariant block without separating its internal eigenvalues, does not identify an index. Merge overlapping approximate intervals into a cluster, recompute a block residual or apply a theorem to the invariant block, then prove the final enclosures are disjoint before claiming indexed isolation.

The Miyajima–Ogita–Rump–Oishi Theorem 4 interface is specifically a full-square generalized symmetric eigenproblem. With \(G_g=X^{\mathsf T}BX\), \(F=I-G_g\), and \(R_g=AX-BXD\), it gives the radius

\[
\widehat\delta=\frac{\beta\|R_g\|_2}{1-\|F\|_2},
\qquad \|F\|_2<1,
\]

with no square root on this denominator and \(\beta\ge\sqrt{\|B^{-1}\|_2}\). Its proof first obtains \(G_g\succ0\), then obtains \(B\succ0\) by congruence/inertia. It does not require or prove \(B-X^{\mathsf T}BX\succ0\). Outward-round the input and bounds; require strict disjointness of ordered intervals to conclude individual isolation. This theorem is not a generic Sturm sequence or inertia algorithm.

Other accepted enclosure interfaces are model-specific: Hermitian residual and cluster methods, conforming differential-operator bounds, an explicitly stated elliptic fixed-point certificate, and validated complex-analytic boundary counts. Use each only with the exact source hypotheses, dependencies, and index/count conclusion recorded in [the source register](sources.md).

### DERIVED restricted Jacobi Sturm signs and LDL inertia

For a finite real symmetric tridiagonal/Jacobi matrix \(T\), diagonal \(a_i\), and adjacent entries \(b_i\), define \(p_0(t)=1\), \(p_1(t)=a_1-t\), and \(p_i(t)=(a_i-t)p_{i-1}(t)-b_{i-1}^2p_{i-2}(t)\). At a shift where every leading minor \(p_1(t),\ldots,p_n(t)\) is certified nonzero, set \(d_i=p_i/p_{i-1}\). The continuant recurrence gives the congruence \(T-tI=L\,\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)L^{\mathsf T}\), with unit lower bidiagonal \(L_{i,i-1}=b_{i-1}/d_{i-1}\). By inertia under invertible congruence, the number of negative eigenvalues of \(T-tI\) is the number of negative \(d_i\), equivalently the sign variations in \(p_0,\ldots,p_n\). Thus \(N_T(t)=\#\{j:\lambda_j(T)<t\}\) at such shifts.

For certified endpoints \(a<b\), \(N_T(b)-N_T(a)\) counts eigenvalues in \((a,b)\) with multiplicity. A count of one isolates an eigenvalue; the \(k\)-th ordered eigenvalue additionally requires endpoint counts \(k-1,k\). Prove a positive gap only after adjacent enclosures are strictly disjoint. With interval coefficients, use outward/validated arithmetic and require every leading-minor interval at each endpoint to exclude zero. A zero-containing interval is inconclusive; this route has no zero-pivot handling, no dense-matrix reduction guarantee, and no general nonsymmetric inertia method. For a nonsymmetric tridiagonal matrix, first prove an exact diagonal similarity to a symmetric representative.

## 6. Keep proofs, computation, and model transfers separate

A theorem may establish a spectral projection, a cluster enclosure, a stationary law, or a gap comparison; these are different conclusions. Check the conclusion field before composing two theorems. PDE domain pullbacks, non-self-adjoint ODE boundary counts, transfer-operator contour bounds, and finite Markov inequalities stay within their stated models unless a separate transfer argument is proved.

Use rational or validated arithmetic when a strict inequality is decisive. Save the exact input, script, runtime, output, and conclusion scope. Floating-point eigenvalues, interval scans without outward rounding, and a symbolic formula without an index proof are exploratory evidence only. The exact finite examples are in cases.md（本机附件：`references/cases.md`）.
