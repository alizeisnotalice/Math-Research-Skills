# Non-holonomic sequence rejection boundary

Published source: Stefan Gerhold, *On Some Non-Holonomic Sequences*,
Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 11 (2004), R87, DOI
10.37236/1840.  The local published edition has SHA-256
`5686d7e83da06c3ee888ce2894938806deba1a0f002a74d20aaf76be9c1fd83a`;
the cited statements and proof are on physical pp.1--3 and 6.

For pairwise distinct positive integers \(a_i,b_j\), define
\[
h(n)=\frac{(a_1)_n\cdots(a_p)_n}{(b_1)_n\cdots(b_q)_n}.
\]
Theorem 1 states that \(h(n)^r\) is not holonomic when
\(r\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathbb Z\).  The admissibility checks are essential:
the parameters must be positive integral and pairwise distinct, the sequence is
not eventually zero, and the exponent is rational but nonintegral.  The proof
first descends any recurrence to its coefficient field (Lemma 2), then combines
positive integral Hadamard powers (Lemma 3) with the valuation obstruction.

The same published source proves that \((n+a)^{bn}\) is non-holonomic for
\(a,b\in\mathbb Q\), \(b\ne0\), after a recurrence is reduced to rational
coefficients and its leading terms force a nontrivial rational relation among
\(e^{bk}\).  This is a holonomy-admission rejection test, not an irrationality
criterion for \(\gamma\), and it does not make its separate conditional
\(\log n\) discussion unconditional.
