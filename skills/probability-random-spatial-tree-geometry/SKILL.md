## Route by model and target

- **SIRSN and spatial-route questions:** separate a typical single route, distance-scale mean stretch, Poisson close-pair law, Poisson window pair-sum, truncated dense-sample network, and the maximum over iid endpoints from one fixed origin. Only cite the exact route or network quantity proved in the selected card. No general A136 conclusion follows from a one-route mean or a pair-summed estimate. The M0092 star estimate is a derived consequence for its specified randomized binary hierarchy only.
- **Spatial FPP and Poisson roads:** distinguish lattice FPP, Poisson-line roads, Delaunay/RNG geodesics, finite-city network optimization, and Palm subscriber-line models. Their state spaces and sampling laws differ. The M0108 book interface is only for nearest-neighbor iid nonnegative weights on \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\), under its separate moment gates; its box-flow rebind has its own narrower assumptions.
- **Tree walks:** first state whether \(\lambda\) weights the parent or \(\beta\) weights children, how the root/added parent behaves, whether leaves occur, survival conditioning, the offspring assumptions, and whether the law is quenched or annealed. Do not exchange the parent-bias and child-bias parameterizations by name alone. Keep transience, positive speed, maximal-speed rates, speed comparisons, and harmonic measure as separate conclusions.
- **Boundary harmonic measure and stationary flows:** identify the boundary and metric, exact bias direction, offspring support/moments, and the environment transformation. Invariance, equivalence, ergodicity, and inducing are distinct steps. A dimension comparison at fixed bias does not imply monotonicity in the bias parameter.
- **Poisson point process identities:** use M0109 only for the complete Chapter 4 unit (physical pp. 38–49); factorial sums are over distinct occurrence indices, not necessarily distinct locations. Mecke or deletion identities alone do not supply route independence, uniform integrability, or a maximum-tail bound.
