## Functional and assumptions

For a unit-intensity Poisson process (X\subset\mathbb R^d), the statistic is the normalized sum of α-power-weighted edge lengths rooted at nodes in (Q_n):
\[
H_n= n^{-d}\sum_{x\in X\cap Q_n}\xi^{(\alpha)}(X-x),\qquad \alpha>d.
\]
The score convention differs for directed, undirected, and bidirected graphs; the paper defines each separately. Theorem 1 assumes scale invariance and (FIN), (FIN2), cone stabilization (STA), continuity away from a null set of finite configurations (CON), and addition stability (INF). These conditions enforce bounded local degree/large-edge density, stabilization, continuity under small perturbations, and a finite influence-zone optimization. They hold for the studied k-nearest-neighbor graphs and suitable planar β-skeletons; the paper explicitly says DT, Gabriel, and relative-neighborhood graphs fail key conditions (FIN)/(FIN2).

For finite configurations φ⊆ψ, (A(\phi,\psi)) is the region of possible added Poisson points that changes an out-neighbor set affecting a scored vertex. The admissible set (B) consists of finite configurations with at least (c_{\rm INF}) points and total excess score at least one. Theorem 1 (PDF p. 6) gives, with μα=ℕξ⁽ᵅ⁾(X∪{0}),
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{-d^2/\alpha}\log\mathbb P(H_n>\mu_\alpha+r)
=-r^{d/\alpha}\inf_{(\phi,\psi)\in B}|A(\phi,\psi)|,
\quad r>0.
\]
Theorem 2 (PDF p. 7) adds (\inf_B|A|>0): conditioned on this rare excess, the large-score contribution is carried by a negligible fraction of nodes; under the additional strict finite-μ₀ optimization gap (equation (11)), the first (m_0) order statistics capture the excess asymptotically.

## Proof dependency chain

The upper bound in §5.1 splits small and large scores. A Poisson-functional weak law/concentration estimate makes the small-score fluctuation negligible at speed (n^{d^2/\alpha}); stabilization plus (FIN)/(FIN2) controls the number and size of exceptional scores. Lemma 9 converts a collection of large scores into a finite witness configuration, and Mecke's formula bounds the probability by the Poisson void cost \(\exp(-|A|\,\tau^{d/\alpha})\). For the lower bound in §5.2, choose a near-minimizer φ,ψ, rescale it to create the excess inside a rare-event box (U_n), enforce one point in small balls and no other points in its influence zone, and keep a separated box (W_n) typical. Independence of the two regions and the Poisson void probability give the matching rate, then optimize over (B). Theorem 2 reuses the exceptional-score bound and compares the optimization cost under a restriction on the top order statistics; condition (11) gives a strict rate gap.

## Scope boundary for A136

The paper concerns a sum of power-weighted graph edges in growing Poisson windows, not route lengths in a SIRSN and not the maximum of routes from a common origin to iid endpoints. Its condensation mechanism is a useful rare-event analogy but proves no A136 tail bound. A near-minimizing local configuration and influence-zone cost cannot be substituted for the shared-network endpoint maximum.
