## Model and typical connection

This is a stochastic subscriber-line model for telecom access networks. A stationary, locally finite tessellation supplies the road segments (T^{(1)}). Higher-level components (HLCs) form a stationary point process on those segments; lower-level components (LLCs) are modeled by a Cox process on the road system. Each HLC receives a serving zone from a tessellation (T_H) (Voronoi zones are a main example), and an LLC is connected to its associated HLC by a shortest route along the segment system. (C^*) denotes the shortest-path length for the typical LLC under the appropriate Palm law; it is a one-connection distribution, not a maximum over endpoints.

## Representation and estimator

Lemma 1 (Neveu exchange formula, PDF pp. 4–5) changes between the Palm views of the two stationary point processes. Lemma 2 (p. 7) then yields
\[
\mathbb E h(C^*)=\lambda_\ell\,\mathbb E\!\int_{S_H^*}h(c(y))\,\nu_1(dy),
\]
where (S_H^*) is the typical serving-zone segment system, (c(y)) is the path length from (y) to its HLC, and ν₁ is segment-length measure. The proof is Neveu exchange followed by Campbell's formula for the conditional Poisson LLC process. In §3.1 the authors split the segment system into (N) monotone pieces with endpoints (A_i,B_i); Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 8–9) gives absolute continuity and
\[
f_C(x)=\lambda_\ell\,\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf 1_{[c(A_i),c(B_i))}(x),\quad x\ge0.
\]
Finiteness of ℕN is used for the density regularity/dominated-convergence step. Theorem 2 (PDF p. 10) establishes unbiasedness and strong consistency of the sample estimator built from iid typical serving-zone segment systems; the rendered page shows the estimator statement and proof via the strong law and the identity above. Theorem 3 proves uniform convergence in the stated estimator setup; §3.4 gives variance/MISE rates under extra moment assumptions, and §3.5 gives the scaling relation.

## Proof scope and A136 boundary

The result is a Palm-typical LLC-to-associated-HLC path in a telecom model, with a sampling scheme for iid typical serving zones. It is neither an SIRSN nor a common-origin iid-endpoint family, and no maximum-route tail is proved. It is a useful comparison for distinguishing a typical route law and its density from a route maximum.
