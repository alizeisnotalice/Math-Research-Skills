## Road metric and scaling

The model is a speed-marked Poisson process of planar lines with intensity proportional to (\mu(d\ell)v^{-\beta}dv), β>2, where μ is invariant affine-line measure. The constant is normalized so that the expected number of roads of speed at least (v_0) hitting a ball of radius (r) scales as (r v_0^{-(\beta-1)}) in dimension two. The scaling map couples space rescaling and speed rescaling. Define the local speed field (V(x)), admissible V-paths, and the driving-time metric (T) as in §1 (PDF pp. 6–8). The authors rely on the established uniqueness of endpoint geodesics in this model.

## Main geometric theorems

- **Theorem 1** (PDF p. 3): almost surely, for every geodesic γ:[0,τ]→ℝ² and every interior time interval [(t_1,t_2)]⊂(0,τ), there is ε>0 with (V(\gamma(t))\ge\epsilon) throughout that interval. Thus geodesics do not pause in the interior.
- **Theorem 2** (PDF pp. 4–5): writing ℒ for all road points and I for intersections of two roads, the geodesic frame is ℒ. Every (x\in\mathcal L\setminus I) is a 2-hub but not a 3-star; every (x\in I) is a 4-hub but not a 5-star; (H_2^+=\mathcal L), (H_4^+=I), and (H_5^+=\varnothing). A hub means that pairwise concatenations of the geodesic arms are geodesics; a star imposes the corresponding simultaneous arm condition.

## Proof mechanisms

Proposition 1 (§2, PDF pp. 8–20) proves coalescence around typical points, road points, and road intersections. The proof constructs good scale events, uses scaling ergodicity and Poisson/Mecke calculations, and combines local fast-road ring/crossing estimates. Lemma 1 (pp. 9–10) approximates an interior geodesic segment by a subsegment of a geodesic with endpoints from a countable dense set; Lemma 2 (p. 10) gives an initial uniqueness segment.

Proposition 2 (§3, PDF pp. 20–26) gives a deterministic finite upper bound on geodesic star order, simultaneously over all points, using annular cut sets, high-speed road counts, Chernoff bounds, and Borel–Cantelli. Proposition 3 (§4, pp. 27–31) shows that an ε-geodesic can have off-road ε-motion only at its endpoints: the proof combines a local-minimizer lemma with a Mecke zero-probability argument for two roads with matching directional speed projections. Theorem 1 then uses endpoint cut points, ε_n=2^{-n}, uniqueness for a countable dense set of endpoint pairs, and Lemma 1.

Sections 5.1–5.2 (PDF pp. 32–45) identify the geodesic frame and hubs. The road-leaving lemmas use Mecke’s formula and multiscale speed-gap events with Borel–Cantelli to rule out off-road departures that could be geodesic. Section 6 (pp. 46–48) studies cut loci, including countability of points with at least three geodesics (Proposition 6). Appendix §7 (pp. 49–55) supplies the mixing, constrained road-speed path, and tail lemmas used earlier.

## A136 boundary

This paper establishes geometric and geodesic-structure facts for the Poisson roads metric. It does not bound the route lengths of a fixed source to iid uniform endpoints in one SIRSN realization, and does not prove integrability of their sample maximum. A136 remains unresolved by this source.
