## Selected chapter: Mecke equation and factorial measures

Theorem 4.1 (printed p. 27; physical p. 39) characterizes a Poisson process η with s-finite intensity measure λ on an arbitrary measurable space by the Mecke identity
\[
\mathbb E\!\int f(x,\eta)\,\eta(dx)
=\mathbb E\!\int f(x,\eta+\delta_x)\,\lambda(dx),\qquad f\ge0\text{ measurable}.
\]
Necessity follows from the Poisson Mecke formula. For sufficiency, test disjoint finite-intensity sets (B_1,\dots,B_m): the identity gives the conditional count recursion (k\pi_k=\lambda(B_1)\pi_{k-1}), forcing a Poisson count independent of the counts in the other sets; induction yields independent Poisson counts. Infinite-intensity sets are handled separately.

For a general counting measure \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\delta_{X_i}\), its \(m\)-th factorial measure is defined by summing over ordered tuples of **distinct indices/occurrences** \((i_1,\ldots,i_m)\), as in (4.10); this does not require distinct spatial values \(X_{i_1},\ldots,X_{i_m}\). If \(\mu\) has an atom of multiplicity at least two, repeated spatial locations can therefore occur in \(\mu^{(m)}\). Distinct locations follow only in the simple/diffuse setting. Theorem 4.4 (printed p. 30; physical p. 42) is the multivariate Mecke formula: the left side integrates against \(\eta^{(m)}\) with the original configuration argument, and the right side inserts \(\sum_i\delta_{x_i}\). Theorem 4.5 (printed p. 31; physical p. 43) is the proper-Poisson form: on its left, successively remove each selected occurrence using \(\eta\setminus\delta_{x_1}\setminus\cdots\setminus\delta_{x_m}\); on its right, leave \(\eta\) unchanged under \(\lambda^m\). This deletion convention handles coincident locations by removing one unit of multiplicity at each step. Corollary 4.10 identifies the factorial moment measure of a Poisson process as \(\alpha_m=\lambda^m\); the chapter then derives the second-moment identity. Theorem 4.7 treats Janossy-measure uniqueness, while Proposition 4.12 states a factorial-moment determinacy criterion under its exponential-growth bound.

## Relevance and strict limitation for A136

These identities justify expectation calculations for counts of Poisson points or ordered/unordered pairs and can support a Palm/pair-frequency normalization. They do not encode routes in a shared network, dependence among routes from a fixed origin, or the supremum over iid endpoints. They supply no missing tail-integrability estimate for A136. In particular, a Mecke count identity cannot turn M0100's typical close-pair law into the fixed-origin endpoint-star maximum.
