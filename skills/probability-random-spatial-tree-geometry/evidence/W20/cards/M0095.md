# M0095 — Optimal spatial transportation networks where link costs are sublinear in link capacity

**Source:** D. J. Aldous, *Optimal spatial transportation networks where link costs are sublinear in link capacity*, Journal of Statistical Mechanics (2008), P03006, DOI 10.1088/1742-5468/2008/03/P03006. Full 12-page author-hosted published PDF, read throughout. PDF SHA256 is in evidence/W20/sources.json.

## Optimization model and main theorem

Given \(n\) city points in a square of area \(n\), route demand \(n^{-3/2}\) between each source-destination pair. A network is made of Euclidean line-segment links; a link of length \(\ell(e)\) carrying total flow \(f(e)\) costs \(\ell(e)f(e)^\beta\), \(0<\beta<1\). “Modestly efficient” means average excess route length is \(o(n^{1/2})\).

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 4; construction and proof pp. 5–10).** With \(\alpha(\beta)=1-\beta/2\) for \(0<\beta\le1/2\), and \(\alpha(\beta)=(1+\beta)/2\) for \(1/2\le\beta<1\), the minimum-cost scale is \(n^{\alpha(\beta)}\), up to the stated critical logarithm at \(\beta=1/2\) and slowly varying factors for the modest-efficiency construction in \(\beta>1/2\). The lower bounds require different regularity hypotheses: a positive fraction of cities have nearest-neighbor distance bounded below for \(\beta\le1/2\); empirical equidistribution in the unit square for \(\beta>1/2\).

The upper-bound construction has three layers:

1. Large scale: a square grid plus a sparse Poisson line process, intensity \(\eta_n=\theta_n n^{-1/2}\).
2. Medium scale: a dyadic hierarchy on the square lattice; Lemma 2 bounds how many edges of each hierarchy type a route uses.
3. Small scale: a direct link from each city to the lower-left vertex of its small cell.

The cost proof separates the contributions: local links cost \(O(n^{1-\beta/2})\); hierarchical edges have a geometric sum that changes regime at \(\beta=1/2\); the large-scale links use Hölder's inequality on total length and aggregate flow, giving \(O(\theta_n^{1-\beta}n^{(1+\beta)/2})\). For the lower bound above \(1/2\), Lemma 4 controls maximum edge flow by \(o(n^{1/2})\) under modest efficiency and equidistribution; below \(1/2\), charge each separated city its necessary incident flow within a small ball.

Section 4 gives a speed-design variant with link cost \(\ell c^\beta s^\gamma\). Its exponent is \(\alpha^*(\beta,\gamma)=1-\beta/2-\gamma/2\) when \(2\beta+\gamma\le1\), and \((1+\beta)/2\) when \(2\beta+\gamma\ge1\), under the same relevant regularity assumptions.

## Relation to W20/A136

This is an optimization model on finite city configurations, not a stationary scale-invariant route process. Its Poisson line process is one component of a hierarchical low-cost network construction; it is not the marked improper Poisson line geodesic SIRSN of M0093/M0094. Theorems compare total construction cost and average route efficiency; they provide no integrability statement for \(\sup_i\operatorname{len}r(0,U_i)\). Treat it as background on route-sharing/network design, not direct theorem coverage for A136.
