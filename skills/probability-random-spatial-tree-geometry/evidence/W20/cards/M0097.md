# M0097 — connected spatial networks and a route-length statistic

**Source:** David J. Aldous and Julian Shun, “Connected Spatial Networks over Random Points and a Route-Length Statistic,” *Statistical Science* 25(3) (2010), 275–288, DOI 10.1214/10-STS335. ## Object and statistic

The paper compares connected spatial networks on a rate-one planar Poisson process (and finite (n)-point uniform samples in a square of area (n)). Examples include the minimum spanning tree, relative-neighborhood and Gabriel proximity graphs, Delaunay triangulation, and the Hammersley network. For a city pair, ℓ(i,j) is shortest-path Euclidean route length, (d(i,j)) is Euclidean separation, and (r(i,j)=\ell(i,j)/d(i,j)-1).

The paper's route-efficiency statistic is
\[
\rho(d)=\text{mean }r(i,j)\text{ over pairs at separation }d,\qquad R=\sup_{d\ge0}\rho(d).
\]
Thus (R) is a supremum over distance scales of **mean stretch**, not the maximum route length or maximum stretch over the realized city pairs. In finite samples the authors replace exact distance by bins and report Monte Carlo estimates. The main purpose is the trade-off between normalized network length (L) and (R), not a tail theorem for a maximum.

## Exact calculation and proof status

Lemma 1 (PDF p. 9) considers a proximity graph with template (A), (c=\operatorname{area}(A)), on the Poisson process. An edge from a typical point to a point at distance (s) is present with probability (e^{-cs^2}); Campbell integration gives mean degree \(\bar d=\int_0^\infty e^{-cs^2}2\pi s\,ds=\pi/c\) and normalized edge length
\[
L=\tfrac12\int_0^\infty s e^{-cs^2}2\pi s\,ds=\frac{\pi^{3/2}}{4c^{3/2}}.
\]
The factor (1/2) counts each undirected edge once. The article then computes/estimates (R) for tractable models by simulation; it does not prove the displayed values. Section 5 defines the finite-sample optimization and discusses the deterministic limiting trade-off (R_{\rm opt}(L)), but explicitly leaves strict decrease and continuity unproved. Section 6.2 points to a separate paper for a sufficient condition for (R<\infty) in proximity graphs; that external proof is not part of this article.

## Relevance and limits for A136

This is useful for preventing statistic confusion: (R=\sup_d\rho(d)) averages over pairs at each distance and then takes a supremum over the distance parameter. It is not (\max_i \ell(0,U_i)), not a supremum over iid endpoints in one shared SIRSN, and it does not settle A136. The underlying discrete Poisson network models and the statistic also differ from Aldous's scale-invariant continuum network axioms.
