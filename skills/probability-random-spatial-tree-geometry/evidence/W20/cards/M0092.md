Full arXiv author PDF arXiv:1204.0817, 56 physical PDF pages, read throughout. ## SIRSN definition and route law

- A route \(r(z,z')\) is a finite-length, non-self-intersecting jagged path; reversed endpoints reverse the route. Routes are pairwise compatible: if two routes meet twice, they coincide on the subroute between their meeting points.
- Finite-dimensional route-network laws are consistent, translation/rotation invariant, and invariant under Euclidean scaling. A measurable family of such laws induces the route process.
- For an independent Poisson process \(\Xi(\lambda)\), \(\mathcal S(\lambda)\) is the union of routes between its points. It is required to be a feasible subnetwork, translation/rotation invariant, nested under coupled intensities, and to transform under scaling with intensity \(\lambda\mapsto c^{-2}\lambda\).
- The three SIRSN finiteness conditions are \(1<\Delta=\mathbb E D_1<\infty\) for the length \(D_1\) of a route between points at unit Euclidean separation; finite edge intensity \(\ell\) of \(\mathcal S(1)\); and finite \(p(1)\), the limiting edge intensity of the long-distance route pieces after deleting unit endpoint neighborhoods. Its scaling law is \(p(r)=p(1)/r\). The weak-SIRSN variant assumes finite \(\ell\) instead of finite \(p(1)\).

## Proved model and theorem chain

- **Proposition 2 (PDF pp. 15–16):** in the dyadic binary-hierarchy construction with \(1/2<\gamma<1\), a minimum-cost path between lattice vertices has Euclidean length at most \(K_\gamma\) times their \(\ell^1\) distance. The route geometry lemmas bound its excursion and show turning coordinates follow admissible height-monotone sequences.
- **Proposition 9 (PDF pp. 22–27):** there is a null set \(A\subset\mathbb R^2\) such that approximating binary-rational endpoints outside \(A\) yield a stable limiting route. The proof builds positive-density “forcing” configurations where routes from a small square to a distant boundary pass through a specified point, then uses Borel–Cantelli.
- **Section 3.7 (PDF pp. 27–28):** a scale-compatible sequence of large uniform translations gives translation invariance; a uniform rotation gives rotational invariance; independent random rescaling by \(C\in[1,2]\), with scale-free density proportional to \(1/c\), upgrades dyadic to full scale invariance. The route-length, network-length, and major-road estimates verify \(\Delta,\ell,p(1)<\infty\) for this model.
- **Lemma 20 (PDF p. 38):** in any SIRSN, \(\ell\le2p(1)\), by splitting route pieces according to their endpoint-neighborhood scale and applying scale invariance.
- **Proposition 18 (PDF p. 37):** the expected number of distinct crossings of the unit circle by sampled long routes from an inner disk to outside an outer disk is at most \(4p(1)/r\) after the stated endpoint separation \(r\). It is a crossing-count bound, not a maximum-length bound.

## A136 is explicitly open

- **Open Problem 34 (PDF p. 50, §8.4.3):** for iid \(U_1,U_2,\ldots\), uniform on the unit disk, under what extra assumptions (if any) is
  \[
  \mathbb E\sup_{i\ge1}\operatorname{len} r(0,U_i)<\infty?
  \]
  The source says route lengths have played a relatively small role in its results and calls this an open question.
- From the axioms and isotropy/scaling, for each single uniform \(U_i\), \(\mathbb E\operatorname{len}r(0,U_i)=\Delta\,\mathbb E|U_i|=(2/3)\Delta<\infty\). This is an inference from the one-route statistic, not an answer to Open Problem 34.
- The endpoints \(U_i\) share one network realization, so the route lengths are generally dependent. Neither finite marginal means nor the finite edge intensity of \(\mathcal S(1)\) directly yields an integrable supremum. Proposition 18 bounds crossings of long sampled routes; it does not bound the lengths of the countable star family from 0. No counterexample or sufficient extra condition is established in this paper.

## Proof dependencies and scope

The binary-hierarchy construction proves existence of one SIRSN model; it does not prove that every route family has an integrable maximum. The structure results on geodesics, coalescence, and transit nodes depend on scale invariance plus the finite \(p(1)\) condition and are separate from Open Problem 34. The open problem remains open in this source.
