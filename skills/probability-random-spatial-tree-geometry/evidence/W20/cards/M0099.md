# M0099 — coalescence of Euclidean geodesics

**Source:** David Coupier and Christian Hirsch, “Coalescence of Euclidean Geodesics on the Poisson-Delaunay Triangulation,” arXiv:1603.05171v1 (2016); published in *Bernoulli* 24(4B) (2018), 3521–3540, DOI 10.3150/17-BEJ943. Complete 21-page preprint read (SHA-256 `019c472c3b8a823886153a69cdf501f5e2feebd1d6c879ff10b24ff03aad4551`). The geodesic model and Theorem 1 were checked on rendered physical PDF p. 3.

## Model and exact statements

Let (X) be a rate-one planar Poisson process. (G(X)) is either its Delaunay triangulation or relative-neighborhood graph; edges have Euclidean length as first-passage weight. For a fixed deterministic direction \(\hat u\), the cited existence/uniqueness result supplies a μhat u-unigeodesic from each Poisson point. Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2) proves that, almost surely for this fixed direction, any two such semi-infinite geodesics eventually coalesce. The quantifier is “for each fixed direction, with probability one”; it is not asserted as one simultaneous probability-one event for every direction.

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 4) concerns branch counts in the shortest-path tree rooted at the Poisson point nearest the origin. It defines χᵣ through the unbounded components remaining after cutting the rooted tree at the radius-(r) disk, and χᵣ(\(\hat u,c\)) for the components whose exiting edge crosses an arc of length (c) near (r\hat u). It proves
\[
r^{-1}\mathbb E\chi_r\longrightarrow0,
\qquad \mathbb E\chi_r(\hat u,c)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is an expected branch-count statement. The paper expressly notes that χᵣ(\(\hat u,c\)) does not converge to zero almost surely; in fact its limsup is at least one a.s. It is not a maximum of route lengths or an A136 endpoint maximum.

## Proof dependencies

For Theorem 1, §2 modifies FPP by forbidding backtracking across a vertical line. This preserves the modified geodesics under Poisson changes on one side of the line, enabling a shielding construction. Proposition 4 (§3) builds a positive-probability shield using Poisson local events and concentration of Delaunay/RNG shortest-path lengths. Stationarity then makes the expected number of shielded noncoalescing geodesics grow like area, contradicting the perimeter order for edges crossing a box boundary (adapted Burton–Keane argument). Crucially, the locally modified paths need not be geodesics in the original model; the proof is organized to transfer only the contradiction.

For Theorem 2, §4 approximates the distant rooted shortest-path tree in total variation by a directed forest of geodesics with the opposite direction. Theorem 1 and stationarity rule out bi-infinite geodesics in that directed forest; a uniform second-moment bound upgrades convergence-in-probability of the directional branch count to convergence of its expectation. Isotropy gives the (r^{-1}\mathbb E\chi_r\to0) conclusion from the directional estimate. This controls branch counts, not lengths of the routes themselves.
