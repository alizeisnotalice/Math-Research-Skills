## Model and typical-pair random variable

An invariant tree network is a Euclidean-group-invariant random tree network connecting a rate-1 planar Poisson point process; Steiner points are allowed, edges are line segments, and the network has no circuits. For fixed (r>0), (D_r) is the network route length between the endpoints of a **typical unordered Poisson-point pair**, under the pair-intensity sampling law restricted to Euclidean separation at most (r). Equivalently, its tail is read as a large-window pair-frequency ratio. This is a typical-pair route random variable, not a supremum over all nearby pairs in one realization.

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 2) gives constants (r_0<\infty), (\beta>0) such that for every invariant tree network, for (r\ge r_0) and (r\le d<\infty),
\[
\mathbb P(D_r>d)\ge \beta r/d.
\]
In the proof, (\chi(r,d)=\mathbb P(D_r\ge d)) is the typical-pair tail. The conversion on PDF p. 9 explicitly compares the expected number of long-route close pairs with the expected total number of close pairs in a square and yields (\chi(r,d)\ge\beta_2 r/d) for (r\ge r_0) and (d/r\ge\rho_0). Integrating the tail yields (\mathbb E D_r=\infty). This does not say (D_r=\infty) almost surely and does not define a network-wide maximum.

## Proof chain

In a large square, take the finite tree spanned by its Poisson points. A centroid divides its branches; a merging argument gives a red/blue partition with both colors occupying between one-third and one-half of the points, so every cross-color route passes through the centroid. Partition into (m\times m) cells. Lemma 1 (PDF pp. 3–4) gives a lower bound for nearby red–blue pairs in balanced cells. Lemma 2 (pp. 4–7), using Peierls contours and independent bad-cell bounds, forces many adjacent cells with different predominant branch colors. Lemma 3 (pp. 7–8) then yields at least (\beta_0 m^4 k) close pairs with route length at least (0.001mk).

Taking expectations and comparing pair counts gives the tail estimate: the proof uses (r=\sqrt2m), (d=0.001mk), a lower bound on the expected count of long-route pairs, and the upper bound (\frac12 n^2\pi r^2\) on the expected total number of unordered close pairs in ([0,n]^2). Dividing these expectations gives the typical-pair frequency (\chi(r,d)); there is no step taking a maximum over the realization. The centroid argument is the tree-specific obstruction, and the theorem is universal over invariant tree networks.

## A136 boundary

Aldous's theorem proves a (1/d)-order lower tail and infinite mean for a typical Poisson pair conditioned through pair-intensity sampling to be within distance (r). It does **not** control (\mathbb E\sup_i\operatorname{len}r(0,U_i)\), where the (U_i) are iid disk endpoints in one shared SIRSN realization. It neither proves nor disproves A136; the fixed-origin iid endpoint-star tail integral remains unproved by this source.
