## Model and main results

The central model is nearest-neighbor first-passage percolation on \(\mathbb Z^d\) with i.i.d. nonnegative edge weights \((\tau_e)\). For a finite path \(\gamma\), its passage time is \(T(\gamma)=\sum_{e\in\gamma}\tau_e\), and \(T(x,y)\) is the infimum over paths from \(x\) to \(y\). This is a lattice metric model; the book does not posit a SIRSN or a common-origin family of iid endpoints.

Theorem 2.1 (physical PDF p. 8) assumes \(\mathbb E\min(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_{2d})<\infty\), for independent copies of an edge weight. It gives an almost-sure and \(L^1\) limit \(T(0,ne_1)/n\to\mu(e_1)\). The proof applies Liggett's subadditive ergodic theorem to \(X_{m,n}=T(me_1,ne_1)\): concatenation yields subadditivity, iid shifts give stationarity/ergodicity, and the integrability condition is verified by bounding \(T(0,e_1)\) by the minimum over \(2d\) disjoint deterministic paths. Theorem 2.16 (physical pp. 15–18) gives the Cox–Durrett shape theorem under \(\mathbb E\min(\tau_1^d,\ldots,\tau_{2d}^d)<\infty\) and \(F(0)<p_c(d)\): the rescaled growth ball converges to a deterministic, compact, convex, axis-symmetric limit shape with nonempty interior. The proof combines subadditivity in rational directions with a difference/interpolation estimate, then rules out a positive-probability sequence of linearly bad deviations.

The remaining chapters survey concentration and variance bounds, geodesic geometry, Busemann functions, growth models, maximal flow, and integrable last-passage models. The proof tools include Efron–Stein resampling, modified Poincaré/Falik–Samorodnitsky inequalities, the Newman–Piza test-path argument, exponential concentration/entropy methods, and geometric skeleton approximations. Section 4 records that the scaling relation \(\chi=2\xi-1\) is heuristic in this general setting and is not established for any distribution in the models surveyed there; the book keeps rigorous wandering and fluctuation bounds separate from KPZ predictions. Section 5 constructs Busemann limits along selected geodesics and uses directional differentiability/uniqueness assumptions for coalescence and no-bigeodesic conclusions; these are direction- and model-specific.

## Max-flow distinction and proof dependency

For a bounded domain with source and sink boundary pieces, Section 7 defines a capacity-constrained flow and its strength. Theorem 7.1 (physical pp. 138–139) is the finite-graph max-flow/min-cut identity: maximum flow equals the minimum sum of edge capacities over a cutset. Theorem 7.2 and Theorem 7.5 discuss surface-order limits in specific lattice boxes under stated geometric and edge-weight assumptions (including finite exponential moments in Theorem 7.5). This is a cutset/capacity functional, not a maximum over route lengths. It must not be substituted for an estimate of \(\mathbb E\sup_i\operatorname{len}r(0,U_i)\).

## Use for A136 and transfer boundary

The book is an auxiliary comparator for subadditive passage metrics, geodesic existence/geometry, and the distinction between path minimization and cutset maximization. Its i.i.d. lattice environment, endpoint-pair passage times, and volume/surface growth statements do not control a shared SIRSN realization with one fixed origin and countably many iid endpoints. In particular, neither a time-constant/shape theorem nor the max-flow/min-cut theorem supplies the missing tail integral for the A136 maximum-route variable. That interface remains open; the book is not counted as evidence that A136 is solved.
