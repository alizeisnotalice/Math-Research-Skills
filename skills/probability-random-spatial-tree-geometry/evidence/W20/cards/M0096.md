# M0096 — Rayleigh random flights on the Poisson line SIRSN

## Model and assumptions

Definition 1.1 (PDF pp. 2–4) treats a SIRSN as a random mechanism assigning a uniquely determined route to each specified pair, with compatible routes, similarity-equivariant finite-dimensional laws, and the paper’s route-network finiteness axioms. The studied process is a discrete-time Rayleigh random flight on a speed-marked Poisson line SIRSN (or the stated SIRSN candidate), quenched by conditioning on the line environment. The walker’s state is an ordered pair of consecutive lines intersecting at its current location; each transition changes to a new line at the next intersection according to a reversible scattering rule.

The paper’s γ is the Poisson line SIRSN parameter; α parametrizes the similarity-equivariant speed dependence. These parameters describe the flight/scattering model, not route-length moments of a fixed-origin iid endpoint family.

## Theorem 3.6: scattering law

For a balanced, delineated, reversible scattering process on Π whose scattering classes are its lines and whose dynamics are similarity-equivariant, Theorem 3.6 (PDF p. 22; proof pp. 22–23) shows that for some real α,

\[
\frac{\kappa(L_1;\Pi\setminus\{L_1\})}{v(L_1)^\alpha}
=\frac{\kappa(L_2;\Pi\setminus\{L_2\})}{v(L_2)^\alpha}.
\]

When the deficit δ vanishes identically, the reversible measure and scattering probability reduce to the speed-ratio formulas in the theorem; nontrivial scattering occurs exactly when α>γ−1. The proof first uses similarity invariance to make κ a measurable function of speed alone, then solves the resulting multiplicative Cauchy equation.

## Stationary environment and recurrence

- Theorem 4.3 (PDF pp. 29–30) gives a stationary relative-environment law for the SIRSN-RRF when α>γ−1: the log relative speed has an asymmetric Laplace density with rates γ−1 and α−(γ−1); the angle has the sine-weighted law; and the reduced marked line environment is an independent copy of Π. Its proof uses the reversible Dirichlet form and the Slivnyak–Mecke formula.
- Theorem 5.2 (PDF p. 33) proves ergodicity of this relative-environment process for every α>γ−1, using a Kozlov-type invariant-function argument and ergodicity of Π.
- Theorem 5.3 (PDF p. 33) states that for a Poisson line SIRSN or candidate with γ≥2, there exists a zero-defect SIRSN-RRF whose speed returns infinitely often to every neighborhood of its initial speed. The proof chooses critical α=2(γ−1), obtains zero mean log-speed increments from the stationary law, and applies the real-valued Kesten–Spitzer–Whitman range theorem (Theorem 5.1, PDF p. 32).

## A136 boundary

This paper constructs and analyzes a random flight **on** a Poisson line SIRSN. The main theorems concern its relative environment and speed recurrence. They give neither an integrable upper tail for \(\sup_i\operatorname{len}r(0,U_i)\) in one shared SIRSN realization nor a counterexample to that claim. A136 remains unresolved by this source.
