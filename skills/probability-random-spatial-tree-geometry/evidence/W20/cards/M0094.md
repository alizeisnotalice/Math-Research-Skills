# M0094 — From random lines to metric spaces

**Source:** Wilfrid S. Kendall, *From random lines to metric spaces*, Annals of Probability 45(1) (2017), 469–517, DOI 10.1214/14-AOP935. Full Warwick accepted-manuscript PDF, 50 physical pages including repository cover sheet, read throughout; mathematical article is PDF pp. 2–48. PDF SHA256 is in evidence/W20/sources.json.

## Model and path space

The marked line process \(\Pi\) has intensity \((\gamma-1)v^{-\gamma}dv\,\mu_d(d\ell)\), where \(\mu_d\) is invariant unoriented-line measure and \(v\) is speed. Its speed-limit field \(V(x)\) is defined through the upper-level line silhouettes. A \(\Pi\)-path is a locally Lipschitz path whose velocity almost everywhere is bounded by \(V\), equivalently travels on a line with sufficient speed or remains stopped. This abstract definition matters in \(d>2\): marked lines almost surely do not intersect, so not every valid path is a sequential concatenation of line segments.

## Exact theorem chain and scope

- **Theorem 2.6 / Lemma 2.9 / Theorem 2.10 / Corollary 2.11 (PDF pp. 14–18):** if \(\gamma\ge d\), finite-time paths starting in a fixed compact set remain in a random bounded region and have a common random Lipschitz bound; the path space is weakly closed and weakly compact. The escape bound is reduced to a scalar comparison ODE driven by the fastest-line speed. Projecting marked lines to generalized distance \(p=|r|^{d-1}\) and meta-slowness \(s=v^{-(\gamma-1)}\) yields a planar Poisson process; its record sequence produces the perpetuity \(X_n=U_n(T_n+X_{n-1})\). Geometric ergodicity and divergence of accumulated travel time prevent escape.
- **Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 19–22):** for \(\gamma>d\), any specified pair is connected by a finite-time \(\Pi\)-path. A recursively branching binary construction joins successively closest points to fastest lines hitting two shrinking balls. The chance that a level-\(n\) connection is too slow decays summably after selecting speed thresholds; the total travel time converges because \(\gamma>d\).
- **Theorem 3.6 / Corollary 3.5 (PDF pp. 23–25):** almost surely all pairs in \(\mathbb R^d\) are connected, and minimum-time geodesics exist by the weak compactness of path space. Theorem 3.6 extends the recursive construction to a whole positive-volume cube.
- **Theorem 3.8 / Theorem 3.11 / Corollary 3.12 (PDF pp. 26–30):** finite-time paths are uniformly approximable by paths using finitely many marked lines, with short slow intervals; under \(\gamma\ge d\), subsequential limits are valid \(\Pi\)-paths. This establishes measurability of the geodesic time.
- **Theorem 4.4 (PDF pp. 31–37):** for \(d=2,\gamma>2\), a geodesic between two specified points is a.s. unique. The proof first shows line intersections along a geodesic are finite unions of intervals bounded by proper encounters (Theorem 4.3); after conditioning on one line, route patterns along it have durations \(S_\sigma+L_\sigma/V\), with a continuous Pareto speed \(V\), so distinct patterns tie with probability zero. This proof uses planar point-line duality.
- **Theorem 5.2 (PDF pp. 37–44):** in \(d=2,\gamma>2\), a specified geodesic has finite expected Euclidean length. Lemma 5.1 first builds a constrained recursive route from the fastest line in a ball, with successive meta-slownesses satisfying \(S_n=S_{n-1}+cE_n/r_n\) and \(r_n<r_{n-1}/2\). The racetrack comparison bounds time spent outside a ball; the scalar speed comparison from Theorem 2.6 and integrable Gamma tails control the remaining excursion length.
- **Theorem 6.2 / 6.3 / 6.4 (PDF pp. 45–48):** in the plane there is a unique geodesic from a specified point to infinity and all such infinite geodesics coalesce eventually; geodesics from one point to two other specified points share a nontrivial initial segment; and the network joining an independent Poisson point process is locally finite, with finite total length in each compact set a.s. The last statement is the pre-SIRSN property, not a finite-mean length-intensity theorem. The paper explicitly leaves the weak/full SIRSN intensity question open at its publication date; it notes Kahn's later solution.

## A136 interface

Theorem 5.2 gives \(\mathbb E\operatorname{len}r(x,y)<\infty\) for each specified planar pair. It does not prove integrability of the supremum over iid endpoints \(U_i\) in a unit disk. Theorem 6.4 is only almost-sure local finiteness for routes between an independent Poisson set; it neither adds a fixed origin to the endpoint family nor gives an expectation bound for the resulting star network. The article itself mentions Open Problem 34's analogue as Aldous's question on expected sup route lengths. Thus finite marginal route means and pre-SIRSN local finiteness cannot be substituted for A136.

## Version boundary

Kendall's 2017 article predates Kahn's full-SIRSN proof for the Poisson-line model. When citing historical claims, preserve its language “pre-SIRSN”; use Kahn (M0093) for the later full SIRSN and all-dimensional uniqueness results.
