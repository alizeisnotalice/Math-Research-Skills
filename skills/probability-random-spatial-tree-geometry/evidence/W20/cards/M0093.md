# M0093 — Improper Poisson line process as SIRSN in any dimension

**Source:** Jonas Kahn, *Improper Poisson line process as SIRSN in any dimension*, Annals of Probability 44(4) (2016), 2694–2725, DOI 10.1214/15-AOP1032. Full arXiv:1503.03976v3 PDF, 34 physical pages, read throughout. PDF SHA256 is in evidence/W20/sources.json.

## Model and hypotheses

The space of unoriented lines in \(\mathbb R^d\) has invariant measure \(\mu_d\), normalized by \(\mu_d([K])=\tfrac12\mathcal H^{d-1}(\partial K)\) for convex bodies \(K\). Mark each line by speed \(v>0\), with Poisson intensity
\[
(\gamma-1)v^{-\gamma}\,dv\,\mu_d(d\ell),\qquad \gamma>d\ge2.
\]
There are countably many lines; slower lines are dense. A finite-time \(\Pi\)-path is locally Lipschitz, and almost everywhere its velocity is either zero or lies along a marked line at no more than that line's speed. In \(d\ge3\), the lines almost surely do not intersect, so valid paths need not be sequential concatenations of line segments.

Kendall's Theorem 2.4, quoted by Kahn, supplies finite-time paths between every pair and attainment of the time infimum, hence a random geodesic metric. Kahn then proves uniqueness and the SIRSN conditions:

- **Theorem 3.1:** a diameter bound for a bounded connected set \(A\), allowing forbidden lines that hit a set \(F\). A nested net construction and a summable failure bound yield an exponential moment for \(T_{A,F}^{\gamma-1}\).
- **Theorem 4.8:** for any specified \(x,y\), the \(\Pi\)-geodesic is a.s. unique in all \(d\ge2\). For \(d\ge3\), Lemma 4.6 proves “many directions” at segment endpoints; Proposition 4.5 shows a finite path using the same lines must pass through such a point; Theorem 4.7 proves all geodesics have the same supporting lines. Dimension 2 uses Kendall's Theorem 4.4.
- **Theorem 5.1:** the Euclidean length \(L_{xy}\) of the geodesic between specified \(x,y\) has finite expectation; the proof in fact gives \(\mathbb E L_{xy}^{\delta}<\infty\) for every \(\delta<\gamma-1\). It combines fastest-line speed tails, the diameter estimate, and a dyadic sequence of radii to control the geodesic's Euclidean excursion.
- **Theorem 6.1:** the total length \(\ell\) inside a unit ball of the network formed by all geodesics between points of a countable dense Poisson sample, after removing endpoint balls of radius 1, has finite mean. More strongly, \(\mathbb P(\ell>C(\log(C_1/\varepsilon))^2)\le\varepsilon\) for small \(\varepsilon\), implying \(\mathbb E e^{\delta\sqrt{\ell}}<\infty\) for some \(\delta>0\). The proof uses a time-diameter bound on an annulus, a finite set of fast lines, pigeonholing, and uniqueness to show all contributing geodesics reuse finitely many subgeodesics.
- **Theorem 6.2:** the Poisson-line geodesic network is a SIRSN for \(\gamma>d\), by the route uniqueness, exact similarity invariance of the marked-line law, Theorem 5.1, and Theorem 6.1.

## A136 interface

This is a concrete SIRSN model and proves integrability of one specified geodesic and a finite-intensity bound on a truncated network joining a dense Poisson set. It does **not** state \(\mathbb E\sup_i\operatorname{len}r(0,U_i)<\infty\) for iid uniform \(U_i\) in the unit disk. The dense-Poisson network in Theorem 6.1 consists of routes between sampled endpoints with their endpoint neighborhoods removed; it is not the star family from a fixed origin. The theorem's local union-length control may be relevant to a future argument, but the missing comparison is not proved here. No all-SIRSN result or answer to Aldous's Open Problem 34 is claimed.

## Proof boundary

Kahn's result upgrades the planar Poisson-line process from Kendall's earlier pre-SIRSN status to a full SIRSN and proves all-dimensional uniqueness. The uniqueness proof in dimensions \(d\ge3\) uses the projected normal distribution on projective space and many-direction forcing; it should not be replaced with a planar line-intersection argument.
