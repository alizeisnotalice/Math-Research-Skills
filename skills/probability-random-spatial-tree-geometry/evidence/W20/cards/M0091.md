# M0091 — True scale-invariant random spatial networks

**Source:** David Aldous and Karthik Ganesan, *True scale-invariant random spatial networks*, PNAS 110 (2013), 8782–8785, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1304329110. Author-hosted published 4-page PDF, fully read, PDF pp. 1–4. Local PDF SHA256 is recorded in evidence/W20/sources.json.

## Model and claims in this overview

- Primitive object: for every pair \(z,z'\in\mathbb R^2\), a route \(r(z,z')=r(z',z)\). Routes satisfy compatibility: if \(y\) lies on \(r(z,z')\), that route is the concatenation of \(r(z,y)\) and \(r(y,z')\). A finite set of points induces a spanning subnetwork by union of its pairwise routes.
- Distributional axioms: translation and rotation invariance and exact Euclidean scaling invariance of all finite sampled subnetworks. For a rate-\(\lambda\) Poisson point process \(\Xi(\lambda)\), the sampled subnetwork \(\mathcal S(\lambda)\) is the union of routes between its points.
- Finiteness statistics: \(\Delta=\mathbb E D_1\), where \(D_1\) is route length between two random points at Euclidean distance 1; \(\ell\), mean length per unit area of \(\mathcal S(1)\); and \(p(1)\), mean length per unit area of the union of route portions at distance at least 1 from both endpoints, after taking the dense-point limit. A SIRSN is assumed to have all three finite. Scaling gives \(p(r)=p(1)/r\).
- The note describes the binary hierarchy model: shortest-time routes on a dyadic lattice with speed \(\gamma^{-s}\) on roads at hierarchy level \(s\), \(1/2<\gamma<1\), extended and randomized to obtain continuum translation, rotation, and scale invariance. It presents that construction as established in the longer technical paper. The Poisson line and dynamic-proximity constructions are only proposed as plausible at this date.

## Exact relevance and limitation for A136

The 4-page article introduces the SIRSN framework but does not prove an integrability result for a countable family of routes from a fixed point. In particular, its axiom \(\mathbb E D_1<\infty\) concerns one sampled route. It does not control \(\mathbb E\sup_i\operatorname{len} r(0,U_i)\), where \(U_i\) are iid uniform points in a unit disk. The authors explicitly defer technical statements and proofs to Aldous (2014), M0092. This overview is contextual evidence, not a proof card for the maximum.
