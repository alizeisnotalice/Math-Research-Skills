## Model, theorem, and exact scope

The model is a uniformly random rooted plane tree conditioned on its complete degree statistic n=(n(i)), where n(i) counts vertices with i children and |n|=Σ_i n(i)=1+Σ_i i n(i). Condition 1.1 (physical pp. 2–3) requires |nκ|→∞ and empirical degree laws p_i(nκ)=nκ(i)/|nκ| converging pointwise to a probability law p (equivalently, weak/total-variation convergence for these discrete laws; the limiting mean may be below 1).

For a finite plane rooted tree T, let N_T(T_n) count fringe subtrees isomorphic to T, and π_p(T)=∏_i p_i^{n_T(i)}. Theorem 1.5 (physical p. 4) applies to any fixed finite sequence T_1,…,T_m; it states E N_{T_i}=π_p(T_i)|nκ|+o(|nκ|), Var(N_{T_i})=γ_p(T_i,T_i)|nκ|+o(|nκ|), and Cov(N_{T_i},N_{T_j})=γ_p(T_i,T_j)|nκ|+o(|nκ|), together with the centered vector CLT at scale sqrt(|nκ|) and covariance matrix Γ_p=(γ_p(T_i,T_j)). The error here is o(|nκ|), not O(1). The stronger uniform O(1) mean/variance/covariance approximations for a fixed target and arbitrary degree statistic are Theorem 1.6 (physical pp. 4–5); Theorem 1.7 (pp. 5–6) is the rare-count CLT, with μ_{nκ}(T)=|nκ|π_{p(nκ)}(T)→∞. The covariance kernel is η_p(T,T′)=(|T|−1)(|T′|−1)−Σ_i n_T(i)n_T′(i)/p_i, using 0/0=0. Its diagonal is γ_p(T,T)=π_p(T)+η_p(T,T)π_p(T)^2; for T≠T′, γ_p(T,T′)=N_{T′}(T)π_p(T)+N_T(T′)π_p(T′)+η_p(T,T′)π_p(T)π_p(T′), with the source's continuity conventions when some π vanishes (definitions (1.8)–(1.10), pp. 4–5). Theorem 1.5 also permits zero limiting π and may then be degenerate; in (1.14) the center can be replaced by |nκ|π_{p(nκ)}(T_i). The positive-variance conclusion in (1.15) requires |T|>1 and π_p(T)>0. Theorem 1.7 lists three exceptions to its nondegenerate rare-count conclusion: one-vertex target, path with p_1=1, and star with p_0=1.

Theorem 1.5 is not a general recursive-tree CLT: it concerns fixed fringe shapes under a conditioned degree sequence and the stated empirical-degree limit. The simply-generated/conditioned-GW applications (Theorem 7.2, pp. 26–29) split into distinct regimes: critical finite variance; stable domain of attraction; or a subcritical heavy-tail case. Appendix B proves only the first two degree-statistic regimes, not the subcritical case.

## Proof chain actually read

1. Tree coding, §2 (pp. 7–9): the degree sequence of a plane tree is represented by its Łukasiewicz walk. The cyclic lemma/Vervaat transform turns a random cyclic shift of a bridge with increments d_i−1 into the unique nonnegative excursion/tree code. This handles conditioning on the multiset of degrees.
2. Exact pattern counts, §3 (pp. 9–12): Lemma 3.1 gives the exact first moment by summing admissible cyclic placements of the target word. Lemma 3.2 gives the joint factorial moments and separates bound marked subtrees (nested/overlapping) from free disjoint subtrees; it collapses free marked subtrees to leaves after subtracting their degree profiles, producing the exact formula used later. Lemma 3.3 handles special cases (physical pp. 9–12). This step is model-specific.
3. Uniform expansion, §4 (pp. 13–14): Lemma 4.1 expands falling factorials (x)_k/x^k under k/x→0. Substitution into exact one- and two-pattern formulas yields the mean and covariance expansions (Theorem 1.6) with uniform O(1) remainder. Chebyshev gives the law of large numbers.
4. Gaussian limit, §5 (pp. 14–21): for positive π_p(T_j), expected counts are order |nκ|. The proof expands the multivariate factorial generating function in a sqrt(|nκ|) window and bounds coefficients by a product exponential estimate (5.7)–(5.14). A symmetrized second-order term produces covariance γ_p−δπ_p. Appendix A’s multivariate Gao–Wormald theorem (Theorem A.1, pp. 33–37) converts controlled high factorial moments into a Gaussian limit under its scale separation and remainder hypotheses. Types with zero limiting probability are removed by variance/mean bounds. Lemma 5.1 proves positivity separately; it does not follow from the CLT alone.
5. Extensions and appendices: §6 (pp. 21–24) transfers ordered plane-tree counts to labelled trees by random labelling and summing over child orderings. §7 (pp. 24–29) uses conditioned-GW degree-profile limits under its three distinct regimes. §8 (pp. 29–33) proves an additive-functional CLT first for finite-support tolls, then for general tolls only under the explicit square-integrability/truncation gates in (8.4) and (8.18); Problems 8.5 remain open. Appendix A (pp. 33–37) proves the multivariate Gao–Wormald theorem via logarithmic factorial transforms, exponential-moment bounds, and moment determinacy; it notes that another theorem may be more flexible for singular covariance. Appendix B (pp. 37–39) proves the conditioned-GW degree-statistic limit by the cyclic lemma/factorial moments and a local limit theorem, with the scope stated above. References occupy pp. 40–41.

## Use and transfer boundary

Reusable method: derive exact factorial moments from a cyclic degree-word representation, isolate overlap terms, and feed a controlled multivariate factorial-moment expansion to a Gaussian theorem. This is a template only if a target model admits the same conditioned-degree/word representation and corresponding exact pattern formula.

A127 interface remains unproved. Critical Beta-splitting trees with circularly ordered leaves are not shown to be uniform plane trees conditioned on a degree profile. The source does not identify the joint law of two leaves at prescribed cyclic separation, nor their covariance. A separate derivation must give the target degree/ordering law and an exact two-marked-gap/fringe counting identity before this proof chain could apply. This source alone proves no A127 pair limit.
