## Model, theorem, and constants

For a rooted labelled tree, an additive functional obeys
\[
F(T)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}F(B_j)+f(T),
\]
where (B_j) are root branches and the toll depends on the relative order of labels. The main model is a **uniform random d-ary increasing tree for fixed (d\ge2)**: each new vertex is attached uniformly to one of the ((d-1)(n-1)+1) available positions. The number of trees of size (n) is ∏\(_{j=1}^{n-1}((d-1)j+1)\).

**Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 3–4).** Suppose the toll satisfies
\[
(C1)\quad \sup_T|f(T)|<\infty,\qquad
(C2)\quad \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mathbb E|f(T_k)|}{k}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad \mathbb E|f(T_n)|\to0.
\]
Then, for constants μ and σ,
\[
\mathbb EF(T_n)=\mu n+\frac{\mu}{d-1}+o(1),\qquad \operatorname{Var}F(T_n)=\sigma^2n+o(n).
\]
If σ≠0, ((F(T_n)-\mu n)/\sqrt{\sigma^2n}\Rightarrow N(0,1)). The mean constant is
\[
\mu=(d-1)\sum_T f(T)\prod_{j=1}^{|T|}\frac1{(d-1)j+d}
=d(d-1)\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\mathbb Ef(T_n)}{((d-1)n+1)((d-1)n+d)}.
\]
The variance constant is the exact two-sum expression in equation (4), p. 4: with
\[
\phi_k(x)=(1-x)^{-1}\int_x^1(1-w)^{d/(d-1)}w^{k-1}\,dw,
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2={}&-\frac{\mu^2}{d-1}-(d-1)\sum_T\frac{f(T)^2-2f(T)(F(T)-\mu|T|)}{\prod_{j=1}^{|T|}((d-1)j+d)}\\
&+d\sum_{T_1,T_2}\frac{(d-1)^{1-|T_1|-|T_2|}f(T_1)f(T_2)}{(|T_1|-1)!(|T_2|-1)!}\int_0^1\phi_{|T_1|}(x)\phi_{|T_2|}(x)\,dx,
\end{aligned}
\]
where sums range over all d-ary increasing trees. Remark 2 allows (C1)–(C2) for a shifted toll (f+c); Remark 3 replaces bounded toll by (F(T)=O(|T|)). These are explicit source relaxations, not grounds to drop (C2).

Theorem 2 is a separate weighted GPORT result with fixed attachment parameter (\alpha>0), matching the positive-parameter model defined on PDF p. 2; its mean centering is (\mu n-\mu/(\alpha+1)+o(1)), and the toll conditions and weighted variance formula are stated separately. The article says the method also applies to GPORTs but treats only d-ary increasing trees in detail; Theorem 2 is stated on PDF p. 4 without its full proof written out. Thus the displayed GPORT statement and formulas were read, but this card does not claim an independently checked complete GPORT proof. It does not follow by changing (d) in Theorem 1.

## Proof chain, examples, and limits

Sections 3–4 encode (F) and its toll in a multivariate exponential generating function (Z(x,a,b)). Differentiating the tree recursion yields exact coefficient recurrences for the mean and variance. Singular expansion at (x=1/(d-1)), coefficient transfer and (C2) give the constants and linear variance. For a toll with finite support, Lemma 6 derives all centered moments from the recurrence for (Z^{(r)}); even moments have Gaussian leading term and odd moments are lower order. For general tolls, truncate at tree size (m), apply the finite-support CLT, and use the variance of the omitted additive functional plus a distribution-function sandwich (Lemma 7). Thus moment induction alone is not the proof for an arbitrary toll.

Section 6 applies the theorem to occurrences of a fixed fringe shape, counts of size-(k) fringe subtrees/leaves, total subtrees through λog(1+s(T)), automorphism-group size, and orbit counts. The automorphism application verifies (C1) by λog(d!) and proves the required expected toll decay; orbit counts instead use the shifted-toll and linear-functional remarks. These are tree-specific verifications.

For the binary leaf count, let (L_n=\mathbb E[\#\text{ leaves in }T_n]). The growth rule gives the exact expectation recurrence (L_{n+1}=L_n+1-2L_n/(n+1)), (L_1=1), because there are (n+1) insertion slots and (2L_n) are attached to leaves. This is an expectation-level recurrence, not a pathwise identity. Its solution for (n\ge2) is (L_n=(n+1)/3), matching the source size-one fringe-tree mean constant (\mu=1/3). `checks/verify_examples.py` verifies the rational recurrence and asymptotic ratio locally; it is a finite combinatorial check, not a proof of the CLT.

The normal limit in Theorem 2, like Theorem 1, requires the stated nonzero asymptotic variance (\sigma^2>0) to standardize by (\sqrt{\sigma^2 n}). Mean and linear-variance asymptotics alone are not a CLT proof: the detailed d-ary proof uses finite-support centered-moment induction and then truncation, an omitted-toll variance estimate, and Lemma 7 for general tolls satisfying (C1)–(C2).

This theorem concerns additive **tree** functionals under the d-ary increasing-tree growth law. It does not give a CLT for a fixed cyclic-distance pair statistic on a random leaf circle. A toll encoding that statistic would still need its own additivity proof and verification of (C1)–(C2); absent that, the source is a method reference only.
