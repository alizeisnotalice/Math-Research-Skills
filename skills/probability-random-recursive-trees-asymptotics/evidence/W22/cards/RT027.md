## Theorem 1: exact conditioned-GW and approximation hypotheses

Let \(\xi\) be a nonnegative integer offspring variable with \(E\xi=1\), \(0<\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}\xi<\infty\), and, for a fixed integer \(\alpha\ge0\), \(E\xi^{2\alpha+1}<\infty\). Let \(T\) be the ordinary unconditioned GW tree, \(T_n\) the rooted ordered GW tree conditioned to have \(n\) nodes (only sizes with positive conditioning probability), and \(\widehat T\) the infinite size-biased/Kesten tree. For \(T^{(M)}\), retain levels of depth at most \(M\). Let \(F(T)=\sum_{v\in T}f(T_v)\), with
\[
f(T)=O(\deg(T)^\alpha),
\]
where \(\deg(T)\) is the root outdegree. The theorem requires positive numbers \(p_M\to0\) such that, uniformly over every \(N\ge M\),
\[
E\left|f(\widehat T^{(M)})-E\{f(\widehat T^{(N)})\mid\widehat T^{(M)}\}\right|\le p_M, \tag{3}
\]
and a sequence of positive integers \(M_n\) satisfying, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
E|f(T_n)-f(T_n^{(M_n)})|\le p_{M_n}. \tag{4}
\]
Set
\[
a_n=n^{-1/2}\bigl(n^{\max\{\alpha,1\}}p_{M_n}+M_n^2\bigr).
\]
The summability and vanishing gates are both required:
\[
a_n\to0,\qquad \sum_{n\ge1}a_n/n<\infty. \tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{F(T_n)-n\mu}{\sqrt n}\Rightarrow N(0,\gamma^2),\qquad \mu=Ef(T),\quad 0\le\gamma<\infty,
\]
and the centering can be replaced by \(EF(T_n)\) because Proposition 7 proves \(EF(T_n)=n\mu+o(\sqrt n)\). The theorem does not assert \(\gamma>0\), nor give a universal closed form for the limiting variance. The offspring moment condition is \(2\alpha+1\), not merely finite variance when the toll grows with degree.

For finite-support truncations \(f^{(N)}(T)=f(T)1_{\{|T|<N\}}\), the proof uses the Janson local-additive CLT and its variance formula
\[
\gamma_N^2=2E\{f^{(N)}(T)(F^{(N)}(T)-|T|\mu^{(N)})\}-\operatorname{Var}f^{(N)}(T)-\frac{(\mu^{(N)})^2}{\sigma^2},\qquad \mu^{(N)}=Ef^{(N)}(T),
\]
then proves \(\gamma_N\to\gamma\) and controls the centered truncation error. This is a limit characterization through local truncations, not a closed-form variance for arbitrary almost-local tolls.

## Proof chain and applications

The proof proceeds through: (i) level-width and root-degree moment bounds for \(T,\widehat T,T_n\), including \(E|\widehat T^{(M)}|=O(M^2)\); (ii) a quantitative local-law comparison between \(T_n^{(M)}\) and \(\widehat T^{(M)}\); (iii) mean and covariance control for the additive functional by splitting node contributions at depth \(M_n\); and (iv) variance control for tolls supported on size ranges. These estimates give \(EF(T_n)=nEf(T)+o(\sqrt n)\) and a uniform-in-\(n\) approximation by the finite-support local functional. A truncation lemma then passes the local Gaussian limits to \(F(T_n)\), with the summability in (5) controlling the tail variance.

Section 6 derives individual applications, not blanket corollaries: log independent-set counts, log matching counts, log dominating-set counts, and four tree-reduction processes. The independent-set example proves nondegeneracy by a separate local replacement argument; the matching and dominating-set examples establish the theorem's approximation conditions but the article says nondegeneracy is not separately proved for every example. For fixed-depth reductions the toll is bounded by root degree, so \(\alpha=1\) and the assumption becomes \(E\xi^3<\infty\). Their constants \(p_M\) are obtained through model-specific pattern-avoidance recursions and exponential contraction; they are not generic consequences of additivity alone.

## Transfer boundary for A127

The result concerns a *critical conditioned GW tree* and an additive sum of root tolls, with approximation in the Kesten-tree local topology and explicit degree-moment and summability gates. A size-dependent Beta split tree is not thereby a conditioned GW tree. Even if a representation were established, this one-tree additive CLT would not supply two leaves at a deterministic circular separation, their joint conditional law, or the A127 covariance limit. Those interfaces remain unproved.
