## Model and exact statements

The critical Beta kernel is \(q(m,i)=m/[2h_{m-1}i(m-i)]\). CTCS uses independent \(\mathrm{Exp}(h_{m-1})\) split holding times; DTCS counts splits/edges. The paper distinguishes ordered/interval-labelled and unordered trees; its consistency theorem is stated for the unordered versions, and sampling/ordering must be retained when applying the representation.

* Theorem 2.3 (PDF p. 9): delete and prune leaf \(n+1\) from CTCS\((n+1)\); the resulting tree has distribution CTCS\((n)\), and likewise DTCS\((n+1)\) to DTCS\((n)\). Pruning suppresses the leaf's parent and merges its other incident edges, with the unary-parent case reduced to the grandparent.
* Theorem 2.4 (PDF p. 9): from CTCS\((n)\), start at the root, choose a uniform target bud, and traverse the path from the root toward that bud. At each position a stop event occurs at rate equal to the reciprocal of the current clade size (per unit CTCS edge/time length). If it stops before the target bud, attach a side bud, equally likely left/right; otherwise extend the target bud by an independent \(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\) branch to make a bud pair. This produces CTCS\((n+1)\). The proof in §2.4 (pp. 9–12) computes visible-split rates from the deletion consistency; Appendix A (pp. 33–37) gives the longer direct density proof. The traversal is root-to-target-bud, not leaf-to-root.
* Corollary 2.6 (PDF p. 10; alternative proof Appendix B p. 38): the branchpoint separating two distinct uniformly sampled leaves in CTCS has an exact \(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\) law. This is a random-pair branchpoint law, not a fixed circular-separation height covariance theorem.
* Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 15): for harmonic descent started at \(n\), occupation probabilities converge to \(a(i)=6h_{i-1}/[\pi^2(i-1)]\), \(i\ge2\), with \(a(1)=1\).
* Theorem 4.5 (PDF pp. 18, 22–23): in the limiting exchangeable-partition construction, if \(P_{t,1}\) is the mass of the distinguished first fragment, \(Y_t=-\log P_{t,1}\) is a subordinator with
  \[
  \mathbb E P_{t,1}^{s}=\exp\{-t[\psi(s+1)-\psi(1)]\},\qquad \Re s>-1,
  \]
  where \(\psi\) is the digamma function. The fragmentation dislocation measure is obtained by pushing \(dx/[x(1-x)]\) on \([1/2,1)\) through \(x\mapsto(x,1-x,0,\ldots)\).
* Theorem 5.1 (PDF p. 24): the clade sizes encountered moving away from a typical leaf form a discrete-time reverse harmonic-descent Markov chain started at 1. Its upward probabilities are
  \[
  q^{\uparrow}(1,j)=\frac{6}{\pi^2(j-1)^2},\quad j\ge2;\qquad
  q^{\uparrow}(i,j)=\frac{i-1}{(j-1)(j-i)h_{i-1}},\quad 2\le i<j.
  \]
  Given a step \(i\to j\), its sibling clade has DTCS\((j-i)\) law; siblings at successive steps are independent, and the side is uniformly left/right. This is a leaf-fringe description for this model; it is not a two-leaf separation theorem.

## Proof dependencies

The finite split rates yield the deletion/pruning consistency and hence a consistent growth chain. The visible-split calculation verifies the reciprocal-clade-size stop rates and Exp(1) extension. The exchangeable partition construction uses consistent sampling/paintbox limits to define clade masses and the fragmentation process. Harmonic-descent occupation probabilities are reversed by the standard stationary-flow identity \(q^\uparrow(i,j)=a(j)q^*(j,i)/a(i)\); substitution gives the displayed fractions, whose sums are checked by telescoping. Independence of side clades follows from the recursive split construction. The Mellin proof in §6 and Appendix C (pp. 28–30, 38–41) derives asymptotics of the occupation measure from its transform and contour inversion.

## A127 transfer boundary

Keep the original CTCS circular/interval leaf order, uniform cyclic shift, and deterministic separation \(r_n\). The paper's typical-leaf fringe chain and its random-pair \(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\) branchpoint law do not determine the covariance conditional on a prescribed circular gap. A one-leaf fringe limit is insufficient to derive \(\rho(n,r_n)\to1-\alpha\). The representation supports a finite-n/marginal or typical-leaf analysis only after the exact sampling convention is respected.
