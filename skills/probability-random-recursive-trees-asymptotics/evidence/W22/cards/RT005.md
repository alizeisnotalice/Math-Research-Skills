## Exact chain and theorem

For $j\ge2$, the discrete decreasing chain has
\[
p(j,i)=\frac{1}{(j-i)h_{j-1}},\qquad 1\le i<j,
\]
and $p(1,1)=1$, where $h_m=\sum_{r=1}^m r^{-1}$. Let $a(n,i)$ be the probability that the chain started from $n$ ever visits $i$. Since the path is strictly decreasing, $a(n,n)=a(n,1)=1$. **Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 2)** states, for each fixed integer $i\ge2$,
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}a(n,i)=\frac{6h_{i-1}}{\pi^2(i-1)}.
\]
The motivation/application stated on p. 2 is exact: in the critical Beta-splitting tree, the expected number of $i$-leaf subtrees of an $n$-leaf tree is $n a(n,i)/i$. The chain tracks clade sizes along the path from the root to a uniformly sampled leaf.

## Proof dependencies and exact constants

The proof separates existence from identification.

1. **Identify a candidate stationary occupation profile (§2, PDF pp. 3–6).** Any pointwise limit must satisfy $a(i)=\sum_{j>i}a(j)p(j,i)$ and $a(1)=1$. The proposed $b(1)=1$, $b(i)=6h_{i-1}/[\pi^2(i-1)]$ satisfies this by the telescoping sum (2.9); the $i=1$ equation uses Euler's $\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$ identity. Uniqueness is not inferred from these equations alone. The proof introduces the overshoot law $\widehat b_k(j)=\sum_{m>k}b(m)p(m,j)$, proves it is a probability distribution using the double sum identity (2.15), and proves it escapes every fixed finite set as $k\to\infty$. Substitution in the iterated flow identity (2.10) then forces any limiting profile to equal $b$.
2. **Prove the limits exist (§3, PDF pp. 6–12).** Embed the chain in continuous time with rates $\lambda(j,i)=1/(j-i)$, total rate $h_{j-1}$. A shift-coupling gives $|a(x,i)-a(y,i)|\le \mathbb P_{x,y}(S_{\rm couple}<i)$. For comparable initial sizes, maximal coupling has instantaneous coalescence rate at least $c-\ell^{-1}$ while the two states remain within a fixed ratio and above $\ell$. The argument uses the cited absorption-time estimate $\mathbb E_xT_1=(6/\pi^2)\log x+O(1)$ (Theorem 3.1, attributed to [4], not proved in this article) and the fact that the chain started high does not reach a fixed level in bounded time (Lemma 3.2, whose direct proof is supplied in §4). For highly disparate initial states, the larger chain first descends while the other is held fixed; Lemma 3.4 bounds the expected logarithmic overshoot $\mathbb E_y[\log x-\log Y_{T_x}]\le K$ by an absolute constant. The comparable-size coupling then applies after the overshoot.
3. **Close the occupation flow (§3.4, PDF pp. 11–12).** Pointwise convergence plus Fatou gives one inequality in the flow equation. The uniform overshoot bound gives vanishing mass from states above $L$ entering a fixed $i$ as $L\to\infty$, proving equality. This completes Proposition 2.1 and then Theorem 1.1.
4. **Direct non-hitting estimate (§4, PDF p. 13).** For $Z_t=X_t^{-1/2}$, the generator satisfies $\mathbb E_x Z_t\le e^{2t}x^{-1/2}$, and hence $\mathbb P_x(T_k\le t)\le e^{2t}\sqrt{k/x}$. This is the supplied proof of Lemma 3.2.

## Model interface and limits

This is a chain-occupation theorem, not a two-leaf theorem. Its direct tree interpretation uses the critical Beta-splitting model's uniform-leaf root-to-leaf path and that model's transition kernel. The authors' result provides a one-leaf fringe/subtree-size input; it does not identify covariance for two leaves at a prescribed circular separation. The theorem is not a general formula for arbitrary decreasing chains: the transition law must match exactly.

For A127, preserve the CTCS construction, circular/interval order, uniform cyclic shift, and deterministic $r_n$ with $\log r_n/\log n\to\alpha$. Neither $a(n,i)$ nor the typical-leaf chain contains that pair sampling information. The target normalized covariance limit $1-\alpha$ remains unproved by this source.
