## Models and theorem gates

The paper treats two separate families: random m-ary search trees built from a uniformly random key permutation, and linear preferential-attachment (PA) trees. It does not prove a common theorem for arbitrary recursive tree laws.

**m-ary search trees.** For a fixed integer (m\ge2), insert (n) distinct keys in random order; each internal node stores up to (m-1) keys. A full node has (m) ordered gaps/children. Results may be stated for ordered, unordered, or timestamp-labelled trees, with the relevant isomorphism convention fixed (Remark 3.1). The limiting fringe-shape mean for a fixed tree (T_i) with (k_i) keys is
\[
\widehat\mu_i=\frac{\Pr(T_{k_i}\cong T_i)}{(H_m-1)(k_i+1)(k_i+2)}.
\]

**Theorem 3.2 (PDF p. 6).** If (2\le m\le26), then for any fixed finite list of pairwise nonisomorphic m-ary search-tree shapes (T_1,\ldots,T_d), the vector (n^{-1/2}(Y_n-\mathbb EY_n)) converges in distribution to a centered multivariate normal with covariance matrix \(\Sigma\). The exact mean can be replaced by (n\widehat\mu). The covariance is nonsingular when every listed tree has at least one internal node. If external-node-only types are included, deterministic gap-count identities can make the covariance singular; this is not a failure of the CLT.

**Theorem 3.5 (PDF pp. 7–8).** For fixed (k>0) and (2\le m\le26), the number (Y_{n,k}) of fringe subtrees with (k) keys satisfies
\[
n^{-1/2}(Y_{n,k}-\mathbb EY_{n,k})\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_k^2),\qquad
\mathbb EY_{n,k}=\frac{n}{(H_m-1)(k+1)(k+2)}+o(\sqrt n),
\]
with (\sigma_k^2>0). The paper also gives the same Gaussian limit when centered by the displayed linear term. This is a fixed-(k) result; it is not a uniform growing-(k) estimate.

**Theorem 3.7 (PDF p. 8).** Let (Z_n) count two-protected nodes (a node is internal and none of its children is a leaf). If (m\le26), then (n^{-1/2}(Z_n-\mathbb EZ_n)\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma^2)) with (\sigma^2>0), and the center can be replaced by (\mu n), where
\[
\mu=\frac{1}{m(H_m-1)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}
\frac{m!}{(m-\ell)!}\,\frac{(m(m-\ell))!}{(m(m-\ell)+\ell+1)!},
\qquad \mathbb EZ_n=\mu n+o(n^{1/2}).
\]
The paper notes a weaker (o(n)) mean result for all (m\ge2), and gives the sharper error (O(n^{\max(\gamma_m,0)})); (\gamma_m) is the largest real part among non-dominant roots of the polynomial in the spectral analysis. Do not extend the CLT past (m=26): the urn eigenvalue condition used here fails for (m\ge27).

**Linear PA (Theorems 3.9 and 3.11, PDF pp. 9–10).** The weights are (w_k=\chi k+\rho\), scaled so (\chi\in\{-1,0,1\}\), (\rho=w_0>0\). For (\chi=-1\), the admissible finite-degree case has (\rho=m\in\mathbb Z\), with the path case (m=1) excluded. Put (\kappa=\rho/(\chi+\rho)=w_0/w_1\). For a fixed finite family of rooted shapes, the centered vector of counts has a (\sqrt n)-CLT with non-singular covariance; the (i)th linear mean coefficient is
\[
\Pr(\Lambda_{k_i}\cong\Lambda_i)\,\frac{\kappa}{(k_i+\kappa-1)(k_i+\kappa)}.
\]
For each fixed (k\), the number of fringe subtrees with (k) nodes has a scalar (\sqrt n)-CLT with positive variance and mean coefficient (\kappa/[(k+\kappa-1)(k+\kappa)]). Here the PA trees may be considered unordered, or ordered using either of the paper's conventions; the shape equivalence relation must match that choice.

## Proof chain: generalized Pólya urns

For the usual integer-valued finite-type generalized urn, the paper assumes (A1) off-diagonal replacement counts are nonnegative and the diagonal replacement coordinate is at least (-1); (A2) all replacement coordinates have finite second moments; (A3) the largest real eigenvalue (\lambda_1) is positive; (A4) it is simple; (A5) the urn starts with a ball of a dominating type; (A6) (\lambda_1) is an eigenvalue of the dominating-type submatrix; and (A7) at every time (n\ge1) there is a ball of a dominating type. Under the normalized left/right Perron eigenvectors and the strict spectral gap \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda<\lambda_1/2\) for every other eigenvalue, Theorem 4.1 gives
\[
n^{-1/2}(X_n-n\lambda_1v_1)\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma).
\]

The general A7 condition is about a *dominating-type* ball; positive activity alone is not a substitute. The application bridge is specific: on PDF p. 13 the authors show that every positive-activity type in their constructed urns is dominating, remove zero-activity types without changing the nonzero spectrum, and verify that a positive-activity ball is always present. That establishes A7 for those urns. For noninteger-valued replacements, Remark 4.4 replaces A1 by A1′: for each type (i), either there is (d_i>0) such that (X_{0,i}) and the incoming replacements (\xi_{1i},\ldots,\xi_{qi}) are multiples of (d_i) and (\xi_{ii}\ge-d_i), or (\xi_{ii}\ge0); in either case off-diagonal replacements are nonnegative. The remaining assumptions A2–A7 still apply. Check this alternative when using a noninteger PA weight; do not silently apply the integer-valued A1 condition.

If the activity-weighted expected replacement is the same constant (c>0) for every drawn type, then (\Sigma=c\Sigma_I), with the convergent covariance integral defined in Eq. (23). If the intensity matrix is diagonalizable, Eq. (26) gives its eigenvector expansion with denominators (\lambda_1-\lambda_j-\lambda_k). The urn theorem also yields the strong law and convergence of means in the applications.

For m-ary search trees, decompose the tree into “living” fringe subtrees from a finite down-set of target shapes; a type's activity is its number of gaps. Adding one random key chooses a gap uniformly, so the replacement law is local to the selected urn type. Fixed-size fringe counts are linear combinations of the type-count vector. The m-ary urn's dominant eigenvalue is (1); the non-dominant roots are controlled by
\[
\phi_m(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(\lambda+i)-m!,
\]
and the strict half-plane bound needed for an ordinary (\sqrt n)-CLT holds exactly in the stated range (m\le26). The proof of Theorem 3.7 builds a separate finite urn tracking enough local configurations to decide protection.

For PA fringe shapes, the authors first describe an infinite-type urn and then, for the linear weight rule, truncate/aggregate the tail into a finite type. The resulting finite urn has dominant eigenvalue (\chi+\rho) and all other eigenvalues negative; the spectral CLT applies. For a fixed (k), the urn type representing degree (k) / a (k)-node fringe is handled separately as appropriate. The long worked sections compute exact variances for ternary/quaternary examples and degree profiles; they are examples of the urn formula, not additional universal limit theorems.

## A127 boundary

All limit theorems here concern one growing key-insertion urn and fixed fringe-shape or local protected-node counts. They do not give a pair law for leaves at a fixed circular separation, a CTCS/DTCS conversion, or the normalized covariance curve in A127. A random fringe count CLT is not a fixed-distance two-leaf result. Any transfer would require a model-preserving urn construction and verification of its state, spectral gap, centering, and covariance.
