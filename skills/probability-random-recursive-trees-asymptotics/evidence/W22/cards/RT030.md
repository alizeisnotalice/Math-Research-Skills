## Model, results, and hypotheses

The model remains a uniformly random rooted plane tree with prescribed degree statistic nκ; it is not a general recursive tree. Condition 2.1 (physical pp. 3–4) requires |nκ|→∞ and empirical degree laws p(nκ) converging to a probability distribution p. The paper treats three distinct counts: (i) fringe subtrees equal to one specified growing plane tree Tκ; (ii) all fringe trees with a specified growing degree statistic; and (iii) all fringe subtrees of a specified size.

For exact growing shapes, Theorem 3.2 (pp. 7–8) first assumes Condition 2.1, p_0<1 and p_1<1, and mκ=|Tκ|→∞; these are global gates for every branch of the theorem. Under all three gates, λκ=|nκ|π_{p(nκ)}(Tκ)→λ∈[0,∞) gives a Poisson limit (including λ=0) with convergence of all moments, while λκ→∞ gives a standard-normal limit with the stated mean/variance convergence. By contrast, Theorem 3.3 (below) has its separate technical condition and does not establish its λ=0 case. Theorem 3.3 (pp. 8–11) supplies another sufficient criterion:
mκ² π_{p(nκ)}(Tκ) Σ_{i∈D(Tκ)} p_i(n_{Tκ})² / p_i(nκ) → 0,
where mκ=|Tκ|. Under Condition 2.1 and the respective finite-positive or diverging-mean regime it gives Poisson or normal convergence. The theorem does not state the zero-mean case; Remark 3.4 (p. 11) says the authors could not rule out extreme behavior when λκ→0 under this criterion. Do not add that case.

Theorem 3.10 (pp. 14–16) gives a total-variation Poisson bound for a fixed degree profile, in terms of the mean and Σ_i n_T(i)^2/n(i); Corollary 3.12 gives consequences under its condition. The Stein proof marks degree positions in the target word and locally rearranges affected windows. Sections 3.3–3.4 (pp. 16–18) review conditional-binomial and exchangeable-pair methods; the source says the former may be weak when one degree profile has only one shape and omits details for the latter, so these are not full alternative proofs established here.

For counts by degree statistic, Lemma 3.14 and Theorem 4.2 (pp. 17–21) use the exact identity relating a profile count to a sum of exact-shape counts; Theorem 4.4 gives a total-variation estimate. For counts by size, equation (5.3) (p. 21) is
E N_m(T_n) = (n/m) P(d_1+…+d_m=m−1),
where the d_i are sampled without replacement from the degree multiset. Equation (5.4) gives the r-th factorial moment by requiring r disjoint cyclic blocks, with the corresponding without-replacement probability and prefactor n(n−rm+m−1)_{r−1}/m^r. Under Conditions 2.1 and 2.3, nonlattice p, and m→∞, n−m→∞, Lemma 5.1 (pp. 21–23) gives the local-limit expectation scale; Theorem 5.2 (pp. 22–24) proves a Poisson limit when m∼a n^{2/3}, with mean (2πσ_p²a³)^{-1/2}. The proof applies a finite-population local limit theorem to one block, then conditions successively on disjoint blocks to obtain fixed factorial moments. Lattice effects are treated separately in examples; nonlattice is a real hypothesis.

## Proof chain and supporting appendix

1. §2 (pp. 3–7): degree-statistic notation, plane-tree and fringe definitions, cyclic degree-sequence/Łukasiewicz representation, and Galton–Watson notation.
2. §3 (pp. 7–18): exact factorial moments, estimates for growing target profiles, Gao–Wormald normal approximation, separate Stein coupling, then Cai–Devroye and exchangeable-pair alternatives. The local coupling yields the TV estimate, not a model-free transfer theorem.
3. §4 (pp. 18–21): exact profile-count identity, Poisson/normal limits and profile TV bound by reduction to exact shapes.
4. §5 (pp. 21–25): without-replacement block-sum representation; finite-population local limit estimate; sequential conditioning and factorial-moment proof of the n^{2/3} Poisson regime. The scale follows from the size-count mean, not a universal tree-size law.
5. §6 (pp. 25–29): conditioned critical GW applications. Theorems 6.1–6.2 preserve finite-variance or stable-domain-of-attraction, positive-support, and local-limit hypotheses.
6. Appendix A (pp. 29–33): Theorem A.3 gives a uniform local limit for sampling a fixed number from a finite population, under empirical convergence to a nonlattice law, bounded population variance, and a Lindeberg condition. Proof conditions on an independent Bernoulli sample count and uses characteristic-function bounds and Fourier inversion. Appendix B (pp. 33–34) sharpens a numerical constant in the Stein TV estimate. References are pp. 35–36.

## Use and transfer boundary

Reusable methods: exact factorial moments for growing fringe patterns; a locally modified degree-sequence Stein coupling; and the finite-population local limit theorem for without-replacement block sums. Keep their input laws distinct, and retain the empirical, moment, nonlattice, and degree-profile conditions.

A127 interface remains unproved. A critical Beta tree with circularly ordered leaves does not come with this source’s uniformly random plane degree-sequence law. Its size-m fringe subtree is not automatically a leaf pair at fixed circular separation. A proof still needs a target-specific bijection or recursion preserving leaf order and the pair sampling measure, followed by a derived block probability and moment/coupling estimates. This preprint’s methods are candidates, not a proof of that bridge.
