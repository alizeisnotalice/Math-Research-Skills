## Random recursive tree profile and exact recursion

A random recursive tree (RRT) begins with root label 1; each new label (jge2) attaches to a uniformly chosen earlier vertex. Let (X_{n,k}) count vertices at distance (k) from the root, (lambda_n=max\{\log n,1\}), and (alpha_n=k/lambda_n\to\alpha). The mean is exactly
\[
\mu_{n,k}=\mathbb E X_{n,k}=\frac{\left[{n\atop k+1}\right]}{(n-1)!},
\]
with unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind. The profile recursion conditions on the uniformly selected root-branch size (I_n\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}); the two resulting profile terms are independent subtree profiles, one at level (k-1) and one at level (k). The exact recurrence and factorial-moment solution are developed in §§2–3 (PDF pp. 8–11), not assumed from a generic divide-and-conquer theorem.

For (0<\alpha<e), the normalized profile has the source fixed-point law
\[
X(\alpha)\overset d=\alpha U^\alpha X(\alpha)+(1-U)^\alpha X^*(\alpha),
\qquad \mathbb E X(\alpha)=1,
\]
where (U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)), (X,X^*,U) are independent on the right, and (X^*\overset d=X); (X(0)=1). The source constructs this law by contraction in a mean-one Zolotarev class.

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3).** If (0\le\alpha<e), then (X_{n,k}/\mu_{n,k}\Rightarrow X(\alpha)). For (m\ge2), the positive moment-convergence assertion is for the first (m) moments in the stated range (0\le\alpha<m^{1/(m-1)}); the source also literally appends “but not the ((m+1))-st moment.” That negative clause is internally inconsistent with Corollary 2 and is isolated here as a defective source-local sentence: do not use it as a theorem or infer a corrected universal failure threshold from it. Retain the positive moment range and Corollary 2 as stated. In particular, second-moment convergence holds for (0\le\alpha<2). Corollary 1 gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X_{n,k})\sim\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)^2}{(1-\alpha/2)\Gamma(2\alpha+1)}-1\right)\mu_{n,k}^2,\qquad 0\le\alpha<2.
\]
The coefficient is strictly positive for (0<\alpha<2,\ \alpha\ne1); at (\alpha=0) and (\alpha=1) it is zero, so the displayed positive-leading-term equivalence cannot be read as (\operatorname{Var}\sim0) there. Since the normalized profile converges in second moment to the constant (X(\alpha)=1) at these two values, the valid conclusion is (\operatorname{Var}(X_{n,k})=o(\mu_{n,k}^2)), not that the finite-n variance is zero. Corollary 2 states convergence of all moments for (0\le\alpha\le1), and that all-moment convergence fails for (1<\alpha<e). This makes the literal negative clause quoted above unusable; no general exact nonconvergence threshold is substituted for it. At (\alpha=1), use Theorem 2 and Corollary 4 at their stated offset scales; at (\alpha=0), the lower-fringe Theorem 3 has its own normalization and range. Distributional convergence reaches (e), while the positive moment-convergence gates are narrower.

At the critical profile center (k=\lambda_n+t_{n,k}), Theorem 2 (PDF p. 5) assumes 
\[
|t_{n,k}|\to\infty,\qquad t_{n,k}=o(\lambda_n).
\]
Then ((X_{n,k}-\mu_{n,k})/(t_{n,k}\lambda_n^{k-1}/k!)\) converges in all moments, and hence in distribution, to (X'(1)), where
\[
X'(1)\overset d=UX'(1)+(1-U)X'^*(1)+U+U\log U+(1-U)\log(1-U).
\]
If (k=\lambda_n+O(1)), the standardized centered profile has no fixed limiting law. Corollary 4 gives the variance equivalent (p_2(t_{n,k})(\lambda_n^{k-1}/k!)^2), with its polynomial coefficients recorded from PDF p. 5 in the visual audit. For the lower fringe, Theorem 3 (PDF p. 6) gives the Berry–Esseen bound, uniformly for (1\le k=o(\lambda_n)),
\[
\sup_x\left|\Pr\!\left(\frac{X_{n,k}-\lambda_n^k/k!}{\lambda_n^{k-1/2}/((k-1)!\sqrt{2k-1})}<x\right)-\Phi(x)\right|=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{k}{\lambda_n}}\right),
\]
and states matching asymptotic mean and variance at that scale.

## Proof chain and what transfers

For the RRT, the proof first solves an exact recurrence for factorial moments and extracts the mean through Stirling-number/generating-function asymptotics. For (0<\alpha<e), it normalizes the two-parameter profile recurrence and applies the contraction method in a Zolotarev metric; the contraction factor is bounded by ((\alpha^s+1)/(\alpha s+1)<1) for the source-selected (s). Positive moment convergence is then obtained from uniform moment bounds and the exact moment recurrence; this proof chain supports the stated positive range and Corollary 2, not the blanket negative clause quoted above. At α=1, the proof instead expands around the deterministic fixed point and uses central-moment recurrences; at (1\le k=o(\lambda_n)), characteristic-function bounds, polynomial decompositions and singularity analysis feed the Berry–Esseen smoothing inequality. These proof paths and parameter restrictions are not interchangeable.

The BST part is a separate recursive-splitting model: a root splits (n) labels into sizes (J_n) and (n-1-J_n), (J_n\) uniform on (\{0,\ldots,n-1\}). A recursive tree maps to a binary increasing tree by the stated sibling/order transformation, after which internal/external profiles have separate definitions. Theorem 5 (PDF p. 33) gives the BST profile limit only for α in the open interval α_-<α<α_+, where the endpoints solve (z-1-z\log(z/2)=0); all-moment convergence is stated only for (1\le\alpha\le2). Theorems 6–7 handle the α=1 derivative regime and α=2 quicksort regime. Do not substitute the BST split law or its thresholds into the RRT theorem.

The model-specific output is a level-profile law and, for small (k), a normal approximation. Neither model supplies a circular leaf order, a fixed cyclic separation, or a theorem for leaf-pair covariance. The common root split is only a recursion analogy for A127 until a pair-sampling and circular-order argument is proved.

## Scope counterexample to the printed negative clause

At (\alpha=0,k=1), the RRT profile is the root degree (D_n=X_{n,1}=\sum_{j=2}^n B_j), where the (B_j) are independent Bernoulli variables with (\Pr(B_j=1)=1/(j-1)). Thus (\mu=\mathbb E D_n=H_{n-1}\to\infty), (\operatorname{Var}D_n\le\mu), and the absolute third cumulant is bounded by (\mu), since cumulants add and each Bernoulli third cumulant has absolute value at most its mean. The identity (\mathbb E D_n^3=\mu^3+3\mu\operatorname{Var}D_n+\kappa_3(D_n)) gives
\[
\left|\mathbb E[(D_n/\mu)^3]-1\right|\le 3/\mu+1/\mu^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here (k/\lambda_n\to0), so the limiting profile is the constant (X(0)=1): this is a strict counterexample to the literal claim that the third normalized moment fails to converge for (m=2,\alpha=0). It contradicts only that negative clause, not the weak profile limit, the positive moment results, or Theorem 2.
