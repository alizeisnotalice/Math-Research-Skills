# RT007 — Probability distributions on cladograms

## Identity and read scope

- David Aldous, “Probability distributions on cladograms,” author-hosted chapter manuscript dated 22 Aug 1996, in *Random Discrete Structures*, IMA Vol. 76 (1996), chapter citation pp. 1–18; DOI 10.1007/978-1-4612-0719-1_1.

## Model and exact source claims

1. **Sampling-consistent partitions and cladograms (§1–2, pp. 1–7).** The opening analogy recalls the Ewens family from exchangeability and subset-deletion consistency. A cladogram is a rooted binary topology on labeled leaves, with no edge-length/time information; it is distinct from a phenogram. For n leaves, the count of rooted non-plane binary cladograms is c_n=(2n−3)!!. The text discusses uniform and Yule cladograms, and the relation between leaf-labeled binary search trees and induced cladograms. These are model distinctions and context, not a theorem identifying biological data with either model.

2. **Axioms and conjecture (§3, p. 8).** Lemma 1: a continuous-time birth/death construction which chooses species uniformly for splitting/extinction induces the Yule cladogram. Aldous gives exchangeability and group-elimination properties and examples (Yule, uniform, comb). Conjecture 2—not a proved classification—asks whether those are the only families satisfying the stated axioms.

3. **Beta-splitting definition (§4.1, pp. 9–11).** A Markov-branching cladogram is built recursively: for a node with n descendant leaves, choose split size i∈{1,…,n−1} with symmetric law q_n(i)=q_n(n−i); then choose uniformly an i-subset of the labels for one child clade and recurse. A symmetric nonnegative interval-splitting kernel f with ∫₀⁺¹ x f(x) dx<∞ gives
   $$q_n(i)=a_n^{-1}\binom ni\int_0^1x^i(1-x)^{n-i}f(x)\,dx,\quad
   a_n=\int_0^1\{1-x^n-(1-x)^n\}f(x)\,dx.$$
   For −1<β<∞, the beta-density choice is proportional to x^β(1−x)^β, and the normalized split weights are proportional to
   $$\frac{\Gamma(\beta+i+1)\Gamma(\beta+n-i+1)}
   {\Gamma(i+1)\Gamma(n-i+1)}.$$
   The family is extended to −2<β≤−1 using this split-weight formula; the stated parameter range is −2≤β≤∞, with β=−2 the comb limit and β=∞ deterministic midpoint splitting. Do not apply the ordinary beta-density integral at β≤−1, where it is not a probability density.

4. **Special parameters and tree asymptotics (§4.1, pp. 10–12; §5, pp. 13–17).** The source identifies β=0 with the Yule/uniform-root-split law q_n(i)=1/(n−1), β=−3/2 with uniform labeled cladograms, and β=∞ with balanced splits. For β=−3/2,
   $$q_n(i)=\frac12\binom ni\frac{c_i c_{n-i}}{c_n},\qquad c_m=(2m-3)!!.$$
   Let Lₙ be the size of a root branch and Bₙ the size of the branch containing a prespecified leaf. Exchangeability gives P(Bₙ=i)=2i qₙ(i)/n.
   For −1<β<∞, Lemma 3 states Lₙ/n ⇒ Xβ with Xβ∼Beta(β+1,β+1), and Bₙ/n ⇒ Yβ with density
   $$f_{Yβ}(x)=Γ(2β+3)/[Γ(β+1)Γ(β+2)]·x^(β+1)(1−x)^β, 0<x<1.$$
   For β=∞, both fractions tend to 1/2. At β=−1, log(min(Lₙ,n−Lₙ))/log n ⇒ U and log(n−Bₙ)/log n ⇒ U, where U is uniform on (0,1). For −2<β<−1 and each fixed i≥1,
   $$P(min(Lₙ,n−Lₙ)=i)→γβ(i); P(Bₙ=n−i)→γβ(i);
   γβ(i)=κβ^(−1)Γ(i+1+β)/Γ(i+1),\quad κβ=∫₀^∞(1−e^(−t))t^βdt.$$
   Proposition 4 defines D̄ₙ (average leaf depth), D̃ₙ (follow the larger child at each split, averaging ties), and D*ₙ (maximum leaf depth); Dₙ is the depth of a specified leaf and E[D̄ₙ]=E[Dₙ]. For −1<β≤∞, the expectations of these three statistics are asymptotic to ρ̄(β), ρ̃(β), ρ*(β) times log n, and each of the four statistics divided by its expectation converges in probability to 1. At β=−1, (E[D̄ₙ],E[D̃ₙ])∼(3π⁻²,6π⁻²)log²n. For −2<β<−1, the expectation vector is asymptotic to parameter-dependent constants times n^(−β−1), and each normalized statistic has a nondegenerate limit in distribution. The proof discussion uses multiplicative interval-split products/branching-random-walk large deviations for depth/height and recurrences/integral estimates at β=−1; it is an outline rather than a full derivation of every estimate.
5. **Limitations (§4.2–4.3, §6, pp. 12, 17–18).** The paper notes that the beta-splitting family generally fails the group-elimination property. Section 6 lists open directions, including classification of sampling-consistent Markov-branching families. No result here supplies a CTCS circular-gap two-leaf joint law or the W22/A127 covariance conclusion.

## Reusable interface and proof obligations

- **Input:** a rooted binary labeled cladogram law specified at every finite clade size by symmetric q_n, with child labels selected uniformly conditional on split size.
- **Output:** a recursively generated random topology; for the beta family, parameter-dependent split fractions and depth/height behavior stated in Lemma 3 and Proposition 4.
- **A127 use:** direct model-family locator and exact comparison point for the critical beta kernel. To reuse any asymptotic statement, prove that the target uses the same finite-n kernel, edge-length convention, leaf order, and sampling measure. It is not enough that both models are called beta-splitting.
- **Not proved here:** the A127 fixed circular separation joint law; any passage from a random root split fraction to a leaf pair conditioned on a deterministic cyclic gap; or a source-level theorem for all edge-length conventions.
