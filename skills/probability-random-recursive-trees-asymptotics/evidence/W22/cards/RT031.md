## Model and parameters

A standard split tree uses an infinite (b)-ary tree, (b\ge2), a fixed capacity (s\ge1), and an i.i.d. split vector (V=(V_1,\ldots,V_b)) in the simplex. The vector need not be exchangeable. The standing nontriviality assumptions include \(\Pr(V_{(1)}=1)<1\) and \(\Pr(V_{(b)}>0)>0\). At a node containing (n_v>s) balls, child counts are
\[
(n_{v1},\ldots,n_{vb})\overset d=
\operatorname{Mult}(n_v-s_0-bs_1;V_1,\ldots,V_b)+(s_1,\ldots,s_1),
\]
where (s_0\ge0\), (s_1\ge0\), and (bs_1\le s+1-s_0). Here (s_0) balls remain at the parent and (s_1) are forced into each child. Internal nodes may hold (s_0) balls; leaves may hold more than (s) in the fractional model.

A fractional split vector satisfies (V_i\ge0), \(\sum_iV_i\le1\). The leftover probability mass in the multinomial allocation is ball loss. The additional growth condition is
\[
\sum_{i=1}^b\Pr(V_i>0)>1,
\]
so positive-mass descendants form a supercritical branching process. The standard split-tree case has \(\sum_iV_i=1\) a.s. For either model, the unique Malthusian exponent \(\beta\in(0,1]\) solves
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^bV_i^\beta=1;
\]
for standard split trees, \(\beta=1\). Let (I\) be uniform on \([b]\), independent of (V), and (V^*=V_I). Then \(\widehat V\) is the (V^*\)-size-biased variable with Radon–Nikodym derivative (d\Pr_{\widehat V}/d\Pr_{V^*}(v)=bv^\beta\), and
\[
\mu=\mathbb E[-\log\widehat V]=\sum_{i=1}^b\mathbb E[-V_i^\beta\log V_i]\in(0,\infty),
\]
where the function (x^\beta\log x) is assigned its continuous-extension value (0) at (x=0).
The source defines the lattice case by (\log V^*\in d\mathbb Z) a.s. for maximal span (d>0), and otherwise calls the model nonlattice; in the lattice case the first-order coefficient can oscillate periodically in (\log n). This literal dichotomy is directly usable when (\Pr(V^*>0)=1). The standing model assumptions permit zero splitter coordinates, so they do not guarantee that (\log V^*) is defined almost surely. If (\Pr(V^*=0)>0), do not infer “nonlattice” or a constant coefficient by treating (\log0) as outside every lattice. Leave the lattice/constant-coefficient interface unverified unless a separate renewal argument checks the positive size-biased splitter (\widehat V) and proves that the corresponding renewal conclusion applies. This is a source notation/proof-interface ambiguity, not a counterexample to Theorem 2.1.

For a toll (\phi), the additive functional is
\[
X_n(\phi)=\sum_{v\in T_n}\phi(T_n^v).
\]
Examples include (\phi\equiv1) for node count, the indicator that the subtree is a single leaf, (\delta_k(T)=\mathbf1(|T|_{balls}=k)), and (\delta_T(T')=\mathbf1(T'=T)). A random fringe tree is a subtree rooted at a uniformly chosen existing node; fractional split trees may lose balls, so ball count, node count, and leaf status are distinct observables.

## Main theorems and exact hypotheses

**Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 6).** If the mean toll (\phi_n=\mathbb E\phi(T_n)) satisfies (\phi_n=O(n^{\beta-\delta})) for some \(\delta>0\), then a bounded continuous (d)-periodic function (\varpi[\phi]) exists such that
\[
\mathbb EX_n(\phi)=\varpi(\log n)n^\beta+o(n^\beta).
\]
In the nonlattice case (\varpi) is constant, subject to the zero-splitter scope above: for direct use of the paper’s printed criterion, require (\Pr(V^*>0)=1). If zero splitters occur, do not claim a constant or periodic classification until the renewal interface has been independently checked. If (\phi_n\ge0) for all (n) and (\phi_n>0) for some (n\ge s_1), then (\varpi) is bounded away from zero. In general the theorem does not provide an explicit (\varpi).

**Theorem 2.2 (PDF pp. 6–7).** Assume Theorem 2.1 and that (\varpi\) is bounded away from zero. Assume also that (\phi(v)) depends only on the child ball counts (n_{v1},\ldots,n_{vb}), and
\[
\mathbb E[\phi(T_n)^2]=O(n^{2(\beta-\delta)})
\]
for some \(\delta>0\). Then
\[
\frac{X_n-\mathbb EX_n}{n^\beta\varpi(\log n)}\xrightarrow[\ell_2]{}Y,
\quad
Y+1\overset d=\sum_{i=1}^bV_i^\beta(Y_i+1),
\]
where the (Y_i) are iid copies of the unique centered square-integrable solution, independent of (V). The variance is
\[
\operatorname{Var}Y=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\sum_iV_i^\beta)}{1-\mathbb E\sum_iV_i^{2\beta}}.
\]
The limit is zero a.s. exactly when \(\sum_iV_i^\beta=1\) a.s.; this always holds for standard split trees, giving an (L_2) weak law. The paper also states joint convergence for finitely many tolls satisfying these gates. This is a contraction-method distributional limit, not a blanket normal limit.

**Explicit fringe-size formulas.** For (s_0=s_1=0), Theorem 2.6 gives a Mellin-transform expression for the asymptotic frequency coefficient of a fringe tree with exactly (k\) balls. In particular, for standard split trees with (\Pr(V^*>0)=1), nonlattice, (\beta=1), and (k>s),
\[
\varpi[\delta_k]=\frac{1}{\mu k(k-1)}.
\]
In the lattice case (under the literal positive-support scope above) the coefficient is a smooth (d)-periodic function with Fourier coefficients given by the same Mellin transform at the lattice frequencies; it need not be constant. For zero-splitter inputs, neither the constant nor periodic coefficient formula should be selected without an independent renewal classification.

**Theorem 2.11 (PDF p. 9).** Under the same zero-splitter classification scope as Theorem 2.1, in the nonlattice case define (\delta_{\ge k}=\sum_{j\ge k}\delta_j\). Assume either (k>s), or both (s\le s_0+bs_1) and (k>s_1). Let (E_x\sim\Gamma(x,1)), independent of (\widehat V), and let the unique integrable perpetuity solve
\[
\Xi\overset d=(E_{s_0+(b-1)s_1}+\Xi)\widehat V.
\]
Then
\[
\varpi[\delta_{\ge k}]=\frac{1}{\beta\mu}\mathbb E[(\Xi+E_{k-s_1})^{-\beta}],
\]
and \(\varpi[\delta_k]\) is the difference of this expression at (k) and (k+1). As (k\to\infty\), \(\varpi[\delta_{\ge k}]\sim(\beta\mu k^\beta)^{-1}\) and \(\varpi[\delta_k]\sim(\mu k^{1+\beta})^{-1}\). The displayed conditions on (k,s,s_0,s_1) are part of the theorem and should not be dropped when using the perpetuity formula.

**Corollary 2.13 (PDF p. 9).** If (V^*\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,s_0+(b-1)s_1)), then
\[
\mu=\psi(a+\beta+s_0+(b-1)s_1)-\psi(a+\beta),\qquad
\varpi[\delta_k]=\frac{\Gamma(a+k-s_1)}{\mu\,\Gamma(a+k-s_1+\beta+1)},
\]
under the same threshold conditions (the source further gives (k\ge s_1) here). The constraint (\mathbb EV^*\le1/b) implies (a\le s_0/(b-1)+s_1), with equality iff \(\beta=1\).

## Proof chain and usable interfaces

The proof of Theorem 2.1 first writes expected toll mass as a sum over nodes, conditions on split vectors, and approximates occupancy counts by their conditional masses. The logarithm of the size-biased splitter turns the depth sum into a renewal equation. The key renewal theorem yields a constant in the nonlattice case or a bounded periodic function in the lattice case. The paper’s printed lattice classification is stated using (V^*), while the renewal step uses the positive size-biased law (\widehat V); with zero splitter coordinates, an application must justify this interface instead of silently treating (\log0) as a nonlattice value. The proof controls the multinomial fluctuations and the boundary of the finite tree; for (s_0+s_1>0), the residual/retained balls require the paper's coupling estimates.

For Theorem 2.2, recursively decompose the centered additive functional over child subtrees. After scaling by (n^\beta\varpi(\log n)), the child terms have coefficients (V_i^\beta); the stated second-moment bound controls the toll remainder. The contraction method gives the unique (L_2) fixed point and its variance. When (s_0+s_1=0), Section 6.1 Poissonizes the subtree sizes and applies Mellin inversion to compute \(\varpi\); with retained/redistributed balls, Sections 6.3–6.5 approximate the negative moments of the perpetuity with explicit error bounds. A Beta-distributed splitter is a special case, not a requirement for the general limit theorems.

Section 3 applies the method to nodes, leaves, internal nodes, balls remaining, protected nodes, outdegree, logarithmic shape, subtree counts, independence number/domination/related graph parameters, generalized quicksort, partial search in quadtrees, random disk triangulation, and fractional fringe-tree examples. Each example inherits the theorem's toll and model gates; the paper does not assert a CLT for each application.

## Zero-splitter classification check

The standing assumptions allow this standard split vector: for (b=2), let (V=(1/2,1/2)) with probability (1/2), and (V=(1,0)) or ((0,1)) with probability (1/4) each. Then (\Pr(V_{(1)}=1)=1/2<1), (\Pr(V_{(2)}>0)=1/2>0), and (\beta=1). For an independent uniform coordinate (I), (V^*=V_I) has masses (1/4,1/2,1/4) at (0,1/2,1), respectively. Hence the source’s printed (\log V^*) criterion is undefined on an event of probability (1/4). The size-biased (\widehat V) instead has masses (1/2,1/2) at (1/2,1), so (-\log\widehat V) is supported on ({\log2,0}) and is arithmetic with span (\log2). Exact rational arithmetic is recorded in [`../checks/RT031_zero_split_lattice.py`](../checks/RT031_zero_split_lattice.py) and its output. This calculation diagnoses the source criterion’s zero-log interface; it is not a counterexample to the asymptotic theorem and does not itself extend its conclusion to zero-splitter inputs.

## A127 boundary

Theorems 2.1–2.2 study additive functionals of the split-tree/fractional-split-tree ball-allocation model. They do not establish that the critical Beta-splitting leaf process has this allocation law, nor do they condition on a fixed circular distance between two leaves. A127 still needs a size-dependent Beta kernel coupling, circular leaf order, and a fixed-separation pair decomposition with a proved error estimate. The paper is useful only after those interfaces are independently established.
