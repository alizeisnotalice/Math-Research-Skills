## Model and scaling hypothesis

The paper treats discrete rooted Markov-branching trees specified by partition laws $q_n$ (size measured either by leaves or vertices) and a continuum self-similar fragmentation tree. Write $\mathcal S^\downarrow$ for decreasing mass partitions and $\nu$ for a dislocation measure. A fragmentation pair $(-γ,\nu)$ has self-similarity index $-\gamma$, with γ>0. Let $\ell$ be slowly varying at infinity. The core rare-macroscopic-split assumption **(H), PDF p. 13, equation (3)** is weak convergence of finite measures on $\mathcal S^\downarrow$:
\[
n^\gamma\ell(n)(1-s_1)q_n(ds)\ \xrightarrow{w}\ (1-s_1)\nu(ds).
\]
The first-size weight $1-s_1$ matters; this is not unweighted weak convergence of $q_n$.

**Theorem 5 (PDF p. 13; leaf-counted trees).** If the family $q=(q_n)_{n\ge1}$ satisfies (H), and $T_n\sim P_n^q$ is equipped with graph distance and uniform probability measure on its leaves $\mu_{\partial T_n}$, then
\[
(n^\gamma\ell(n))^{-1}T_n\ \xrightarrow{d}\ T_{\gamma,\nu}
\]
in rooted Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov topology.

**Theorem 6 (PDF p. 13; vertex-counted trees).** In addition to (H), assume $q_1((1))=1$ and either $0<\gamma<1$, or γ=1 with $\ell(n)\to0$. If $T_n\sim Q_n^q$ is equipped with graph distance and uniform vertex measure $\mu_{T_n}$, the same normalization converges to $T_{\gamma,\nu}$ in rooted GHP. These restrictions are part of the theorem; for vertex-counted trees, heights are at most $n-1$, which rules out nontrivial continuum limits in the excluded large-scale cases. **Proposition 7 (p. 14)** supplies at least one compatible family $q_n$ for every fragmentation pair with γ>0, with $\ell\equiv1$; it does not say that an arbitrary target kernel satisfies (H).

The principal application **Theorem 8 (PDF p. 15)** treats critical conditioned Galton–Watson trees with offspring law $\xi$ on $\mathbb Z_+$, $\xi(0)>0$, mean 1, and support generating $\mathbb Z$ (so conditioning is available for every sufficiently large size). It splits into finite-variance and power-law stable cases. The proof first represents the conditioned GW tree as a vertex-counted $Q_n^q$ family and checks (H); its assumptions are not interchangeable with the general theorem's bare split-kernel hypothesis.

## Proof chain and technical dependencies

Sections 3–4 (PDF pp. 22–53) establish the fragmentation-tree background and prove Theorems 5–6 and Proposition 7. Put $a_n=n^\gamma\ell(n)$. Under the leaf-counted version of (H), write (H') for (H) together with $q_1(\varnothing)=1$. The proof route is:

1. **Tagged partitions (§3.1–3.2 and §4.1, PDF pp. 23–42).** Lemmas 26–29 use the size-biased finite-restriction identity for exchangeable partitions, the $1-s_1$-weighted assumption (H), and Potter bounds for $n^\gamma\ell(n)$ to prove Skorokhod convergence of the tagged block-size process, stopped split times, and descendant block masses to their fragmentation counterparts. The $a_n$ scaling is applied to graph distances; the transition chain tracks marked-leaf mass.
2. **Reduced sampled trees (§3.2.4 and §4.2; PDF pp. 32–43).** Proposition 25 gives the exact recursive decomposition of the finite reduced tree at its first split: conditional on the stopped partition and its block sizes, the child reduced trees are independent with their respective conditional tree laws. Proposition 22 is the matching continuum fragmentation-tree decomposition. Induction on the number of sampled labels, using Lemma 29 for the first split and these two recursive identities, proves Proposition 30: for each finite label set $B\subset\mathbb N$, $a_n^{-1}T^{B}_{[n]}\Rightarrow R(T_{\gamma,\nu},B)$.
3. **From reduced trees to full-tree GH, then GHP (§§4.3–4.4; PDF pp. 43–52).** Proposition 31 upgrades the reduced-tree convergence to unmeasured GH convergence. The key uniform bound is Lemma 33: for every $p>0$, $\mathbb P_{P_n^q}(\mathrm{ht}\ge xa_n)\le C_p x^{-p}$ for all $x>0,n\ge1$, proved by induction from (H), slow variation and the Markov-branching recursion. Lemmas 32–34 control the omitted branches and invoke the standard approximation criterion for convergence by finite reduced trees. To add the uniform leaf measure, the proof samples $k$ i.i.d. uniform leaves, uses the reduced-tree convergence and exchangeability, and identifies every subsequential measured limit through its sampled-point trees; as $k\to\infty$ these converge to $T_{\gamma,\nu}$. This yields the rooted GHP conclusion of Theorem 5, not just convergence of the reduced skeleton.
4. **Vertex-counted trees (§4.5; PDF pp. 52–53).** The proof adds one “ghost leaf” adjacent to every non-leaf vertex, converting the vertex-counted tree to a leaf-counted family. Lemma 36 checks that this transformed split law still satisfies (H) with the same $(-γ,\nu)$ and ℓ precisely when $0<\gamma<1$, or γ=1 and ℓ(n)\to0. The transformed leaf-counted theorem then gives the limit; the ghost-edge/measure comparison transfers it to uniform vertex measure. The gate is substantive and cannot be omitted.

Sections 5–6 (PDF pp. 54–78) verify the framework for conditioned GW and uniform unordered trees. The conditioned-GW proof uses the offspring-law size bias and regular variation to obtain its split-law scaling. The uniform unordered-tree application is not itself an exact Markov-branching family at each finite $n$; it first compares its root split distribution to a Markov-branching approximation and controls the residual. The application hypotheses and estimates cannot be dropped when importing those examples.

## Transfer boundary for A127

This paper can be a candidate route to a global metric-measure scaling limit only after proving that the target discrete tree satisfies the exact Markov-branching definition, the appropriate leaf- or vertex-count convention, the same graph metric, the exact (H) convergence with a slowly varying factor, and the needed GHP tightness. The critical Beta tree has ordered clades and its CTCS uses random exponential edge lengths; neither the exact edge metric nor A127's cyclic leaf order/pair sampling is supplied by Theorems 5–6. Even a proved GHP limit controls empirical/uniform samples, not automatically the covariance of two leaves conditioned to have deterministic circular separation $r_n$. The A127 $1-\alpha$ limit remains unproved.
