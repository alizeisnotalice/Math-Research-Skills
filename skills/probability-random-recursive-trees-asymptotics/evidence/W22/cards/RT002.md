## Model and exact objects

For a clade of size (m\ge2), its ordered split size (i,m-i), (1\le i<m), has
\[
q(m,i)=\frac{m}{2h_{m-1}i(m-i)},\qquad h_j=\sum_{r=1}^j r^{-1}.
\]
The kernel sums to one because \(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}[i(m-i)]^{-1}=2h_{m-1}/m\). In CTCS the split waiting time is independent exponential with rate (h_{m-1}); in DTCS each split adds one edge. (D_n) and (L_n) are respectively the CTCS time-height and DTCS edge-height of one uniformly sampled leaf. The maximum tree heights are different statistics, treated separately.

For a uniformly selected leaf, the child-clade size (k<n) has probability \(1/[h_{n-1}(n-k)]\). The Laplace transforms obey, for (n\ge2),
\[
\phi_n(u)=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\phi_k(u)}{n-k},\qquad
f_n(u)=\frac{e^u}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{f_k(u)}{n-k},
\]
with \(\phi_1=f_1=1\). Thus
\[
\mathbb ED_n=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\left(1+\sum_{k<n}\frac{\mathbb ED_k}{n-k}\right),\quad
\operatorname{Var}D_n=\frac1{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k<n}\frac{\operatorname{Var}D_k+(\mathbb ED_n-\mathbb ED_k)^2}{n-k};
\]
for edge height, \(\mathbb EL_n=1+h_{n-1}^{-1}\sum_{k<n}\mathbb EL_k/(n-k)\) and the variance recurrence has an additional \(-1\) term.

The source defines \(\phi_n(u)=\mathbb E e^{uD_n}\) (called a Laplace transform there) and gives a denominator \(h_{n-1}-u\). Use real \(u<1\) as the common domain for all \(n\ge2\): \(D_2\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)\), so its positive exponential moment diverges for \(u\ge1\). Do not use the displayed recurrence outside this common domain.

## Main conclusions and their qualifications

* Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3): \(\mathbb ED_n=\zeta(2)^{-1}\log n+O(1)\), and \(\operatorname{Var}D_n=(1+o(1))\,2\zeta(3)\zeta(2)^{-3}\log n\). **Conditionally on the paper's numerically supported h-ansatz** (a full asymptotic expansion of the mean remainder), the sharper form is \(\mathbb ED_n=\zeta(2)^{-1}\log n+c_0-[2\zeta(2)]^{-1}n^{-1}+O(n^{-2})\), with \(c_0\) only numerically estimated, and \(\operatorname{Var}D_n=2\zeta(3)\zeta(2)^{-3}\log n+O(1)\) (Proposition 2.2 p. 11; Proposition 2.3 pp. 12–14). The leading variance equivalent does not require the ansatz.
* Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 3):
  \[
  \mathbb EL_n=\frac{\log^2 n}{2\zeta(2)}+\frac{\gamma\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)^2}\log n+O(1),\qquad
  \operatorname{Var}L_n=\frac{2\zeta(3)}{3\zeta(2)^3}\log^3n+O(1).
  \]
  Here \(\gamma\) is Euler's constant. Proposition 2.7 proves the mean estimate. Proposition 2.8 states the variance expansion and derives exact recurrence (2.43). The last bounded-residual step in its p. 24 proof is held as an unverified proof interface: a unit-row-sum weighted recurrence and bounded-error term do not, by themselves, justify a uniformly bounded residual. Indeed, the same weights \(w_{nk}=1/[h_{n-1}(n-k)]\) with forcing 1 give the source mean recurrence \(m_n=1+\sum_{k<n}w_{nk}m_k\), whose solution is \(\mathbb EL_n\sim[2\zeta(2)]^{-1}\log^2n\) (Theorem 1.2/Proposition 2.7). This illustrates why the recurrence form alone is insufficient; it is not the variance-residual sequence and does not refute Proposition 2.8. The paper's separately reported leading-order result and CLT are not withdrawn. Finite-n recurrence calculations in `../checks/kernel_recurrence_check.txt` are not evidence for a uniform \(O(1)\) remainder.
* Theorem 1.7 (PDF p. 4): both \(D_n\) and \(L_n\), centered by the leading means in Theorems 1.1–1.2 and divided by the square roots of the leading variances displayed there, converge in distribution to \(N(0,1)\), with convergence of all moments. The recursive Laplace-transform proof is in §§2.7 and 2.10 (PDF pp. 19–22 and 26–29); it does not rely on the h-ansatz.
* Theorem 1.6 / Theorem 2.4 (PDF pp. 3–4, 14–17): choose two distinct leaves uniformly from all pairs in the same tree; let their **time-heights** be \(D_n^{(1)},D_n^{(2)}\). Conditional on the h-ansatz, the correlation \([\mathbb E(D_n^{(1)}D_n^{(2)})-(\mathbb ED_n)^2]/\operatorname{Var}D_n=O(1/\log n)\). The proof derives a two-leaf product recursion, solves its leading \(\log^2n\) and \(\log n\) terms, then controls the recurrence remainder.
* Tree maximum bounds are distinct from a uniform-leaf result: Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 3; proof §2.6 pp. 17–19) gives polynomial tails above \((2+\varepsilon)\log n\) for the maximum time-height. Theorem 1.5 (PDF pp. 3, 24–26) gives the edge-height bound with \(\beta=\min_{\alpha>1/\log2}[\alpha+4\alpha^2\zeta(3)/(\alpha\log2-1)]\approx42.9\).

## Proof dependencies

The split-kernel normalization and uniform leaf selection give the exact size-biased child law, hence the one-leaf recurrences. Differentiating the Laplace recursion gives the moment identities. The authors establish comparison sequences through Euler–Maclaurin/harmonic-sum estimates; the variance proof uses the integral \(\int_0^1\log^2(1/x)/(1-x)\,dx=2\zeta(3)\). For the CLT they construct matching upper/lower recursive envelopes for the Laplace transforms and invoke Curtiss convergence; this gives leading moments independently of h-ansatz. The two-leaf result has a separate product recurrence and **does** use the h-ansatz to replace the mean precisely enough to cancel the \(\log^2 n\) terms.

## A127 transfer boundary

This paper's random pair is sampled uniformly over all distinct leaf pairs. A127 instead keeps the planar/circular interval leaf order and chooses a uniform cyclic shift at a specified deterministic separation \(r_n\) with \(\log r_n/\log n\to\alpha\). Neither the pair correlation theorem nor the one-leaf CLT controls this separation-conditioned covariance. It is invalid to substitute the random-pair result for the desired \(1-\alpha\) limit. The finite-n split/height recurrences are useful direct inputs; the pair asymptotic remains an unproved interface.
