## Additive parameter and theorem-level gates

For a rooted tree \(T\) with branches \(T_i\), \(F(T)=\sum_iF(T_i)+f(T)\). The paper's abstract and proofs use bounded tolls, and every class requires exponentially small mean absolute toll in its own size-\(n\) tree law. In particular, boundedness is used to obtain \(|F(T)|=O(|T|)\) in the simply generated proof even though Theorem 2's displayed hypothesis restates only the average-decay gate. Do not apply the result to a toll merely because one of its averages is small.

For the simply-generated family, the weight sequence \(\phi_j\ge0\) has \(\phi_0>0\) and \(\phi_j>0\) for some \(j>1\); \(\Phi(t)=\sum_j\phi_jt^j\) has radius \(R\), and Theorem 1 assumes an interior \(\tau\in(0,R)\) solving \(\tau\Phi'(\tau)=\Phi(\tau)\). With \(\rho=\tau/\Phi(\tau)=1/\Phi'(\tau)\) and span \(d=\gcd\{j:\phi_j>0\}\), the Meir–Moon square-root coefficient asymptotics apply. Theorem 2 (PDF p. 5) requires
\[
\frac{\sum_{|T|=n}w(T)|f(T)|}{\sum_{|T|=n}w(T)}=O(c^n),\qquad 0<c<1.
\]
Then \(E F(T_n)=\mu n+O(1)\), \(\operatorname{Var}F(T_n)=\sigma^2n+O(1)\), with
\[
\mu=\tau^{-1}\sum_Tw(T)f(T)\rho^{|T|},
\]
\[
\sigma^2=\mu^2\left(1-\frac{\Phi(\tau)}{\tau^2\Phi''(\tau)}\right)
+\tau^{-1}\sum_Tw(T)f(T)(2F(T)-f(T))\rho^{|T|}
-\frac{2\mu}{\tau}\sum_Tw(T)f(T)|T|\rho^{|T|}.
\]
If \(\sigma\ne0\), \((F(T_n)-E F(T_n))/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}F(T_n)}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\). The zero-variance case is real (e.g. leaf counts in full binary trees).

**Pólya trees (Theorem 4, PDF p. 12).** If \(t_n\) counts Pólya trees and \(t_n^{-1}\sum_{|T|=n}|f(T)|=O(c^n)\), then \(E F(T_n)=\mu n+O(1)\), \(\operatorname{Var}F(T_n)=\sigma^2n+O(1)\), and a Gaussian limit holds when \(\sigma\ne0\). The exact mean constant is
\[
\mu=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_T\left(f(T)\rho^{|T|}+F(T)\frac{\rho^{2|T|}}{1-\rho^{|T|}}\right)}{\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n\ge1} n t_n\frac{\rho^{2n}}{1-\rho^n}}.
\]
The paper says the variance constant has an explicit but long formula; it does not print that formula, so none is reconstructed here.

**Recursive trees (Theorem 5, PDF pp. 15–16).** If \((n-1)!^{-1}\sum_{|T|=n}|f(T)|=O(c^n)\), then for any \(a\in(c,1)\), \(E F(T_n)=\mu n+O(a^n)\), \(\operatorname{Var}F(T_n)=\sigma^2n+O(a^n)\), and the centered Gaussian limit holds for \(\sigma\ne0\). The constants are
\[
\mu=\sum_T\frac{f(T)}{(|T|+1)!},
\]
\[
\sigma^2=\sum_T\frac{f(T)(2F(T)-f(T))}{(|T|+1)!}
+\sum_{T_1,T_2}\frac{f(T_1)f(T_2)}{(|T_1|+1)!(|T_2|+1)!}
\left(\frac{|T_1||T_2|}{|T_1|+|T_2|+1}-|T_1|-|T_2|\right).
\]

**Binary search trees (Theorem 6, PDF pp. 19–20).** If \(n!^{-1}\sum_{|T|=n}|f(T)|=O(c^n)\), then, for any \(a\in(c,1)\), the mean and variance are \(\mu(n+1)+O(a^n)\) and \(\sigma^2(n+1)+O(a^n)\), and the CLT holds if \(\sigma^2\ne0\). The mean constant is \(\mu=\sum_T2f(T)/(|T|+2)!\). Its variance constant (Theorem 6) is
\[
\sum_T\frac{2f(T)(2F(T)-f(T))}{(|T|+2)!}-\mu^2
+\sum_{T_1,T_2}\frac{4f(T_1)f(T_2)}{(|T_1|+2)!(|T_2|+2)!}\left(
\frac{|T_1||T_2|}{|T_1|+|T_2|+1}-|T_1|-|T_2|
+\frac{|T_1||T_2|}{(|T_1|+|T_2|+2)(|T_1|+|T_2|+3)}
+\frac{|T_1|^2|T_2|^2}{(|T_1|+|T_2|+1)(|T_1|+|T_2|+2)(|T_1|+|T_2|+3)}\right).
\]
All sums are over the corresponding tree class. The constants and denominators above were visually checked on the original physical p. 19.

## Proof chain and model differences

For simply generated trees, the bivariate OGF satisfies the root-branch functional equation with the toll correction \(u^{-f(T)}\); the average-exponential bound makes the correction analytic beyond the dominant square-root singularity. Differentiating at \(u=1\) gives the mean and variance constants; singularity analysis gives linear moments. The quasi-powers/implicit-function lemma for the bivariate generating function gives asymptotic normality. For Pólya trees, the cycle-index equation has an infinite sum, but terms \(k\ge2\) are analytic beyond the dominant singularity; the rooted-labelled-tree singularity carries through. Recursive-tree EGF and BST EGF are handled separately: recursive trees have a logarithmic pole; BSTs use a Riccati differential equation, linearized by \(U=-V_x/V\), and the zero of analytic \(V(x,u)\) moves analytically with \(u\). These are four distinct model proofs, not one theorem over arbitrary recursive tree laws.

## Transfer boundary for A127

This is a useful additive-toll method only after verifying a root-branch decomposition and the appropriate averaged exponential toll bound under the *target* law. Beta-splitting has size-dependent clade splits, not any of the four fixed classes above. The theorem says nothing about fixed circular leaf-gap covariances, and neither a one-root additive CLT nor a generic variance constant supplies the required two-leaf coupling. The A127 interface remains unproved.
