## Model and exact claims

For the random recursive tree (RRT), every increasing rooted non-plane labelled tree on `[n]` is equally likely; equivalently, when label `i` is inserted, it chooses its parent uniformly from the `i−1` existing vertices. `Y_{n,k}` is the number of vertices at root-distance `k`. The theorem assumes `k,h≥1`, so both level counts have positive variance; the root level `k=0` is deterministic. Put `α_{n,k}=k/log n`, `β_{n,h}=h/log n` and suppose these converge to `α,β∈[0,2)`. Define

\[
 f(u,v)=\frac1{\Gamma(u+v)(u+v-uv)}-\frac1{\Gamma(u+1)\Gamma(v+1)},\qquad
 p(s,t)=c_2st+c_1(s+t)+c_0,
\]

with constants `c_i` as in (1.3), `c_3=f_v(α,1)`, `c_4=−f_{vv}(α,1)/2`, and `s_{n,k}=k−log n`, `t_{n,h}=h−log n`. Theorem 1.1 (printed p. 4; physical p. 4) states

\[
\rho(Y_{n,k},Y_{n,h})\sim
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sqrt{(2k-1)(2h-1)}}{k+h-1},&\alpha=\beta=0,\\
0,&\alpha=0,\ \beta\ne0,\\
\dfrac{f(\alpha,\beta)}{\sqrt{f(\alpha,\alpha)f(\beta,\beta)}},&\alpha,\beta\ne1,\\
\dfrac{c_3t_{n,h}+c_4}{\sqrt{f(\alpha,\alpha)p(t_{n,h},t_{n,h})}},&\alpha\ne1,\ \beta=1,\\
\dfrac{p(s_{n,k},t_{n,h})}{\sqrt{p(s_{n,k},s_{n,k})p(t_{n,h},t_{n,h})}},&\alpha=\beta=1.
\end{cases}
\]

Symmetry supplies the swapped cases. In the first branch the square root is over the numerator only. Corollary 1.3 gives a sharp asymptotic sign change at `β=1` for fixed `α∈(0,2)`. A zero correlation conclusion is not an independence theorem.

The RRT width `W_n=max_k Y_{n,k}` satisfies Theorem 1.2 (printed pp. 6–7):

\[
\frac{W_n}{n/\sqrt{2\pi\log n}}\longrightarrow1\quad\text{a.s.},\qquad
\mathbb E W_n=\frac{n}{\sqrt{2\pi\log n}}\left(1+O((\log n)^{-1/4}\log\log n)\right).
\]

The same article separately treats binary search trees (BSTs), built from a uniform random permutation. It distinguishes internal-profile counts `I_{n,k}` from external (null) nodes `X_{n,k}`. Write

\[
\phi(u,v)=\frac{uv}{(2u+2v-uv-2)\Gamma(u+v-1)}-\frac1{\Gamma(u)\Gamma(v)},\qquad
\varphi(u,v)=\frac{\phi(u,v)}{(1-u)(1-v)}.
\]

Theorem 4.1 (external nodes, printed p. 21) applies for `α,β∈(2−√2,2+√2)`. Its asymptotic correlation is `φ(α,β)/√(φ(α,α)φ(β,β))` when `α,β∉{1,2}`; if `α∉{1,2}` and `β∈{1,2}`, it is

\[
\frac{\phi_v'(\alpha,\beta)t_{n,h}-\tfrac12\phi_{v^2}''(\alpha,\beta)}{\sqrt{\phi(\alpha,\alpha)\,p(\beta,\beta;t_{n,h},t_{n,h})}},
\]

and if both limits are in `{1,2}` it is

\[
\frac{p(\alpha,\beta;s_{n,k},t_{n,h})}{\sqrt{p(\alpha,\alpha;s_{n,k},s_{n,k})p(\beta,\beta;t_{n,h},t_{n,h})}},
\quad p(j,\ell;s,t)=\phi_{uv}''(j,\ell)st-\tfrac12\big(j\phi_{u^2v}'''(j,\ell)t+\ell\phi_{uv^2}'''(j,\ell)s\big)+\tfrac{j\ell}{4}\phi_{u^2v^2}^{(4)}(j,\ell).
\]

Here the critical offsets are centered at the corresponding limiting depth: `s_{n,k}=k−α log n`, `t_{n,h}=h−β log n`. The stated uniform second-moment expansion is on compact subintervals of `(2−√2,2+√2)`, bounded away from the endpoints. Theorem 4.1 has two sign changes, at `1` and `2`.

For internal nodes, `I_{n,k}`, Theorem 4.2 (printed p. 23) uses `varphi` above, has `α,β` in the same open interval, and gives `varphi(α,β)/√(varphi(α,α)varphi(β,β))` when `α,β≠2`. If `α≠2, β=2`, the expression is

\[
\frac{\varphi_v'(\alpha,2)t_{n,h}-\tfrac12\varphi_{v^2}''(\alpha,2)}{\sqrt{\varphi(\alpha,\alpha)q(t_{n,h},t_{n,h})}},
\]

and if `α=β=2`, it is `q(s_{n,k},t_{n,h})/√(q(s_{n,k},s_{n,k})q(t_{n,h},t_{n,h}))`, where

\[
q(s,t)=\varphi_{uv}''(2,2)st-\big(\varphi_{uv^2}'''(2,2)s+\varphi_{u^2v}'''(2,2)t\big)+\varphi_{u^2v^2}^{(4)}(2,2).
\]

Its limiting correlation has a sign change at `2`. The external width expectation is asymptotic to `n/√(4π log n)` with relative error `O((log n)^−1/4 log log n)` (p. 22). These are different formula families: do not replace either BST result with the RRT theorem or with the other BST profile.

## Proof mechanism and usable method

For RRT, a bivariate probability generating function for two level counts is derived from the recursive insertion rule; equation (1.5) is the starting exact identity. The authors obtain uniform coefficient/covariance estimates through singularity analysis in the two variables and a saddle-point expansion, including special treatment near the critical ratio `1` (printed pp. 9–12). For width, the proof combines adjacent-level second-moment bounds, a chaining argument over central levels, control of tails using expected profile estimates, and a martingale limit for the profile polynomial (printed pp. 12–20). Some martingale details are referred back to earlier work, so this is not a self-contained new proof of that limit mechanism.

For BSTs, the article derives the corresponding external/internal profile recurrences and covariance formulas (printed pp. 21–25); it explicitly says the covariance proof is analogous and omits details in places. The reusable lesson is a **model-specific two-index recurrence + uniform two-variable analytic estimate**, not a general theorem for arbitrary tree profiles.

## Transfer boundary for A127

These are depth-profile level counts in increasing recursive trees or permutation-built BSTs. A127 asks for a pair of CTCS leaves selected at deterministic circular separation in their ordered cyclic labels. No statement here preserves that deterministic separation, CTCS split law, or ordered-label conditioning. Use only as an auxiliary design pattern after deriving the target's own bivariate recursion, centering/variance, and uniform analytic bounds. It proves no A127 fixed-separation covariance limit.
