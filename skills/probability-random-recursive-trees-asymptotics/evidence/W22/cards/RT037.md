## Definitions and exact theorem interfaces

1. **Tree sampling topology (§§1–6, printed pp. 849–860).** A finite tree is treated as a measured metric space with uniform vertex measure. For each r, sample r iid vertices and examine their pairwise distance matrix; convergence of all fixed-r matrix laws defines tree-limit convergence. A long dendron D=(T,d,ν) has a real-tree base and a probability measure on T×[0,∞); the induced distance is d(x,y)+a+b. For the one-point base Υ_ν, sampled pair distances are sums A_i+A_j, i≠j, with iid A_i∼ν. For ν=δ_a, all off-diagonal distances equal 2a.

2. **Constant-limit criterion (Theorem 3.13, printed p. 855; physical 7–8).** For deterministic rescaled trees c_nT_n and a≥0, convergence to Υ_a is equivalent to
   $$c_n d_n(\xi^{(n)}_1,\xi^{(n)}_2)\xrightarrow{p}2a,$$
   where the two vertices are iid uniform on T_n. The proof reduces fixed-r matrix convergence to convergence of one off-diagonal coordinate because the limit matrix is constant. This is a metric-limit criterion, not a source of a model-specific distance limit.

3. **Random-tree annealed/quenched equivalence (Theorem 8.2, printed p. 864; physical 16–17).** For random rescaled trees and fixed a≥0, (i) c_nT_n→pΥ_a, (ii) annealed c_nd_n(ξ_1,ξ_2)→p2a, and (iii) the conditional tail probability given T_n tends to zero in probability are equivalent. The proof uses Z_n→p0 iff E(|Z_n|∧1)→0, conditional Markov bounds and bounded convergence. This is stated for a constant limiting distance; do not generalize to random dendron limits without extra argument. Theorem 8.3 gives relative compactness/tightness via tightness of random pair distances.

4. **LCA bridge for logarithmic trees (Theorem 12.2, printed p. 875; physical 27).** If a random uniform vertex has depth divided by log n converging in probability to a≥0, and the LCA depth of two iid uniform vertices divided by log n tends to zero, then pair distance divided by log n tends to 2a, hence the tree limit is Υ_a. Proof is the identity d(v,w)=d(v,o)+d(w,o)−2d(v∧w,o). It requires both the depth and negligible-LCA hypotheses.

5. **Conditioned critical GW / Brownian CRT (Theorem 9.1, printed pp. 866–867; physical 18–19).** If offspring is critical with 0<σ²<∞, then n^(−1/2)T_n⇒σ^(−1)T_{2e} in this tree-limit sense. Proof is explicitly a sketch importing Aldous's contour-process theorem: n^(−1/2)C_{T_n}(2(n−1)t)⇒(2/σ)e(t), excursion coding, and coupling edge-uniform measure to vertex-uniform measure within distance 1. The main contour theorem proof is cited, not reproved.

6. **Complete-condensation conditioned subcritical GW (Theorem 10.1, printed p. 868; physical 20–24).** Assume 0<κ=Eζ<1, E R^ζ=∞ for every R>1, and complete condensation Δ(T_n)=(1−κ)n+o_p(n). Then without rescaling T_n→pΥ_ν, where ν=Geom(1−κ) on positive integers, so P(ν=ℓ)=(1−κ)κ^(ℓ−1). Proof chain: fringe-tree frequencies; high-degree vertex and descendant-level mass; two iid vertices lie on opposite hub branches with negligible LCA correction; then sampled distances converge to sums of independent geometric twig heights. The complete-condensation hypothesis is essential; the paper notes cases with several large vertices.

7. **Simply generated type III (Theorem 11.1, printed pp. 873–874; physical 25–26).** For the stated type-III weight regime with complete condensation Δ(T_n)=n−o_p(n), the trees converge without rescaling to the star limit Υ_1. This is a separate extreme-condensation regime, not an extension of Theorem 10.1 to arbitrary subcritical laws.

8. **Split trees and generalized split trees (§13; Theorems 13.1 and 13.4, printed pp. 876–885; physical 28–37).** For ordinary split trees with finite branching b, random split vector V, and χ=Σ_i E[V_i log(1/V_i)]∈(0,∞), Theorem 13.1 gives T_n/log n→pΥ_(1/χ). For generalized split trees, the construction has fixed finite b≥2, allocation vectors N^(n)∈N_0^(b+1) summing to n, independent copies at vertices, and hypotheses (ST1) N_0^(n)≤C_0 a.s., (ST2) N^(n)/n⇒(0,V_1,…,V_b), and (ST3) for every n, P(max_{1≤i≤b}N_i^(n)=n)<1, plus P(max_i V_i=1)<1. Theorem 13.4 yields iid-uniform-vertex pair distance divided by log n tending to 2/χ, and hence the same Υ_(1/χ) limit. Proof dependencies: size-biased child fraction W^(n)⇒W, E[−log W]=χ; an L^1 coupling/uniform-integrability step; a renewal-style sum of log child fractions for a random ball; transfer ball depth to uniform-vertex depth using size controls; and a fixed-level second-moment/LCA bound EΣ_|v|=K V_v²=(EΣ_iV_i²)^K→0. Then apply Theorem 12.2. These assumptions include bounded branching and allocation-specific anti-one-child controls; they are not generic tree assumptions.

9. **CMJ branching-process trees (Theorem 14.3, printed pp. 885–890; physical 37–42).** For the source's supercritical CMJ tree, assume (A1) μ{0}<1; (A2) nonlattice intensity measure (stated as convenience); (A3) EN>1 and N≥1 a.s.; (A4) a Malthusian parameter α>0 with μ(α)=1; (A5) μ(θ)<∞ for some θ<α; (A6ψ) when stopping by a characteristic, E sup_t e^(−θt)ψ(t)<∞ for some such θ; and (A7) E[W_α log W_α]<∞, W_α=Σ_i e^(−αξ̄_i). With β=∫_0^∞ t e^(−αt)μ(dt)<∞, Theorem 14.3 gives T_n/log n→pΥ_(1/(αβ)). Proof: an imported Nerman/Biggins height/profile result establishes random-vertex depth constant; a martingale fringe-proportion argument and a collision-probability limit show LCA depth is o_p(log n); then Theorem 12.2 applies. This applies to specified CMJ models only after a valid embedding and all moment/lattice hypotheses are checked.

10. **Abstract limit representation / proof boundary (§15, printed pp. 890–891; physical 42–43).** The final proof of Theorem 3.15 uses ultraproduct machinery from Elek–Tardos and supplies the paper's additional argument; it is not an elementary finite recurrence. Acknowledgments and the bibliography occupy the last pages.

## Reusable interface and A127 transfer gate

- **Input:** a measured finite-tree sequence plus an explicitly chosen sampling measure and distance scaling. For class theorems, preserve the source's offspring law, split allocations or CMJ process and exact side conditions.
- **Output:** (a) a tree-limit criterion from sampled distances; (b) under class-specific assumptions, a deterministic twig-height limit such as Υ_(1/χ) or a CRT limit.
- **A127 use:** structural methods for a valid future representation, particularly sampling-topology choice, pair-distance criterion, size-biased split renewal and LCA negligibility. RT037 does not model circularly ordered CTCS leaves and proves no fixed-gap joint law, pair covariance, or beta-splitting-specific limit. Every mapping from CTCS to GW/split/CMJ must be proved before any theorem here applies.
