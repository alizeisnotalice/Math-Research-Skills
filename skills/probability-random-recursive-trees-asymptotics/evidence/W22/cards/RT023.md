## Model and additive toll interface

Fix a finite alphabet \(A\), \(|A|>1\), with i.i.d. letters of probabilities \(p_a>0\), \(\sum_a p_a=1\). Each of the \(n\) input strings is an independent infinite string with independent letters of this law. The trie \(T_n\) has the strings as its external leaves/keys. For Poissonization, take \(N_\lambda\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\) input strings, giving \(\widetilde T_\lambda\). Put
\[
H=-\sum_{a\in A}p_a\log p_a>0,\quad P(\alpha)=\prod_{j}p_{\alpha_j},\quad d_p=\gcd\{-\log p_a:a\in A\},
\]
where \(d_p=0\) denotes the aperiodic case. The prefix \(\alpha\) is present with expected input mass \(\lambda P(\alpha)\); incomparable prefix subtries are independent in the Poisson model.

A toll \(\varphi\) defines the additive functional \(\Phi(T)=\sum_{v\in T}\varphi(T_v)=\sum_{\alpha\in A^*}\varphi(T^\alpha)\), with the paper's external-leaf convention. The singleton toll \(\chi=\varphi(\bullet)\) requires explicit correction terms because leaves contribute \(N_\lambda\). The modified fringe trie \(\widetilde T^{\alpha+}_\lambda\) has law \(\widetilde T_{\lambda P(\alpha)}\); this and prefix independence are the key probabilistic interfaces.

## Mean, variance, covariance, and bounded-toll CLT

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 11–12).** If for some \(\epsilon>0\), as \(\lambda\to\infty\),
\[
\mathbb E\varphi(\widetilde T_\lambda)=O(\lambda^{1-\epsilon}),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}\varphi(\widetilde T_\lambda)=O(\lambda^{1-\epsilon}),
\]
define \(f_E,f_V,f_C\) by (3.4)–(3.6), including the \(\chi\lambda e^{-\lambda}\) singleton corrections. Then
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda)}{\lambda}=\chi+H^{-1}\psi_E(\log\lambda)+o(1),\quad
\frac{\operatorname{Var}\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda)}{\lambda}=\chi^2+H^{-1}\psi_V(\log\lambda)+o(1),
\]
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda),N_\lambda)}{\lambda}=\chi+H^{-1}\psi_C(\log\lambda)+o(1).
\]
For \(d_p=0\), \(\psi_X=f_X^*(-1)=\int_0^\infty f_X(x)x^{-2}dx\); for \(d_p=d>0\), \(\psi_X\) is continuous and \(d\)-periodic with Fourier coefficients \(f_X^*(-1-2\pi mi/d)\) and renewal sum \(d\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{kd-t}f_X(e^{t-kd})\). The Fourier series is absolutely convergent for \(X=E\), or if \(f'_X(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{-\epsilon_1})\). The leaf toll must not be dropped: when \(\chi=0\), (3.4)–(3.6) reduce to \(f_E=E\varphi\), \(f_V=2\operatorname{Cov}(\varphi,\Phi)-\operatorname{Var}\varphi\), and \(f_C=\operatorname{Cov}(\varphi,N_\lambda)\). Lemma 3.6 gives \(f_C(\lambda)=\lambda f'_E(\lambda)\) and \(f_C^*(s)=-s f_E^*(s)\) in its stated strip.

**Theorem 3.9 (PDF pp. 14–15).** For bounded \(\varphi\), suppose \(\varphi=\varphi_+-\varphi_-\) for bounded tolls whose additive functionals \(\Phi_\pm\) are increasing. If \(d_p=0\), then
\[
\frac{\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda)-E\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda)}{\sqrt\lambda}\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma^2),\quad
\frac{\Phi(T_n)-E\Phi(T_n)}{\sqrt n}\Rightarrow N(0,\widehat\sigma^2),
\]
with all absolute moments, where
\[
\sigma^2=\chi^2+H^{-1}f_V^*(-1),\qquad
\widehat\sigma^2=H^{-1}f_V^*(-1)-H^{-2}f_C^*(-1)^2-2\chi H^{-1}f_C^*(-1).
\]
For general \(d_p\ge0\), the normal laws are asymptotic approximations \(\overset d\approx\) with the oscillatory variances \(\sigma^2(\lambda)=\chi^2+H^{-1}\psi_V(\log\lambda)\) and \(\widehat\sigma^2(n)=H^{-1}\psi_V(\log n)-H^{-2}\psi_C(\log n)^2-2\chi H^{-1}\psi_C(\log n)\). If \(\liminf_n\operatorname{Var}\Phi(T_n)/n>0\), both Poissonized and fixed-size statistics standardized by their actual standard deviations converge to standard normal, with all absolute moments. Also \(E\Phi(T_n)=E\Phi(\widetilde T_n)+o(\sqrt n)\). The theorem's mean expansion to order \(o(n)\) is generally not accurate enough for centering a CLT: the paper explicitly warns against replacing exact means by that expansion.

**Theorem 5.3 / Theorem 5.5 (PDF pp. 36–38)** give a more flexible route. In Theorem 5.3, for some \(r>2\), require approximations \(E\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda)=a(\lambda)+o(\sqrt\lambda)\), \(\operatorname{Var}\Phi=b(\lambda)+o(\lambda)\), \(\operatorname{Var}\Phi=O(\lambda)\), \(\operatorname{Var}\varphi=o(\lambda)\), and \(E|\varphi-E\varphi|^r=O(\lambda^{r/2})\). Then the centered Poisson statistic is asymptotically approximated by a normal with variance \(b(\lambda)/\lambda\); if \(b(\lambda)=\Omega(\lambda)\), the standardized limit is \(N(0,1)\), with absolute moment convergence for orders \(s<r\). Theorem 5.5 additionally needs \(\operatorname{Cov}(\Phi(\widetilde T_\lambda),N_\lambda)=c(\lambda)+o(\lambda)\), the decomposition into increasing \(\Phi_\pm\), and (5.3)–(5.5) for each sign. It yields fixed-n variance \(b(n)-c(n)^2/n+o(n)\), and a normal approximation with variance \([b(n)-c(n)^2/n]/n\); standardization requires this numerator to be \(\Omega(n)\). These are trie-model theorems, not general branching-tree theorems.

## Proof dependencies and applications

Sections 2.1–2.12 establish trie strings, external-node convention, Poissonized construction, fringe additivity, periodicity, Mellin transforms, and distributional approximation with moment convergence. In §3 and Appendix A, the prefix sum of the toll's expected contribution is a renewal sum over \(\lambda P(\alpha)\); Mellin transform and renewal theory give the entropy factor \(H^{-1}\) and the periodic oscillation. The variance proof uses a two-prefix covariance sum and absolute convergence. Section 4 works out sizes, fringe-size/tree counts, protected nodes, logarithmic subtree counts and shape; it contains a warning example where nontrivial tolls can yield deterministic fixed-n functionals and zero variance. Section 5 states Poisson and conditional CLTs; §6 proves them by decomposing the trie at depth \(m(\lambda)\), where the level-(m\) modified fringe tries are independent, controlling the remainder and applying Lyapunov/Lindeberg plus Cramér–Slutsky. For fixed n, conditioning on the Poisson leaf count uses stochastic monotonicity (Nerman's theorem) and subtracts the regression covariance \(c^2/n\). Sections 7–8 prove the renewal asymptotics and the bounded-toll theorem. Appendices A–C prove the renewal/Mellin statement, the distribution-plus-moments approximation lemma, and a lower bound showing aperiodic error can decay unusually slowly.

**Theorem 4.4 (PDF pp. 24–25; equations (4.30)–(4.31)).** For each fixed \(k\ge1\), the conditional probability that a uniformly sampled node of \(T_n\) has a fringe trie with \(k\) external leaves is given by (4.30), with an \(o_p(1)\) error. For \(k\ge2\), its leading term is \(\psi_{E,k}(\log n)/(\psi_{E,i}(\log n)+H)\); for \(k=1\), it is \(H/(\psi_{E,i}(\log n)+H)\). If and only as assumed in the convergence clause, \(d_p=0\), these conditional probabilities converge in probability to \(1/((1+H)k(k-1))\) for \(k\ge2\) and \(H/(1+H)\) for \(k=1\). When \(d_p>0\), (4.30) gives periodic oscillatory asymptotics instead; Remark 4.5 says the quenched statement also implies its annealed counterpart. In particular, substituting \(H=\log2\) for a symmetric binary alphabet is not licensed as a limit by (4.31), since that letter law is periodic with \(d_p=\log2>0\); use the periodic expression (4.30) there. This result concerns a uniformly sampled node's fringe trie, not Beta-splitting leaf clades.

## Transfer boundary for A127

The paper assumes independent strings with fixed letter probabilities and a prefix tree with independent Poissonized subtries. The critical Beta-splitting model instead has a size-dependent split kernel and the target statistic conditions two ordered leaves on a deterministic circular gap. Neither the trie recursion nor the one-leaf fringe law proves that pair-conditioned covariance. Even a toll CLT would require a model-preserving representation, exact toll moment bounds, monotonicity decomposition, Poisson-deconditioning covariance, and nonzero variance. A127 remains open.
