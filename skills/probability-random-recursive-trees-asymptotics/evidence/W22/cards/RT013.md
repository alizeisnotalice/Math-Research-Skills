## Statistics and rooted theorem

For a finite tree $T$, the distance profile $\Lambda_T(k)$ counts ordered vertex pairs at graph distance $k$, with the diagonal pairs at distance zero included. The profile is linearly interpolated between integer arguments. In the conditioned Galton–Watson model, the offspring distribution has mean 1 and finite variance $\sigma^2>0$; $T_n$ is conditioned to have $n$ vertices along sizes of positive conditioning probability.

**Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 2; equation (3)).** In the space $C[0,\infty]$,
\[
n^{-3/2}\Lambda_{T_n}(x n^{1/2})\xrightarrow{d}\frac{\sigma}{2}\Lambda_e\!\left(\frac{\sigma x}{2}\right),
\]
where $\Lambda_e$ is the Brownian-excursion pair-distance density, characterized for bounded measurable $f$ by
\[
\int_0^\infty f(x)\Lambda_e(x)\,dx
=2\int_{0<s<t<1} f\!\left(e(s)+e(t)-2\min_{u\in[s,t]}e(u)\right)dsdt.
\]
This normalization is verified both on the rendered theorem page and in the later contour-process proof (PDF pp. 21–22); it is σ/2 multiplying the whole profile, with argument σx/2.

## Proof route, unrooted extension and regularity

Sections 10–12 (PDF pp. 19–24) convert the finite-tree contour process into an empirical pair-distance measure. The rescaled contour converges to $2e/\sigma$. For a compactly supported test function, the ordered vertex-pair sum is a Riemann sum over contour times and converges to the excursion distance measure. Tightness of the profile is then obtained using averaged re-rootings and moment/width bounds; the authors identify the continuous density through the excursion distance functional. The contour-level weak convergence alone only gives a measure-valued limit; it does not by itself establish convergence of the pointwise profiles in $C[0,\infty]$.

**Theorem 11.2 (PDF p. 22)** gives the matching unrooted simply generated result under additional weight-sequence hypotheses. The weight sequence $w$ must have an exponential generating function with positive radius of convergence; its associated equivalent probability sequence $p$ must be choosable with mean $\mu(p)=1$ and finite variance $\sigma^2(p)<\infty$. With $\Lambda_n$ the distance profile of the unrooted weighted tree, the same convergence holds with $\sigma=\sigma(p)$. Lemma 11.1 (same page) first proves convergence against every continuous compactly supported $f$: the limiting pair-distance measure integrates $f((2/\sigma)d(s,t;e))$ over $[0,1]^2$. The proof compares to a leaf-biased rooted tree whose total-variation distance tends to zero, isolates its unique large branch, and invokes the rooted modified-GW result. The GHP/profile limit is not asserted without these weight and finite-variance conditions.

The final regularity analysis is in §15 (PDF pp. 28–39): it proves that the excursion distance density is smoother than the excursion local-time profile, including Hölder regularity below order 1 and almost-everywhere differentiability; these regularity claims concern the limiting Brownian-excursion density, not an arbitrary tree's finite profile. §§13–14 treat Wiener-index consequences and moment estimates; §16 records further remarks/open questions.

## A127 transfer boundary

This theorem concerns the empirical profile of all ordered vertex pairs in finite-variance conditioned GW trees, after $n^{1/2}$ graph-distance scaling. It is a useful template for turning an empirical pair-distance measure into a profile when the needed tightness and continuity estimates are available. It does not condition the pair on a deterministic circular separation, preserve an ordered/cyclic leaf labeling, or give a CTCS exponential edge metric. Nor does it provide the corresponding scaling for the size-dependent critical Beta tree. Thus it does not prove the fixed-separation covariance $\rho(n,r_n)\to1-\alpha$ in A127.
