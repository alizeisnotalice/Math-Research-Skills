## Model, definitions, and exact transfer

All simply generated trees in the paper are rooted, ordered (plane). Given nonnegative weights (w_k), define
\[
\Phi(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_kt^k,
\]
with radius of convergence \(\rho\). The parameter \(\tau>0\) is the unique solution of \(\tau\Phi'(\tau)=\Phi(\tau)<\infty\) in \([0,\rho]\), if one exists; otherwise \(\tau=\rho\). In either case \(\Phi(\tau)<\infty\), and
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\tau^k}{\Phi(\tau)},\qquad \Phi_\tau(t)=\frac{\Phi(\tau t)}{\Phi(\tau)}.
\]
For each size \(n\) with at least one positive-weight tree, \(T_n\) is sampled proportional to \(w(T)=\prod_{v\in T}w_{d^+(v)}\). The associated Galton–Watson offspring law is \((\pi_k)\); it is critical in the interior-solution case and subcritical in the no-solution boundary case, and is a.s. finite in both. Only admissible sizes are meaningful.

For a rooted tree \(T\), let \(T_v\) be the fringe subtree at node \(v\), and let \(T_*\) be the fringe subtree at a uniformly sampled node. A node is protected if its fringe root is not a leaf and none of its children is a leaf. If \(p_p(T)\) is the protected-node fraction, then \(p_p(T)=\Pr(T_*\text{ has protected root}\mid T)\).

**Theorem 1.1 (PDF p. 3).** For simply generated \(T_n\), the annealed probability \(\mathbb E p_p(T_n)\) converges to
\[
p_* = \Phi_\tau(1-\pi_0)-\pi_0
=\frac{\Phi(\tau-\tau w_0/\Phi(\tau))-w_0}{\Phi(\tau)}.
\]
The quenched fraction \(p_p(T_n)\) converges in probability to the same \(p_*\). The proof invokes the fringe limit in Theorem 3.1 and conditions on the GW root degree: for \(k\ge1\), all \(k\) children must be nonleaves, contributing \(\pi_k(1-\pi_0)^k\); summing gives \(\Phi_\tau(1-\pi_0)-\pi_0\).

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 4).** The annealed law of \(T_{n,*}\) converges pointwise on every finite ordered tree to the corresponding unconditioned GW tree \(T\). The quenched conditional point probabilities \(\Pr(T_{n,*}=S\mid T_n)\) converge in probability to \(\Pr(T=S)\) for each fixed finite \(S\). The cited general result is Janson (2012), Theorem 7.12; the paper does not require a finite offspring variance for this fringe convergence statement.

**Theorem 5.1 (PDF p. 7).** For each fixed integer \(\ell\ge1\), the annealed and quenched proportions of \(\ell\)-protected nodes converge (the latter in probability) to the probability the GW root is \(\ell\)-protected, with
\[
p^*_{0}=1,\quad p^*_{1}=1-\pi_0,\quad p^*_{\ell}=\Phi_\tau(p^*_{\ell-1})-\pi_0\quad(\ell>1).
\]
This is again a root-fringe event; it is not a two-point or covariance theorem.

## Other fringe limits and proof mechanisms

Sections 6–7 repeat the fringe-root argument for random binary search trees and random recursive trees, whose limiting fringe trees are not the simply-generated GW law. For BSTs, Aldous's limiting fringe tree is \(T_N\) with \(\Pr(N=n)=2/((n+1)(n+2))\), \(n\ge1\), and can be realized as the continuous-time binary tree at an independent \(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\) time. The paper obtains protected-root probability \(11/30\) by integrating its explicit non-protection probability (6.3) against \(e^{-t}dt\); fixed-depth protection uses the integral recursions (6.5)–(6.7). For recursive trees, the limiting fringe size has \(\Pr(N=n)=1/(n(n+1))\), \(n\ge1\), and is a Yule tree at an independent \(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\) time. The protected probability is \(1/2-e^{-1}\), with \(\ell\)-protection recurrence (7.3). These are one-node fringe statements and use each model's own fringe law.

## W22 use and non-transfer to A127

This paper is a clean local-event template: identify a statistic as a measurable property of a sampled fringe tree, prove the correct annealed/quenched fringe convergence, then evaluate the limiting root event. It does not control two leaves at a prescribed circular separation, a conditional pair law, or a normalized height covariance. Simply generated, BST, and recursive-tree fringe limits are not interchangeable with the size-dependent Beta-splitting clade law. A127 still needs a joint, separation-conditioned construction and its own error bounds.
