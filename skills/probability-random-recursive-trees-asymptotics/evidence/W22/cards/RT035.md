## Model and standing assumptions

There are finitely many (q) types, activities (a_i\ge0), replacement changes ξ_i, and initial state (X_0\) with (\mathbb E|X_0|^2<\infty). A type-(i) urn draw occurs with probability proportional to (a_iX_{n,i}) and changes the composition by ξ_i. The continuous-time embedding makes an active type-(i) ball live an independent exponential time with rate (a_i) (mean (a_i^{-1})) and replace itself by (\xi_i+\delta_i). A type with (a_i=0) has zero split rate and does not split. The matrix acts on column vectors and is
\[
A_{ij}=a_j\mathbb E\xi_{ji};
\]
this orientation matters when importing formulas from urn papers using (A^T).

The main theorems assume (PDF pp. 4–5):
- (A1) ξ\(_{ij}+\delta_{ij}\ge0\) a.s. for every coordinate;
- (A2) (\mathbb E\xi_{ij}^2<\infty\) for all (i,j);
- (A3) the largest real eigenvalue λ\(_1>0\);
- (A4) λ\(_1\) is simple;
- (A5) the initial urn has a ball of a dominating type;
- (A6) λ\(_1\) belongs to the dominating class.

Here a type (i) is dominating if it can reach every type; the paper allows reducible matrices but imposes the explicit (A4)–(A6) conditions. Under irreducibility these hold automatically. The base results condition urn limits on essential non-extinction. Remark 4.2 separately extends to tenable urns by a superball transform and describes a still broader extension that needs an additional assumption; those extensions do not erase (A1) from the main theorem.

## Exact theorem interfaces

Let Λ be the eigenvalues of (A), with Jordan projections (P_\lambda), nilpotent parts (N_\lambda), and (d_\lambda) the maximum Jordan block size minus one. Split them at the half-growth line:
\[
\Lambda_I=\{\lambda:\Re\lambda<\lambda_1/2\},\quad
\Lambda_{II}=\{\lambda:\Re\lambda=\lambda_1/2\},\quad
\Lambda_{III}=\{\lambda:\Re\lambda>\lambda_1/2\}.
\]
Theorem 3.1 (PDF pp. 9–11) gives (e^{-\lambda_1t}X(t)\to Wv_1\) a.s. The Λ\(_I\) projection, normalized by (e^{-\lambda_1(t+x)/2}), converges in (D(-\infty,\infty)) to (W^{1/2}U_I(x)), a stationary Gaussian process with covariance Σ\(_I e^{(y-x)(A'-\lambda_1/2)}\). At critical eigenvalues, for every (k=0,\ldots,d_\lambda),
\[
t^{-(k+1/2)}e^{-\lambda xt}Q_{\lambda,k}P_\lambda X(xt)\Rightarrow W^{1/2}U_{\lambda,k}(x)\quad\text{in }D[0,\infty).
\]
For supercritical eigenvalues,
\[
t^{-k}e^{-\lambda t}Q_{\lambda,k}P_\lambda X(t)\xrightarrow{a.s.}\frac1{k!}N_\lambda^kW_\lambda.
\]
The theorem specifies the Gaussian covariance kernels and asymptotic independence of the three spectral families; critical Jordan blocks produce the stated polynomial/logarithmic factors. The results also hold under conditioning on (W>0), equivalently essential non-extinction.

For urn time (n), Theorem 3.22 (PDF p. 18) assumes the same (A1)–(A6) and the **strict** gap (\Re\lambda_2<\lambda_1/2); then, conditional on essential non-extinction,
\[
n^{-1/2}(X_n-n\lambda_1v_1)\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma),\quad
\Sigma=\lambda_1\int_0^\infty\psi(s,A)B\psi(s,A)'e^{-\lambda_1s}\,ds-\lambda_1^2v_1v_1',
\]
with (\psi(s,A)=e^{sA}-\lambda_1v_1a'\int_0^s e^{tA}dt) and (B=\sum_i a_iv_{1i}\mathbb E(\xi_i\xi_i')). Theorem 3.23 treats equality (\Re\lambda_2=\lambda_1/2) with normalization \((n\log^{2d+1}n)^{-1/2}\) and Jordan index (d=\max\{d_\lambda:\Re\lambda=\lambda_1/2\}\). Theorem 3.24 treats (\Re\lambda_2>\lambda_1/2): the appropriately normalized centered urn is tight and generally oscillatory; a distributional limit is only stated in the singleton real secondary-eigenvalue case. Replacing this three-case split by the strict-gap Gaussian theorem is invalid.

For continuous-time stopping at (\tau_b(z)=\inf\{t:b\cdot X(t)\ge z\}), Theorem 3.15 (PDF pp. 15–16) additionally requires (b\cdot v_1>0) and conditions on essential non-extinction. It supplies the leading composition limit and spectral fluctuation scales. The urn and drawn-ball versions use explicit dummy types; Theorems 3.21–3.31 (PDF pp. 18–22) state the resulting laws. The variance chapter (PDF pp. 24–28) gives diagonalizable and deterministic-replacement reductions (Lemmas 5.1–5.6).

## Proof chain and limits

The continuous-time proof (§§8–9, PDF pp. 37–53) starts from the martingale (Y(t)=e^{-tA}X(t)). A compensator lemma for jump sums yields exact bracket formulas; Jordan projections bound the brackets in each of the three spectral regions. A martingale functional CLT gives the Gaussian limits below the half-line and at the half-line; (L^2)-bounded martingale convergence gives supercritical modes. A conditional convergence lemma then restores the random (W^{1/2}) factor and proves independence. §10 (pp. 53–57) obtains the supercritical (W_\lambda) distributional fixed-point equation by splitting at the first death, proves uniqueness via an (L^2) probability metric, and derives exact second moments for simple eigenvalues. §11 (pp. 57–66) converts continuous time to hitting times using (\tau_b(z)\sim\lambda_1^{-1}\log z), controls overshoots, and adds dummy types to recover ordinary urn time and draw counts. The covariance integrals are then evaluated by Jordan/eigenvector algebra.

The paper’s applications (PDF pp. 30–37) include two-type urns, random 2–3 trees, recursive-tree outdegrees, plane recursive trees, rotations, m-ary search trees, and branching random walks. These examples each verify their own finite-type urn representation and spectrum. The theorem does not provide an urn encoding for an arbitrary tree statistic.

In particular, this source does not prove a fixed-cyclic-distance leaf-pair law for A127. The proved interfaces are finite-type urn composition/count limits with (A1)–(A6), plus the specified stopping/dummy-ball reductions. To use any such method for fixed circular leaf separation one must first construct the exact state, verify the replacement law and hypotheses, and prove that its output equals the desired statistic; no such bridge is supplied here.
