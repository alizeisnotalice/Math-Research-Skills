## Exact model representation

The linear PA tree starts from one root and adds vertices one at a time, attaching each new vertex to (v) with probability proportional to (w_{d^+(v)}), where
\[
w_k=\chi k+\rho,\qquad \rho>0.
\]
After scaling, it suffices to take \(\chi\in\{-1,0,1\}\). For \(\chi=-1\), require \(\rho=m\in\mathbb N\) so weights stay nonnegative up to degree (m); the path case \(\chi+\rho=0\) is trivial. Ordered versions label children by order of appearance; the PA tree is otherwise naturally unordered.

The split-tree representation has countably infinite potential branching. Each node (v\in\mathbb N^*) receives an independent split vector (P^{(v)}=(P_i^{(v)})_{i\ge1}), a probability vector. A ball arriving at an empty node occupies it; a ball arriving at a full node is routed to child (i) with probability (P_i^{(v)}). The split vectors are sampled once and held fixed during construction. The resulting (T_n^P) is the occupied-node subtree after (n) arrivals.

**Theorem 1.5 (PDF pp. 3–6).** If \(\chi+\rho>0\), then, as unordered rooted trees, for every (n) the linear PA tree (T_n^{\chi,\rho}) has the same law as (T_n^P), with
\[
P\sim\operatorname{GEM}\!\left(\frac{\chi}{\chi+\rho},\frac{\rho}{\chi+\rho}\right).
\]
The entire growing sequences have the same joint law after relabelling children by order of appearance. The same unordered-tree statement holds with the size-ranked split vector (P\sim\operatorname{PD}(\chi/(\chi+\rho),\rho/(\chi+\rho))). It does not preserve the split-tree's original child labels, which may receive balls out of order. For \(\chi=-1,\rho=m\), Theorem 6.3 separately identifies m-ary increasing trees with a finite (m)-component symmetric split vector (\operatorname{Dir}(1/(m-1),\ldots,1/(m-1))).

The proof uses the total PA weight of a tree with (m) nodes,
\[
w(T)=(m-1)\chi+m\rho=m(\chi+\rho)-\chi,
\]
to recursively choose the principal subtree receiving the next vertex. Principal-subtree sizes follow the Chinese restaurant/Pitman–Yor scheme, and their limiting proportions have GEM law. Conditional on those proportions, the recursive routing is the split-tree construction. Appendix A.1 gives a direct urn proof of the GEM size limit; Appendix A.2 gives an exchangeable-sequence argument. The ordered-tree conclusion requires the stated order-of-appearance relabelling.

## Pair statistic and limit

For a rooted tree (T), (h(v)) is depth and (v\wedge w) is the last common ancestor. Define the **ordered** pair sum over distinct nodes
\[
Y(T)=\sum_{v\ne w}h(v\wedge w).
\]
An unordered-pair convention divides this by two. Including diagonal terms changes it by total path length, which is only (O(n\log n)) a.s. in the setting used, so it does not affect an (n^2) limit.

**Theorem 5.1 (PDF pp. 9–11).** For the countably branching split tree with i.i.d. split vector (P), assume
\[
\Pr(0<P_i<1\text{ for some }i)>0.
\]
Then (Y_n=Y(T_n^P)) satisfies (Y_n/n^2\to Q\) almost surely, where
\[
Q=\mathbb E\bigl[f(X_1,X_2)\mid(P^{(v)})_{v\in\mathbb N^*}\bigr],
\quad \mathbb E Q=\frac{1}{1-\mathbb E\sum_iP_i^2}-1<\infty,
\]
and
\[
Q\overset d=\sum_{i\ge1}P_i^2(1+Q_i),
\]
with (Q_i\) iid copies of (Q), independent of (P). Here (X_k) is ball (k)'s infinite route through the split tree and (f(X_1,X_2)) is the length of the longest common initial route segment. The size-biased split variable (W=P_I), with (\Pr(I=i\mid P)=P_i), also gives \(\mathbb EQ=\mathbb EW/(1-\mathbb EW)\).

The proof conditions on all split vectors, turning ball routes into iid infinite sequences. The conditional average of the common-prefix length is the displayed (Q); the strong law for U-statistics gives the limit for the route-prefix approximation. A union bound over at most (n^2) pairs and a Borel–Cantelli argument show the maximum common-prefix length, and hence the tree height, is (O(\log n)) a.s. The discrepancy from ancestor–descendant node pairs is then (O(n\log^2 n)) a.s., negligible after division by (n^2). The fixed-point equation follows by conditioning on the first split and recursing into child subtrees.

For the GEM vector, its size-biased pick has (W\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\rho/(\chi+\rho),1)); thus \(\mathbb EW=\rho/(\chi+2\rho)\), \(\mathbb E\sum_iP_i^2=\rho/(\chi+2\rho)\), and Corollary 5.2 gives
\[
\mathbb E Q=\frac{\rho}{\chi+\rho}.
\]
This is a fixed-point/expectation description, not a closed-form law for all of (Q).

## A127 transfer boundary

The (n^2) pair sum is over all ordered pairs of **nodes**, not pairs of leaves at a fixed circular separation. The theorem averages route-prefix overlap over every pair and uses the exchangeable GEM split representation. It does not establish a conditional pair law given fixed cyclic leaf gap, a leaf-only covariance, or \(\rho(n,r_n)\to1-\alpha\). In particular, an iid-route U-statistic does not produce the A127 fixed-separation statistic without a new sampling design, a circular-order construction, and a proved approximation error. Record the common-ancestor decomposition as an analogy only.
