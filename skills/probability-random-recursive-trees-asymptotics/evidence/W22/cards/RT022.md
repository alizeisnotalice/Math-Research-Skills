## General CMJ setup and standing assumptions

Each individual has an iid life history whose child-birth point process is \(\Xi=\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{\xi_i}\), with ordered birth ages (0\le\xi_1\le\xi_2\le\cdots\). Let \(\mu=\mathbb E\Xi\) be the intensity measure and
\[
\widehat\mu(\theta)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(dt).
\]
The paper's standing assumptions are: (A1) \(\mu\{0\}=\mathbb E\Xi\{0\}<1\); (A2) \(\mu\) is nonlattice (not supported on any (h\mathbb Z), (h>0)); (A3) \(\mathbb EN>1\), and for simplicity (N\ge1) a.s. (the usual extinction version holds conditional on survival); (A4) a Malthusian parameter \(\alpha>0\) solves \(\widehat\mu(\alpha)=1\); and (A5) \(\widehat\mu(\theta)<\infty\) for some \(\theta<\alpha\). The nonlattice assumption is used for constant rather than periodic asymptotics.

For a nonnegative right-continuous characteristic \(\phi(t)\), an extra Nerman condition (A6) is \(\mathbb E\sup_{t\ge0}(e^{-\theta t}\phi(t))<\infty\) for some \(\theta<\alpha\). A stopped tree is (T_n=T_{\tau(n)}), with
\[
Z_t^\psi=\sum_{x:\sigma_x\le t}\psi_x(t-\sigma_x),\qquad \tau(n)=\inf\{t:Z_t^\psi\ge n\},
\]
where \(\psi\) is a chosen weight/characteristic and (T_t) is the family tree by time (t). If \(\psi\equiv1\) and births are non-tied, (T_n) has exactly (n) nodes. In m-ary search trees the stopping weight instead tracks keys; node count is then random.

Nerman's LLN under (A1)–(A6) gives \(Z_t^\phi/Z_t\to m_\phi=\alpha\int_0^\infty e^{-\alpha t}\mathbb E\phi(t)dt\) a.s. The paper derives two consequences useful below:

- **Theorem 5.12 (physical p. 15; printed p. 67).** Under (A1)–(A5), every nontrivial nonnegative weight has \(\tau(n)<\infty\) a.s. and \(\tau(n)\to\infty\). If (A6\(_\psi\)) also holds, then \(|T_n|/n\to1/m_\psi\) and \(\tau(n)/\log n\to1/\alpha\) a.s. The size-ratio conclusion is explicitly excluded in the lattice case.
- **Theorem 5.14 (physical pp. 16–17; printed pp. 68–69).** Under only (A1)–(A5), a uniformly sampled node's fringe subtree has annealed limit (T=T_\tau), where (\tau\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\alpha)) is independent of the CMJ process. For each finite rooted tree (S), the quenched fraction of nodes whose fringe tree is (S) converges a.s. to \(\Pr(T\cong S)\); equivalently, for every subtree-only node property (P), the fraction (n_P(T_n)/|T_n|\to p_P(T)\) a.s. and the conditional fringe law converges. In particular
\[
p_P(T)=\int_0^\infty\alpha e^{-\alpha t}p_P(T_t)\,dt.
\]
Proof: apply Nerman's LLN to the bounded indicator characteristic \(\mathbf1\{T_t\in P\}\), then stop at \(\tau(n)\). Thus a fringe limit is generally an exponential-time-stopped CMJ tree, not a size-conditioned GW tree.

## Extended fringe trees and ancestor bias

An extended fringe tree records a uniformly sampled node together with its descendants and its nearest (k) ancestor generations, with the sampled node marked. **Theorem 5.25 (physical pp. 21–22; printed pp. 73–74)** assumes (A1)–(A5); it proves that a uniform node's depth tends to infinity in probability and the extended fringe tree converges annealed and quenched a.s. to a sin-tree. The ancestor life histories are biased: if child (i) is the distinguished heir, its ancestor history is weighted by \(e^{-\alpha\xi_i}\); (q_i=\mathbb E e^{-\alpha\xi_i}\) and \(\mathbb E\sum_i e^{-\alpha\xi_i}=1\). Proof: reduce finite marked-path probabilities to Theorem 5.14 and factor the survival-to-age probability along the path, yielding the exponential tilts.

**Theorem 5.26 (physical pp. 22–25; printed pp. 74–77)** extends some events depending on all ancestors. Let (P_0,Q) depend only on a node and its descendants and define (P) as (P_0) at the node with no ancestor satisfying (Q). In addition to (A1)–(A5), require
\[
\mathbb E\widehat\Xi(\alpha)^2<\infty,
\quad \mathbb E e^{\delta\Lambda}<\infty\text{ for some }\delta>0,
\quad \Lambda=\sup\{t:T_t\in Q\}.
\]
Then the quenched fraction of nodes satisfying (P) converges a.s. to the sin-tree probability. The proof truncates the number of ancestors, applies Theorem 5.25, and controls the tail by a weighted Nerman characteristic. The additional exponential tail and second moment are real hypotheses.

## Model-specific fringe limits and worked results

Sections 6–12 construct separate CMJ representations and derive model-specific fringe laws. A few exact interfaces relevant to W22 are:

- **Random recursive trees (RRT), §6.1:** the exponential-time Yule-tree construction yields \(\Pr(|T|=k)=1/[k(k+1)]\), (k\ge1\).
- **Binary search trees (BST), §6.2:** the limiting fringe size has \(\Pr(|T|=k)=2/[(k+1)(k+2)]\), (k\ge1\); its tree is the corresponding CMJ family tree stopped at an independent exponential time.
- **Linear PA, §6.4:** for (w_k=\chi k+\rho), put \(\kappa=\rho/(\chi+\rho)\). The limiting fringe-size law is \(\Pr(|T|=k)=\kappa/[(k+\kappa-1)(k+\kappa)]\). Theorem 6.9 identifies the ancestor's heir age as \(\operatorname{Exp}(\rho)\) and gives an independent shifted-rate pure-birth process for ordinary children; Theorem 6.10 proves an exponential heir age forces linear weights (up to the first zero weight). Theorem 6.11 treats an explosive general-PA regime, where a uniform node's fringe tree becomes a singleton in probability.
- **m-ary search trees, §7:** the CMJ individual carries a label giving keys in the node; a random key insertion produces the correct size-stopped tree. For the tree with internal nodes only, Theorem 7.10 gives \(\Pr(K=k)=2/[(k+1)(k+2)]\), (k\ge1\), for the number of keys in the limiting fringe tree. Theorem 7.11 gives \(|T_n|/n\to1/[2(H_m-1)]\) a.s. Theorem 7.13 gives the node fractions with (k) keys, and Theorem 7.14 the outdegree law.
- **Two-protected m-ary search nodes, §10:** Theorem 10.3 gives the limiting fraction among internal-tree nodes
\[
P_2(m)=\frac{2}{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\frac{m!}{(m-\ell)!}\frac{(m(m-\ell))!}{(m(m-\ell)+\ell+1)!}.
\]
This is obtained by evaluating the root-protection probability in the asymptotic fringe tree; it is a fraction of **nodes**, not keys.
- **Fragmentation trees, §9:** use a conservative split/dislocation model stopped at a deterministic threshold/time; lattice and nonlattice qualifications matter, and their construction is not automatically Devroye's ball-allocation split tree.
- **Height, §13:** with each CMJ child displaced by one generation, Biggins's branching-random-walk theorem yields (H(T_t)/t\to\gamma) a.s. and **Theorem 13.2** gives (H(T_n)/\log n\to\bar\gamma=\gamma/\alpha\) under (A1)–(A5) and (A6\(_\psi\)). The constant satisfies \(\gamma^{-1}=-\inf_{\theta>0}\log\widehat\mu(\theta)/\theta\) in this unit-generation setup. **Theorem 13.19** strengthens to every (L_r), (r>0), when additionally (A*) holds: there exists \(\delta>0\) with \(\mathbb E e^{\delta\xi_2}<\infty\).

The survey also covers rank (§10), maximal clades (§11), restricted sampling/random keys (§12), pure-birth representations and exact order-statistic identities in Appendices A–C. Section 14 explicitly records that general CLTs for CMJ stopped-tree fringe counts remain open; even within m-ary search trees the normality question has a phase transition, so a fringe LLN does not imply a Gaussian limit.

## Distinct-work check: RT022 versus RT019

RT022 is a 2017 *Probability Surveys* review (DOI 10.1214/16-PS272) on general CMJ stopped-tree embeddings and many model examples. RT019 is Holmgren–Janson's distinct 2015 *Electronic Journal of Probability* research paper “Limit laws for functions of fringe trees for binary search trees and random recursive trees” (20, no. 4, 1–51; DOI 10.1214/EJP.v20-3627). In the RT022 bibliography, RT019 is reference [59], whereas RT022 itself is separately listed as [61]; the survey also refers to RT019 as prior work. Different title, year, journal, DOI, length, and scope confirm these are not versions of one work. Use RT019's source-specific toll summability and CLT, not the survey's broad CMJ law, when citing its Gaussian theorem.

## A127 transfer boundary

The survey establishes one-node fringe and extended-fringe limits for specifically constructed CMJ trees, plus model-specific height/rank/clade results. Its uniform node or uniform key sampling and ancestor-tilt law do not prescribe two leaves at a fixed circular separation. The CTCS Beta-clade model is not identified here as one of the CMJ examples. No result in this survey proves the A127 fixed-separation pair covariance or its proposed limit; the joint leaf-pair sampling law and its asymptotic coupling remain unproved.
