## Model and main theorem

A particle of mass x≥1 splits into a fixed b≥2 daughter vector V=(V_1,…,V_b) on the simplex, with ΣV_j=1 almost surely and each V_j<1 almost surely; zero coordinates are allowed. Splitting is repeated independently until mass drops below 1. N(x) counts internal nodes of mass at least 1; N(x)=0 for x<1, and N(x) d= 1+Σ_j N^{(j)}(V_jx). External count is (b−1)N(x)+1. This is continuous conservative fragmentation, not an integer-labelled leaf-splitting model.

Set φ(z)=Σ_j E[V_j^z], φ(1)=1, and α=−φ′(1)=Σ_j E[−V_j log V_j]>0. Characteristic roots solve φ(λ)=1, with λ_1=1; let λ_2 be the next root by real part. Theorem 1.3 (physical p. 5; refinements in Theorem 3.1, pp. 8–10) has three regimes:

- If σ=Re λ_2<1/2, then E N(x)=α^{-1}x+o(√x), Var N(x)∼βx with β>0, and (N(x)−α^{-1}x)/√x converges to N(0,β).
- If σ=1/2, with roots on that line simple and the additional interior-support Condition A′, then E N(x)=α^{-1}x+O(√x), Var N(x)∼βx log x, and the centered count normalized by √(x log x) is asymptotically normal, β>0.
- If σ>1/2, and λ_2 and its conjugate are the only roots at that real part and are simple, the mean has a leading oscillatory term Re(γx^{λ_2}); (N(x)−α^{-1}x)/x^σ is approximated in the paper’s ℓ_2 metric by Re(Ξ e^{i Im(λ_2) log x}), with the stated O(x^{κ−σ}) rate, where Ξ is from a unique complex distributional fixed point T(η)=L(Σ_j V_j^{λ_2} Z_j) in M_C^2(γ). The approximation is periodic in log x, not convergence to one fixed law.

Condition A is absolute continuity of each marginal on (0,1) (an atom at 0 is allowed); it implies the technical transform condition B used for root expansions. In the critical case, A′ additionally requires an interior point in the support of the joint split-vector law. Simplicity/uniqueness of roots and the root-line exclusions are separate gates; do not state the trichotomy without them.

## Proof chain actually read

1. §1, physical pp. 1–7: defines the conservative threshold model and roots of φ(λ)=1, states the trichotomy, introduces ℓ_s/Zolotarev metrics, and gives the root conditions.
2. §2, physical pp. 7–8: gives further preliminaries and analytic bounds under Condition A. §3 begins on physical p. 8 and continues through p. 19; Theorem 3.1 (pp. 8–10) provides the root expansion used for mean and variance, with residues and constants under B(δ). This is the complex-analytic input, not a black-box asymptotic for arbitrary dislocation laws.
3. §3, physical pp. 8–19: log change N*(t)=N(e^t), X_j=−log V_j turns the mean recurrence into a renewal equation. Exponential tilt ν(du)=e^{-u}μ(du) is a probability law of mean α; the nonlattice renewal theorem gives m(x)∼α^{-1}x. For sharper terms, a smoothed Laplace transform m̃*(z)=1/[z(1−φ(z))] is inverted and its contour shifted; residues at characteristic roots yield the expansion. The variance obeys its own renewal equation with forcing from the squared centered sum of branch means; root expansions identify β. Lemma 3.7 uses Fourier/Parseval to express the variance constant and proves positivity under the stated support condition.
4. §4, pp. 19–20: defines the minimal ℓ_s and Zolotarev metrics used for recursive limit transfer; moment matching is required for finiteness.
5. §5, pp. 20–31: Theorem 5.1 is a continuous-time contraction theorem for normalized recursive vectors Y_t=Σ_r A_r(t)Y^{(r)}_{T_r(t)}+b_t. It requires coefficient convergence in ℓ_s, strict contraction EΣ_r ||A_r*||^s_op<1, negligible coefficients attached to subproblems with T_r(t)≤τ, and the paper’s moment/centering/local boundedness conditions. Proof: compare to the limiting fixed-point operator in ζ_s, first bound normalized distances by Δ≤ξΔ+R, then show the limsup vanishes by separating large and small subproblem times. Corollary 5.2 gives the Gaussian case under its stated variance normalization. Theorem 5.3 handles complex-root oscillatory means under extra mean-expansion, coefficient, and contraction assumptions. These are conditional tools; they do not create a target recursion.
6. §6, pp. 31–33: applies contraction results to the three fragmentation regimes, checking coefficients/tolls from the preceding expansions.
7. §7, pp. 33–38: examples: uniform binary interval split has φ(z)=2/(1+z), root 1, α=1/2, and exact E N(x)=2x−1; m-ary and rectangle examples exhibit root-phase thresholds, some reported numerically. Beta and simplex examples retain their own dislocation laws.
8. §§8–9 and references, pp. 38–41: lattice and deterministic counterexamples show why regularity/randomness conditions matter. §9 explicitly leaves rigorous transfer to discrete split trees for future work. Complete bibliography was read.

## Use and transfer boundary

Reusable method: a normalized recursive distributional equation can yield a unique limit via a contraction metric once the coefficients, small-subproblem terms, centering, moments, and strict contraction are verified. The renewal/root analysis is useful for a genuine continuous fragmentation equation.

A127 interface remains unproved. The paper itself does not prove discrete transfer in §9. Critical Beta-splitting uses integer clade sizes and circularly ordered leaves; it is not the continuous conservative mass fragmentation assumed here. No result identifies fixed circular leaf-separation counts, their joint recursion, or a target coefficient operator. A127 requires that equation and all contraction gates to be derived separately; do not use the continuous theorem as an established CTCS limit.
