# Method guide: renewal, Palm sampling, queue recurrence, and coupling

Use method selection（本机附件：`references/method_selection.md`） to choose a family and method ID. The exact accepted-scope index（本机附件：`references/accepted_scope_index.md`） is authoritative for the 32 admitted source/use pairs, conditions, locators, and transfer limits. This guide explains the proof interfaces; its examples do not enlarge those accepted scopes.

## 1. Fix the sampling law and input state

Record whether the source samples at an arbitrary time, a renewal epoch, a service completion, a polling visit, or a cycle boundary. Under a stationary renewal process these laws need not agree: a time-stationary origin samples intervals with length bias, while the Palm version anchors an event at the origin. PASTA is specific to Poisson arrivals relative to an observation filtration. Wolff prints an LAA using only the past observation path, but R06/R13 found that this alone does not justify the joint-past conditioning in the paper's martingale proof. For an operational positive use, require future increments independent of the joint past `σ{A(s),U(s):s≤t}`, or a filtration where `A` has compensator `λdt` and bounded `U` is predictable; then the compensated integral gives vanishing normalized error and the arrival/time fractions agree whenever either limit exists. This guard does not guarantee either limit. Wolff's nonhomogeneous extension uses the intensity-weighted time average. Neither result extends to general renewal arrivals by stationarity alone.

For iid renewal gaps, use the age and residual-life information needed to determine the conditional future input law; include queue, service residuals, phase, and visit counters required by the model. A general stationary-ergodic process is not Markovized just by adding the time since its last event.

The exact reader computation in the SymPy script（本机附件：`evidence/originals/d0133930f3a70752-l06_exact_boundary_examples.py`） and saved JSON（本机附件：`evidence/originals/4beb9b8cbdb2bd59-l06_exact_boundary_examples.json`） checks deterministic gap 2 with uniform stationary phase: mean time-stationary age is 1, while prearrival age is 2. It is an exact counterexample to general-renewal PASTA, not a general Palm theorem.

## 2. Palm inversion, cycle bias, and phases

For a time-stationary ergodic discrete-time marked point process with finite positive intensity `λ=E X*_0`, use Sigman–Whitt Theorem 4.1 (physical pp.14–16) in its own within-slot point ordering:

`P(Ψ⁰∈A)=λ⁻¹ E[Σ_{i=0}^{X*_0−1} 1{θ_iΨ*∈A}]`.

For simple points this is conditioning the time-stationary process on a point at the origin; for batches it averages over every point shift, so a typical point sees batch-size bias. The reverse direction uses Theorem 6.1 (pp.24–25), with finite Palm mean cycle `E U⁰_0=λ⁻¹`, to recover time averages by cycle sums. These identities require the source's stationarity, ergodicity, finite positive rate, and integrability; they do not create a stationary queue law.

For period-`d` input, preserve each phase intensity and Palm mark law. Sigman–Whitt Proposition 7.5 (pp.32–33) gives `E L_l=Σ_{n≤l} λ_n P⁰_n(W_0>l−n)` under its phase-wise finite-mean assumptions. Do not average away the phase before applying the formula.

For stationary point processes, Last–Penrose Chapter 9 gives the continuous-space counterpart: Theorem 9.1's refined Campbell formula defines the Palm law for a stationary locally finite process with finite positive intensity, and the Voronoi identity (9.17) recovers the time-stationary law by volume-weighted Palm cycle integration. In one dimension, Exercise 9.4 gives the Palm–Khinchin count identity under stationarity, simplicity, finite intensity, and no empty configuration. These identities convert already-existing stationary and Palm laws; they do not prove queue stability or existence. The source's local-limit Proposition 9.5 has an additional continuity requirement for its unshifted form, and Theorem 9.6's inversion section assumes the stationary simple process is nonempty. See the complete Chapter 9 unit card（本机附件：`references/source_cards/PR-ab7087220d10.md`）.

Holroyd–Peres Theorem 1 gives a nonrandomized extra-head shift `Y` for a unit-intensity, translation-invariant ergodic simple point process: `T_{−Y}Π` has the Palm law, and `Y` is a function of the same stationary configuration. Unit intensity is a standing hypothesis of the paper, including Theorem 1. The explicit balanced-allocation route (Theorems 13 and 21) also uses unit intensity; its Gale–Shapley allocation assigns one unit of Lebesgue volume to each point and the mass-transport proof identifies the shifted law as Palm. Theorem 21's proof is on physical pp.16–17. Theorem 16's broader continuum transport equivalence is stated without proof. This is a stationary-to-Palm shift coupling, not a renewal-cycle inversion formula, a queue-stationarity proof, or a general displacement-tail estimate. Any nonunit-intensity use requires a separate rescaling argument. See the source card（本机附件：`references/source_cards/EXSRC-9aabfa895d6944e7.md`）.

For a zero-delayed renewal count, Lindvall couples the ordinary process to a stationary delayed version using an independent equilibrium delay with density `λ(1−F(y))`. Under iid nonnegative non-lattice gaps with finite first and raw second moments, shared future gaps give `U(t)−λt=E[U(t−Y'_0,t]]`. Blackwell convergence plus `U(t−Y'_0,t]≤U(Y'_0)` and integrability imply `U(t)−λt→λ² E[Y²]/2`. This is a proof of the renewal-function second-order term, not a trajectory coalescence, TV rate, or queue-stability theorem; it imports Blackwell's theorem and a linear-growth bound. See the complete 2-page Lindvall paper card（本机附件：`references/source_cards/EXSRC-0291117ccd86d3ac.md`）.

## 3. Stability and stationary queue construction

Separate a stationary solution of a workload recursion from positive Harris recurrence of an augmented Markov process. For Foster–Lyapunov or fluid methods, record the recurrence notion, irreducibility/small-set assumptions, primitive moments, and service discipline. A sufficient drift/fluid bound is not an iff criterion.

For the scalar Lindley recursion, Györfi–Morvai Theorem 1 (physical pp.2–5) assumes a **two-sided stationary ergodic increment sequence** with strict `E Z<0`. Its stationary solution is the pullback supremum of backward partial sums; the recursion from any finite initial state eventually equals that stationary recursion after an a.s. finite time. Their G/G/1 corollary requires the **net-increment sequence itself** to be stationary ergodic. Separate stationarity of arrivals and services is not enough. This exact scalar recursion result does not imply positive Harris recurrence for a larger queue/environment state or TV convergence of that full state. Proposition 1 (pp.7–11) also shows that negative mean can coexist with tails heavier than every exponential.

For FIFO GI/GI/c, Blanchet–Pei–Sigman use the arrival-epoch Kiefer–Wolfowitz recursion and a sample-path random-assignment (RA) upper dominator, then apply a dependent-coordinate dominated coupling-from-the-past algorithm. The base queue assumes iid renewal gaps, independent iid service, finite positive means, and `ρ=E S/E T<c`. Cite expected termination separately: Prop.4.1 adds `P(T>S)>0` and an `S^{2+ε}` moment; Prop.4.2 replaces that condition with `S^{2+ε}` and `T^{2+ε}` moments and gives only a proof outline referring to [7]. Its `P(T>S)=0` regeneration illustration is for `c=2`; the general-`c` states are delegated to Sigman (1988). A perfect arrival-epoch sampler does not by itself prove TV convergence from every initial full environment state.

For the time-stationary continuous-time workload, retain Blanchet–Pei–Sigman's Eq. (23): `W* =d R(W₀+S e−T_e f)^+`, with ordinary service `S` and equilibrium interarrival residual `T_e`; the paper states `W₀,S,T_e` are independent. PASTA removes the arrival/time law distinction for Poisson input; general renewal input keeps `T_e`. Do not replace `S` by an equilibrium residual service variable.

For A008, Chernova–Foss–Kim's adaptive model is exactly three queues with standard cycle `1→2→3→1` and reduced cycle `1→3→1`; queue 2 being empty in a standard cycle triggers the reduced next cycle. The global assumption `ζ>ζ*` must be stated. Its augmented strong-Markov state includes residual clocks, cycle flag and service counter; arrivals/services/walks are mutually independent IID with finite first means, and each interarrival law is unbounded-support and spread-out. Limited-service stability is sufficient when all three strict tests hold: (2.2) `ρ₀+(λ₂/l₂)ζ<1`; (2.3) `ρ₀+min{(λ₁/l₁)(ζ+ζ*)/2+λ₂(ζ−ζ*)/2,(λ₁/l₁)ζ}<1`; (2.4) the same expression with `λ₃/l₃` in place of `λ₁/l₁`. Instability follows if any of (2.5) `ρ₀+(λ₂/l₂)ζ≥1`, (2.6) `ρ₀+(λ₁/l₁)(ζ+ζ*)/2+(λ₂/l₂)(ζ−ζ*)/2≥1`, or (2.7) the corresponding expression with `λ₃/l₃` holds, but transience requires a strict inequality. The tests match as an iff only for `l₂=1`; the source leaves a gap when `l₂>1`. Gated/exhaustive service instead has iff boundary `ρ₀<1`, with `ρ₀=1` unstable but only `ρ₀>1` stated transient. Transfer lemmas invoke Dai (1995, 1996) and Dai–Hasenbein–Kim (2007); cite rather than claim these general fluid-to-Harris arguments are reproved in Chernova.

For A007, Winands Theorem 3.3 gives a positive **Poisson** branching-polling baseline under fixed-ratio deterministic setups: the tagged waiting time has the stated uniform limit. The scaled number at the queue's own polling instant is a separate deterministic limit. Boon–Boxma–Winands Eq. (7) is the former; physical p.5's polling-epoch statement is the latter. General renewal input remains Conjecture 4.2; a finite-mean FLLN or simulation table does not establish uniform residual-age control, stationary tightness, or limit interchange.



For a **distinct two-station adaptive model**, Chernova–Foss–Kim (2012) assume independent Poisson arrival rates `λ₁,λ₂>0`, independent IID service laws with finite positive means `b₁,b₂`, independent exponential travel times of mean `γ>0`, and `ρ=λ₂b₂<1`. Station 2 is exhaustive; station 1 serves one customer on a standard cycle and at most `1+m` customers after the server finds station 2 empty, for a fixed integer `m≥0`. Theorem 1 (physical p.4) says the server-arrival embedded chain is positive recurrent iff `r>λ₁`, with `B₁*(λ₂)=E[e^(−λ₂σ₁)]`, `p₀₀=(1+γλ₂)^(−2)(B₁*(λ₂))^(1+m)`, `p₁₀=(1+γλ₂)^(−2)B₁*(λ₂)`, `Eν=1+(1−p₀₀)/p₁₀`, and `r=(Eν+m)(1−ρ)/((2γ+b₁)Eν+mb₁)`. This is a source-specific G11.01 Foster threshold and G11.04 saturated-cycle calculation, not a general Markovization result. The authors call the physical p.5 proof only a short outline; retain its Foster and random-walk-coupling dependencies. At `m=0` or `γ=0` the full-distribution dependence vanishes. See the exact source/use cards（本机附件：`references/source_cards/PR-9e6384b2ee2f.md`） and bounded-use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-9e6384b2ee2f.md`）.

For a polling discipline with independent offspring replacement, Resing's actual read copy is CWI Report BS-R9128 (Dec. 1991), the cited work's report precursor, not the 1993 journal PDF. At the fixed server-visit epochs, Property 1 and Theorems 3–4 identify a multitype branching chain with immigration in every state for positive switchovers and only at state zero without switchovers. Theorem 5's Bernoulli-type equivalence is an offspring spectral-radius criterion; a stationary/ergodic epoch law additionally requires Theorem 1's primitive/irreducible/aperiodic chain and finite logarithmic immigrant-moment conditions (and the separate reduced-matrix argument for exhaustive service). This is G11.04 source-scoped epoch branching, not an arbitrary-time or general-renewal theorem. See the read-copy/page card（本机附件：`references/source_cards/PR-e193a03e210b.md`）.

Dai's fluid-limit route requires the exact open multiclass queue state, including class/order and residual arrival/service clocks, independent IID primitive sequences, finite positive means, unbounded spread-out nonnull interarrivals, and the source routing/work-conservation hypotheses. Theorem 4.1 and Definition 4.1 identify actual subsequential fluid limits; Theorem 4.2 transfers stability of that actual fluid-limit model to positive Harris recurrence via compactness, renewal-count uniform integrability and Theorem 3.1. If actual limits are verified to lie in a larger associated fluid solution set, stability of the entire larger set is sufficient; a bad path only in the larger set does not establish an actual-limit counterexample or stochastic instability. See the Dai use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-e9b1eb342c6a.md`）.

Dai–Meyn provides a staged fluid-to-moment and recurrence route for an open multiclass network with the exact residual-clock state. For integer `p≥1`, retain independent IID primitives and the source `(p+1)`-moment assumptions; fluid stability first yields time-average p-moment bounds. Positive Harris recurrence and pointwise resolvent-weighted `f_p`-variation/moment convergence additionally require A3 or A3′ small-set and chain hypotheses. The `f_p` limit is pointwise, not uniform geometric ergodicity or stationary-family interchange; use this as G11.02/G11.03, not G11.06. See the Dai–Meyn use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-feab9dd5dcf2.md`）.

Dai–Weiss provide a concrete fluid drainage construction for a single-route reentrant line. Under their three-buffer conditions `m₁+m₃<1` and `m₂<1`, the two linear workload forms in Lemma 3.2 drain every fluid path at a uniform rate. Their four-buffer example separates fluid stability from instability: a work-conserving fluid policy has a non-draining periodic path at `m₂+m₄=1` and divergent cycle minima when `m₂+m₄>1`. The stochastic transfer to positive Harris recurrence needs the paper's separate independent-IID primitive, finite-mean, unbounded/spread-out arrival and full discipline-state assumptions. Fluid instability by itself is not stochastic transience. See the scoped Dai–Weiss use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-1c9a13921bc0.md`）.

Gamarnik–Hasenbein give a conditional policy-existence converse: global weak instability of the associated fluid model, their A/B/C primitive and hitting assumptions, and the fluid-decomposition property (FDP) combine with uniform tracking bounds to construct a nonidling policy with almost-sure linear queue growth. FDP is proved for two stations. The conclusion is existence of a source-constructed policy for each initial state; it does not establish that every policy or a specified fixed static policy is unstable, nor a positive-Harris equivalence. See the scoped use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-2cea472b1695.md`）.

For heavy-traffic queue moments, Eryilmaz–Srikant's MaxWeight result combines uniform orthogonal-moment control with an `O(1/ε)` projected workload bound along one fixed face and fixed geometry. The resulting `ε Q^ε` family is tight in the full finite-dimensional vector, by `||εQ||≤ε<c,Q>+ε||Q^⊥||` and Markov's inequality. Uniform bounded increments, a fixed nonnegative face normal, and uniform constants are essential. Tightness does not identify a unique subsequential law or justify stationary-limit interchange. See the scoped Eryilmaz–Srikant use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-1b62e72f2b3b.md`）.

For a diffusion with constant invertible noise matrix, Eberle's reflection coupling gives exponential contraction in a source-built concave-cost `W_f` when the radial curvature profile is eventually positive and satisfies the stated local integrability condition. Eberle–Guillin–Zimmer Theorem 2.1 adds an explicit Lyapunov term to the additive cost and obtains `W_ρ` contraction under one-sided curvature, geometric drift, and a separate growth condition. R06 accepted this as G11.01/G12.01, not G11.03 or a petite-set construction. These are diffusion Wasserstein methods, not queue-chain TV theorems; the exact source cards retain their curvature signs, constants and state-space assumptions. See the bounded use card.（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-a0977052ad49.md`）

For a finite Markov chain, Bubley–Dyer's linear-extension example illustrates the full path-coupling chain: a fixed poset, valid adjacent swaps with a specified concave proposal, uniform invariant law, weighted transposition metric, and one-step expected-distance contraction. The Appendix A metric is half the sum of element-position displacements; the mixing proof is the unnumbered Section 3 argument. For a different state graph, construct its own transitions, invariant law and metric estimate before using the coupling inequality.

## 4. Coupling and total variation

For any coupling `(X,Y)` of `μ,ν`, use `d_TV(μ,ν) ≤ P(X≠Y)`. In a Markov-chain convergence argument, verify that the second chain has the invariant law, both coordinates follow the correct kernel, and the coupling remains together after first meeting. Then a coupling-time tail bounds TV.

Weak convergence, pathwise closeness, a small Wasserstein cost, or vanishing discrepancy density does not imply exact meeting or small TV. The exact shared-noise witness in the saved JSON（本机附件：`evidence/originals/4beb9b8cbdb2bd59-l06_exact_boundary_examples.json`）, generated by the SymPy script（本机附件：`evidence/originals/d0133930f3a70752-l06_exact_boundary_examples.py`）, has mean path distance `ε`, meeting probability zero, and TV distance one.

Iglehart–Whitt Parts I–II provide a positive finite-horizon process-limit method when the limiting channel paths are continuous (for example under a joint Brownian FCLT), so the net-input and reflection-map steps are valid. Part II allows within-channel dependence in its stated process input, but marginal J1 convergence alone does not justify subtraction at a shared limiting jump with displaced prelimit jumps; for discontinuous limits, prove joint net-input convergence and mapping continuity separately. The standard/modified comparison also needs its source-specific small-shift control. These theorems do not give stationary-law convergence, TV convergence, or exact finite-time coalescence.

Ferrari–Martin remains a prior-read source on the separate R07 reading-acceptance gate. It is not in the accepted 32-source set, and this skill provides no callable theorem interface from it. Do not use its propositions as support for a proof here; its appearance in the roster is only an unadmitted background/nontransfer note pending that gate.

For finite Markov chains, LPW Chapter 25 gives a positive exact-sampling route when the *averaged update law* preserves the target: if `X~pi` is independent of a fresh random map `U`, then `F(X,U)~pi`; an individual realized map need not preserve `pi`. Use the source's independent sequence of random maps, and require the backward composite map to eventually collapse every state almost surely. In monotone CFTP, require an order-preserving common update with minimum and maximum states, reuse the same indexed past maps across every expanded horizon, and stop only when the extremal trajectories meet. This returns an exact stationary sample; it is not an approximation justified by small path distance. The hard-core certificate bound cited in that chapter imports Haggström–Nelander. The chapter is not a queue-application theorem; any queue use needs a separately constructed state, map law, invariance proof, and total-coalescence argument. See the complete Chapter 25 use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-8b8fb25d9f49.md`）.

Aldous–Feng Theorem 1 (physical pp.5–6) gives a whole-map renovating event for its fixed-parameter random-map chain. Generate two independent batches of `k` IID `theta` points, map each state to its `j`th nearest point in the first batch, and break ties by sample index. If the entire first batch lies in a single selection cell induced by the second batch, the two-step composite sends every initial state to the same second-batch point. This event has probability at least `k^(1-k)`, giving `P(T>2t) <= (1-k^(1-k))^t` over disjoint blocks. It is a fixed-`theta` coupling/CFTP route, not contraction of the nonlinear map `theta -> pi(theta)` and not a queue-stationarity result. See the bounded use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-452b1dea025a.md`）.

Paulin's positive interface couples each entire conditional future rather than replacing coordinates separately. Under its Polish product-space and regular-conditional-law assumptions, the Goldstein suffix coupling and the Markov block construction yield an upper-triangular mismatch matrix `Gamma`; with finite `tau(epsilon)` in the source pairwise-TV convention, `Gamma_ii=1`, `Gamma_i,i+1<=1`, and `Gamma_ij<=epsilon^(j-i-1)`. Its geometric row and column sums give `||Gamma||_2 <= (2-epsilon)/(1-epsilon)`, which feeds the source Lipschitz concentration bound. The suffix-coupling construction imports Goldstein/Fiebig/Samson. Concentration is a consequence of the constructed dependence bound, not proof of mixing or queue recurrence. See the specific-use card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-ef8c95745749.md`）.

Shy Couplings Example 2.3(ii) (physical p.3) is a useful negative selection example for the coupling inequality: take circle Brownian motion `X` and `Y_t=e^(i theta) X_t` for a fixed `0<theta<2pi`. Rotation equivariance gives both marginals the same mixing kernel, but their squared chord distance is `2-2cos(theta)>0` (so the chord distance is its square root); this valid joint Markov coupling never meets. It shows that marginal mixing does not make an arbitrary chosen coupling coalesce; it does not rule out other couplings. See the scoped source card（本机附件：`references/uses/PR-6cd7f64e3473.md`）.

Zverkina is not an admitted positive method in this draft. This is a scoped statement counterexample, not a whole-paper verdict. The `α=3/2` first-moment branch, exponential branch, stronger-hypothesis repairs, other local statements, and queue applications were not adjudicated. No G10.03/G12.03 positive-use or quota credit is admitted. See the source card（本机附件：`references/source_cards/EXSRC-aadcdd118ec41613.md`）, non-entry use record（本机附件：`references/uses/EXSRC-aadcdd118ec41613.md`）, R14 neutral report（本机附件：`evidence/originals/e261b16908e0434c-review.json`）, and R06 scoped resolution（本机附件：`evidence/originals/c9bca21a826dc530-review.json`）.

For a sticky Markovian coupling, Efficient Markovian Couplings Lemma 3.5 (physical pp.27–28) uses starts with different `phi_2` values to force an exponential coupling rate `mu <= mu_2`, provided the leading nonconstant spectral mode is simple and the coupling stays together after meeting. A repeated leading eigenvalue requires the full nonzero eigenspace projector difference instead of a one-term eigenfunction expansion. Theorem 3.7(i) (physical p.28 and proof pp.29–31) gives a matching mirror construction only for its reflected-Brownian obtuse-triangle setting; it does not cover right or acute angles. Mitrophanov's uniform-ergodicity article gives only a conditional G12.06 bridge after a stationary Markov model is independently specified. On a standard Borel space, if `X_n` is stationary with invariant `pi` and the source full-variation bound is `sup_x ||delta_x P^n-pi||_var <= C rho^n`, where `||.||_var` is twice set-supremum TV, conditioning on `X_0` gives `beta(n)=1/2 integral pi(dx)||P^n(x,.)-pi||_var <= C rho^n/2`. This beta identity is a separate derivation, not a theorem stated in the paper. No queue uniform-ergodicity premise or split-chain construction follows.
