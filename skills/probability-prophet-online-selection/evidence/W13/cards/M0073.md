## Stopping-rule results read in Chapter 3

Let \(Y_n=y_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) be the reward at stage n, \(\mathcal F_n=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\), and let admissible stopping rules respect this filtration. The chapter assumes

- **A1:** \(\mathbb E[\sup_n Y_n]<\infty\);
- **A2:** \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}Y_n\le Y_\infty\) almost surely.

Theorem 1 (physical p. 26, printed p. 3.3) proves existence of an optimal rule under A1–A2. Its proof regularizes a maximizing sequence of rules, takes successive maxima to obtain an increasing sequence of stopping times, and uses A2 plus Fatou–Lebesgue dominated by \(\sup_nY_n\) to show the limiting rule attains the supremum.

Define
\[
V_n^*=\operatorname*{ess,sup}_{N\ge n}\mathbb E[Y_N\mid\mathcal F_n].
\]
The essential supremum is needed because the family of stopping rules is uncountable; Lemma 3 gives a countable subfamily attaining the essential supremum. Under A1 alone, Theorem 2 proves
\[
V_n^*=\max\{Y_n,\mathbb E[V_{n+1}^*\mid\mathcal F_n]\}
\]
(physical p. 28, printed p. 3.5). The rule \(N^*=\inf\{n:Y_n=V_n^*\}\) need not be optimal under A1 alone. Theorem 3 (physical p. 29, printed p. 3.6) says it is optimal if an optimal rule exists, in particular under A1–A2; equivalently stop at the first \(Y_n\ge W_n^*\), where \(W_n^*=\mathbb E[V_{n+1}^*\mid\mathcal F_n]\). The proof truncates an arbitrary optimal rule at \(N^*\), then uses near-maximizing continuation rules to rule out strict improvement on any positive-probability event where the truncated rule stops earlier.

## One-choice prophet inequality

For a finite horizon n and independent nonnegative \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), the online decision maker stops once at a finite time, whereas the prophet sees all values and obtains \(M_n=\max_iX_i\). Theorem 5 (physical pp. 34–35, printed pp. 3.11–3.12) states a constructive factor 2. Let m be a median of \(M_n\), so \(\Pr(M_n<m)\le1/2\) and \(\Pr(M_n>m)\le1/2\), and let \(\beta=\sum_i\mathbb E[(X_i-m)_+]\). Then \(\mathbb E M_n\le m+\beta\). If \(m\le\beta\), the pure threshold rule (s(m)=\min\{i:X_i>m\}\) (using n as a fallback when no value exceeds m) has expected reward at least \((m+\beta)/2\); if \(m\ge\beta\), the analogous rule (t(m)=\min\{i:X_i\ge m\}\) with fallback n does. Thus \(\mathbb E M_n\le2V_n^*\).

The proof uses independence to factor the event that the strict-exceedance threshold has not fired before i from \(X_i\), the median tail bound, and nonnegativity for the fallback reward. Atom handling matters: the source gives separate strict and weak threshold rules rather than suppressing ties. The chapter notes that replacing nonnegativity by nonnegative means is false. This result concerns a single accepted value and does not prove any k-unit prophet ratio for k>1.
