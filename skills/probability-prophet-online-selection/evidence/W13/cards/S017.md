## Model and main theorem statements

There are m divisible unit-supply items and n sequential players. Player i draws a valuation `V_i` from a finite-support distribution `D_i`, independently across players; each realized valuation is a nonnegative function on `[0,1]^m`. The online allocation must respect supply coordinatewise. The paper's main model assumes valuations are monotone, concave, and DR-submodular (equivalently, diminishing marginal returns coordinatewise, under the regularity conditions in the paper). The **philosopher** benchmark is the optimal online policy; the **prophet** benchmark is the offline optimum. They are distinct.

- **Theorem 1.1 (pp. 3–4):** for OWDI with monotone concave DR-submodular valuations, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves `2/3` of the philosopher/online benchmark. The proof first collapses the configuration relaxation to a low-dimensional concave program by Jensen's inequality, then rounds it with capped online contention resolution. The concavity of the valuations is used for the benchmark relaxation and is not part of the general Capped-OCRS rounding theorem itself.
- **Theorem 3.2 (p. 8):** for any feasible solution x of the online concave relaxation (1) and any scaling function s, an online policy has expected welfare at least `s(1) Σ_{i,k} p_{i,k} v_{i,k}(x_{i,k}/p_{i,k})`. A scaling function is concave `s:R_+→R_+`, satisfies `s(z)≤min(z,1)`, and for all z≥0 and µ∈(0,1], `(1−µ)s((z−s(z))/µ)+µs(z/µ)≥s(z)` (Definition 3.1, pp. 7–8). Appendix C.3 (pp. 25–27) supplies `s(z)=1−1/p(z)`, `p(z)=1+z+z²/2+z³/2`, with `s(1)=2/3`. The paper verifies concavity by reducing `2(p′)²−p″p` to `(2+9z²+8z³+6z⁴)/2≥0`, and `s(z)≤z` to `z²+z⁴≥0`; the scaling inequality is then handled by nonnegative-polynomial checks on pp. 25–27.
- **Theorem 1.2 (pp. 4, 13–16):** efficiently computable linear per-unit prices obtain a 1/2 approximation to the offline/prophet benchmark for monotone concave DR-submodular valuations. The factor is tight even for one divisible item, two players, and linear valuations (Theorem 5.6, pp. 20–21). This is not a philosopher guarantee.
- **Theorem 5.3 (pp. 18–20):** computing an optimal online policy is #P-hard even for one divisible item with monotone concave DR-submodular valuations. The reduction is from #Knapsack; it is an exact-computation hardness result, not a claim that a 2/3 approximation is impossible.

## Proof of the philosopher guarantee

The online relaxation has type-indexed expected allocations `x_{i,k,j}`, constrained, for each item j, by predicted remaining supply `λ_{i,j}=1−Σ_{ℓ<i,r}x_{ℓ,r,j}` and `x_{i,k,j}≤p_{i,k}λ_{i,j}`. Concavity allows the configuration LP over distributions of fractional bundles to be replaced by this low-dimensional concave program without lowering the relaxation value (Section 2 and Appendix B, pp. 5–7, 21–23). It upper-bounds the online benchmark.

For a single item, given a scaling function s, Algorithm 1 sets the target conditional allocation to `(x_{i,k}/p_{i,k})s(R_i/λ_i)` when type k occurs, where R_i is actual remaining supply. The inequalities `s≤1` and `s(z)≤z` give the per-player cap and supply feasibility. Lemma 3.5 tracks `φ(i)=E[s(R_i/λ_i)]`; conditioning on `R_i`, concavity of s bounds the transition by the endpoint mixture, and the scaling-function inequality makes φ nondecreasing. As `R_1=λ_1=1`, each type gets expected allocation at least `s(1)x_{i,k}/p_{i,k}` (pp. 9–10).

For multiple items, the rounding is run separately per item. Conditional on a player's realized valuation, the received vector Y is coordinatewise bounded by the prescribed target x/p and has coordinatewise expectation at least `αx/p`. Lemma 3.6 uses concavity along each coordinate and DR-submodularity to telescope marginal gains, proving `E[f(Y)]≥αf(x/p)` even when coordinate allocations are correlated through the shared player type. Lemma 3.7 then sums over types and players to obtain `α` times the concave relaxation. With s(1)=2/3 this proves Theorem 1.1 (pp. 10–12, Appendix C pp. 23–27).

## Prophet guarantee and balanced prices

For an offline-relaxation optimum `x*`, define each type's Aumann–Shapley coordinate price by `q_{i,k}=∫₀¹∇v_{i,k}(t x*_{i,k}/p_{i,k})dt`, then aggregate one price per item with welfare weights `SW_j=Σ_{i,k}q_{i,k,j}x*_{i,k,j}` and set posted price `p_j=SW_j/2` (pp. 13–14). Lemma 4.2 shows continuous supporting-price inequalities for every `0≤y≤x`; Lemma 4.4 splits welfare into revenue and utility and proves the balanced-price bound for any monotone DR-submodular valuations and a feasible comparison allocation. Concavity is needed to compute the relaxation solution efficiently. Applying the lemma to the offline relaxation yields the 1/2 prophet ratio (pp. 14–17). In contrast, Lemma 4.6's non-concave existence statement is not the efficient construction.

For Theorem 5.3, reduce a #Knapsack instance `(L,a_1,…,a_n)` to `n+1` players and one item, with `L'=L+1`: player 0 has linear value `w x`; each later player is inactive with probability 1/2 or has capped linear value `L' min(a_i/L',x)`. If R supply is left after player 0, the optimal later-player value is `E[min(RL',a(S))]` for a uniformly random subset S. A threshold comparison in w determines whether an optimal policy allocates any supply to player 0; binary search recovers `L'P[a(S)≥L']`, and complementing this count gives the #Knapsack solutions (pp. 18–20). Theorem 5.6's prophet lower bound uses player 1 with value x and player 2 with value x/ε with probability ε, otherwise zero: `E[OPT]=2−ε`, every online algorithm's value is at most 1, and the ratio tends to 1/2 (p. 20).

## Scope and limitations

- The type distributions have finite support and are independent across players. Feasibility is fractional unit supply; the paper's theorem is not a discrete-item allocation theorem.
- The 2/3 theorem is vs the optimal online philosopher benchmark and needs concave DR-submodular valuations. The 1/2 theorem is vs the offline prophet benchmark. Do not swap the benchmarks.
- The scaling-function inequality is an exact pointwise condition; the paper does not derive it from an ODE. The single-item scaling proof and multi-item decomposition are separate steps.
- The #P-hardness theorem concerns exact computation of the optimal online policy. It is not an impossibility result for approximation or for computing the prophet relaxation.
