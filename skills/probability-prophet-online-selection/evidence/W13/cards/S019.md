## Formal problem B058: the unresolved logarithmic factor

For `U=[n]`, the paper has independent nonnegative, known-distribution coordinates `X_i`, a prefix-closed family \(\mathcal F\) of feasible probe sequences, and reward `f(X_P)` for a monotone norm `f:R^n_{≥0}→R_{≥0}`. The adaptivity gap is the ratio between optimal adaptive and optimal nonadaptive expected rewards. In the corollary and open question, `2≤r≤n` bounds the maximum feasible sequence length.

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 4; detailed form Corollary 6.5, PDF p. 38) proves an upper bound `O(log r · log n / log log n)` for arbitrary monotone norms. Section 7 (PDF p. 39) compares this with the best known lower bound `Ω(log n / log log n)` and identifies the remaining `O(log r)` factor as open, pointing to nonbinary weight vectors in the XOS representation. This is the precise unresolved B058 interface.

The same paper resolves nearby but distinct cases: Bernoulli probing with binary-XOS objective has a tight `Θ(log n / log log n)` adaptivity gap (Theorem 1.3 / Theorem 4.3), while monotone symmetric norms have an `O(1)` gap (Corollary 6.6; the conclusion states a bound of 2050). Neither subcase settles general monotone norms with arbitrary prefix-closed `F`.

## Proof mechanism visible in the read pages

Theorem 6.2 (PDF p. 38) reduces general nonnegative variables and a general monotone norm to a Bernoulli probing instance with `n log_8 r` elements and maximum feasible sequence length `r log_8 r`, at a constant-factor loss. Appendix C, Lemma C.1 and its proof (PDF pp. 45–46) first truncate large/small coordinate contributions and round value scales geometrically; Lemma C.2 (p. 46) proves a telescoping activation identity for the threshold Bernoulli variables, preserving the adaptive decision-tree leaf probabilities. The transformed objective in Theorem 6.2 remains a monotone norm. Substituting the Bernoulli XOS bound gives Corollary 6.5’s refined general-norm upper bound.

The lower-bound comparison in §7 is cited there from prior work; its construction and proof were not read in this partial pass. Do not treat the cited lower-bound proof as independently checked here. The open issue is to prove a matching lower bound with the relevant `r`-dependence, or improve the upper bound to remove the `log r` factor while retaining the general monotone-norm, independent-coordinate, prefix-closed model.

## Transfer boundary

This is an adaptivity comparison for probing strategies, not a prophet inequality or an online/offline benchmark ratio. Preserve the precise observable outcome model, prefix-closed feasibility, nonnegative independent coordinates, the general monotone norm, `r`, and the adaptive/nonadaptive comparator. Restricting to Bernoulli variables, binary-XOS functions, or symmetric norms changes the question. Numerical enumeration of small policy trees can search for examples, but cannot close the universal asymptotic gap.
