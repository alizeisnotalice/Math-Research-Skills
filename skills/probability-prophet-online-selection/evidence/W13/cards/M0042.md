# M0042 — Semi-infinite programming, duality, discretization and optimality conditions

**Source.** Alexander Shapiro, “Semi-infinite programming, duality, discretization and optimality conditions,” *Optimization* 58(2) (2009), 133–161, DOI 10.1080/02331930902730070. Read the complete 33-physical-page Optimization Online author manuscript. It has printed page markers 1–32; physical p. 33 carries printed p. 32 and the final references.

## Model and dual spaces

The paper studies the SIP 
\[
(P)\quad \min_{x\in\mathbb R^n} f(x)\quad\text{s.t. }g(x,\omega)\le0\quad(\forall\omega\in\Omega).
\]
It distinguishes the algebraic function space \(Y=\mathbb R^\Omega\), whose dual multipliers have finite support by definition, from the topological setting \(Y=C(\Omega)\). Under (A1), compact metric \(\Omega\) and jointly continuous \(g\), the dual of (C(\Omega)) is the space of finite signed Borel measures; nonnegative multipliers give the measure-valued Lagrangian. Convexity/proper-closed assumptions and Slater/regularity assumptions are invoked theorem by theorem, not globally interchangeable.

**Theorem 2.1 (printed §2; physical pp. 6–7).** For a convex SIP with perturbation value \(\vartheta\) and \(\vartheta^{**}(0)<+\infty\), strong duality `val(D)=val(P)` holds iff \(\vartheta\) is lower-semicontinuous at 0. Strong duality together with a nonempty primal solution set holds iff \(\vartheta\) is subdifferentiable at 0; then `Sol(P)=−∂ϑ(0)`. This is a value-function duality criterion; it is not an unconditional multiplier-existence statement.

## Finite reduction / discretization

Theorem 3.1 relates strong duality to ε-discretizability under the stated finite-problem strong-duality/attainment assumption (A2); Corollary 3.1 says a convex problem with a nonempty bounded optimal set is discretizable. Under the specific strict-feasibility assumption (A3)—for every (n+1) indices there exists a point strictly satisfying those constraints—Theorem 3.2 gives a finite set of at most \(n\) indices with
\[
\operatorname{val}(P)=\operatorname{val}(P_m)=\operatorname{val}(D_m)=\operatorname{val}(D),
\]
assuming the other listed hypotheses: convex \(P\), closed `dom f`, (A1), and finite primal value. Theorem 3.3 uses Helly’s theorem: a reducible convex SIP has a value-preserving discretization with at most \(n\) constraints for finite value, and at most \(n+1\) if its value is (+∞). Do not omit the distinction between exact finite reduction and approximate ε-discretization.

## First- and second-order conditions

The first-order section derives KKT/saddle-point conditions with nonnegative multipliers supported on active indices. Assumption (A4) is: Ω is compact metric; f and each g(·,ω) are real-valued C¹; and ∇g is continuous on ℝⁿ×Ω. Under (A4), MFCQ gives nonempty bounded multiplier representations with at most n support points (Theorem 4.5); conversely, nonempty bounded Λₙ₊₁ implies MFCQ, so support bounds and constraint qualifications have directional hypotheses. The tangent/linearized cone relation becomes equality under MFCQ, which is used in the first-order growth criterion (Theorem 4.6).

**Theorem 5.1 (physical p. 22; rendered p. 22).** Assumption (A5) is that \(\Omega\) is compact metric, \(f\) and each \(g(\cdot,\omega)\) are real-valued C², and \(\nabla^2g\) is continuous on \(\mathbb R^n\times\Omega\). If \(\bar x\) is locally optimal, the active index set is nonempty, and MFCQ holds, then
\[
\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar x)}\left\{h^T\nabla^2_{xx}L(\bar x,\lambda)h-\sigma(\lambda,T^2(h))\right\}\ge0
\quad\text{for all }h\in C(\bar x).
\]
Here \(C(\bar x)\) is the critical cone and σ is the second-order tangent/curvature term. The paper explicitly notes σ≤0; it vanishes when Ω is finite. The SIP term therefore cannot in general be silently deleted as an identity. Theorem 5.2 gives a strict second-order sufficient condition under its additional regularity assumptions; §5.2’s no-gap result (Theorem 5.4) further assumes MFCQ, finite active set, second-order regularity at active points, and quadratic growth for the inner maximization problem.

## Discretization convergence rate

**Theorem 6.1 (physical pp. 29–30).** For nonempty bounded S⊂F, assume p-th order growth on S with p∈{1,2}; (A4); g(x,·) is uniformly Lipschitz on Ω for x in a neighborhood V of S; MFCQ holds at each x∈S; and the discretized problem has εₘ-optimal solutions x̂ₘ with εₘ=O(ρₘ) and dist(x̂ₘ,S)→0. Then
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\hat x_m,S)=O(\rho_m^{1/p}).
\]
If Ω⊂ℝ^ℓ, g is C², and every maximizer set Ω*(x̄) is contained in the interior of Ω, the paper improves this to O(ρₘ^(2/p)). Boundary maximizers do not satisfy that improved-rate hypothesis automatically; the author notes adding boundary points to the discretization may be needed.

**Proof chain.** The rate proof first bounds the distance between discretized and continuous feasible sets by O(ρₘ) using uniform Lipschitz continuity, then combines that estimate with the p-growth inequality and ε-optimality to bound dist(x̂ₘ,S). For interior maximizers, the active-index derivative in ω vanishes; a C² Taylor expansion improves the feasible-set distance to O(ρₘ²), which yields the squared mesh exponent after applying the same growth estimate.

## Applicability limits and local check

These are SIP facts with the displayed convexity, compactness, smoothness, MFCQ, growth, and mesh assumptions. They do not supply a guarantee for arbitrary nonconvex constraints or for grids without a mesh/Lipschitz estimate.
