## Benchmarks and arrival assumptions

The paper has three objects that should stay separate: (i) a k-unit prophet, compared to the expected sum of the k largest realized rewards; (ii) a k-unit OCRS; and (iii) a knapsack OCRS / stochastic knapsack algorithm, chiefly compared to the ex-ante assignment LP `UP(H)`, not the integral offline knapsack optimum. The LP reduction routes independent known requests to resources, then applies an OCRS guarantee per resource. Hence LP-tight OCRS ratios imply LP-relative online reward guarantees; they do not automatically imply equality against the weaker integral prophet.

For OCRS, an online adversary may choose the next query based on the past but not on unrevealed future activations/sizes. The guarantees do not hold for an almighty adversary who knows all realizations before ordering. The k-unit policy is γ-conservative: it prioritizes sample paths with least capacity used. The knapsack policy is best-fit: for each realized size it selects paths with the most utilization on which the item still fits, while preserving empty paths for a possible final size-one item. Sizes are observed before accept/reject; this differs from models where size is revealed only after commitment.

## k-unit OCRS and LP-relative multi-unit prophet theorem

Definition 1 (PDF pp. 10–11) has independent active queries with probabilities `p_t`, capacity k, and `Σ_t p_t≤k`; the objective is the largest γ such that each query, conditional on being active, is served with probability at least γ. The paper's primal LP (4) tracks `x_{ℓ,t}`, the ex-ante probability query t is served as the ℓth acceptance; its dual is (5) (PDF pp. 15–18).

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 18):** for fixed `p`, Algorithm 1's recursively constructed `x_{ℓ,t}(θ*)` is optimal for the LP, where `θ*` is the unique solution of `Σ_{τ<T}x_{k,τ}(θ*)=1−θ*`. The algorithm's ex-ante state interpretation appears on PDF p. 17. Proof: feasibility reduces to that single terminal constraint (Lemma 6); the left side varies continuously and monotonically in θ (Lemma 7); construct a feasible dual with complementary slackness (Theorem 1 proof, Appendix B.4–B.5, PDF pp. 44–55).

Splitting an activation probability into smaller pieces cannot increase the optimum (Lemma 2, PDF pp. 18–19). Thus the worst case is the uniform mesh `p_t=1/N` with `Nk` arrivals and `N→∞`, i.e. a rate-one Poisson process on `[0,k]`. Under that limit, the state variables satisfy the piecewise ODE in Definition 3 (PDF p. 20; rendered): for `ℓ<k`, `dỹ_ℓ/dt=θ−1+ỹ_{ℓ−1}` until `ỹ_ℓ` reaches `1−θ`, then `dỹ_ℓ/dt=ỹ_{ℓ−1}−ỹ_ℓ`; for the final state, `dỹ_k/dt=θ−1+ỹ_{k−1}` after its activation time. Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 20–21; proof Appendix B.8, pp. 58–59) states that the unique root `ỹ_{k,γ*_k}(k)=1−γ*_k` equals `inf_{p:Σp_t=k} LP^OCRS_k(p)`. The Euler approximation converges uniformly in θ with error at most `(e^(2k)−1)/N` (Appendix B.8), providing the bridge from finite LPs to the ODE. The resulting γ*_k is strictly larger than `1−1/√(k+3)` for `k>1` and is tight for this OCRS.

The random-routing reduction in LP (3) then gives the same γ*_k approximation to `UP(H)` for the multi-unit/resource-allocation problem. For the prophet comparator, `Prophet≤UP(H)` gives a lower bound, not equality. For k=2 the authors give a separate finite/Poisson construction with a **numerically optimized** upper bound 0.6269 against the actual prophet (Proposition 1, PDF pp. 19 and 57); this is only an “almost tight” comparison to γ*_2≈0.6148, not an exact prophet ratio.

## General stochastic knapsack OCRS

Definition 2 (PDF p. 12): query t independently realizes a nonzero size `d∈(0,1]` with known probability `p_t(d)` and is otherwise inactive; `Σ_d p_t(d)≤1`, `d p_t(d)≤1`, and `Σ_{t,d}d p_t(d)≤1`. Size is observed before the irrevocable decision. The target is service probability at least γ conditional on every nonzero size realization.

Algorithm 2 (PDF pp. 22–24) chooses a utilization threshold `η_t(d)` so exactly γ measure of currently feasible sample paths is served, randomizing at a boundary atom. Lemma 3 (PDF p. 24) proves the invariant

`μ_t(0,b]/γ ≤ exp(−μ_t(b,1−b]/γ)`, for `0<b≤1/2`.

Induction over query sizes yields a sufficient lower bound on empty-path mass; Theorem 3 (PDF p. 24; proof Appendix C.2, pp. 60–61) shows thresholds remain feasible for every horizon when `γ=1/(3+e^(−2))≈0.319`. Theorem 4 (PDF p. 24; proof Appendix C.3, pp. 61–62) proves tightness using a small size-ε request, many size-`1/2+ε` requests, and a final size-one request. This is optimal for the stated knapsack OCRS / its LP-relative reduction, not an exact general integral-knapsack prophet ratio. Size discretization for polynomial-time implementation costs an additional multiplicative `K/(K+1)` (Section 5.1, PDF pp. 25–26).

## Unit-density knapsack: proved lower guarantee with an open gap

When reward equals size and capacity is one, the authors replace a uniform service probability by a non-increasing schedule `γ_1≥…≥γ_T`. Theorem 5 (PDF p. 29; proof Appendix D.2, pp. 64–65) bounds the empty-path probability from below using the accumulated expected utilization `Σ_{τ≤t}γ_τψ_τ`; Lemma 5 (PDF pp. 30–31; proof D.3, pp. 65–66) constructs a feasible schedule by averaging a continuous envelope `h_{γ_0}` over the intervals whose lengths are `ψ_t`.

Optimizing `∫_0^1 h_{γ_0}(t)dt` yields the reported guarantee `0.3557` relative to `UP(H)` (PDF pp. 30–31). The value is obtained numerically from the envelope optimization (the paper reports `γ_0≈0.3977`); treat the decimal as a numerical evaluation of the proved feasible construction, not as a symbolic exact constant. Proposition 3 (PDF p. 31; proof D.4, pp. 66) supplies an upper bound `(1−e^(−2))/2≈0.4323` for every online algorithm relative to the same LP, via `T` requests of deterministic size `1/2+1/T` and activation probability `2/T`. The authors explicitly leave closing this gap open. Keep this LP benchmark distinct from the integral offline knapsack benchmark, where other online guarantees may apply.
