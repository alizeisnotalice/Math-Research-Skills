## Model and notation

There are `n` independent, known, nonnegative distributions `F_i`; values arrive in a uniformly random permutation, and at most `k` are accepted irrevocably. The static threshold `t` is selected before the order and does not change with history. The prophet gets `E[sum of the k largest realized values]`. The paper also uses an ex-ante LP benchmark `LP_k(F)` with `Prophet_k(F)≤LP_k(F)`.

For a threshold `t` and tie probability `p∈[0,1]`, let `B_i~Ber(p)` independently across i and independently of all values and other randomness, and define the **effective demand**

`D_{t,p}(V,B)=Σ_i [1{V_i>t}+1{V_i=t}B_i]`.

This counts every strict exceedance and independently keeps each item tied at t with probability p. Its summands are independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities `q_i=P(V_i>t)+pP(V_i=t)`. Use `D_{t,p}` in utilization, expected-demand, and stockout calibrations. It reduces to strict `D_t=Σ_i1{V_i>t}` when there is no atom at t (or p=0). Set `UT_k(d)=min(d/k,1)`, `AR_k(d)=min(1,k/(d+1))` (the expected share of a uniformly ordered set of d qualifying items accepted under capacity k), and `γ_k=1−e^(−k)k^k/k!`.

## Proved static-threshold results

- **Theorem 1, PDF pp. 4–5:** for every `ε>0`, no static threshold policy guarantees more than `γ_k+ε` times the prophet value uniformly over all independent instances. Explicit asymptotic examples establish tightness of this static-policy ceiling.
- **Theorem 2, PDF pp. 5–6; proof §§3.1–3.3, PDF pp. 16–18:** if the threshold (with randomized tie handling when needed) is calibrated so `E[UT_k(D_{t,p})]=γ_k`, its expected value is at least `γ_k·Prophet_k(F)` for every independent instance in uniform random order. In fact the proof first obtains
  `ST_k(F)/Prophet_k(F) ≥ ST_k(F)/LP_k(F) ≥ min(E[UT_k(D_{t,p})], E[AR_k(D_{t,p})])` (Lemma 1, rendered PDF p. 17).
  It then minimizes the AR expectation over independent Bernoulli parameters `q_i=P(V_i>t)+pP(V_i=t)` subject to the UT expectation using the Bernoulli optimization theorem (Theorem 4); an optimizer can be taken with all probabilities equal (Lemma 2), so the minimizer is `D~Bin(n,q_{n,k})` with `E[UT_k(D)]=γ_k` (PDF p. 18). Lemma 3 gives `q_{n,k}≤k/n`; since `AR_k` decreases in demand, `E[AR_k(Bin(n,q_{n,k}))]≥E[AR_k(Bin(n,k/n))]`. The Poisson limit of the latter is exactly `γ_k`. Lemma 7 (Appendix A, PDF p. 29) verifies `E[UT_k(Pois(k))]=E[AR_k(Pois(k))]=γ_k`. This limiting lower bound, together with the monotonicity of the finite-dimensional minimization problem `Φ_n`, is what yields the every-n theorem; the argument is not merely a claim that a Poisson approximation is accurate at finite n.
- **Theorem 3, PDF pp. 7–8; proof pp. 19–20:** the different calibration `E[D_{t,p}]=k` guarantees `min(k/(k+1),γ_k)` times prophet, tight for this calibration; it achieves `γ_k` when `k>4`. It is not the utilization calibration in Theorem 2.
- **Theorem 4, PDF p. 9; proof Appendix C, PDF pp. 34–38:** for arbitrary `f,g:N_0→R`, positive integer n, and any feasible `φ`, the finite Bernoulli optimization `min E[f(D_p)]` subject to `E[g(D_p)]=φ`, `D_p=Σ_i Bernoulli(p_i)`, has an optimum with every `p_i∈{0,p,1}` for a common `p∈[0,1]`. The proof is a structural extremality argument; Theorem 2 applies it to the pair `(AR_k,UT_k)` and Theorem 3 to `(1{d<k},d)`.

## A002: stockout calibration is an open conjecture

Stockout is the event `D_{t,p}≥k`, with probability `P(D_{t,p}≥k)`. Section 5.2 (PDF p. 24, rendered) proposes calibrating this to `P(Pois(k)≥k)` and explicitly says “we conjecture” it guarantees `γ_k` for `k>1`. The k=1 case coincides with expected utilization and is known. For `k>1` this paper contains no theorem proving the conjectured stockout guarantee. The exact-rational check `evidence/W13/math_check_terminal_corrections.py/.out` enumerates one finite atomic example and confirms its effective-count PMF equals the independent Bernoulli convolution with `q_i`; this checks the representation only, not the prophet guarantee.

The preceding Proposition 1 gives a useful negative result for a different calibration: if `k<5`, then for any target expected effective demand `φ`, some instance defeats a policy calibrated to `E[D_{t,p}]=φ` below `γ_k` (PDF p. 24). Proposition 2 gives the IID exception at target effective demand k (same page). Neither proposition settles the stockout conjecture. Retain independent randomized ties for atoms and distinguish numerical searches from a universal proof.
