# M0054 — Lucier, “An Economic View of Prophet Inequalities”

**Reader:** W13. **Read status:** partial. Read the complete article's PDF pp. 1–6 plus pp. 14–15 and 24; the local 24-page file is the target article only (printed pp. 26–49, original issue PDF pp. 28–51). Review pending.

## Exact single-item result read

The article surveys prophet inequalities and posted-price interpretations. In its independent, nonnegative, single-item setting, a sequential posted price / first-threshold policy yields at least half of the expected offline maximum. The economic proof in §3 (Theorem 3.1, PDF pp. 5–6; printed pp. 30–31) posts `p=(1/2) E[V*]`, where `V*=max_i V_i`. Revenue is `p P[sold]`. If the good remains unsold, every buyer had an opportunity, and the expected buyer surplus lower bound is at least `(1/2) E[V*] P[unsold]`; adding the two terms gives expected welfare at least `(1/2)E[V*]`. Independence is used because whether the item remains unsold before buyer i is independent of that buyer's value.

This is a single-item, one-choice result. Its factor is not a multi-unit k-prophet result, and this proof is not evidence for A001's finite-n/large-n identity.

## Threshold and order notes

The introduction (§2, PDF pp. 2–4) presents the dynamic-programming optimal policy as a non-increasing sequence of acceptance thresholds in the known independent-distribution model. It also recounts Samuel-Cahn's order-oblivious median-threshold argument and calls out tie-breaking at a threshold atom: always accept or always reject need not both work; at least one of the two conventions obtains the bound. Lucier's alternative expectation-based threshold has an explicit tie-independent guarantee in Theorem 3.1.

## Reading limits

This is a survey/tutorial, not a proof of the W13 multi-unit, random-order stockout, general OCRS, or unit-density knapsack gaps. Remaining sections on multi-dimensional mechanisms are not recorded as read. Use it for the economic intuition and the stated one-unit proof interface only.
