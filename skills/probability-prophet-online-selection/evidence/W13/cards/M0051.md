# M0051 — Bayesian Combinatorial Auctions: Expanding Single Buyer Mechanisms to Many Buyers

**Source.** Saeed Alaei, “Bayesian Combinatorial Auctions: Expanding Single Buyer Mechanisms to Many Buyers,” arXiv:1106.0961v4 (revised 15 June 2012), 34 physical pages; later *SIAM Journal on Computing* 43(2) (2014), 930–972, DOI 10.1137/120878422. This card reads the full arXiv v4 manuscript, not the journal pagination. PDF p. 1 title/version and p. 34 closing proof were checked; official arXiv v4 metadata was checked.

## γ-conservative magician (Theorem 4)

A magician sees boxes in order; box (i) breaks a wand with ex-ante probability (x_i), and at most (k) wands may be broken. Before box (i), let (W_i) count prior breaks and (F_{W_i}) be its ex-ante CDF. For target γ, choose
\[
\theta_i=\min\{\ell:F_{W_i}(\ell)\ge\gamma\}.
\]
Open with probability 1 when (W_i<\theta_i), with probability ((\gamma-F_{W_i}(\theta_i-1))/(F_{W_i}(\theta_i)-F_{W_i}(\theta_i-1))) at equality, and 0 when above. With exact (x_i), the ex-ante open probability is exactly γ; if (x_i) are only upper bounds on break probabilities, it is at least γ by stochastic domination.

The recursion in Definition 2 is a CDF recursion: for ℓ≥0,
\[
F_{W_{i+1}}(\ell)=s_i^{\ell}x_iF_{W_i}(\ell-1)+(1-s_i^{\ell}x_i)F_{W_i}(\ell),
\]
with initial CDF (F_{W_0}(\ell)=1) for ℓ≥0 and 0 otherwise; (s_i^\ell=P(\text{open}\mid W_i=\ell)). This CDF—not a PMF—is the quantity used to choose thresholds.

**Theorem 4.** For γ≤1−1/√(k+3), a k-wand γ-conservative magician opens each box with ex-ante probability at least γ and never needs more than k wands. With exact break probabilities the opening probability is exactly γ. The proof splits into (a) thresholding attains the prescribed ex-ante probability as long as θᵢ≤k−1, and (b) an analysis of the barrier/random-walk process proves that sufficient γ bound keeps all thresholds ≤k−1. The paper separately defines the sharper optimal uniform parameter γ_k; Theorem 4 supplies the explicit lower bound γ_k≥1−1/√(k+3), not an equality formula for γ_k.

Theorem 5 gives the obstruction: no magician can guarantee opening probability (1-k^k/(e^k k!)+\varepsilon) for every box, for any ε>0; Stirling makes the obstruction asymptotically (1-\Theta(k^{-1/2})). Do not conflate this adaptive-magician bound with the paper’s different `non-adaptive` discussion.

## Prophet and market-expansion consequences

**Theorem 6 (k-choice sum; PDF p. 10, footnote 11).** Under the source's simplifying assumption that the independent value distributions have no point masses, choose threshold τ with ΣᵢPr[V_i>τ]=k. Feed box probabilities (x_i=Pr[V_i>\tau]) to a γ_k-conservative magician; accept (V_i) iff its box is opened and (V_i>\tau), up to k choices. The stated expected payoff is at least γ_k times the prophet’s expected k-choice sum. The proof uses the magician’s opening probability and compares the threshold-surplus contribution against the standard ex-ante convex relaxation that upper-bounds the prophet. The authors say the result extends with “slight modifications” when point masses are allowed; the displayed strict-threshold equation need not be solvable with atoms, so use an independent tie/quantile modification appropriate to this model rather than applying this statement unchanged. This is for independent values and the specified k-choice setting; it is not a general arbitrary-feasibility prophet bound.

**Theorem 7 (pre-rounding).** Given for each buyer an α-approximate incentive-compatible single-buyer mechanism (M_i) and corresponding concave benchmark (R_i), if every (R_i) has a budget-balanced cross-monotone cost-sharing scheme, then for γ∈[0,γ_k] the pre-rounding mechanism is DSIC, lies in the target mechanism class (M), and is a γα approximation to its optimum. The proof runs one magician per item with capacity (k_j); feasibility of the benchmark assignment gives Σᵢxᵢⱼ≤k_j, so an available-item set includes item (j) with probability at least γ. Budget-balanced cross-monotone cost shares make the expected benchmark retained at least a γ fraction, then α-approximation and the benchmark relaxation yield γα. Incentive compatibility follows because the single-buyer mechanism remains IC for every available set and other reports affect only that set.

**Theorem 8 (post-rounding) is BIC, not DSIC.** Under the same α-single-buyer/concave-benchmark setup, additionally assume: (A′1) exact ex-ante allocations are available; (A′2) objective has form (Obj_i(t_i,X_i,P_i)=Obj_i(t_i,X_i,0)+c_iP_i), (c_i\ge0), and the zero-payment objective has budget-balanced cross-monotone cost sharing; (A′3) the output belongs to (M), which is not restricted to stronger-than-BIC truthfulness; and (A′4) valuations are weighted matroid-rank functions. Then for γ∈[0,γ_k] post-rounding lies in (M), is BIC, and is a γα approximation. Exact allocation probabilities ˆxᵢⱼ are used as the magician break probabilities, which gives the per-item retention probability exactly γ; payment scaling and the cost-sharing structure support objective retention and BIC. Do not transfer the DSIC conclusion of Theorem 7 to Theorem 8.
