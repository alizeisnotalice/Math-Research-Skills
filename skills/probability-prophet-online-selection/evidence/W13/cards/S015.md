## Model and theorem statements

Let `X₁,…,Xₙ` be i.i.d. nonnegative random variables with finite mean. The online decision maker knows `F`, observes values sequentially, and may stop once to select one value. Write `X_(1)≥…≥X_(n)` for order statistics. The benchmark is the **average of the top ℓ values**, `E[(1/ℓ)Σ_{i=1}^ℓX_(i)]`, not the maximum except when `ℓ=1`. The finite-horizon worst-case ratio is
`CR_ℓ(n)=inf_F sup_τ E[X_τ] / E[(1/ℓ)Σ_{i=1}^ℓ X_(i)]`, and `CR_ℓ=inf_{n≥ℓ}CR_ℓ(n)` (pp. 2–3).

- **Theorem 1.1 (p. 3; exact characterization).** `CR_ℓ` is the unique solution in `[0,1)` of
  `1/ℓ! ∫₀^∞ [ν^(ℓ−1) / ( e^ν(1/CR_ℓ−1)+Σ_{i=0}^ℓ ν^i/i! )] dν = 1`.
  Reported values are `CR₁≈0.745`, `CR₂≈0.966`, `CR₃≈0.997`, `CR₄≈0.9998`, `CR₅≈0.999993`. The equation as printed, including `ℓ!`, was checked against the PDF rendering and numerically reproduces the table to its stated precision.
- **Theorem 1.2 (pp. 3, 10–11).** For every positive integer `ℓ`, `CR_ℓ>1−e^(−ℓ)`. This is an exponential lower bound, not the exact value; the paper says its numerical behavior is roughly `1−10^(−ℓ)`.
- **Theorem 1.3 (pp. 4–5, 12–15; choosing k items).** For `n≥max(k,ℓ)`, the benchmark remains the average of the top `ℓ` order statistics, while the online policy selects at most `k` items and is scored by their **average**. If `CR_{k,ℓ}(n)` is the worst-case ratio, then
  `liminf_(n→∞) CR_{k,ℓ}(n) ≥ (CR_ℓ/k) Σ_{j=1}^k [ Π_{t=1}^j θ_{t,ℓ} ]^(−1)`.
  Here `θ₁,ℓ=1`; the other `θ₂,ℓ,…,θ_{k,ℓ}` are the unique parameters for which the paper's Gamma-CDF boundary-value system (1.6) has a solution with `bʲ(0)=0`, `bʲ(1)=1`. `γ_x` is the CDF of `Gamma(x,1)` and `γ_x^(−1)` its quantile. This is a **one-sided asymptotic lower bound**. The authors explicitly do not prove it is tight or that `CR_{k,ℓ}=liminf_n CR_{k,ℓ}(n)` (pp. 15–16).
- **Theorem 1.4 (p. 5; static-threshold policies).** For every `k,ℓ≥1` and `n≥max(k,ℓ)`,
  `| CR^S_{k,ℓ}(n) − (1/k)Σ_{j=1}^k Pr[Gamma(j,1)≤ℓ] | ≤ O(1/n)`.
  A static policy uses one fixed threshold `T`, accepting values above it until `k` items have been chosen. The threshold `F^(−1)(1−ℓ/n)` gives the lower-bound proof; a two-point, rare-high-value instance gives the asymptotically matching upper bound (pp. 16–17, 36–39). For `k=1`, `CR^S_ℓ=1−e^(−ℓ)`, and Corollary 6.3 states the dynamic `CR_ℓ` is strictly larger.

## Proof chains and auxiliary results

**Theorem 1.1.** Algorithm 1 uses item-indexed Beta-quantile thresholds. The paper proves a finite-`n` ratio lower bound by equalizing the per-index guarantees, which yields a nonlinear difference boundary-value problem (Propositions 2.1–2.3, pp. 6–8). Beta-to-Gamma convergence and a uniform-convergence/Dini argument give a limiting ODE (Lemmas 3.1–3.2, pp. 8–10); its unique boundary solution yields the displayed integral equation and `CR_ℓ≥ℓ/c_ℓ` (Proposition 3.3). The reverse inequality adapts an extremal random-arrival distribution from [35], evaluates both gambler and prophet limits, and matches `ℓ/c_ℓ` (Proposition 4.1, pp. 11–12). The text explicitly cautions that the doubling comparison yields `CR_ℓ=liminf_n CR_ℓ(n)`, not monotonic decrease of `CR_ℓ(n)`.

**Theorem 1.2.** Starting from the integral equation, compare its denominator with a Gamma-tail expression and integrate a logarithmic derivative to show `c_ℓ<ℓ/(1−e^(−ℓ))`; this implies `CR_ℓ=ℓ/c_ℓ>1−e^(−ℓ)` (pp. 10–11). The numerical values in the theorem's table are not used as proof.

**Theorem 1.3.** The multi-selection quantile algorithm has probabilities `ρᵢʲ` indexed by selection number `j`; equalizing them produces coupled discrete recurrences (Lemma 5.2). Existence and scaling of their boundary solutions (Proposition 5.3), then convergence to the Gamma ODE system (Proposition 5.4), yield the stated `liminf` via Proposition 5.1 (pp. 12–15). Proposition 5.5 reduces the finite-`n` worst-case distribution to finite support of size `2+k(k−1)/2+k(n−k)` on `[0,1]` (pp. 15–16); this aids finite-dimensional upper-bound searches but does not prove the asymptotic lower bound tight.

**Theorem 1.4.** For the fixed threshold `F^(−1)(1−ℓ/n)`, the expected contribution of the `j`-th accepted item is `γ_j(ℓ)/ℓ` up to an explicit `O(1/n)` correction, while Jensen's inequality bounds the top-ℓ benchmark. For the upper bound, a rare-large-value two-point distribution reduces the limit to a Poisson calculation (Propositions 6.1–6.2, pp. 16–17, proofs pp. 36–39). Here `γ_j(ℓ)=Pr[Gamma(j,1)≤ℓ]`; the result applies to i.i.d. values, not arbitrary independent distributions.

## Other extensions and limits

- For a nonincreasing finite probability vector `p` used to weight prophet order statistics, Proposition 7.1 gives `CR(p)≥1/(p₁c(p))` through a mixture-of-Gamma integral equation (pp. 18, 25–26). The formula is OCR-fragile; do not quote its display without checking the PDF. For support `{1,2}`, `CR₁≤CR(p)≤CR₂`.
- Proposition 7.2 gives the distinct “worst case for the prophet” quantity `D_{k,ℓ}=max{ℓ/k,1}` (p. 19). Do not confuse it with `CR_{k,ℓ}`.
- The body assumes absolutely continuous `F` for exposition; atoms are said to be handled by random tie-breaking, as in the cited quantile work (p. 5 footnote). The i.i.d. assumption is essential to the stated model: the paper contrasts the exponential top-ℓ guarantee with the tight `1−1/(ℓ+1)` bound for independent non-identical values.
- The paper's multi-selection theorem does not settle finite-`n` monotonicity or asymptotic tightness. The static-threshold theorem is a separate policy class and benchmark; it does not give the dynamic `CR_ℓ` value.
- Local computation in `../reader_batches/L09_batch2/computations/s015_integral_roots.py` uses composite Simpson quadrature and bisection to check equation (1.2) against the displayed table; it is a numerical consistency check, not a proof of uniqueness or of the theorem.
