## Model and exact percentile thresholds

Let `X_1,…,X_n` be independent nonnegative variables with finite expectation, and write `X_n^*=max_i X_i`. The mortal observes values sequentially, has no recall, and may select at most `k` observations, where `n>k`; the payoff is the maximum of the selected values. The prophet benchmark is `E[X_n^*]`. This is a multi-stopping-time problem, not a `k`-unit sum-reward prophet problem.

For `j=1,…,k`, choose `b_k≤…≤b_1` and `α(j)∈[0,1]` satisfying the exact quantile condition

`P(X_n^*>b_j)+α(j)P(X_n^*=b_j)=j/(k+1)`.

For each threshold rule `t_{α(j)}(b_j)`, the paper uses one `U_j~Unif(0,1)` independent of the values and shared across all times: stop at the first `i` with `X_i>b_j` or with `X_i=b_j` and `U_j≤α(j)`, with terminal fallback at `n`. For distinct thresholds the `U_j` are independent; for repeated thresholds they are coupled as the same uniform, with `α(j)≤α(s)` when `j<s` (PDF p. 4; printed p. 1087). This coupling makes the threshold events nested and handles atoms exactly.

**Theorem 3.1 (PDF p. 5; printed p. 1088).** The rule selecting the observations at stopping times `t_{α(1)}(b_1),…,t_{α(k)}(b_k)` satisfies

`E[max_j X_{t_{α(j)}(b_j)}] ≥ (k/(k+1)) E[X_n^*]`.

Equivalently, the prophet-to-mortal factor is `(k+1)/k`. The proof expands each threshold-stop payoff into its threshold contribution plus a positive excess term (Lemma 2.1); conditioning on the event that earlier threshold levels did not trigger and using independence lower-bounds these excesses by the corresponding prophet tail excess. Summing the resulting inequalities across the `k` quantiles gives the theorem (PDF pp. 4–6; printed pp. 1087–1089).

For `k=2`, the 2/3 guarantee applies to this simple percentile rule, but it is not an optimality claim for all two-choice policies. **Remark 3.2 is an impossibility bound, not a 4/5 algorithm guarantee:** it says that the inequality `E[X_n^*] < γ max_{t_1,t_2} E[X_{t_1}∨X_{t_2}]` cannot hold uniformly for any `γ<5/4`, for threshold rules or even all stopping rules (PDF p. 8; printed p. 1091). The example has `n=3`, `0<β<1/2`, `X_1=β`, `X_2=2β` with probability `1/2` and 0 otherwise, and `X_3=1` with probability `β` and 0 otherwise. The prophet value is `β(5−3β)/2`; the best two-choice mortal value is `β(2−β/2)`, attained by the pair `(β,2β)`. Thus the prophet/mortal ratio is `(5−3β)/(4−β)→5/4` as `β↓0`. This rules out a uniform mortal/prophet guarantee strictly greater than `4/5`; it does not establish a policy guaranteeing `4/5`. The authors also give an infinite-horizon threshold extension under the stated finite expected-supremum condition and discuss negative lower-orthant dependence (Corollary 3.1 and Remark 3.3, PDF p. 8; printed p. 1091).

## Use and limits

The saved exact-rational check `evidence/W13/math_check_terminal_corrections.py/.out` evaluates the displayed example at β=1/10 and verifies the exact gap to 5/4; the limit itself follows from the simplified algebraic gap, not from numerical extrapolation. This check verifies the example only.

This gives a clean finite-horizon benchmark for a mortal maximizing the best among k selected observations. It must not be transferred to maximizing the **sum** of k accepted values, to random-order secretary models, or to a k-capacity resource-allocation policy. The source’s exact atom handling and no-recall stopping convention are part of the theorem.
