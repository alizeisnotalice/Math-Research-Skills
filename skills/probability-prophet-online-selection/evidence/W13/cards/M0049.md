## Model and theorem

A finite matroid has ground set `U` and independent sets `I`. Each element `x` has an independent nonnegative weight `w(x)` drawn from a known distribution; the prophet observes all weights and takes a maximum-weight basis. The online algorithm sees each revealed weight once and immediately accepts/rejects subject to matroid feasibility. The main theorem guarantees expected online weight at least `OPT/2` for every matroid, which is tight already at rank one (abstract and §1, PDF pp. 1–3). For an intersection of `p` matroids, the paper proves factor `4p−2` (online value at least `OPT/(4p−2)`), with an `O(p)` lower bound; it is order-optimal up to constants (§§1,5; PDF pp. 3, 10–11).

The guarantee holds for a fixed prespecified order and also for the paper’s **online weight-adaptive adversary**: after seeing prior revealed elements and their realized weights, it chooses the next item without seeing that item’s current weight or any future weight (§2, PDF pp. 4–5). This is not an adversary that sees all realized values before choosing an arbitrary permutation; keep that stronger model separate from M0050.

## Threshold algorithm and proof chain

Let `w'` be an independent copy of all weights. At a prefix-selected independent set `A`, the paper takes a maximum-weight basis `B` under the independent copy `w′` and partitions it into `C(A),R(A)` so that `A∪R(A)` is a basis and `w′(R(A))` is maximized among such partitions (PDF pp. 5–6). Here `C(A)` is the part the unrestricted prophet chose in place of `A`, and `R(A)` is what the prophet restricted to extending `A` adds. The single-matroid threshold at item `x_i` is

`T_i = (1/2) E[w'(R(A_{i−1})) − w'(R(A_{i−1}∪{x_i}))]`

or equivalently the expected marginal change in the complementary phase-2 set, with the same factor `1/2` (Definition/algorithm, PDF pp. 5–6). Accept `x_i` iff it is feasible and `w_i≥T_i`.

The proof formalizes these thresholds as `α`-balanced with `α=2` (Definition 1). Proposition 1 shows every monotone algorithm with α-balanced thresholds earns at least `OPT/α` against an online weight-adaptive adversary (PDF pp. 6–7). The chain: threshold revenue on selected items dominates expected copied weight in `C(A)`; positive excesses of selected weights dominate the copied positive excesses on the residual part `R(A)` because the adversary cannot observe the current weight before choosing the item; the balanced-threshold inequality controls this residual term. Adding these parts recovers at least `1/α` of the copied prophet basis, whose expectation is `OPT`.

For p matroids, Proposition 3 yields `(α−p)/(α(α−1))` for `α≥2`; choosing `α=2p` gives `1/(4p−2)` (PDF pp. 9–11). The paper notes a near-optimal α choice; `2p` is the simple stated choice. Section 6 separately transfers the bounds to BMUMD truthful mechanisms/revenue, with regularity and mechanism-specific assumptions; those applications are not the online welfare theorem (PDF pp. 12–16).

## Use and limits

Use the 1/2 theorem for independent nonnegative known weights and matroid feasibility under the specified online decision model. Do not apply it to arbitrary order adversaries who see current/future realized values, to matching without identifying its matroid structure, or to a sample-limited policy. The intersection guarantee is `1/(4p−2)` for the displayed simple choice, not an exact optimum for every p.
