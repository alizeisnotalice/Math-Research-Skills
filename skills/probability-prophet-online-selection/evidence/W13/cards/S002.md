## Model and benchmark

There is a finite type set \(\mathcal T\). Type-i agents arrive as independent Poisson processes of rates \(\lambda_i\), have independent exponential abandonment clocks with rates \(\mu_i>0\), and can be matched in pairs with directional reward \(r_{ij}\). A greedy policy fixes an acceptable match set/order for each type in advance and makes an eligible match as soon as possible. The benchmark \(\mathrm{OMN}(\mathcal I)\) sees the complete arrival/departure history and chooses a maximum-reward matching; values are long-run steady-state reward rates.

The paper calls departures homogeneous if either (a) all \(\mu_i\) are equal, or (b) the compatibility/reward graph is bipartite and rates are equal within each side (Definition 2, PDF/printed p. 4). In the bipartite case, within-side pairs are incompatible/nonpositive as specified by Definition 1. These are structural restrictions on abandonment, not assumptions that all arrival rates or rewards are equal.

## Main guarantees

**Theorem 1 (PDF/printed p. 4).** For every instance with homogeneous departures, the greedy policy returned by Algorithm 1 satisfies
\[
V(\mathrm{ALG}(\mathcal I),\mathcal I)\ge\tfrac12\,\mathrm{OMN}(\mathcal I).
\]
The factor is relative to the omniscient long-run reward-rate benchmark, not a finite-horizon prophet or static matching LP.

**Theorem 2 (PDF/printed p. 4).** For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists a homogeneous-departure instance for which every **Markovian** policy \(\pi\) has \(V(\pi,\mathcal I)<(1/2+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OMN}(\mathcal I)\). The formal statement is for Markovian policies; do not silently strengthen the quantifier to all history-dependent policies.

**Theorem 3 (PDF p. 41; Appendix C.1).** With two types and equal cross-type rewards \(r_{12}=r_{21}\), the proposed greedy policy still obtains at least (1/2\) of OMN even without homogeneous departure rates. This is a two-type extension only.

**Conjecture 1 (PDF/printed p. 25).** The authors conjecture the same (1/2) guarantee for arbitrary heterogeneous departure rates and arbitrary type sets. They report 100 simulations without a counterexample, which is not a proof.

## Proof chain for Theorem 1

For a candidate acceptable-pair set \(M\subseteq\mathcal T\times\mathcal T\), the authors define a family \(\mathrm{LP}^{\mathrm{ALG}}(\mathcal I,M)\) over steady-state type counts \(n_i\), directed match rates \(x_{ij}\), and nonnegative prefix slacks \(\psi_{Sj}\). The balance equations are
\[
n_i\mu_i+\sum_j x_{ij}+\sum_j x_{ji}=\lambda_i.
\]
For each receiver type \(j\) and each prefix \(S\) of its acceptable priority order, the LP also constrains prefix match flow by \(\lambda_j\gamma_S\sum_{i\in S}n_i\), where
\[
\gamma_S=\frac{1-e^{-\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i/\mu_i}}{\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i/\mu_i}
\]
with continuous extension \(\gamma_\varnothing=1\). Suitable basic feasible LP solutions can be translated into priority orders (Algorithm 1, PDF pp. 11–15).

Lemma 1 (PDF p. 6) establishes the availability bound when the set \(S\) has a common abandonment rate: for any Markovian policy \(\pi\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(\sum_{i\in S}N_i^\pi>0\right)\ge\gamma_S\,\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i\in S}N_i^\pi\right].
\]
Its proof uses stationary flow and a cut inequality, then bounds the stationary probability of an empty set through a truncated-Poisson comparison. The common-rate condition is necessary for this lemma as stated (Appendix C.3, PDF p. 43).

Proposition 1 (PDF pp. 16–18) shows that if the LP solution's tight-prefix sets satisfy this availability lemma, its corresponding greedy policy earns at least the LP objective. The algorithm deletes zero-flow matches and finds a suitable LP by iteratively shrinking the candidate match set. Proposition 2 (PDF pp. 19–20) upper-bounds OMN by \(\mathrm{LP}_{\mathrm{OMN}}\), then by a relaxation \(\mathrm{LP}_{\mathrm{OMN}}^{\mathrm{REL}}\) which drops future-information constraints while preserving arrival-rate and subset bounds. Proposition 3 (PDF pp. 21–22) proves
\[
\mathrm{LP}_{\mathrm{OMN}}^{\mathrm{REL}}(\mathcal I)\le2\,\mathrm{LP}^{\mathrm{ALG}}(\mathcal I,\mathcal T\times\mathcal T)
\]
by LP duality, comparing a dual feasible solution after scaling the balance constraints by \(\lambda_i/\mu_i\). Together,
\(\mathrm{OMN}\le\mathrm{LP}_{\mathrm{OMN}}\le\mathrm{LP}_{\mathrm{OMN}}^{\mathrm{REL}}\le2\mathrm{LP}^{\mathrm{ALG}}\le2V(\mathrm{ALG})\), which gives Theorem 1.

## Transfer boundary

The main theorem's proof needs homogeneous abandonment rates on the relevant prefix sets; the two-type theorem does not settle arbitrary multi-type heterogeneous rates. This continuous-time model has persistent agents and random departures, unlike one-shot online matching or independent prophet selection. Keep the omniscient steady-state benchmark and policy class explicit. Numerical experiments do not resolve Conjecture 1.
