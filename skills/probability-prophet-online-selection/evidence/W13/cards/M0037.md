## Models and finite-horizon characterization

There are `n` sequential agents, each with an independent nonnegative value revealed in fixed order; a policy accepts at most `k` irrevocably and may adapt to the current value, time, and remaining capacity. The prophet benchmark is the expected sum of the `k` largest realized values. Separately, the paper studies the ex-ante LP relaxation, which upper-bounds prophet value; ratios against these two denominators must not be merged.

For the IID problem let `γ_{k,n}=inf_F E[DP_{k,n}(F)]/E[OPT_k(F)]`, where the common nonnegative distribution `F` may depend on `n`. Theorem 1 (PDF p. 15) gives exact finite-n reductions of the worst-case ratios to explicit LPs; the discrete finite-support representation is developed in Definitions 1–4, Proposition 2, and the primal/dual constructions (PDF pp. 8–15). In quantile coordinates, the prophet and ex-ante LP values are expressed using increments `Δ_j` and the Bernoulli-count terms `Q_j=E[min(Σ_i Bernoulli(G_{ij}),k)]` versus `min(Σ_j G_{ij},k)` (Proposition 2, PDF pp. 11–12). The adaptive DP value is represented by a finite LP (5), then dual variables are collapsed into the state/acceptance variables of (6)–(7) (PDF pp. 12–15). These are exact finite-horizon characterizations, not an asymptotic approximation.

For IID distributions with infinitely granular support, Theorem 7 and Definitions 16–18 give a semi-infinite LP; Theorem 8 identifies the exact IID DP/ex-ante and static-threshold ratio (PDF pp. 23–26). Proposition 5 gives a discretization error guarantee under its explicit mesh/tail conditions (PDF p. 28). Continuous/unbounded distributions are treated through quantile functions and the stated strong-duality/Slater conditions in Appendix A (PDF pp. 36–37); do not drop those regularity conditions when transferring the LP argument.

## A001: exact for k=1, unresolved for k>1

For `k=1`, Theorem 10 (PDF p. 31; proof Appendix E, pp. 58–59) proves

`inf_{n≥1} γ_{1,n} = lim_{n→∞} γ_{1,n} = θ* ≈ 0.745`.

Here each `γ_{1,n}` is already the infimum over IID `F` at that `n`; this is not a claim about convergence for each fixed `F`. Proof chain: reduce the IID finite-n problem to the exact LP; for `k=1` show the relaxation is exact; compare relaxed LP values at `n` and `2n` (Lemma 9, rendered PDF p. 31); express the optimizer through a Hill–Kertz recurrence with boundary values; pass to an ODE and identify its boundary-value constant `θ*`. This comparison proves the infimum/limit result without proving termwise monotonicity of the original ratios.

For general fixed `k>1`, the paper gives the large-n lower bound

`γ_{k,n} ≥ (1−24k(ln n)^2/n) Σ_{j=1}^k θ*_j`

for all sufficiently large `n` (Theorem 2, PDF pp. 8 and 14–16), with constants from a nonlinear system. The paper's Introduction and conclusion (PDF pp. 6 and 29) explicitly call `inf_n γ_{k,n}=lim_n γ_{k,n}` an open question; n=1000 LP bounds are numerical computations and do not prove equality. The theorem has a vanishing but nonzero finite-n loss and therefore cannot settle an infimum over **all** n. Do not upgrade the numerical observation to a theorem.

## Policy classes and adjacent application

The paper also separates static/oblivious threshold rules from adaptive dynamic programming policies (Theorems 2–6, PDF pp. 16–23), and derives stochastic sequential assignment bounds (PDF pp. 25–27). Transfer those claims only with the policy class and denominator stated in the corresponding theorem. The SSAP application does not change the A001 open status for general `k`.

## Proof boundary

The exact LP reductions are finite-n statements; the k=1 limit uses a special recurrence/ODE comparison. No proof in this source establishes that same recurrence, a monotone sequence `γ_{k,n}`, or the required all-n lower bound for general `k`. The ODE-based multi-unit lower bound and finite LP numbers are evidence of a candidate constant, not proof that it equals the worst finite-n ratio.
