## Feasibility and arrival models

There is an item set \(M\) and elements/products \(j\) using bundles \(A_j\subseteq M\), with \(|A_j|\le L\). The fluid feasibility constraints are \(\sum_{j:i\in A_j}x_j\le1\) for each item i and \(0\le x_j\le1\). An OCRS sees independently active elements and must accept an active element immediately and irrevocably only when its bundle is still feasible. In the **random-element** model, elements are partitioned into adversarial batches \(N_t\), with at most one element active in each batch and \(\sum_{j\in N_t}x_j\le1\). In a random-element RCRS, batches arrive in a uniformly random order; in a standard RCRS each batch is a singleton. Arrival/adversary definitions and fluid LP denominators must not be merged.

## Reductions to online resource allocation

- **Theorem 2.6 (PDF p. 15):** an \(\alpha\)-selectable random-element OCRS implies discrete-time NRM expected reward at least \(\alpha\,\mathrm{LP\mbox{-}SPLIT}\). With time-homogeneous product rates \(\lambda_{j,1}=\cdots=\lambda_{j,T}\), an \(\alpha\)-selectable random-element RCRS gives the same factor.
- **Theorem 2.7 (PDF p. 16):** for Poisson-arrival NRM, an \(\alpha\)-selectable standard OCRS gives expected reward at least \(\alpha\,\mathrm{LP\mbox{-}POIS}\). With time-homogeneous rate functions \(\lambda_j(y)\), an inactive-oblivious standard RCRS gives the same factor.
- **Theorem 2.8 (PDF pp. 16–17):** if an \(\alpha\)-selectable standard OCRS (respectively inactive-oblivious standard RCRS) is available, it yields a random-element OCRS (respectively RCRS) with guarantee
\[
\frac{\alpha-(1-e^{-\varepsilon(1+2\varepsilon T)})}{1+\varepsilon}
\]
on inputs with at most T batches, each \(\varepsilon\)-bounded, for \(\varepsilon\le1/2\). This loss is part of the theorem; do not state an unconditional black-box equality between batched and singleton models.
- **Theorem 2.9 (PDF pp. 17–18):** an \(\alpha\)-selectable random-element OCRS/RCRS gives an \(\alpha\)-fraction of the OCA fluid-LP value for general valuations; in the single-minded case, standard OCRS/RCRS suffices.

## Random-element OCRS: matching baseline and impossibility

**Theorem 3.2 (PDF pp. 19–20).** The recursive exact-selection rule chooses an active feasible j with probability \(\min\{1,\alpha/\Pr(F_j)\}\), where (F_j) means all items in (A_j) remain available. With \(\alpha=1/(1+L)\), it is \(\alpha\)-selectable. Inductively require \(\Pr(Z_j\mid X_j=1)=\alpha\); a union bound gives \(\Pr(F_j)\ge1-\alpha\sum_{i\in A_j}\sum_{j'<t:i\in A_{j'}}x_{j'}\ge1-L\alpha=\alpha\), using the per-item fluid constraints.

**Theorem 3.4 (PDF pp. 21–22; proof Appendix B.2, pp. 52–53).** If L is a prime power, no random-element OCRS exceeds (1/(1+L)). A finite affine plane of order L supplies \(L^2\) items and \(L(1+L)\) L-item products partitioned into \(1+L\) batches of parallel lines. Within a batch products are disjoint; across batches every pair intersects once. The construction makes the union-bound feasibility loss tight in the limit, also producing the corresponding fluid-LP reward hardness. This impossibility is for the general random-element model; it does not apply unchanged to singleton/standard OCRS.

## Standard OCRS: strict improvement

**Theorem 3.8 (PDF p. 27, printed p. 26; proof chain §3.3, pp. 22–27).** The same exact-selection recursion is \(\alpha\)-selectable on standard OCRS inputs for L≥2 whenever
\[
\kappa(\alpha):=1-\alpha(1+L)+\alpha^2\frac{L-1}{L}
\left(\frac{1-\alpha(1+L)+\alpha/(2L)}{1-\alpha L+\alpha/(2L)}\right)^{2L}\ge0.
\]
The proof improves the union bound on item unavailability by inclusion–exclusion. A bilinear optimization for the pair-overlap term yields the displayed \(\kappa\). Its derivative is negative, while \(\kappa(1/(1+L))>0\), so the unique root \(\alpha^*>1/(1+L)\) certifies a strict improvement; for L=2, \(\alpha^*\approx0.33336\). The explicit gain decays roughly as \(\exp(-\Theta(L\log L))\) for large L. This is an exact separation from the random-element OCRS factor for prime-power L, but not an explicit uniform-size gain.

## Random-order RCRS results

**Theorem 4.2 (PDF p. 30, printed p. 29; proof Appendix C.3, pp. 59–62).** Attenuated greedy (Algorithm 1), which accepts a feasible active element with an independent survival coin \(B_j\sim\mathrm{Ber}(b(x_{j,t}))\), where
\[
b(x)=\frac{(L-x)(1-e^{-L})}{L(1-e^{-(L-x)})},
\]
is an \(\alpha\)-selectable **random-element RCRS**. For L=2 the theorem gives
\[
\alpha=\frac{3-6e^{1/2}+e+8e^{3/2}+21e^2+14e^{5/2}+7e^3}{16e(1+e^{1/2}+e)^2}\approx0.397.
\]
For L≥3 it gives the rendered expression
\[
\alpha=\frac{(e^L-e^{1/L})\left[1-\left(1+\frac{(e^L-1)(L^2-1)}{(e^{1/L}-e^L)L^2}\right)^L\right]}{(e^L-e)(1+L)}.
\]
The proof conditions on the focal batch time, controls dangerous earlier batches by Lemma 4.3, and uses Schur concavity (Lemma 4.4) to identify the worst batch-mass vector before integrating. The guarantee is strictly above (1/(1+L)), but the general optimal random-element RCRS factor remains open.

**Standard RCRS, equation (28), Proposition 4.6, Theorem 4.7 (PDF pp. 34–36; printed pp. 33–35).** For singleton arrivals, seek a decreasing selection function \(c:[0,1]\to[0,1]\) with \(c(1)>0\) satisfying
\[
c(y)\le1-L\int_0^y c(z)\,dz+\frac{L-1}{L}\left(\int_0^y c(z)(1-z)^L\,dz\right)^2.
\]
The equality solution strictly dominates \(e^{-Ly}\), so \(\int_0^1c>(1-e^{-L})/L\) for L≥2; for L≥5 it also exceeds the offline random-element CRS upper bound \((1-(1+L)^{-(1+L)})/L\). Numerically reported lower bounds are ≥0.441 at L=2 and ≥0.321 at L=3. The recursive Algorithm 2 discretizes [0,1] into K phases and uses estimated feasibility probabilities. If \(K\ge2L/c(1)\), Theorem 4.7 gives selectability
\[
\left(1-\frac{L}{Kc(1)}\right)\int_0^1c(y)\,dy.
\]
Thus K→∞ approaches the integral factor. The proof uses induction on arrival-time phases, removes conditioning on the focal element's random arrival time, and adds a positive pair-availability term to the union bound. The exact formula in (28) was visually checked on rendered PDF p. 35.

**Theorem D.3 (PDF pp. 64–66).** If the item/product incidence structure is an L-partite hypergraph (items split into L groups and each product uses at most one item per group), the paper's random-element OCRS also strictly beats (1/(1+L)). The extra structural hypothesis is essential to the stated pair-overlap estimate.

**Theorem E.1 (PDF pp. 66–67).** For prime-power L, even an offline algorithm for the accept-reject NRM instance cannot exceed \((1-(1+L)^{-(1+L)})/L\) relative to the fluid LP, using the affine-plane configuration. This is an offline CRS/LP upper bound, not a bound on the online random-order RCRS algorithm's value in another benchmark.

## Open questions and transfer boundary

Section 5 (PDF p. 36, printed p. 35) asks for the optimal random-element RCRS guarantee; the authors suggest \((1-e^{-L})/L\) as a possibility but state that neither their upper nor lower bounds settle it. They also ask whether random-element OCRS can beat 1/7 at L=6, where the affine-plane prime-power obstruction does not apply. Preserve the NRM/OCA-to-OCRS reductions, batch mass bound, inactive-oblivious requirement for Poisson random-order transfer, and the exact fluid-LP denominator. A standard RCRS result does not transfer to arbitrary batches without Theorem 2.8's loss, and a fluid LP bound is not an integral offline prophet guarantee.
