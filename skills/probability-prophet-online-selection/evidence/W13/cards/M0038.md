## Exact finite-n model and quantile LP

The `(k,n)` stochastic prophet inequality has `n` IID nonnegative values, at most `k` irrevocable acceptances, and prophet value equal to the expected sum of the `k` largest order statistics. In the body the value distribution is taken continuous on `R_+`; the authors state that a discrete distribution can be perturbed by arbitrarily small noise. Keep that approximation argument explicit when applying a result to atoms.

Theorem 1 characterizes the exact finite-n optimal ratio `γ_{n,k}` by an infinite-dimensional linear program `[P]_{n,k}` in quantile space (PDF pp. 6–8). Its variables are nonnegative nonincreasing inverse-quantile values `h` and dynamic-program state functions `d_{t,ℓ}`; the constraints range over times, capacity states, and quantile `q`, with prophet normalization `∫g_{n,k}(q)h(q)dq≥1`. Propositions 1 and Lemmas 1–2 establish equivalence between this minimax quantile LP and the original worst-case ratio. This exact LP does not itself identify the large-n limit.

## Theorem 2: ODE dual-fitting lower bound

For each fixed `k`, there is `n_0(k)` such that for all `n≥n_0(k)`,

`γ_{n,k} ≥ (1−24k(ln n)^2/n) Σ_{j=1}^k θ*_j`.

The constants are defined by a coupled nonlinear differential system on `[0,1]`, with upper incomplete gamma function `Γ_ℓ(x)=∫_x^∞ t^(ℓ−1)e^(−t)dt` and boundary conditions `y_j(0)=1`, `lim_{t↑1}y_j(t)=0` (system (1)–(3), rendered PDF p. 3). Its first equation is

`(Γ_k(−ln y_k))' = k![1−1/(kθ_k)] − Γ_{k+1}(−ln y_k)`,

and for `j=1,…,k−1`,

`(Γ_k(−ln y_j))' = k!−Γ_{k+1}(−ln y_j) − (θ_{j+1}/θ_j)(k!−Γ_{k+1}(−ln y_{j+1}))`.

For `k=2`, the paper reports `θ*_1≈0.346`, `θ*_2≈0.483`, sum `≈0.829`. This is the coefficient in a **provable lower bound with finite-n loss**, not an exact finite-n ratio.

Proof chain: (a) formulate the exact primal quantile LP; (b) derive its weak dual (Lemma 3, PDF pp. 11–13); (c) truncate and explicitly construct a feasible dual solution from solutions of the ODE system; (d) bound the finite-horizon feasibility error by `24k(ln n)^2/n`; then apply weak duality (Theorem 2 proof, PDF pp. 8 and 11–16). The small multiplicative loss is part of the theorem and cannot be suppressed at a fixed `n`.

## A001 status and open statements

`γ_{n,k}` is the worst-case over `F` for that particular `n`; the result is uniform in `F` for each sufficiently large `n`, not convergence for a single fixed distribution. The conclusion (PDF p. 29) says it remains open whether `inf_{n≥1} γ_{n,k}=Σ_j θ*_j`, and whether the large-n lower bound extends to every `n`. The paper describes strong dual equality as a belief and the ODE constants as conjectured tight; neither is established. The numerical n=1000 LP values (PDF p. 28) are computational lower bounds with their stated discretization accounting, not proof of the all-n equality.

The stochastic sequential assignment application sets its finite-n ratio to `α_n=min_{k∈[n]}γ_{n,k}` (PDF pp. 25–27). It is a separate consequence of the proved per-k bounds; do not use it as a proof of general-k A001's `inf`/limit identity.
