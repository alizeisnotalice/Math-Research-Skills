## Model and benchmark

The ground set (N) is finite; values (v_i\sim D_i\) are independent, known, and nonnegative; feasible sets form a downward-closed packing family \(\mathcal F\), with polytope \(P_{\mathcal F}=\operatorname{conv}\{1_S:S\in\mathcal F\}\). In the prophet model each realized value is revealed once and the algorithm accepts/rejects irrevocably while maintaining feasibility. The ex-ante relaxation is

\[
\max_{x\in P_{\mathcal F}}\sum_{i\in N}x_i\,\mathbb E[v_i\mid v_i\text{ lies in its top }x_i\text{ quantile}].
\]

It upper-bounds expected offline optimum by interpreting \(x_i\) as the probability that item \(i\) belongs to the offline optimum. An OCRS uses independent Bernoulli activations with means \(x_i\); a c-selectability guarantee means each item is accepted with probability at least \(c x_i\). The adversarial-order model here gives the order to the algorithm in advance; it is weaker than an almighty/online adversary that chooses order after seeing realizations. RCRS averages over a uniformly random arrival permutation.

## Theorem cards

**Theorem 1.4 (PDF p. 4): factor-preserving bridge.** For (0\le c\le1), a c-approximation ex-ante prophet policy for a packing family, under adversarial known order (respectively uniformly random order), yields a c-OCRS (respectively c-RCRS) for that family. The paper proves this through an exponential LP whose variables represent deterministic online algorithms; its dual is interpreted as an ex-ante prophet instance. This is an equivalence/transfer for the same family and arrival model, not a license to change the benchmark or adversary.

**Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.6 (statement PDF p. 4; proof PDF pp. 9–13): matroids.** For every matroid, the ex-ante prophet value is achievable within factor (1/2) under adversarial order known in advance, and within factor (1-1/e) under a uniformly random order. By Theorem 1.4 these give a (1/2)-OCRS and a ((1-1/e))-RCRS. Both constants are optimal even for rank one (PDF p. 5): two-item adversarial-order examples force the (1/2) limit; for random order, with (x_i=1/n), the probability no item is active is ((1-1/n)^n\to e^{-1}), limiting total selectable mass to (1-1/e).

## Proof dependencies and exact construction

For the matroid ex-ante inequality, first reduce general independent distributions to top-quantile Bernoulli values (Lemma 3.3, PDF p. 9). Decompose the feasible fractional vector (x) into independent sets and use the induced correlated auxiliary value distribution \(\widehat D\); its marginal activation/value quantities preserve the ex-ante objective. Define residual value \(R(A,\widehat v)\) and base prices (b_i(A)) for the matroid contraction after the current independent set (A).

For adversarial known order, accept (i) only if feasible and (v_i>\alpha b_i(A)), with \(\alpha=1/2\) (Theorem 3.4, PDF pp. 9–10). Lemma 3.5 lower-bounds expected revenue by \(\alpha(r(0)-r(n))\), where (r(t)) is residual value after the first (t) elements; Lemma 3.6 lower-bounds utility by \((1-\alpha)r(n)\). Summing yields at least \(\alpha r(0)=\frac12\sum_i x_i y_i\), the ex-ante objective for the Bernoulli instance. The conversion back to arbitrary distributions is the preceding quantile reduction.

For random order, give each item an independent (U_i\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]) and process by increasing (U_i). The threshold multiplier is \(\alpha(t)=1-e^{t-1}\), and the rule accepts a feasible item when its value exceeds the corresponding \(\alpha(t)b_i(A_t)\). Claim 3.9 and Lemma 3.10 (PDF pp. 11–13) bound revenue below by \(-\int_0^1\alpha(t)r'(t)dt\) and utility below by \(\int_0^1(1-\alpha(t))r(t)dt\). Since \(1-\alpha(t)+\alpha'(t)=0\), integration by parts gives \((1-1/e)r(0)\). The random-order result is thus tied to a uniform random permutation (equivalently iid continuous timestamps), not arbitrary order selection.

## Transfer boundary

The paper explicitly leaves a (1/2)-OCRS against the almighty/online order adversary open (PDF p. 5). Matroid ex-ante factors are not automatic guarantees for arbitrary packing families, dynamic markets with departures, batched activations, or a different offline denominator. The threshold/revenue proof also uses the matroid base-price/residual-value structure. Use this source for B043/B046 only after matching the feasibility polytope, activation model, and adversary definition.
