## Model and ratios

There are n sequential requests and a set of servers on the line. Greedy irrevocably assigns each request to the nearest currently free server; the cost is the sum of distances. The competitive ratio is \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{greedy})]/\mathbb E[\mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{OPT})]\) under the stated input distribution. Keep these models separate:

1. **Fully random:** n servers and n requests are mutually independent uniform points on [0,1].
2. **Fully random with linear excess:** n requests and \((1+\varepsilon)n\) independent uniform servers, for fixed constant \(\varepsilon\in[0,1]\).
3. **Random requests / semi-random:** n servers are adversarially chosen, while the n requests are IID uniform on [0,1].

The random-requests model is not random server locations or random arrival order. The ratios concern cost, not reward maximization.

## Main theorems and proof chains

**Theorem 1 (PDF p. 4; printed p. 2).** In the fully random model greedy is constant-competitive. The proof compares greedy with the constant-competitive hierarchical greedy algorithm \(A^H\). For each m, define hybrid \(H_A^m\), which uses A on the first m requests and ordinary greedy afterward. The Hybrid Lemma (Lemma 5, PDF pp. 31–32; printed pp. 29–30) bounds \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{cost}(H_A^{m-1})-\mathrm{cost}(H_A^m)]\) by a constant times
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\left(1+\log\frac1{\mathrm{cost}_m(A)}\right)\mathrm{cost}_m(A)\right].
\]
Its proof couples the two hybrids' free-server sets; when they differ, at most one server differs on each side, and the gap/potential argument bounds the one-step cost change. Hierarchical greedy's expected total cost is \(O(\sqrt n)\), and its cost is sufficiently spread over levels/requests to sum the hybrid losses to \(O(\sqrt n)\). The expected offline optimum is \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\), so the ordinary greedy cost is also \(O(\sqrt n)\), giving a constant ratio. The detailed hybrid proof is Appendix D (PDF pp. 31–41), with the remaining fully-random estimates in Appendix E (pp. 42–44).

**Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 5, 13–14; printed pp. 3, 11–12).** For every fixed constant \(\varepsilon\in[0,1]\), greedy is constant-competitive in the fully random \(\varepsilon\)-excess model. For \(\varepsilon>0\), the proof uses concentration/barrier estimates rather than the hybrid lemma and bounds greedy's expected cost by a constant depending on \(\varepsilon\); the cited offline-cost scale is \(\Theta(1/\varepsilon)\) (Lemma 16, PDF p. 14). For \(\varepsilon=0\), Theorem 1 applies. The constants are not asserted uniform as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

**Theorem 3 (PDF p. 5; printed p. 3; proof PDF pp. 15–16 and Appendix F).** In the random-requests model the greedy ratio is \(O(\log n)\). The upper-bound proof uses a hybrid comparison with an online constant-competitive neighboring-match algorithm, together with the hybrid lemma and the relevant cost estimates (PDF pp. 15–24; Appendix F, pp. 45–47).

**Theorem 4 (PDF pp. 5, 19; printed pp. 3, 17).** In the same random-requests model the greedy ratio is \(\Omega(\log n)\); with Theorem 3 the ratio is \(\Theta(\log n)\). The lower bound constructs an adversarial server set with a large mass at 0, a server-free initial interval, and geometrically arranged remaining locations. The proof bounds \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]=O(n^{3/5})\) and \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{greedy})]=\Omega(n^{3/5}\log n)\) (overview §5.1–5.3, PDF pp. 16–24; full proof Appendices H–I, pp. 50–84). The paper also states an instance-free \(\Theta(\log n)\) conclusion for this model.

## Unresolved extension

Appendix A.4 (PDF p. 30; printed p. 28) leaves open the fully random **sublinear excess** regime with n requests and \(n+s_n\) uniform servers where \(s_n=o(n)\). The paper notes a change from the no-excess expected scale \(\Omega(1/\sqrt n)\) per final request/cost contribution to \(O(1/n)\) once excess is linear, but does not prove an interpolation theorem for intermediate \(s_n\). Do not extend Theorem 2 to a vanishing or n-dependent excess fraction based on its fixed-constant quantifier.

## Transfer boundary

The hybrid lemma is for this line metric and its coupled free-server sets; the constant-competitive comparator is a particular online algorithm. Theorems 1–2 assume random server locations, whereas Theorems 3–4 deliberately allow adversarial servers. None implies a prophet ratio for independent rewards or an optimal matching result in higher dimensions. The paper's higher-dimensional experiments are numerical, not theorems.
