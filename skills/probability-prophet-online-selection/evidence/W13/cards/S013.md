## Main statements and exact scope

- **Theorem 1 (pp. 2, 7–8; negative result).** For every integer `k≥1` and every `ε>0`, there is a prophet-inequality instance on a graphic matroid of girth at least `k` for which no `(1/2+ε)`-competitive algorithm exists. The displayed hard instance fixes the order using only the instance/distributions, so the result already holds for the paper's **fixed-order adversary**. It does not say that high girth is sufficient for a positive ratio approaching one.
- **Theorem 2 (p. 3; positive result).** Every prophet-inequality instance whose feasibility family is a `k`-fold union of a matroid admits a `(1−O(√(log k/k)))`-competitive algorithm. The guarantee is against the **almighty adversary** and the usual prophet benchmark `E[max_{S∈F} Σ_{e∈S}v_e]`. The proof passes through a `(1−O(√(log k/k)))`-selectable online contention-resolution scheme (OCRS).
- **Theorem 3 (pp. 4, 15; bicriterion concentration).** Let `X∼Ber(p)` have independent coordinates and let `f:{0,1}^n→R` be monotone and 1-Lipschitz in Hamming/ℓ₁ distance. For `s∈(0,1]`, define `X^(s)∼Ber(e^(−s)p)` by scaling every Bernoulli parameter; for every `t>0`,
  `Pr[f(X^(s)) ≥ E f(X)+t] ≤ exp(−s t)`.
  This dimension-free upper-tail statement concerns the **down-scaled** input `X^(s)` with benchmark `E f(X)`. It is not a tail bound for `f(X)` itself.

## Proof mechanisms

**Theorem 1.** Start from a dense graph `G_n` with `m≥n log n` edges and girth at least `k`; subdivide each edge into a two-edge path through a new vertex, giving a graphic matroid of girth at least `2k`. For pair `i`, the first edge has value 1 and the second has value `1/ε` with probability `ε`, otherwise 0; reveal pairs in fixed order. Offline can choose the larger edge of every pair, giving `OPT≥m(2−ε)`. A forest can include both edges in at most `n−1` pairs. Thus the online value is at most `m+n(1+1/ε)`; with `m≥n log n`, the ratio is at most `1/(2−ε)+o_n(1)`, then tends to `1/2` as `ε↓0` (Claim 8, pp. 7–8). The printed theorem's “girth k” is satisfied by this stronger at-least-`2k` construction.

**Theorem 2.** For matroid `M`, the construction lifts the ground set to `E×[k]` and works first in an extended `k`-fold union. Its occupancy function
`ω_e(S)=k−rank(S∪({e}×[k]))+rank(S)`
is monotone and 1-Lipschitz (Lemmas 13, 15–16; pp. 12, 21). `KFoldProtect` builds a proper protected set whose restriction remains an extended `k`-fold union (Lemma 18; p. 22 uses a telescoping expected-rank bound). The OCRS applies the protected-greedy chain scheme after independent thinning by `e^{−(1−b)}` (Algorithms 3–4, p. 13). For a layer, `f(S)=ω_e(S∪N_1)` has `E f(X)≤bk`; setting `b=1−√(log k/k)`, `s=1−b=√(log k/k)`, and `t=√(k log k)` gives `Pr[f(X^(s))≥k]≤exp(−st)=1/k` by Theorem 3. The conditional selectability is therefore at least `1−1/k`; Fact 10 and Lemma 13 transfer the extended OCRS guarantee to the original `k`-fold union, with factor `(1−1/k)b e^{−(1−b)}=1−O(√(log k/k))` (pp. 13–14). This chain and formula were checked against the PDF rendering on p. 14.

**Theorem 3.** The proof uses the modified log-Sobolev inequality (Lemma 21, p. 16) and a parameter-dependent transform, not the ordinary moment-generating function: `F(λ)=E[e^{λ Z^(λ)}]` where `Z^(λ)=f(X^(λ))`. A derivative identity (Lemma 23), the 1-Lipschitz decrement bound (Lemma 24), and scalar inequalities for `φ(u)=e^u−u−1` (Facts 25–26) yield `λF′(λ)≤F(λ)log F(λ)` (Lemma 22, pp. 17–19). With `G=log F` and `G₀(λ)=λE[Z^(0)]`, the quotient `(G−G₀)/λ` is nonincreasing and tends to zero at 0, hence `F(λ)≤exp(λE f(X))`. Markov's inequality at `λ=s` gives the theorem (pp. 17–18). No empirical or computational evidence is used in this proof.

## Boundaries and caveats

- The `O(√(log k/k))` loss and the bicriterion slack both matter: here `s=√(log k/k)` and `t=√(k log k)`. An OCR text extraction drops radical signs on pp. 14–15; the original PDF render shows the square roots and the identity `st=log k`.
- Remark 19 and Example 29 (pp. 15, 22–23) show that replacing `f(X^(s))` by `f(X)` is not a valid general concentration claim, even for the particular occupancy function in the construction. Preserve the scaling in downstream uses.
- The positive prophet/OCRS result is for `k`-fold matroid unions, not all matroids of large girth, arbitrary downward-closed families, or arbitrary packing constraints. The large-girth result is a separate negative statement.
- The negative theorem is proved against fixed order; the positive theorem handles the stronger almighty adversary. Do not reverse or conflate these quantifiers.
- This paper's theorems are analytic. The local computation in `../reader_batches/L09_batch2/computations/s013_bicriterion_sanity.py` only checks parameter identities and finite numerical values; it does not establish the concentration inequality or OCRS theorem.
