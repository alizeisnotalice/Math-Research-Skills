## Scope and principal classical comparisons

This is a historical survey, with literature cutoff in 1992; its statements describe what was known by the authors at that time. It organizes independent-variable prophet inequalities by objective, information, order, and number of choices. It records the classical one-choice multiplicative bound `E[max_i X_i]≤2 sup_τ E[X_τ]` for independent nonnegative variables with finite expectations, and the bounded `[0,1]` additive estimate `E[max_i X_i]−sup_τ E[X_τ]≤1/4`; the paper discusses their sharpness and variants in §§1–2 (PDF pp. 2–6; printed pp. 192–196). IID refinements are described through recurrences and extremal-law reductions with references to the primary papers (PDF pp. 4–7); use M0034/M0037 for theorem-level claims rather than treating the survey as their proof.

Section 3 covers ordered selection and multiple-choice models, keeping the gambler’s and prophet’s choice capacities distinct (PDF pp. 7–9; printed pp. 197–199). Section 4 catalogues proof methods—backward induction, dilation, induction, conditioning, constrained optimization, conjugate duality, dynamic-program verification, algebraic inequalities, and moment methods (PDF pp. 9–13; printed pp. 199–203). Those are a map of techniques, not general-purpose lemmas without their original hypotheses.

## Open questions as historical evidence

Section 5 lists five open questions as of the survey’s publication, including comparisons between prophet and threshold-policy values and extensions involving averages/exchangeability (PDF pp. 13–14; printed pp. 203–204). These are historical open questions only; the survey does not establish their present status. Resolve current status against later primary sources before describing any as open.

## Use and limits

Use this source for terminology, historical context, and locating classical arguments. It does not settle the current A001 multi-choice infimum/limit problem, and a survey’s pre-1992 open-question list must not be repeated as a current conjecture without rechecking later work.
