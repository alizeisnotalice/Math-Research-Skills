## Two different stopping models

The paper treats (i) independent, not necessarily identically distributed nonnegative values `X_i` revealed in a uniformly random permutation, with a **nonadaptive** threshold `τ_i` fixed per identity before arrival; and (ii) **adaptive** thresholds after each rejection, but only for IID nonnegative values and one irrevocable choice. Both compare the policy to `E[max_i X_i]`. These are not k-capacity results, and the IID adaptive theorem is not the heterogeneous random-order theorem.

### Theorem 1: nonadaptive random-order guarantee

For every finite collection of independent nonnegative `X_i ~ F_i`, there are fixed thresholds `τ_i` such that the expected reward is at least `(1−1/e) E[max_i X_i]` (Theorem 1, PDF pp. 4–5; printed pp. 1455–1456). The policy accepts the first arriving `i` whose value exceeds its threshold. The proof reduces to the Bernoulli Selection Lemma: letting `Y_i=1{X_i>τ_i}`, `q_i=P(Y_i=1)`, and `b_i=E[X_i | Y_i=1]`, a feasible fractional vector `z_i≤q_i`, `Σ_i z_i≤1` can be rounded by selecting a subset with expected average successful prize at least `(1−1/e)` times the fractional optimum, which in turn lower-bounds `(1−1/e)E[max_i b_iY_i]`. A random permutation makes the first qualifying identity uniform among qualifiers, giving the threshold policy's expected payoff. Their tight example shows the nonadaptive factor cannot be improved uniformly, even for IID values.

## Theorem 2: adaptive IID, one-slot guarantee

For every finite `n` and IID nonnegative values, the dynamic-programming optimal thresholds `τ_i` satisfy

`E[max(X_1,…,X_n)] ≤ β* E[X_t]`,

where `t=min{i : X_i ≥ τ_i}` and `β*≈1.341` is the unique solution of

`∫_0^1 [ y(1−ln y) + β−1 ]^(−1) dy = 1`.

Thus `1/β*≈0.745` is a valid universal one-slot IID prophet ratio and is sharp over the family as `n` varies; this does not say every fixed finite `n` has exact worst-case ratio `1/β*`. Theorem 2 is stated on PDF p. 5 (printed p. 1456) and proved in §4 on PDF pp. 13–14 (printed pp. 1464–1465). The auxiliary proofs of Lemmas 6–7 used in the ODE comparison are in Appendix D, PDF pp. 24–25 (printed pp. 1475–1476); Appendix A instead contains technical results for §2.

### Proof chain and tie handling

1. For quantile `q`, write `R(q)=∫_0^q F^(−1)(1−u)du`. A randomized quantile-threshold rule partitions `[0,1]` into intervals and equalizes the induced recurrence coefficients `ρ_i`; Lemmas 4–5 (§4, PDF pp. 11–12) yield `E[max] ≤ n γ_1 E[X_r]`, with `γ_1=1−x_1^(n−1)`.
2. The interval equalization gives a Hill–Kertz recurrence with endpoints `x_0=1`, `x_n=0`. Its terms are dominated by the unique solution to `y'(t)= y(t)(ln y(t)−1)−(β−1)`, `y(0)=1`; Lemmas 6–7 (PDF p. 13) give existence, monotonicity/convexity, and the comparison.
3. Choose β so the inverse trajectory runs from `y=1` at `t=0` to `y=0` at `t=1`. Separation of variables gives the displayed integral equation (PDF p. 14). This bounds `nγ_1≤β*` for every finite n. Hill–Kertz tight examples, whose finite-n best factors converge to `β*`, establish sharpness of the universal constant.
4. When a threshold atom occurs, the proof randomizes between accepting at `X≥τ` and `X>τ`. The quantile reward is linear over the atom interval, so one of the two deterministic tie conventions does at least as well as the randomized rule; the optimal dynamic-programming thresholds are indifferent at equality. Do not silently assume a continuous distribution.

## Posted-price transfer and A001 boundary

Corollaries in Section 5 transfer the threshold rules to single-item posted prices via positive (ironed) virtual values and compare revenue to Myerson's optimal auction revenue; regularity is used for the direct virtual-value representation, while nonregular distributions require ironing. This is a revenue theorem with its own assumptions, not a statement about arbitrary buyer strategic behavior.

For A001, M0036 supplies the one-slot IID threshold upper comparison at each finite `n` and the sharp universal constant. It does **not** prove `inf_F`/`n→∞` interchange for `k>1`; nor does a fixed-F asymptotic theorem settle an infimum whose near-worst-case distribution can vary with n. M0037's exact `k=1` result is the relevant explicit limit/infimum equality.
