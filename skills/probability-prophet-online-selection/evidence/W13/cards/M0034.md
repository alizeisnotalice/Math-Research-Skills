## Theorem A: finite-horizon IID multiplicative comparison

Let `n>1`, and let `X_1,…,X_n` be IID nonnegative random variables with finite expectation and common law `F`. Let

`E_n(X)=E[max(X_1,…,X_n)]`,  `V_n(X)=sup_{t∈T_n} E[X_t]`,

where `T_n` is the set of stopping times for the natural observation filtration. Theorem A (PDF p. 1; printed p. 336) states `E_n(X) ≤ a_n V_n(X)` for the best universal factor `a_n=1+α_n`. Here `α_n` is the unique positive root of `G_n(α)=1`, defined by `T_{0,n}(α)=φ_n(0,α)`, `T_{j,n}(α)=φ_n(T_{j−1,n}(α),α)`, `G_n=T_{n−1,n}`, and `φ_n(w,α)= n/(n−1) w^((n−1)/n)+α/(n−1)`. The factor is not attained by a nontrivial finite-expectation distribution; equality in the ratio bound is approached by the finite-support extremal constructions of Proposition 4.4 (PDF pp. 8–9; printed pp. 343–344).

The paper gives `1/(3e) ≤ α_n ≤ 1/(e−1)`. For `n=2`, `α_2=3−2√2≈0.171573`, so `a_2=4−2√2≈1.171573`; its table reports `a_100≈1.337` and `a_10000≈1.341` (PDF pp. 4, 8; printed pp. 339, 343). These are finite-horizon worst-case factors.

## Theorem B: finite-horizon additive comparison for bounded IID values

For IID `X_i∈[a,b]`, Theorem B gives `E[max_i X_i]−V_n(X) ≤ β_n(b−a)`. The sharp additive constant `β_n` is attained and is specified by the paper’s second recurrence/root equation `H_n(β)=1` (PDF pp. 1, 9; printed pp. 336, 344). Examples reported include `β_2=1/16`, `β_100≈0.1101`, and `β_10000≈0.113`.

## Proof route and asymptotic boundary

The proof begins with the stopping dynamic program `V_j=E[X∨V_{j−1}]` and its optimal threshold rule (Lemma 2.1, PDF p. 2). Dilation/convex-order lemmas reduce the sharp comparison to a simple finite-support law whose support is built from the stopping values (Lemmas 2.3–2.5). The authors then express both the prophet maximum and stopping value through cumulative probabilities, optimize a finite-dimensional ratio, derive the recurrence for its unique interior maximizer, and construct laws approaching the factor (Theorem A proof, PDF pp. 5–9). The bounded additive problem has a parallel finite-support reduction and extremizer (Theorem B proof, PDF pp. 9–10).

The authors explicitly say they believe `α_n` and `β_n` increase to limits, but do **not** prove monotonicity or those limits (Remark 5, PDF p. 10; printed p. 345). Their separate fixed-law remark says `R_n(X)=E[max(X_1,…,X_n)]/V_n(X)→1` and the additive gap tends to zero for each fixed `F`; it is not uniform over laws. Thus this source gives the finite-n worst-case value `inf_F V_n(F)/E[max_iX_i]=1/a_n` (for nonzero finite-expectation laws) but does not justify exchanging that infimum with `n→∞`. Keep an `F_n` sequence of near-extremizers in view. M0037 separately proves the one-choice limiting identity; do not attribute that result to this 1982 paper.

## Use and limits

Use this source for exact finite-n IID one-choice threshold comparisons, sharp additive bounds under bounded support, and the historical proof-reduction toolkit. It does not prove the multi-choice `k>1` infimum/limit equality, nor does pointwise fixed-`F` convergence provide the required uniformity.
