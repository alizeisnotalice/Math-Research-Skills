## Vertex-arrival result and exact hypotheses

For a weighted bipartite graph, the right side is known in advance; left-side vertices arrive online and reveal all incident edge weights. The order-oblivious greedy-based algorithm samples a prefix of length K∼Binom(|L|,p), computes greedy on sample vertices plus the current vertex to choose a candidate edge, and matches it if its right endpoint is still free. “Order-oblivious” means the guarantee holds for every arrival order after the sampling phase.

- **Theorem 2.6 (printed p. 10; physical p. 11):** for every p∈[0,1], this algorithm is order-oblivious p(1−p)/(1+p)-competitive.
- **Corollary 2.7 (printed p. 10; physical p. 11):** at p=√2−1 the ratio is 3−2√2≈0.171573=1/(3+2√2)≈1/5.828. Theorem 2.8 constructs a sequence showing tightness for every p, for any post-sample arrival order.

The proof builds a directed line graph of original edges, processes vertices in descending edge weight, and colors their incident-edge clusters red/sample or blue/online. A node is active exactly when the corresponding edge is in greedy on the sample plus that edge. “Qualifying” active blue edges are guaranteed candidates and survive the worst post-sample order. Lemma 2.4 gives E[ALG]≥(1−p)E[Greedy(sample)]. Lemma 2.5 proves and tightly attains E[Greedy(sample)]≥p/(1+p)·OPT, by splitting each optimal-edge weight between endpoints with α=1/(1+p). Multiplying yields Theorem 2.6.

## AOSp and two-faced models

These are distinct from the random-order model above.

- In **AOSp**, an adversary fixes a weighted instance; each left vertex independently enters the history set H with probability p; the remaining vertices arrive in an adversarial order. The benchmark is E[OPT(G[O])] on the online complement O. Theorem 3.1 (printed p. 14; physical p. 15) gives the tight ratio p(1−p) for p≤1/2. Theorem 3.3 (printed p. 15; physical p. 16) shows random thinning of H gives 1/4 for p>1/2. The theorem's proof couples H and O using shared uniform random variables and applies the tight sample-greedy bound.
- The paper defines a stronger **two-faced batched online-selection** model: an adversary picks two weight vectors per batch, one is sampled uniformly as history, the other is online; the adversarial order may depend on the revealed pair. Theorem 4.6 (printed pp. 17–18; physical pp. 18–19) generalizes the order-oblivious-to-two-faced reduction to batches. It implies a 3−2√2 guarantee for bipartite vertex arrivals (Corollary 4.7), and Theorem 4.8 proves an upper bound 2/5 for any algorithm in that two-faced matching model.
- For general-graph **edge arrivals**, Theorem 5.8 (printed p. 25; physical p. 26) gives the order-oblivious ratio c(p)=p²(1−p)/2 for p≤(√5−1)/2, and c(p)=(1−p)(2p−1)/(2p) above that threshold. Corollary 5.9 yields 3/2−√2≈0.085786 in the two-faced edge-arrival model. Theorems 5.10–5.11 give separate AOSp bounds; for p>1/√2, thinning the sample achieves 3/2−√2.

## Boundaries for use

The sample-prefix random-order guarantee, independent-sampling AOSp guarantee, two-faced guarantee, and single-sample prophet inequality are separate models with different benchmark/input semantics. The paper's black-box reduction preserves an order-oblivious guarantee in the two-faced model; it does not identify AOSp with random order. For edge arrivals in general graphs, the authors explicitly leave open whether their tight analysis of the algorithm is the optimal algorithmic guarantee. Do not transfer any matching ratio to ex-ante constraints or OCRS without an additional theorem.
