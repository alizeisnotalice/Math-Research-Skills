## Model and precise conclusions

The basic model has n>1 independent, nonnegative item values with known distributions. In **order selection**, the algorithm chooses the item order before seeing values, then accepts at most one value online. In **prophet secretary**, the arrival order is uniformly random; Theorem 3 studies the stronger order-aware version, where the algorithm knows that order in advance. The benchmark is E[max_i v_i].

- **Theorem 1 (p. 3):** there is a 0.7258-competitive order-selection algorithm. This is a threshold algorithm using independent, carefully chosen arrival times; it is proved first to have 0.7258-approximate stochastic dominance (ASD).
- **Theorem 2 (pp. 3, 13–16):** for every finite-support order-selection instance, existence of a Γ-competitive algorithm on all such instances implies existence of a Γ-ASD algorithm on all such instances. The proof reduces to a finite set of deterministic order/threshold policies, writes a primal LP whose constraints are the tail comparisons, proves the primal value is at least Γ, and uses strong duality to mix deterministic policies into an ASD policy. The paper notes analogous extensions to order-aware/order-unaware prophet secretary and constrained orders, with the deterministic policy space changed accordingly.
- **Theorem 3 (pp. 4, 16–17):** no algorithm achieves a ratio strictly above 0.7254 on every order-aware prophet-secretary instance. The construction has N=10^5 iid variables with a 12-level rare-value distribution and one deterministic value a=0.82. The optimal stopping value is computed for each of the N+1 positions of the deterministic item, then averaged over its uniform position. The paper reports the limiting ratio 0.725398<0.7254, and uses the ε→0+ limit to infer a positive ε instance with ratio below 0.7254. In Eq. (7), each nonzero support value b_i contributes b_i times the probability that the maximum equals b_i; that expected-value contribution is not the atom probability mass by itself. This also upper-bounds the order-unaware variant.

Here ASD means P[ALG>x] ≥ Γ P[max_i v_i>x] for every x>0. For nonnegative values, integrating the tails gives the expected-value guarantee. Theorem 2 is stated only for finite supports; do not silently promote that exact statement to arbitrary distributions.

## Theorem 1 proof chain and hypotheses

For a common decreasing threshold τ(t), set p_i(t)=P[v_i>τ(t)], q_i(t)=P[max_{j≠i}v_j>τ(t)], and

    g(t,Γ)=1+Γ(Σ_i (1−q_i(t))p_i(t)−t),
    f_i(t,Γ)=Γ q_i'(t)/g(t,Γ) · exp(−Γ∫_0^t q_i'(s)p_i(s)/g(s,Γ) ds).

Each item independently draws an arrival time with density f_i on [0,1) and the remaining probability at t_i=1; items arrive in increasing time and the first item above threshold is accepted, while t=1 is rejected. The construction is well-defined exactly when ∫_0^1 f_i(t,Γ)dt≤1 for every i. Theorem 4 gives this at Γ_PT≈0.7251; Theorem 5 says well-defined densities yield Γ-ASD.

Section 3 generalizes to item-dependent continuous, strictly decreasing, surjective thresholds τ̄_i(t), with p̄_i(t)=P[v_i>τ̄_i(t)], q̄_i(t)=P[max_{j≠i}v_j>τ̄_i(t)], and ḡ=1−ΓΣ_i∫_0^t p̄_i(s)q̄_i'(s)ds. **Theorem 6 (pp. 7–11)** gives ASD if the corresponding f̄_i are well-defined. Its proof decomposes P[ALG>x] into accepted-above-threshold and threshold-crossing terms, derives each through the probability of no earlier acceptance (Lemma 7), and identifies their sum with ΓP[max_i v_i>x] (Lemma 8). In these framework sections the authors assume continuous distributions with positive densities on contiguous supports, so p_i,q_i are strictly increasing; discrete instances are handled by approximation as cited in the paper.

For Γ*=0.7258, Section 4 uses Scheme I (Peng–Tang common threshold) when every f_i integrates to at most one. Otherwise, choose an adverse item 1 and use Scheme II: keep the common threshold for i≠1, and transform its exceedance probability through a piecewise-linear increasing map h, with c=0.28 and ε→0+. **Theorem 9 (pp. 12, 17–28)** proves all adjusted densities integrate to at most one. Its quantitative proof uses weak-adverseness estimates (Lemmas 18–20), the exclusion of a second weakly adverse item, and Lemmas 21–22; the constants are partly verified numerically in the paper. Theorem 1 then follows by applying Theorems 5 or 6 in the two respective cases. This is a specialized continuous-arrival-time construction, not a static-threshold theorem.

## Scope and transfer limits

This source supports a separation between the full-information **order-selection** single-choice prophet problem and **random-order prophet secretary**, including the order-aware hardness. It does not prove a result for multiple-unit selection, sample-based models, bipartite matching, or a static threshold rule. The 0.7254 hardness attaches to the specified order-aware random permutation model, not arbitrary arrival processes.
