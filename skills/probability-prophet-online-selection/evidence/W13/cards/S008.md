## Models and headline results

**Single-choice prophet secretary.** There are independent, known item distributions; the items arrive in a uniformly random permutation and the algorithm accepts at most one value. Theorem 1 (pp. 5, 11–14, 33–35) gives a 0.688-competitive algorithm. The authors assume discrete supports and disjoint supports for exposition; continuous distributions are approximated and ties can be broken by infinitesimal perturbations.

The algorithm uses **activation probabilities/rates**, rather than only direct acceptance thresholds. For each item-value pair, it designs an activation probability so that the first activated item is accepted. Lemmas 2–5 compute activation and acceptance probabilities; the sufficient condition in Lemma 7 lower-bounds acceptance of every item-value pair that is the prophet maximum. A warm-up constant-rate rule gives 1−1/e (Theorem 8). The two-stage step-rate rule gives 0.694 only under the explicit smallness condition Σ_{i,v}(2x_i^v−p_i^v)_+≤1−ln 2 (Theorem 10); it is not the final general guarantee.

The main algorithm singles out i₀, the item with largest x₀=P[i₀ is the maximum], and uses a three-stage activation-probability rule for it, while the other items use two-stage activation rates. Lemmas 11–14 bound the exceptional concentration contributed by the largest item and verify all rates/probabilities are valid. In §2.5, Lemma 15 is the fixed-s=2, 0.686 threshold-search bound. Appendix C generalizes the algorithm by optimizing s; Appendix D proves Lemma 35, which establishes the 0.688 claim by a computer-assisted grid/search argument: s is selected piecewise as 3, 2.5, or 2 according to x₀; x₀,h₀ are rounded to a 10⁻⁴ grid; a finite search verifies a threshold triple. The paper does not give closed-form β choices for every instance. The appendix's numerical verification is part of this theorem's stated proof; the rendering here confirms the formulas but does not independently reproduce that large search.

**Prophet secretary matching.** There is a bipartite type graph with offline vertices known in advance and n online vertices whose types are independent and known-distribution; each arrives at an independent uniform time in [0,1], revealing its type/incident edge weights. The algorithm may match it to an unmatched offline vertex. The expected maximum matching is upper-bounded by the LP in Section 3.1; the authors assume a 1-regular LP instance after adding dummy types/vertices.

- **Theorem 17 (physical p. 15):** a hybrid that runs multistage activation-based matching (MAM) with probability 0.8 and constant-activation-except-one-large-vertex (CAR) with probability 0.2 is 0.641-competitive. The theorem sentence calls the second component “CPR,” while its displayed guarantee uses Γ_CAR and Section 3.5 names that algorithm CAR; this card follows the displayed Γ_CAR guarantee. The proof combines Theorem 22's edge-selection probability Γ_MAM(xᵘ)x_i^(u,v) with Theorem 27's Γ_CAR(xᵘ)x_i^(u,v); the pointwise inequality in xᵘ is verified with computer assistance. MAM is designed for infinitesimal contributions (its limiting ratio is about 0.645); CAR complements larger contributions and ranges from 1−1/e at x=0 to √3−1 at x=1. Neither component alone gives the hybrid's 0.641 worst-case bound.
- **Theorem 18 (pp. 15, 27):** there is a 0.641 approximation for query-commit stochastic bipartite matching. This follows through the stated reduction: simulate online arrival times and decompose each online vertex's target match-probability vector into extreme points corresponding to query orders. The query-commit model additionally assumes independent edge existence and commits on a successful query.

## Transfer boundaries

The single-choice result is a full-information, random-order result. The matching theorem is for vertex arrivals with independent online-vertex types, known distributions, and an LP upper bound; it is not a claim for adversarial order, ex-ante constraints, arbitrary matching online models, or multi-unit static thresholds. The query-commit corollary requires its independent edge-existence model and the reduction in Appendix A. Do not conflate prophet secretary, query-commit, order selection, and sample-based matching.
