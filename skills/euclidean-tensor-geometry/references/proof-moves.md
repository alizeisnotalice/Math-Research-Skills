# Proof moves and failure modes

The formulas here describe the pinned manuscript. They are not a license to assume a tensor-price model for other objects.

## Spectral semiring and resource accounting

Fix a field, a finite site set S, and finite-dimensional local vector spaces. Restriction T → U uses one linear map at each site. Identify groups of legs at the same site as one local factor. Grouped tensor product puts corresponding factors at each site; direct sum uses disjoint local blocks. A spectral point F preserves 0, 1, sums of those blocks and grouped products and satisfies F(U) ≤ F(T) under restriction. For positive diameter, the Euclidean price at that placement is H(T)=δ(S) log₂ F(T), where δ(S)=H(Δ_S(2)). Here Δ_P(q) is the diagonal that copies one q-valued basis label to every site of P, with trivial legs elsewhere; H(Δ_P(q))=δ(P) log₂ q. The q law follows by taking powers and comparing label counts against neighboring powers of 2. Use H(0)=-∞ and treat the single-site case separately.

- **Pair:** δ({a,b})=d(a,b). A dimension-q wire routed between two sites has cost d(a,b) log₂ q. Composing with a local contraction gives a transport upper bound; reversing a move gives the absolute difference bound obtained by summing each leg's displacement times the logarithm of its dimension.
- **Shared label:** δ(P) is the normalized mean width of the convex hull of the sites in P; a triangle contributes half its perimeter. A spanning tree connecting P gives a direct resource construction and hence an upper bound by its total length (auxiliary vertices are allowed); a restriction to any pair gives the diameter lower bound. Neither tree length nor diameter may replace normalized mean width by equality in general. Distinct tensor factors at common sites are not automatically independent chances or disjoint physical mass.
- **Product/contraction:** add log prices for grouped products and use monotonicity for the output; expand the network's resources before charging endpoints. Fixed placements allow exact additivity for grouped independent products even when an adversarial output network might mix those products, provided that network is actually covered by the comparison.
- **Ordinary sum:** same output sites and spaces; auxiliary sites may vary. Let δ=δ(S)>0. Then H(Σ_i T_i) ≤ δ log₂ Σ_i 2^(H(T_i)/δ) ≤ max_i H(T_i)+δ log₂(number of summands). Prove that ordinary addition is a restriction of block direct sum. Do not add log prices for ordinary sums, and don't omit δ or the number of summands. Cancellation can lower the result, not invalidate the upper estimate.
- **Border closure:** at a fixed format and fixed placement, an upper sublevel bound extends to the Zariski closure under the relevant algebraically closed-field model. Argument: degree-m tensor powers from the family span the corresponding powers of closure points; at most polynomially many terms in m for fixed coordinate dimension, so the above sum penalty divided by m vanishes. This is not a simultaneous uniform estimate when format/dimension grows with m. Track exact polynomial identities and scalar extensions separately.
- **Inverse direction:** one monotone gives an obstruction when the target's price exceeds the purported source budget. For any asymptotic existence direction, invoke the precise Strassen comparison theorem and its universal quantifier over relevant spectral points, with its hypotheses; a single favorable price comparison is not sufficient.

## Reusable comparisons

- **Variable cuts, one coordinate system.** Put the two terminals of each graph edge at opposite unit vectors on its own orthogonal axis. In the resultant graph product, each edge-wire contributes its price; summing the terminal vectors of a vertex set cancels internal edges, leaving squared norm equal to the cut size. In the hierarchy manuscript, moving supply sites creates a uniform per-summand gap while output terminals stay fixed. In the circuit paper, the balanced-gate step extracts a sum of tensors of low rank on possibly different balanced cuts, so pricing all terms at one placement replaces an invalid attempt to use a different flattening for each side of the inequality. This requires the actual coefficient-extraction/depth-reduction argument for the target model; the coordinate trick alone is not a circuit lower bound.
- **Degenerate placement tests.** The finite Fourier manuscript duplicates row/column labels in a four-leg matrix tensor. Collapsing one rectangle dimension reads log rank; collapsing the other reads log support size. This audits consistency and supplies endpoint estimates but does not by itself prove the full Fourier complexity result: its batch lower bound needs the character-table argument and its upper bound uses prediction with sparse exact corrections. Check that label duplication and the chosen phase/gadget preserve the correct tensor.
- **Compression contradicted by a separate obstruction.** The matrix paper compares a trivalent-network compression theorem against a uniform-in-copy-count volume lower bound for list distinctness. If transferring this strategy, track input size, number of copies, exterior resource charge, the class of networks, and the range where the lower bound is uniform. One-copy lower bounds cannot simply be multiplied if batched networks may share gates.
- **Broadcasting while returning the old resource.** For disjoint nonempty P and Z, the sunflower manuscript's lemma extends the coherent label from P to P∪Z and returns a fixed old resource. The lower bound subtracts old price only because that resource is returned unchanged and factors from the enlarged label. Its construction achieves the incremental price plus a sublinear overhead in bit length for fixed sites. A correlated or depleted by-product is not a catalyst. In the combinatorial application, the transversal word reduction and the positive-characteristic degree constraint are substantive additional steps.

## Sanity checks

Verify δ(single site)=0, δ(two sites)=distance, and normalization under rescaling; rederive transport and the sum penalty before using placement optimization. On any proof transfer list exactly which step bounds the target below and which uniformly bounds candidates above. Test exact versus border, characteristic two versus odd characteristic, and circuits versus tapes where these distinctions actually occur. Do not use numerical approximations, PDF headlines, or version-agnostic links as verification of the underlying theorems.
