## Weak duality and exact gap certificate

For a problem with objective `f_0(x)`, inequalities `f_i(x)≤0`, and equalities `h_i(x)=0`, define

`L(x,λ,ν)=f_0(x)+Σ_i λ_i f_i(x)+Σ_i ν_i h_i(x)`, `g(λ,ν)=inf_x L(x,λ,ν)`.

For `λ≥0`, every primal-feasible x satisfies `g(λ,ν)≤f_0(x)`, hence `d*≤p*` (Eq. 5.2 and surrounding discussion). A dual point is useful only if it belongs to `dom g`; a value `−∞` gives a vacuous bound. Strong duality and attainment are additional conclusions, not part of weak duality.

Section 5.5.1, “Suboptimality bounds,” (printed pp. 241–242; PDF pp. 255–256) states that for any primal-feasible (x) and dual-feasible ((\lambda,\nu)), both (p^*) and (d^*) lie in ([g(\lambda,\nu),f_0(x)]). This is a section, not a numbered theorem. Thus the primal-dual gap bounds suboptimality; a zero gap certifies optimality. Relative-gap criteria require a denominator with the appropriate sign and nonzero value.

## Qualification, KKT and sensitivity

For a convex problem with convex objective/inequalities and affine equalities, Slater’s condition (a point in `relint D` strictly satisfying the nonlinear inequalities and satisfying equalities) yields strong duality and, when `d*>−∞`, dual attainment. Refined Slater treats affine inequalities as non-strict. Feasible finite LPs meet this refinement. Without a qualification, do not assume equality of primal and dual values or dual attainment.

Complementary slackness (Eq. 5.48) is a property of attained optimal primal-dual pairs with zero gap. KKT (Eq. 5.49) is necessary at an optimal pair under differentiability and the stated regularity for a convex formulation; KKT is sufficient for convex problems, and Slater supplies necessity for the convex case. For nonconvex differentiable problems, KKT is only a necessary condition at regular optima, not a global certificate.

Under strong duality and an attained dual optimum, Eq. 5.57 gives the one-sided perturbation bound `p*(u,v)≥p*(0,0)−λ*ᵀu−ν*ᵀv`. The derivative formula in Eq. 5.58 additionally needs differentiability of the value function at zero. A subgradient does not furnish an unproved two-sided sensitivity estimate.

## Boundary and use

Use this chapter to structure a finite optimization certificate: state primal/dual conventions, verify feasibility, and report a rigorous gap. For SDP, complementary slackness is a trace inner-product condition; zero inner product need not force one of two nonzero PSD matrices to vanish. Equivalent primal formulations can have different-looking duals. None of these finite-dimensional facts proves the analytic minimax, online-policy, or asymptotic interchange needed in A001/A002/A005.
