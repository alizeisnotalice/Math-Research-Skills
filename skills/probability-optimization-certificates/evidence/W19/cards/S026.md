## What the paper establishes empirically and algorithmically

This is a computational status report, not a general theorem about all MIP algorithms. It describes hybrid exact-rational branch-and-bound: symbolic rational presolving, exact repair of rounded primal candidates, safe dual-bound methods (bound-shift and project-and-shift), iterative refinement/exact LP solving, and verifiable certificate logging. The paper reports experiments on selected “fpeasy”/“numdiff” sets and MIPLIB 2017; its time and solve-count comparisons are empirical for those test sets and implementation versions.

An exact primal repair fixes selected integer variables and solves the remaining continuous subproblem exactly; if the exact subproblem is feasible, the result is an exact feasible incumbent. A floating-point incumbent by itself is not enough. Safe dual bounds require exact or rigorously bounded arithmetic, particularly where coefficient sizes or conditioning defeat ordinary tolerance checks.

Certificate generation records a proof derivation/checkable branch-and-bound evidence (building on VIPR); the article notes that some presolving features had to be disabled because they did not emit certificates. A verifier can catch invalid proof steps but does not independently prove the solver implementation correct. Certificate production/checking has measured overhead, so the paper reports both correctness and computational cost.

## Boundary and use

Use as evidence that exact/certified solver workflows can be practical and how to separate heuristic candidate generation from proof. Do not quote their experimental speedups as universal guarantees. Finite MIP proof machinery certifies only the encoded rational instance; a separate analytic argument is required for every continuum, asymptotic, or stochastic-model claim.
