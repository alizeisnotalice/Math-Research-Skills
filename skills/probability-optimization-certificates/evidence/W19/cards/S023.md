# S023 — Dym: quasi branch and bound for smooth global optimization

**Reader:** L13 · **coverage:** complete arXiv v1, physical pp. 1–21 · **review:** pending

Nadav Dym, “Quasi Branch and Bound for Smooth Global Optimization,” arXiv:2005.13728v1 (2020-05-28), 21 pages. The official arXiv metadata, version, and local PDF identity match.

## Core definition and global correctness

For a continuous f on a compact cube C₀, f*(C)=min_{x∈C}f(x). A quasi-lower bound qlb(C) is required to satisfy qlb(C)≤f*(C) **only for cubes containing a global minimizer** (Definition 3, physical p.5). It may exceed the local minimum on a cube without a global minimizer. Pruning qlb(C)>ub is safe: if C contained a global minimizer, qlb(C)≤f*≤ub would contradict it. Do not use qlb as a lower bound on every cube.

Theorem 2 (Appendix A, PDF pp. 19–20) proves that for every fixed ε>0 breadth-first qBnB terminates after finitely many steps at an ε-optimal point when f is continuous and the sampling/bound discrepancy is controlled by a modulus ψ(r)→0 as cube radius r→0. The induction preserves every global minimizer in the active generation; longest-edge subdivision makes radii shrink geometrically. qBnB(3)'s third-order bound supplies ψ(r)=3L₃r³, so this finite ε-termination follows without nondegeneracy. Nondegeneracy is used only for the separate eventual-exactness conclusion.

## Algorithm statements and proof conditions

- qBnB(2) (Theorem/Proposition 1, PDF pp. 6–8): assume an unconstrained minimizer over C₀ and a C¹ function with L₂-Lipschitz gradient (or Hessian upper spectral bound). For cube center x_C and radius r, sample x(C)=x_C and set qlb(C)=f(x_C)−L₂r²/2. If x*∈C is a global minimizer, g(x*)=0; Taylor’s upper inequality from x* to x_C gives qlb(C)≤f*. Its gap is L₂r²/2, hence second-order convergence. The source proves the standard Lipschitz-gradient lower bound is no larger than this qlb, for each cube. Constrained-qBnB(2), §4.2, uses a boundary-aware sample satisfying condition (11); cubes too large relative to the box receive qlb=−∞. Do not transfer the unconstrained formula to boundary minimizers.
- qBnB(3), Proposition 2 and Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 9–12): assume f∈C² on an open neighborhood U₀, the problem is unconstrained, and the Hessian is L₃-Lipschitz. For a radius-r cube centered at x₀ with the required 2r neighborhood inside U₀, first test λ_min(H(x₀))≥−L₃r. Define λ̄=max{0,5L₃r−λ_min(H(x₀))} and m=3L₃r, and r₀=r, r_{k+1}=3L₃ r_k²/(2m). Newton iterations on f̂(x)=f(x)+λ̄||x−x₀||²/2 are checked against the source’s step/distance bounds (16)–(17). Failure certifies that the cube cannot contain a minimizer; success supplies x(C)=x̂* and qlb(C)=f̂(x̂*)−λ̄r²/2. The theorem states qlb is valid and f(x(C))−qlb(C)≤3L₃r³. This gives the modulus required by Appendix Theorem 2 and hence finite termination for each fixed ε>0 without a nondegeneracy assumption. Eventual exactness is the separate claim requiring finitely many minimizers with positive-definite Hessian. Numerical Newton stopping introduces a separate ε_Newton correction (§5.1).
- The combined qBnB(2+3) uses third-order bounds only when the 2r-neighborhood is valid and 3L₃r³≤L₂r²/2; otherwise it uses qBnB(2) (§5.1, p.12).

## Evidence and transfer boundary

Sections 6 and Appendix B give experiments and a conditionally Lipschitz-differentiable application; they support practical comparison but do not strengthen the theorem hypotheses. The exact quadratic probe verifies one qBnB(2) bound and confirms it is tighter than its Lipschitz-gradient counterpart on that finite interval; it is not a proof of the general result. The source does not give a universal bound for constrained qBnB(3), nor an online/probabilistic interpretation.
