## Main certificates and hypotheses

The paper considers a finite LP minimizing (c^Tx) subject to (A_E x=b_E) and (A_Ix\ge b_I). For a feasible (x), if a multiplier λ exists with (A^Tλ=c) and λ_I\ge0), then

\[
c^Tx-\lambda^Tb=\lambda^T(Ax-b)=\lambda_I^T(A_Ix-b_I)\ge0.
\]

This is Proposition 3.1: it gives a lower bound valid for every feasible point. Proposition 3.2 adds complementary slackness (\lambda^T(Ax-b)=0) at a feasible candidate (x^*); then (x^*) is optimal and its objective is (\lambda^Tb). These are sufficient conditions and do not require the paper’s later full-rank/boundedness assumptions.

Theorem/Proposition 7.1 gives necessity as well. Assume the constraints are consistent, (\operatorname{rank}(A)=n), and (c^Tx) is bounded below on the feasible set. Then any feasible (x), not necessarily a vertex, is optimal iff there is an (m)-vector (\lambda) with (A^T\lambda=c), (\lambda^T(Ax-b)=0), and (\lambda_I\ge0); the optimal value is (\lambda^Tb). The proof first obtains an optimal vertex and multiplier from the perturbation/working-set results in §6, then uses Proposition 3.3 to show that the same multiplier is complementary at every optimal point.

Lemma 9.1 states the alternative for any (m\times n) matrix (A) and (c\in\mathbb R^n): exactly one of (i) (\exists y\ge0:A^Ty=c), or (ii) (\exists p:Ap\ge0,\ c^Tp<0). Exclusivity follows by multiplication. For existence, the authors embed the alternative into a bounded, consistent LP with rows (A,I,-I), then apply Proposition 7.1; this does not rely on a rank assumption for the original (A).

## Proof path and boundaries

The chain is: feasible descent directions (Lemma 2.5) → multiplier lower bounds and complementary-slackness sufficiency (Propositions 3.1–3.3) → nondegenerate vertex working-set multipliers (§§4–5) → a perturbation handling degenerate vertices (§6) → necessity at arbitrary optima (Proposition 7.1) and optimal working sets (§8) → Farkas alternative (Lemma 9.1). The argument is elementary finite-dimensional LP geometry and perturbation, not an infinite-dimensional separation result.

**Use:** a finite exact LP candidate can be certified by independently checking primal feasibility, (A^T\lambda=c), multiplier signs, and complementary slackness/objective equality. The necessity direction requires consistency, full row-system rank (n), and a finite lower bound. The lemma’s Farkas alternative itself has no rank hypothesis. This paper supplies no compactness, minimax, semi-infinite, distribution-uniform, or (n\to\infty) conclusion.
