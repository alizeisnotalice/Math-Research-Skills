# S021 — Blekherman–Riener: symmetric nonnegative forms and SOS

**Reader:** L13 · **coverage:** complete arXiv v4, physical pp. 1–27 · **review:** pending

Grigoriy Blekherman and Cordian Riener, “Symmetric nonnegative forms and sums of squares,” arXiv:1205.3102v4 (2020-02-17). PDF identity and version match the official arXiv record; the original PDF is 27 pages.

## Theorems and scope

For fixed degree 2d, the symmetric nonnegative cone P⁽ˢ⁾_{n,2d} and symmetric SOS cone Σ⁽ˢ⁾_{n,2d} lie in the fixed-dimensional space H⁽ˢ⁾_{n,2d} (dimension π(2d) once n≥2d). The paper studies two distinct identifications across n: symmetrization/direct-limit maps φ and the power-mean basis maps ρ. Limits depend on the chosen transition maps; never conflate them.

- Under symmetrization maps, Proposition 2.3 gives isomorphisms for n≥2d and Theorem 2.4 says both limiting cones are full-dimensional (PDF pp. 3–4).
- Under power-mean coordinates, Proposition 2.6 gives nesting along multiples, not every adjacent n; Theorem 2.8 proves full-dimensional limits and convergence of the sequences of cones (PDF pp. 4–5, proof §6 pp. 21–23).
- Half-degree principle, Proposition 3.1: a symmetric degree-2d form is nonnegative iff it is nonnegative at points with at most max(2,d) distinct coordinates. Corollary 3.3 reduces finite-n checks to the finite weight grid W_n; Theorem 3.4 characterizes the asymptotic nonnegative cone by Φ_f(s,t)≥0 on Δ×R^d. In the reverse implication, rational weights with positive coordinates are approximated and then realized at some multiple ah≥2d (PDF pp. 6–7).
- Representation-theoretic SOS reduction: Theorem 4.2/Corollary 4.4 express invariant SOS through PSD matrix polynomials; Theorem 4.10 gives the higher-Specht free basis; Theorem 4.15 gives a uniform fixed-degree decomposition indexed by λ⊢2d with weighted-homogeneous SOS matrix coefficients B_λ and symmetrized Gram matrices Q^λ_n (PDF pp. 7–15). Dual membership is equivalent to PSD invariant quadratic form (Lemma 4.17); extreme dual rays are characterized by the maximality/hyperplane condition on W<2> (Proposition 4.20, pp. 14–15).
- Quartics: Theorem 5.1 characterizes Σ⁽ˢ⁾_{n,4} by two PSD 2×2 blocks and a nonnegative scalar component. Theorem 5.3 classifies strictly positive boundary SOS quartics; Example 5.4 proposes a strict-containment witness for each n≥4. Theorem 5.5 states Σ⁽ˢ⁾_{n,2d}⊊P⁽ˢ⁾_{n,2d} except for binary forms, quadratic forms, and ternary quartics (PDF pp. 16–20).
- In the power-mean limit, Theorem 2.9 proves P₄=S₄ (proof uses the quartic boundary classification and discriminant argument, §§6–7, PDF pp. 21–26). The equality P₂d=S₂d for every d is only Conjecture 1, stated open on PDF pp. 4, 25–26.

## Formula audit of Example 5.4

The notation is fixed in §2.1: (p_i^{(n)}=n^{-1}sum_j X_j^i) and (p_lambda^{(n)}) is the product of these normalized power means over the parts of (lambda). Thus the five (p_lambda^{(n)}) in Example 5.4 are the stated basis and ℓ acts on them by the five displayed values; no raw-power-sum rescaling is implicit. On the rendered original arXiv v4 physical p.20, the printed data are
\[
(a,b,c,d)=(1,-13/10,1,-5/4),\qquad
f^{(n)}=a^2p_{(4)}+2abp_{(3,1)}+(c^2-a^2)p_{(2^2)}
 +(2cd+b^2-2ab)p_{(2,1^2)}+(d^2-b^2)p_{(1^4)},
\]
with the functional values, listed below in the polynomial's basis order,
\[
\ell(p_{(4)}^{(n)})=397/200,\quad
\ell(p_{(3,1)}^{(n)})=25/16,\quad
\ell(p_{(2^2)}^{(n)})=63/40,\quad
\ell(p_{(2,1^2)}^{(n)})=5/4,\quad
\ell(p_{(1^4)}^{(n)})=1.
\]
The source prints the values in a different order: (p_{(4)},p_{(2^2)},p_{(3,1)},p_{(2,1^2)},p_{(1^4)}). Exact substitution gives
\[
\ell(f^{(n)})=1\cdot\frac{397}{200}-\frac{13}{5}\cdot\frac{25}{16}+0\cdot\frac{63}{40}+\frac{179}{100}\cdot\frac54-\frac{51}{400}\cdot1=\frac{13}{400},
\]
while the sentence below the displayed functional says this is zero. The identity is therefore inconsistent as printed. The PSD tests themselves check out: the two (2\times2) block determinants are (1/80) and (31/6400), and the scalar block is positive for (n\ge4). They show dual feasibility of the printed ℓ, but with ℓ(f)>0 they do not show that this ℓ exposes (f^{(n)}) on the SOS boundary. No search for an alternative exposing functional or independent reconstruction of this family is claimed.

The DOI version of record (Discrete & Computational Geometry 65 (2021), 764–799, DOI 10.1007/s00454-020-00208-w) was checked in Springer’s full-text HTML against the arXiv v4 passage: it retains the same Example 5.4 constants and the same ℓ(f)=0 assertion. The author-hosted older draft has different numbering and a different example, so it is not used to overwrite the identified arXiv/publisher versions. No erratum was located in the checked arXiv and publisher records. Classify this narrowly as a version-confirmed, source-local printed arithmetic inconsistency affecting this example’s displayed boundary certificate. It does **not** by itself disprove Theorem 5.5, whose statement is also attributed in the paper to Goel–Kuhlmann–Reznick; that theorem is not independently reproved here. ## Transfer limits

This source supplies exact finite-dimensional symmetric SOS representations and carefully qualified n→∞ cone limits in specified coordinates. It does not establish minimax interchange, policy measurability, prophet inequalities, arrival-order conventions, or generic SOS exactness. Do not promote Conjecture 1 to theorem.
