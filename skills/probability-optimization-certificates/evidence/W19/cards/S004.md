## Model and results

The paper studies conic pairs in real linear spaces with convex cones (P,Q), a linear map (A), and the value function (h_c(b)). It distinguishes primal value, dual value and attainment rather than treating equality of values as a consequence of feasibility. Proposition 1 (PDF p. 5) records weak duality and the value-function characterization: (h_c(b)=\operatorname{val}(P_{c,b})\ge\operatorname{val}(P^*_{c,b})); finiteness and existence of optimal solutions are separate conditions.

Proposition 2 (PDF pp. 5–6) assumes (Y_0=\operatorname{span}(A(P)-Q)) with its induced topology and at least one of four regularity alternatives: a local value bound in a neighborhood of (b); a strict feasible point (Ax_0-b\in\operatorname{int}_{Y_0}Q); Fréchet spaces with closed (P,Q,Y_0) and (b\in\operatorname{icr}(A(P)-Q)); or finite-dimensional (Y_0) and the same intrinsic-core condition. It concludes either the value is −∞ throughout the intrinsic core, or a dual representation exists and the primal value function is continuous at (b); then subdifferentiability gives no gap and dual attainment. Corollary 3 (PDF p. 6) gives a convex-core-topology version under (b\in\operatorname{icr}(A(P)-Q)). These are sufficient conditions with the stated alternatives, not unconditional strong duality.

The Gale example in §3 (PDF pp. 8–10) makes the gap function explicit under a perturbation (b=(b_1,0)), (b_1>0). Lemma 7 characterizes dual-feasible perturbations through the sequence growth parameter (\beta=\liminf_k\beta_k/k). Proposition 8 yields (g(c,b)=b_1\max\{0,\beta_0-\beta\}) and distinguishes when the dual supremum is attained. The formula is specific to this example. Section 4 (PDF pp. 11–13) revisits the Van Slyke–Wets example and identifies failures in operator/space assumptions that block an unqualified appeal to the abstract duality results.

## Proof path and use limits

The results reduce conic values to convex value functions, use conjugacy/subdifferential facts under relative-interiority or local boundedness hypotheses, and then compute a concrete nonclosed-image example to expose gap and attainment behavior. The topology and the modeling space of constraints are part of the theorem input. A Slater-like point is sufficient in the stated setting, but failure of Slater alone does not imply a gap, and a finite-dimensional proof cannot be applied to probability-law spaces without checking the relevant topology and hypotheses.

**Use:** guard every conic-LP transfer by recording the cone, ambient space, topology, intrinsic core, and whether the claim is zero gap, attainment, or both. The paper does not establish any prophet inequality, online policy guarantee, or limiting interchange.
