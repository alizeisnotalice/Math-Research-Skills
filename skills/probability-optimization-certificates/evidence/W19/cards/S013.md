## Model, theorem scope, and proof chain

The paper writes the primal as
\[
(P)\quad \sup\{\langle c,x\rangle:b-Ax\in K\},\qquad
(D)\quad \inf\{\langle b,y\rangle:A^*y=c,\ y\in K^*\},
\]
with finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces and closed convex cone K. Weak duality always holds. Ordinary strong duality follows under primal Slater feasibility (a slack in ri K); the paper addresses failure of this qualification.

The **minimal face** F_min is the unique face of K whose relative interior contains the feasible slacks. Replacing K by F_min leaves the primal feasible set unchanged and makes the reduced system strictly feasible; the dual over F_min* then has strong duality. Section 3’s facial-reduction algorithm iteratively chooses a reducing certificate in L∩F_{i−1}*, where L=ker(A,b)*, and sets F_i=F_{i−1}∩y_i^⊥. The invariant is F_min⊆F_i; finite termination reaches F_min. The step bound is ℓ=min{ℓ_K−1, dim L}, with ℓ_K the maximum face-chain length (Theorem 1, PDF pp. 11–12).

Theorem 2 (PDF p. 13) represents F_min* as a projection of an extended nonlinear conic system. For **nice** K, defined by K*+F^⊥ closed for every face F of K, Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 (PDF pp. 14–16) give an extended conic representation and a strong extended dual for feasible (P). For K=Sⁿ_+, the tangent-space constraints become explicit PSD constraints, yielding an SDP extended dual; the length may be chosen ℓ=min{n, n(n+1)/2−m−1} under the stated independent-constraint assumptions (Corollary 1).

Sections 5–6 (PDF pp. 17–20) compare simplified and rescaled tangent systems and explain recovery of Ramana’s SDP dual. In the final section the author records an extended representation for F_min and asks whether analogous K*-only tangent formulations exist for other cones; these are open questions, not proved results.

## Proof interface and transfer limits

The construction is certificate-based and finite-dimensional. For SDP, the matrix tangent condition is checked by a block PSD constraint with auxiliary nonsymmetric W and scalar β; the PSD cone is both nice and self-dual. Facial reduction repairs the conic duality qualification, but does not certify that a numerical solver found exact reducing certificates. It does not give compactness or an interchange theorem for probability laws, policy spaces, or an n→∞ limit. See coverage for each section’s physical pages.
