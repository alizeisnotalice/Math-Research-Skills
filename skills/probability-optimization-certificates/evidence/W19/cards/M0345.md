## Exact result and assumptions

Theorem 3.4 (printed p. 174; PDF p. 5) states a minimax equality for compact convex spaces (M,N) and a real-valued function (f) that is quasi-concave in the first variable, quasi-convex in the second, and upper-semicontinuous in the first / lower-semicontinuous in the second. For an optimization application, spell out the ambient real linear topological spaces and induced topologies in which convexity, compactness, and semicontinuity are being asserted; the article's theorem statement itself does not name a specific normed or locally convex topology. The paper’s convention makes quasi-concavity and quasi-convexity mean convexity of the corresponding superlevel and sublevel sets. The result is

\[
\sup_{x\in M}\inf_{y\in N}f(x,y)=\inf_{y\in N}\sup_{x\in M}f(x,y).
\]

The proof uses finite open covers obtained from a strict hypothetical minimax gap, a minimal finite subcover, and two KKM lemmas. The finite reduction corollary (Corollary 3.3, printed p. 174; PDF p. 5) allows (M,N) convex spaces, with at least one compact, under the same quasi-concave-convex and separate semicontinuity conditions. Its proof reduces to a finite convex hull; §3.2 explicitly works in a linear space. Do not silently add a normed, Hausdorff, or locally convex assumption absent from the source.

Theorem 4.2 (printed p. 175; PDF p. 6) gives the Kneser–Fan variant: (M) compact, (N) arbitrary, (f) concave-convexlike and upper-semicontinuous in the first variable. The dual variant has (M) arbitrary, (N) compact, and lower-semicontinuity in the second variable. “Convexlike” here is the paper’s pairwise comparison property; it should not silently be replaced by ordinary concavity/convexity without checking the definition.

## Boundary and use in the probability tool

This theorem can justify exchanging an optimization over randomized strategies with an adversarial choice only after specifying the topologies, compactness, convexity/convexlikeness, and semicontinuity. It does not itself give compactness of an infinite-dimensional probability-law class, attainment of a threshold, or closure under tail constraints. Sion gives a counterexample in §3.6 when upper semicontinuity is removed. For online models, also prove that the randomization/strategy set is convex in the relevant representation and that the payoff has the required semicontinuity.

**Migration gap:** the theorem supplies a minimax exchange lemma; it does not prove the model-specific feasibility or limiting statements in A001/A002/A005.
