## Formal statements and certificate chain

The article fixes polyhedra P(A,b)={x:Ax≥b} and minimizes cᵀx. Its dual is maximize bᵀu subject to Aᵀu=c, u≥0. Weak duality is proved directly. Theorem 1 states the primal/dual strong-duality relation and attainment when both sides are feasible with finite optimal values (physical pp. 4–6); retain the paper’s infeasible/unbounded cases separately rather than infer equality from feasibility alone.

A basis I selects n linearly independent rows; x_I=A_I^{-1}b_I. Reduced costs u_I=A_I^{-T}c. If the basis is feasible and u_I≥0, the extended reduced-cost vector is dual feasible and has the same objective value, so weak duality certifies optimality (pp. 5–6). A negative reduced cost gives a decreasing direction; if that direction is feasible, it certifies unboundedness. Otherwise the ratio test selects a leaving constraint.

Degeneracy is handled by symbolic lexicographic perturbation of b (not a floating ε), with the lexicographic pivot rule. The perturbed basis objective strictly decreases, and a finite basis-height measure proves termination (pp. 7–12). Phase I augments the system with nonnegative slack variables; its optimum equals the known lower benchmark iff the original pointed LP is feasible, and the dual of Phase I yields an infeasibility certificate otherwise (pp. 13–15). The general case is reduced to the pointed case by x=v−w, v,w≥0 (p.15).

Corollary 2 / `infeasibleP` (pp. 4, 13) gives Farkas: P(A,b)=∅ iff there is d≥0 with Aᵀd=0 and bᵀd>0. The implementation proves correctness of Boolean feasibility/boundedness predicates by reflection in Coq. Section 7 derives strong duality, Farkas, separation from a finite convex hull, and Minkowski’s theorem for bounded polyhedra; for Minkowski, boundedness is the paper’s ℓ₁-coordinate test and the proof uses simplex optimal bases (pp. 16–17).

## Transfer boundary

This is a proof-assistant formalization over an ordered field, with a termination and correctness proof for the symbolic simplex implementation. It provides an exact certificate architecture for finite polyhedral feasibility/LP subproblems. It is not an executable interval-arithmetic validation of arbitrary floating solver output, not a conic SDP theorem, and not a proof of compactness, measurable policy existence, minimax exchange, atom/tie rules, or an n→∞ limit. The exact LP/Farkas examples in the batch are small arithmetic illustrations only.
