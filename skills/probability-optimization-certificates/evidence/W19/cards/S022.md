# S022 — Ahmadi–Majumdar: DSOS/SDSOS inner relaxations

**Reader:** L13 · **coverage:** complete arXiv v3, physical pp. 1–44 · **review:** pending

Amir Ali Ahmadi and Anirudha Majumdar, “DSOS and SDSOS Optimization: More Tractable Alternatives to Sum of Squares and Semidefinite Optimization,” arXiv:1706.02586v3 (2018-08-29). The local 44-page PDF matches the official metadata/version.

## Definitions and exact optimization representations

SOS constraints give SDP Gram-matrix tests. The paper defines DSOS and SDSOS as inner approximations:
\[
\mathrm{DSOS}_{n,2d}\subseteq \mathrm{SDSOS}_{n,2d}\subseteq \mathrm{SOS}_{n,2d}\subseteq \mathrm{PSD}_{n,2d}.
\]
A polynomial is DSOS iff it has a Gram representation p=zᵀQz with Q diagonally dominant (Theorem 3.4); equivalently it is a nonnegative sum of monomial squares and pairwise (m_i±m_j)^2 blocks (Definition 3.1). SDSOS iff it has such a Gram matrix Q that is scaled diagonally dominant (Theorem 3.6); equivalently Q has factor width at most 2 and decomposes into PSD 2×2 principal blocks (Theorem 3.7, Lemma 3.8). DD gives an LP and SDD gives an SOCP; for fixed degree d the program size is polynomial in n (Theorem 3.9, rendered physical p.14). These are sufficient certificates, generally conservative; no converse to SOS/nonnegativity is implied.

The r-hierarchy tests p(x)(Σ_i x_i²)^r for DSOS/SDSOS. Theorem 3.14: an even positive-definite form becomes r-DSOS for some r (via Pólya’s theorem). Proposition 3.15 gives positive-definite forms that are not r-SDSOS for any r when 0<a<1, so the evenness hypothesis is material. Theorem 3.16 gives a transformed 2n-variable r-DSOS certificate for general positive-definite forms; the displayed perturbation and degree-doubling must be retained (PDF pp. 15–17). The paper exhibits the Motzkin form in 2-DSOS and another non-SOS ternary sextic in 1-DSOS (Examples 3.12–3.13).

## Further methods and limits

Applications in §4 are computational comparisons and case studies, not universal approximation theorems. §5 describes iterative change of basis (objective-specific nested inner cones), column generation, and factor-width k>2. For FW₃ the local PSD blocks are 3×3 but O(n³) such constraints can be costly; a general SOCP representation for FW₃ is expressly unavailable (PDF pp. 18–34). Software appendix pp. 35–39 documents the SPOT/iSOS interface; references pp. 40–44 are included in this full read.

For probability/online arguments, a DSOS/SDSOS or SOS relaxation can bound only the finite polynomial subproblem under its modeling assumptions. Numerical performance tables do not prove the relaxation exact, nor do they justify probability-law compactness, threshold attainment, or a limit interchange.
