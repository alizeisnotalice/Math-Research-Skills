# S024 — Mohammadi–Peet: SOS/moment relaxations inside branch and bound

**Reader:** L13 · **coverage:** complete arXiv v2, physical pp. 1–8 · **review:** pending

Hesameddin Mohammadi and Matthew M. Peet, “Combining SOS and Moment Relaxations with Branch and Bound to Extract Solutions to Global Polynomial Optimization Problems,” arXiv:1609.01019v2 (2017-04-23), 8 pages. Identity and version match official arXiv metadata.

## Model and algorithm

The problem is polynomial optimization min f(x) subject to g_i(x)≥0 with nonempty compact feasible set S. The authors define a greatest-lower-bound (GLB) positivity problem and use SOS / dual moment hierarchies on each rectangle. Because the GLB uses strict positivity f−λ>0, read λ* as the supremal bound/value; do not infer a maximizing certificate is attained at a minimizer.

For a box [a,b], add interval polynomials w_j(x)=(b_j−x_j)(x_j−a_j)≥0 and form the degree-k bounded quadratic module M⁽ᵏ⁾_{ab}; B_k(a,b) returns the SOS GLB relaxation (eqs. (14)–(16), PDF p.5). The algorithm selects an active rectangle of minimum volume subject to its GLB being within mη/(l+1) of the current best lower bound, bisects a longest edge, computes two new B_k values, and returns the centroid after l iterations (Algorithm E_k, physical pp. 5–6).

Lemma 2 (PDF p.6) states monotonicity under nested boxes, M⁽ᵏ⁾_{γδ}⊆M⁽ᵏ⁾_{αβ} when γ≤α<β≤δ and k≥2. Its proof uses the Appendix Lemma 3 interval identity to transfer each outer-box interval generator into an SOS combination of the inner-box generator plus a square. For bounds λ(a,b), λ(c,d) the consequence is λ(a,b)≤λ(c,d); the source also records k≥degree(g_i)+2 for the subsequent comparison.

Theorem 1 (PDF pp.6–7): if S is nonempty compact and S⊆C(a,b), for 0<ε<1, η<ε, and l>n log₂(L√n/η) (L=max_i(b_i−a_i)), there exists a sufficiently large relaxation order k such that E_k returns x with a feasible y∈S satisfying f(y)−f*≤ε and ||y−x||<ε. The proof uses finite rectangle-grid cardinality, pointwise convergence of the SOS hierarchy on that finite family, nested-box monotonicity, and an induction showing one selected chain of l boxes. For fixed k, a residual error can remain; the paper’s case study visibly illustrates that limitation. Corollary 1 adds approximate constraint satisfaction by uniform continuity on the bounding box.

## Proof and evidence limits

The paper’s numerical example is approximate: its returned point has nonzero equality residuals, so it is not an exact optimizer certificate. The exact one-dimensional Appendix identity check is only an instance of the interval SOS identity, not proof of the general lemma. Exactness depends on the Archimedean/compact feasible-set hypotheses and sufficiently large k; no explicit usable bound on k is supplied. SOS/moment convergence does not by itself replace the outer BnB proof.
