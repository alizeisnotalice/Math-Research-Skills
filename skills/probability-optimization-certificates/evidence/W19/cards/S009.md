## General optimality framework

The generalized moment problem is univariate on a specified Borel support set (\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R), with a probability law constrained by moment data and a dual function required to dominate the target payoff on all of (\Omega). Theorem 1 (PDF p. 9) is conditional on strong duality for the particular primal/dual pair. A candidate distribution and dual vector are optimal iff (i) the dual function is globally feasible on (\Omega), (ii) the primal moment conditions hold, (iii) complementary slackness holds on the candidate support, and (iv) the tangent condition holds at the support points that are differentiable interior points. Sufficiency is weak duality plus equality of the two objectives; necessity uses strong duality and the supporting/tangency argument. The global dual inequality is essential: checking only equality at support points is insufficient.

## Three structured moment problems

Section 3’s Theorem 2 (PDF p. 14) is posed over nonnegative laws, (F\in\mathcal M(\mathbb R_+)) with support (\mathbb R_+=[0,\infty)); its dual inequality is required for every (x\in\mathbb R_+). It treats the first-moment normalization (M_1=1), (t)-th moment (M_t>1), (t>1), and maximizes (\mathbb E[(X-q)_+]). Its first branch is exactly (0<q\le (t-1)M_t^{1/(t-1)}/t); the second branch applies for (q>(t-1)M_t^{1/(t-1)}/t). Thus the theorem covers q>0 and not q=0. Above the threshold it gives a semi-closed expression parameterized by a root of equation (1). Proposition 1 and Algorithm 1 supply a bisection procedure for a root on the specified interval; the procedure solves this stated moment problem and is not a general black box for all moment constraints.

Section 4’s Theorem 3 (PDF p. 24) is a normalized problem on laws supported on `R_+`: set the threshold to `q=1`, impose the stated first- and second-moment normalization (including `M_2=γ M_1²`, `γ>1`) and the source’s feasibility conditions, then minimize the centered upper partial second-moment objective `E[(X−1)_+²]−M_+²`, where `M_+=E[(X−1)_+]`. The subtracted square and normalization are part of the theorem’s value; it is not simply the raw upper partial second moment. Lemmas 7–11 derive the possible optimal support structure and formulas.

## Proof map and limitations

The framework constructs primal support points and a globally feasible dual majorant, then uses moment feasibility, complementary slackness and tangency to certify equality. Electronic-companion Sections EC.1–EC.3 (PDF pp. 43–56) give the omitted proofs for the three structured applications. Section 6’s numerical experiments illustrate the formulas only. The finite-support principle cited in the introduction is prior work, not proved as a universal theorem here; do not infer a support-size bound without the cited theorem’s hypotheses. The paper flags extension to nonconvex/discrete moment problems and multivariate moments as beyond its present results.

**Use:** formulate the exact support domain and moment constraints, prove primal/dual strong duality, check the dual inequality globally, and use the tangency conditions only at eligible differentiable interior support points. A root finder is a computational step after existence/bracketing is proved; numerical convergence alone does not certify a universal moment bound.
