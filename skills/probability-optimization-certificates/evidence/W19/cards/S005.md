## Projection and duality claims

The object is a semi-infinite LP: finitely many primal variables and infinitely many indexed linear constraints. Section 2 develops a Fourier–Motzkin/projection elimination procedure, distinguishing clean and dirty variables; this yields finite-support multiplier information only under the specific projection structure. Section 3 separates feasibility from the value formula. Theorem 3.1 is the primal-feasibility characterization, not the value identity. Lemma 3.5 (PDF p. 15) gives (v(\mathrm{SILP})=\max\{S,L\}), with (S=\sup_{h\in I_3}\widetilde b(h)) the supremum of the projected finite-stage boundary values and (L=\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\omega(\delta)), where \omega(\delta)=\sup_{h\in I_4}\{\widetilde b(h)-\delta\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}|\widetilde a_k(h)|\} is nonincreasing in \delta. The limit is the asymptotic boundary term in Lemma 3.5, not a small-\delta limit. Theorem 3.18 characterizes zero duality gap for the stated primal/SILP and finite-support dual pair through primal feasibility and (S\ge L). Theorem 3.19’s strong-duality statement uses the source’s strict boundary condition `S>L` and attainment of the supremum defining `S`, in addition to primal feasibility; in this source, strong duality includes attainment of both the primal and dual optima. Do not infer either attainment from the value identity or from feasibility alone.

Section 4 studies feasible sequences: Lemma 4.2 approximates finite (L) by a sequence of finite subproblems, and Theorem 4.3 shows when its limit is a finite primal optimum. Such a sequence argument does not itself imply that the dual optimizer has finite support. Section 5 transfers the projection method to convex programs, including a Slater sufficient condition (Theorem 5.2) and an example with zero gap despite failure of Slater (Example 5.4). Section 6 derives generalized Farkas alternatives using the same elimination framework.

## Proof chain, scope, and use

The core proof maps an infinite family of inequalities to successive one-variable projections; the clean/dirty classification tracks whether elimination preserves finite certificates or leaves a limiting obstruction. This exposes the boundary term (L), which is why replacing a strict inequality or an attainment hypothesis by mere feasibility is unsafe. The electronic companion supplies technical details for the conic and convex extensions and sufficient finite-approximability conditions.

**Use:** before using finite LP dual variables for an online allocation or probability constraint, identify the exact index set, variable space, projection boundary term, and whether the desired conclusion is zero gap, strong duality, or attainment. A finite truncation alone is not a proof that the infinite constraint family is certified. This paper does not establish a prophet benchmark or an online adaptivity ratio.
