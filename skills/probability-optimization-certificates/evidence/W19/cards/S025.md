## Certificate structure

For rational LP data, an exact linear combination of constraints with admissible multiplier signs derives a target inequality. If (Ax\ge b), (A'x\le b'), and (A''x=b''), multipliers (d\ge0,d'\le0,d''\) free yield

\[
(d^TA+d'^TA'+d''^TA'')x\ge d^Tb+d'^Tb'+d''^Tb''.
\]

A feasible dual certificate plus a matching primal bound proves LP optimality. Rational data admit rational multipliers for the finite LP certificate; the checking arithmetic must preserve exact signs and equality.

For pure integer variables and integer coefficients in the derived left side, rounding its rational right side upward yields a Chvátal–Gomory cut. This step depends on integrality of the expression on every feasible integer point; it is invalid for arbitrary continuous variables.

For branch-and-cut, the proof is represented as a derivation sequence with assumptions encoding branch cases. A split such as \(a^Tx\le\delta\) or \(a^Tx\ge\delta+1\) covers integer-feasible points only when (a^Tx\) is integral on that lattice. Child proofs can be combined by unsplitting their assumptions. A complete proof ends in contradiction for infeasibility or a certified objective bound for optimality.

## Boundary and use

This gives a design pattern for small, independently checkable finite certificates; it is not a proof that a solver implementation is correct, nor that a truncated search tree covers all cases unless every split and branch is validated. Floating-point solver output is only a candidate until a certificate is checked using exact or rigorous arithmetic. No finite certificate proves a statement uniform in (n) or over a continuum of distributions.
