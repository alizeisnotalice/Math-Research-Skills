# Stephen James Tate (2013), “Virial Expansion Bounds”

- Source: *Journal of Statistical Physics* 153(2), 325–338 (2013), DOI [10.1007/s10955-013-0831-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-013-0831-8); arXiv:1303.6444. Local PDF: `evidence/fulltexts/C08.pdf` (14 pages; SHA-256 `4f12ace2197f70ca9b6e2f8c6ee90d4c5d193c73e911153a62563f499d606379`).
- Reader: W15; full PDF pp. 1–14.
- Scope: classical-gas virial-series convergence bounds derived from cluster-coefficient bounds.

## Result and hypotheses

The model has a central pair potential Φ on ℝᵈ, stability constant B satisfying `U_N≥−BN`, and (where the Lebowitz–Penrose bound is used) finite temperedness quantity `C(β)=∫_{ℝᵈ}|e^(−βΦ(0,x))−1|dx`. An alternative hard-core/tail quantity `R(β)` is defined in equation (2.9). Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 3) converts a coefficient bound of the form
\[
|n b_n|\le a\,\frac{n^{n-1}}{n!}\,b^n
\]
into explicit bounds on virial coefficients and a positive lower bound for the radius of convergence `R_Vir`, expressed through the Lambert W function. Corollary 2.2 (PDF pp. 3–4) inserts the Lebowitz–Penrose/Ruelle cluster bound, yielding an improved virial-radius bound on the `C(β)^(−1)e^(−4βB)` scale. Corollary 2.3 uses the alternative `R(β)` cluster bound. Proposition 2.4 (PDF p. 4, equation (2.21)) identifies when the alternative is stronger: `1.6R(β)<C(β)`.

The general derivation uses Lagrange inversion and coefficient bounds (Section 4, PDF pp. 5–6); the two model-specific bounds and comparisons are derived in Sections 5–7. Thus the theorem controls a particular virial expansion after obtaining a cluster-coefficient majorant.

## Transfer limit

This is not a general theorem about any entropy or capacity series. It gives no deletion-channel polymer representation, no uniform bound over input laws, and no license to interchange the capacity supremum with a series.
