## B051: small-deletion capacity

### Direct theorem and normalization

For the binary i.i.d. deletion channel with deletion probability d, normalize information by input bits. Kanoria–Montanari Theorem 1.1 (full-paper card I03, PDF pp. 1–2) proves for each fixed ε>0, for sufficiently small d depending on ε,

`C(d)=1+d log₂(d)−A₁d+A₂d²+O(d^(3−ε))`.

The stationary-ergodic variational characterization is Lemma 2.2 (PDF p. 5); the i.i.d.-run achievability construction is Lemma 4.1 (PDF p. 8). Section 6 (PDF pp. 26–27) asks whether higher-order terms exist and whether the resulting series converges. The theorem proves neither an A₃ coefficient nor all-orders expansion nor a convergence radius. Keep the logarithm base, fixed-ε quantifier, and ε-dependent range/error constants explicit.

The 50-digit Decimal calculation in `evidence/W16/validate_constants.py` (earlier archive) checks finite cutoffs for A₁ and A₂. It is arithmetic evidence only; it is not a rigorous infinite-series tail bound or evidence for capacity-series convergence.

### Achievable bounds and historical context

Drinea–Mitzenmacher Theorems 2–3 (I05, PDF pp. 6–7) provide constructive lower bounds from typical-output type/block counts and a jigsaw decoding argument. Khodaiemehr–Feng–Duman (I14, arXiv v2 read in full) gives a learned-law evaluation of that functional for geometric or finite-support run laws and an augmented VTR bound for every finite-support law. Its nonconvex search proposes candidate distributions without a global-optimality claim; Proposition 4 certifies finite interval evaluations at specified laws, not the search. Mitzenmacher’s I06 survey is historical through 2009 and must not be cited for current-best-bound or present-day open-status claims.

Cheraghchi I10 (full arXiv v4) supplies a mean-limited-channel relative-entropy dual bound (Theorem 1), a D-repeat reduction (Theorem 4/Corollary 5), Poisson-repeat bounds, and inverse-binomial deletion bounds including Theorems 35 and 38. These are analytical upper bounds in that source model; they prove no higher-order coefficient or convergent series for B051. Other bounded comparators: I07 uses four deletion-process genie disclosures and the finite fixed-block auxiliary channel in §II (input length `L`, output length `R`, `D=L−R` uniformly selected deletion positions); I08 gives a run-length/genie converse and a separate (`d\to1`) asymptotic; I09 is the seven-page TR-07-04 report, which states Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 but explicitly defers their detailed proofs to the full version; its present report text includes a collision-argument sketch. I11 combines finite auxiliary deletion DMC bounds and a heuristic run-law score; the reported lower bound is valid because a selected law is substituted into the rigorous Drinea–Mitzenmacher construction, not because the heuristic equals the true code rate. I12 treats both deletions and substitutions in the small-probability regime. I13 proves separate small-`α` expansions for two insertion models, but neither insertion model proves a deletion-only expansion. None establishes a higher-order deletion-only series. I05 Eq. (16) contains the binomial-coefficient **difference** inside the logarithm and the three nested sums `Σ_k Σ_(i,z,r,s) Σ_(t∈F(i,z,r,s))`. I05 Proposition 1 is a typical-output concentration result for block counts `B_k` and typed counts `B_{t,k}`; it is not an entropy-tail correction. The retained-mass correction `(1−ε)log(1−ε)` appears in I14 Proposition 1 for the I14 truncation of the DM functional; keep those propositions and functionals separate.

These achievability papers do not prove exact capacity or an all-orders small-d expansion. A lower-bound optimization is not a converse and a candidate-law certificate is not an optimizer certificate.

### Entropy-rate and Gibbs variational tools

Gray I15 is a 324-page book read as one complete selected chapter plus partial sections: Chapter 2 §§2.1–2.7 in full (PDF pp. 39–68 / printed pp. 17–46), and Chapter 9 introduction/§§9.2–9.3 only (PDF pp. 190–194). Chapter 2 proves finite-alphabet stationary entropy-rate existence, upper semicontinuity/affinity, and ergodic decomposition under its integrability assumption; it also states relative-entropy-rate limits with Markov-reference and absolute-continuity hypotheses. The partial Chapter 9 pages discuss shift-compatible channels. These facts do not justify interchanging a source supremum with a small-d limit or series.

Ruelle C04 studies a lattice Gibbs model with at most one particle per site and absolutely summable translation-invariant interactions. Its Theorem 3 variational pressure identity and Theorem 4 generic-set uniqueness are not deletion-channel capacity statements. Do not infer equivalence from the shared phrase “entropy variational principle.”


Shannon I01 is a complete 55-page combined corrected reprint of the July and October 1948 installments. Theorem 3 (PDF pp. 13–14) is the source's entropy-per-symbol typical-sequence result for the finite-state ergodic Markov source it defines; Theorem 11 (pp. 22–24) is an asymptotic discrete-channel coding/equivocation theorem. Theorem 17 (physical PDF p. 43) imposes an average transmitter-power limit `P`; Theorem 20 (pp. 46–47) instead uses peak allowed transmitter power `S`, defined on p. 46, and gives bounds/asymptotics under that peak constraint. Keep these power constraints distinct. Appendix 7 (pp. 54–55) defines information rate by measurable partitions and proves data processing for received-signal postprocessing. These are foundational definitions and results, not a deletion-channel small-d expansion or a license to exchange the B051 input-law supremum with a limit. Differential entropy itself is coordinate-dependent; information differences are invariant under common one-to-one changes of variables.

## A007: large switch-over times with renewal input

Boon–Boxma–Winands R01 proves the stated Poisson-arrival benchmark and labels the general-renewal extension Conjecture 4.2/Open Problem 2. The source is a problem statement, not a proof of the renewal extension, and an old statement alone does not certify current open status. Van der Mei R04 proves a uniform large-setup limit under Poisson arrivals, deterministic setups, and fixed gated/exhaustive service; its random-setup counterexample shows the theorem’s key relation can fail. Winands R06 treats general branching-type policies: deterministic setups have their own large-setup theorem, while stochastic setups are covered only in the ordered limit `ρ↑1` followed by mean setup growth. Neither source resolves the general-renewal, fixed-load A007 limit.

Resing R07 is a canonical-use comparator from the complete 18-page CWI Technical Report BS-R9128 (1991); the later 1993 journal version is not the artifact read. Under Poisson arrivals, general service times, and per-customer iid replacement Property 1, Theorem 3 (PDF p. 9) gives an embedded multitype branching process with immigration in every state when switchovers are present; Theorem 4 (pp. 11–12) has state-zero immigration when switchovers vanish. Exhaustive/gated and the stated binomial variants satisfy the property; 1-limited/Bernoulli service generally do not. Theorem 1 stationary PGF also needs a primitive subcritical mean matrix, irreducibility/aperiodicity, and the stated immigration log moment. This says nothing about general-renewal arrivals, large-setup scaling, arbitrary-time queues, or waiting-time laws.

R13 is a separately accepted canonical-use auxiliary only: W14-004 is a two-queue adaptive Poisson polling example whose stability criterion includes a service-time Laplace-transform value. It is not a large-setup or general-renewal asymptotic and gives no A007 waiting-time limit. R08 accepted the bounded W16 use after v1; the exact copied decision row is in `evidence/W16/source_approvals/R13-R08-usage-decision-candidate.json`. Neither source is a new full-text read.

For regenerative rewards, Sigman R02 Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 2) requires positive recurrence and `E[∫₀^{X₁}|f(X(s))|ds]<∞`; the limit is `E[R₁]/E[X₁]`. Sigman R03 Theorem 2.1 (PDF p. 11) requires i.i.d. cycle pairs and `E|R|<∞`; its expectation limit proof uses uniform integrability (PDF pp. 11–12). The renewal excess cdf is `F_e(x)=λ∫₀ˣP(X>u)du` (PDF p. 3). Polling times are not automatically regeneration times. For nonexponential renewal input, preserve age/residual life and prove stability/tightness and the stationary-to-fluid limit exchange before claiming a waiting-time limit.

## Use cluster and virial criteria within their model

- Scott–Sokal C05 Theorem 2.10 (PDF pp. 17–18) equates finite repulsive-lattice-gas zero-freeness/log-series criteria; Theorem 4.1 (PDF p. 50) gives the sharp finite-event LLL bound under its closed-neighborhood conditional-event hypothesis. The soft-core extension requires its additional weak-dependence condition. Neither result supplies a channel event model.
- Bovier–Zahradník C12 Theorem 1 (PDF p. 3) gives a sufficient hard-core polymer criterion with the explicit `δ`, `L(δ)`, and activity-weight hypotheses; the proof is by induction on a finite polymer set (PDF pp. 5–6).
- Poghosyan–Ueltschi C15 Theorem 2.1 (PDF physical p. 4 / printed article p. 3) assumes stability `∏_{i<j}|1+ζ(x_i,x_j)|≤∏_i e^{b(x_i)}` for every `n` and almost every tuple, plus either Assumption 2, `∫|ζ(x,y)|e^{a(y)+2b(y)}|μ|(dy)≤a(x)`, or the distinct Assumption 2′ using the paper's capped complex potential `\bar u` and weight `e^{a(y)+b(y)}`; both are pointwise `|μ|`-a.e. in `x`. The additional global condition is `∫e^{a(y)+2b(y)}|μ|(dy)<∞`. Its rooted estimate is `(e^{a(x₁)}−1)e^{2b(x₁)}`, with `e^{b(x₁)}` replacing `e^{2b(x₁)}` under the 2′ route. Theorem 2.2–2.3 are on PDF physical p. 5 / printed article p. 4; read the exact route-specific hypotheses in the card. Proposition 6.1 and the tree estimates are model-specific. C14 is an unverified citation candidate (its claimed DOI/title-author identity was not validated), and C15 does not validate or replace it; neither repairs unread C01.
- Ueltschi C02 Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2) gives a measure-space cluster criterion with pointwise weighted integrability and the separate global exponential-integrability condition. Theorem 3 adds weighted correlation estimates. Use every hypothesis from the card.
- Fernández–Procacci C03 Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 5–6) is a sufficient hard-core abstract-polymer criterion, quantified over every polymer; its closed-neighborhood partition polynomial includes only mutually compatible neighbors. Theorem 4’s monotone iteration requires a supersolution `Tρ(μ)≤μ`.
- Jansen–Kolesnikov C06 Theorem 2.1 is necessary and sufficient only in its nonnegative pair-potential correlation/activity setting; for stable potentials with negative parts the stated direction is sufficient only. Its improved polymer/subset-polymer criteria remain model-specific.
- Lebowitz–Penrose C10 (1964), on physical PDF p. 3 / printed p. 843, obtains the finite-volume virial-radius lower bound in Eq. (3.11), `0.28952/((u+1)B)`, from the Penrose cluster-coefficient majorant for a stable continuum gas with finite Mayer integral `B`. Keep the nearby coefficient bounds separate: Eq. (3.13) is for finite-volume virial coefficients `k|β_k(V)|`, while Eq. (3.14) is for infinite-volume irreducible Mayer cluster integrals `k|β_k|`; Eq. (3.15) gives the infinite-volume radius consequence. The paper also gives a hard-core maximum-density upper bound and a Vitali-limit result under a bounded simply connected analytic-continuation domain (PDF §§4–6). This remains a classical-gas statement with those model and limit hypotheses; it is only a structural comparator for B051. Tate C08 bounds a classical-gas virial expansion after cluster-coefficient bounds and stability/temperedness assumptions. Procacci–Yuhjtman C07 uses a Penrose identity and minimum-spanning-tree partition to improve Mayer/virial radii for stable tempered pair potentials. Neither yields a generic entropy-series theorem or a deletion-channel representation.
- Bissacot–Fernández–Procacci–Scoppola C13 Theorem 5 is an improved finite-event LLL criterion under a dependency graph and a specified cluster-expansion polynomial. Its condition uses the closed-neighborhood polynomial `φ*_x(μ)` in `p_x≤μ_x/φ*_x(μ)`, while the exact probability exponent is the open-neighborhood polynomial `\tilde φ*_x(μ)`, with `φ*_x=μ_x+\tilde φ*_x`; do not conflate them. The exact event family and dependency/lopsidependency structure must be provided; the theorem does not apply to a channel-capacity series by name alone.
- The original C01 paper has no verified full text in this batch. C06 and C02 discuss related criteria, but do not mark C01 as read or rely on its old withdrawn card. The read C12 and C15 papers are related methods, not substitutes for claiming the original C01 theorem.
