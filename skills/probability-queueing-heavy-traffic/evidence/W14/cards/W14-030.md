## Model and functional limit

The paper studies a queue with \(r\) independent renewal arrival channels and \(s\) independent potential-service renewal channels. The primitives in these \(r+s\) streams are mutually independent iid nonnegative random variables with finite variance. Customers join a common FCFS line. Its **modified** comparison model keeps potential servers running while idle and assigns each customer to the server that can finish service first; this makes the queue length an exact reflection of the aggregate net input.

Let \(p\) be the traffic intensity (aggregate arrival rate divided by aggregate potential service rate). Under the paper's square-root functional scaling, Theorem 2.1 (physical PDF p. 7; printed p. 156) states that at \(p=1\) the modified queue process converges weakly to the reflection of a standard Brownian motion at zero, with the reflected process having the one-dimensional Bessel-process law. Theorem 2.2 (statement: physical PDF p. 7, printed p. 156; proof continues on physical p. 8, printed p. 157) treats \(\rho>1\) and states \(Q_n^{\prime}\Rightarrow\xi\), the unreflected net-input limit. The theorem statement is on p. 7; p. 8 continues its proof and gives Corollary 2.2. These are process-level weak limits, not stationary-distribution assertions.

Theorem 3.1 (§3, physical pp. 8–13) couples the modified system to the standard FCFS first-idle-server model using the same renewal streams and shows that their scaled queue processes have the same heavy-traffic limit. Sections 4–9 carry the weak-limit method to departures, service-channel queue counts, loads and virtual waiting time (under identical-service assumptions), embedded arrival/departure sequences, inverse counting times, and busy-period functionals.

## Proof route and limits

1. Apply the renewal-process functional central limit theorem to each independent channel and sum the limits to obtain the net-input Brownian motion.
2. In the modified system, express queue length through the continuous one-dimensional reflection map; use the continuous mapping theorem.
3. For the standard system, bound the discrepancy from the modified system under a shared-data construction and transfer the same weak limit.
4. Obtain secondary process limits by continuous mappings, embedded-sequence arguments, or inverse processes.

The result does **not** show stationary convergence, total-variation convergence, finite coupling, or a stationary approximation under arbitrary stationary ergodic input. Part II (W14-031) expands the weak-input setting; neither paper settles A011's total-variation question.
