## SRBM and the BAR

The paper studies a semimartingale reflected Brownian motion in the nonnegative orthant, with drift, covariance matrix and reflection matrix $R$. Under the completely-$\mathcal S$-matrix condition, Proposition 1 (physical PDF p. 4) gives existence and uniqueness in law of the SRBM and its Feller/strong Markov properties. If a stationary distribution exists, Proposition 2 (p. 5) gives its uniqueness, absolute continuity, ergodicity and invertibility of $R$.

Proposition 3 (physical PDF p. 6; printed p. 70) derives the basic adjoint relationship from stationarity. Writing $p_0$ for the interior density and $p_i$ for the face densities, it has the form

$$
\int_S (\mathcal L f)(x)p_0(x)\,dx+\frac12\sum_i\int_{F_i}(D_i f)(x)p_i(x)\,d\sigma_i(x)=0,
\qquad f\in C_b^2(S),
$$

where $\mathcal L f=\tfrac12\sum_{j,k}\Gamma_{jk}\partial_{jk}f+\mu\cdot\nabla f$ and $D_i f=v_i\cdot\nabla f$ is the derivative in the reflection direction. Proposition 4 (physical PDF pp. 6–7) is the converse in a probability-admissible density class: if $p_0$ is a probability density and the integrable face densities $p_i$ are nonnegative and jointly satisfy the BAR, they define the stationary density and boundary measures.

## Numerical method and the exact gap

Section 4 changes variables to a reference density $q$, setting $r=p/q$, and represents the BAR as orthogonality to a closed subspace $H\subset L^2(q\,dA)$, where the source measure is $dA=(dx;\frac12d\sigma_1,\ldots,\frac12d\sigma_d)$. Proposition 5 (physical PDF p. 9) characterizes the stationary density when $r\in L^2$: it is orthogonal to $H$ and has normalization one; conversely, a nonnegative normalized $L^2$ solution orthogonal to $H$ gives the stationary density. Proposition 6's projection formula for the candidate density is conditional on Conjecture 1. That conjecture (physical PDF p. 7) asserts nonnegativity of normalized integrable BAR solutions; it is not proved in this paper. Proposition 7 gives convergence of the finite-dimensional approximations under the stated $L^2$ and conjecture assumptions; when $r\notin L^2$, weak convergence is only conjectured.

Section 5 first defines a *standard* SRBM (Definition 5, physical PDF p. 11 / printed p. 75) by $\operatorname{diag}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{diag}(R)=I$, invertibility of $R$, and $\max_i |(R^{-1}\mu)_i|=1$. Proposition 8 gives a scaling reduction for an SRBM with a stationary distribution and finite steady-state mean. In this standard normalization, Proposition 9 (physical PDF p. 12 / printed p. 76) says a product-form stationary distribution exists iff

$$
\gamma\equiv -R^{-1}\mu>0,
\qquad 2\Gamma_{jk}=R_{kj}+R_{jk}\quad(j\ne k).
$$

In that case $p_0(z)=C\exp(-2\gamma\cdot z)$ and each face density $p_i$ is the restriction of this exponential to $F_i$. Equation (27) has no $\gamma_j$ or $\gamma_k$ multipliers. This is a criterion for the source's standard SRBM data; do not apply its displayed form directly to arbitrary unnormalized covariance/reflection matrices. Any use after Proposition 8 must first verify that scaling reduction's hypotheses and transformation. Proposition 9 cites Harrison–Williams, Theorem 9.2, and says their additional Minkowski-matrix assumption is not used in its proof. Sections 6–7 report numerical comparisons and a queueing-network application. These results supply a BAR and a conditional $L^2$ numerical route; they do not establish uniqueness in an unrestricted analytic class or in a queueing probability class absent the displayed positivity/integrability assumptions. Preserve the distinction between the paper's Conjecture 1 and Proposition 4's proved nonnegative-density converse.
