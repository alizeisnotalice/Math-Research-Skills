## Generalized switch and heavy-traffic assumptions

The discrete-time generalized switch has a finite state space of channel/service states and a finite set of feasible service vectors in each state. Arrivals are bounded and IID over time; components of the same arrival vector may be correlated. MaxWeight is used, and the arrival rate approaches a boundary point of the capacity region as lambda(epsilon)=(1-epsilon)nu. Unlike a complete-resource-pooling model, several active capacity faces can leave a multidimensional cone/subspace for state-space collapse (SSC).

## Theorem 1: asymptotically tight mean combinations

Let P index the active faces at nu, let C contain an independent set of their normal vectors, and let H=C(C^T C)^(-1)C^T be the projection onto their span. For any weight vector w in the intersection of the active faces, Theorem 1, PDF p. 9, gives the one-sided upper bound

    E<w,q(epsilon)> <=
      [1^T(H ◦ Sigma_a(epsilon))1
       + 1^T((C^T C)^(-1) ◦ Sigma_B)1]/(2 epsilon)
      + K(epsilon),

where epsilon K(epsilon) tends to zero. The theorem further states that if the arrival covariance converges componentwise to Sigma_a, then epsilon E<w,q(epsilon)> converges to half the bracket with Sigma_a in place of Sigma_a(epsilon). The Hadamard products and the two covariance terms retain the arrival correlation and the random-channel/service contribution; they must not be collapsed to the independent-arrival formula.

The proof is based on a steady-state drift identity after first proving SSC. Proposition 1 supplies a policy-independent single-server lower bound for weights z in the cone K with a nonnegative representation in active-face normals. Proposition 2 establishes heavy-traffic SSC to K, and hence to H. In the Theorem 1 proof (§6.1), the quadratic drift of the H-projected queue is decomposed; MaxWeight's active-face service estimate makes the service deficit O(epsilon), SSC controls the residual terms, and a least-squares step yields the closed-form covariance expression. The term for unused service is bounded using the face geometry and SSC rather than discarded.

## What polynomial drifts do and do not determine

For the 2x2 input-queued switch, §5.1 derives an underdetermined linear system for the scaled individual queue means: six equations in eleven unknown boundary terms. Theorem 2 and §5.2 turn these constraints into linear-programming bounds; numerical examples compare the bounds with simulation. Thus low-degree polynomial drift identities and mean equations do not determine each queue mean, much less a full joint stationary law or transform uniqueness.

Sections 4.1–4.2 apply Theorem 1 to a correlated-arrival input-queued switch, parallel-server systems, an N-system, and an ad hoc wireless network. In examples with full-dimensional SSC, the covariance formula simplifies; that special geometry is not an assumption-free extension to arbitrary non-CRP networks. The appendices provide the lower-bound coupling, the SSC proof, generalizations of Theorem 2, and detailed proofs of Theorems 1 and 2.

## Transfer to B054

This source supports asymptotic mean combinations under multidimensional SSC and shows why polynomial drift equations can leave individual means underdetermined. It does not identify a joint weak limit, prove uniqueness of a BAR pair, or establish B054's probability-admissible transform/boundary-measure uniqueness. Those require separate tightness, admissibility, and uniqueness arguments.
