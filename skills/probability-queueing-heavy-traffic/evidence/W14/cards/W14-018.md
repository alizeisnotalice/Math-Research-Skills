# W14-018 — Eschenfeldt–Gamarnik, supermarket model in heavy traffic

**Reading:** Complete paper, PDF pp. 1–39. Local PDF and extracted text: data/ordos_primary_138.pdf and .txt. Formula-heavy PDF pp. 9–10 were rendered and visually checked. See reading_coverage.json#W14-018 for section-by-section coverage.

## Model and scaling

There are n parallel rate-one exponential servers; Poisson arrivals occur at rate n lambda_n, and each arrival samples d>=2 queues independently with replacement, joining a shortest sampled queue. Let S_i^n(t) be the fraction of queues with at least i customers and define T_i^n(t)=eta_n(1-S_i^n(t)). The traffic satisfies lambda_n=1-beta/eta_n for fixed beta>0 and eta_n tends to infinity. For the process limit, the paper also assumes eta_n=O(sqrt(n)) and imposes a weighted-norm tightness condition and a coordinate bound on the initial state (Theorem 2, PDF p. 9).

## Process convergence and ODE characterization

Theorem 2, PDF p. 9, proves that T^n converges on each finite time interval to the unique solution of

    T_0(t)=0,
    T_i(t)=T_i(0) - d integral_0^t (T_i(s)-T_(i-1)(s)) ds
                     + integral_0^t (T_(i+1)(s)-T_i(s)) ds,  i>=1.

The full theorem requires: convergence in the weighted sequence space of T^n(0), uniformly bounded initial weighted moments, and T_i^n(0)<=eta_n^alpha through i<=i*_alpha for some alpha<1/2, in addition to the rate and eta_n scaling above. It is a finite-horizon process limit to a deterministic infinite ODE system, not a reflected Brownian limit.

The proof first writes the finite-n process through Poisson random time changes (Section 2), rescales it, and proves existence, uniqueness, and continuity for the stopped integral equation in Section 4 (Theorem 1). Sections 5–6 represent the approximation error by martingales and show those martingales vanish in the weighted norm; the stopping event has probability tending to zero under the stated initial bounds. The ODE comparison and Gronwall estimates then yield Theorem 2.

## Equilibrium and steady-state result

For the limiting ODE, Theorem 3, PDF p. 10, gives fixed points pi_i=pi_1(d^i-1)/(d-1), unique up to pi_1. Theorem 4 proves exponential attraction in the weighted error norm at rate (sqrt(d)-1)^2. Theorem 5, same page, gives the finite-n steady-state lower bound

    E[S_i^n(infinity)] >= 1 - (1-lambda_n)(d^i-1)/(d-1).

Consequently, for any diverging omega_n, a fraction approaching one of queues have length at least log_d(eta_n)-omega_n with probability approaching one, yielding the stated delay lower bound. This is a lower bound, not a matching typical-delay upper bound. The paper does not prove convergence of the stationary distribution to the ODE fixed point; the stationary interchange remains a separate open issue.

## Transfer limit

The paper demonstrates that a high-load process limit can be deterministic and infinite-dimensional. It does not establish a stationary distribution limit, an SRBM approximation, or any B054 transform/BAR uniqueness statement. The initial-state, Poisson/exponential, d-choice, and eta_n conditions are essential to its process theorem.
