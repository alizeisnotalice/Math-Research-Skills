## Counterexample to the fluid converse in dimension six

For an SRBM in \(\mathbb R_+^6\), take drift \(\theta=(-1,\ldots,-1)^T\), covariance \(\Sigma=I\), and reflection matrix \(R=J_1+J_2\), where \(J_1\) is the all-ones matrix and \(J_2\) is the displayed perturbation matrix in Eq. (1.13). Its diagonal entries are one and all entries of \(R\) are positive, so \(R\) is completely-S. The parameters satisfy

\[
\delta_i>0,\quad \delta_2+\delta_3\le \delta_4/6,\quad
\delta_1\le\delta_3\le0.1,\quad \delta_4<1,
\]

with example \((\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3,\delta_4)=(0.05,0.05,0.05,0.6)\). Theorem 1.2 (physical PDF p. 4) proves that this SRBM is positive recurrent but has a divergent linear fluid path. In the article's LCP convention, \(y(t)=ut,\ z(t)=vt\) with \(u=e_1\) and \(v=\delta_1e_6\) satisfies \(u,v\ge0\), \(v=\theta+Ru\), \(u\cdot v=0\), and \(v\ne0\); hence \(|z(t)|\to\infty\). The path is degenerate because \(u\) and \(v\) have only two positive coordinates in total. Independent extra coordinates extend examples to every \(d>6\).

## Positive-recurrence proof route

The paper controls coordinate differences and boundary regulators through Brownian maximum estimates (§2). In §3, four coordinates \(Z_2,\ldots,Z_5\) act as Brownian “predators” that hit zero in finite expected time relative to \(Z_1\); a Foster criterion turns a suitable stopping-time contraction into positive recurrence. Section 4 states five quantitative propositions covering the cases in which the sixth coordinate grows or shrinks. Sections 5–6 prove these estimates and combine them to verify the Foster drift.

Theorem 1.1 is the sufficient Dupuis–Williams condition: if every fluid path is attracted to the origin, the SRBM is positive recurrent. This example disproves the converse in dimensions \(d\ge6\), not in dimensions 4 or 5; it is an SRBM counterexample and does not directly settle queueing-network stability.
