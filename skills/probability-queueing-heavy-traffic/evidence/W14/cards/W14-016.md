## Models and first-moment results

The paper treats (i) Join-the-Shortest-Queue (JSQ) routing to parallel servers and (ii) MaxWeight (MW) scheduling over a finite set of feasible service vectors. The systems are discrete-time, with bounded arrivals and service; the paper states IID and independence conditions separately for each model in §2. The heavy-traffic parameter epsilon measures distance to the total service rate for JSQ or to a selected capacity face for scheduling.

For any feasible routing/scheduling rule, a comparison with a hypothetical single-server queue gives a Kingman-type lower bound (Section 3). For MW scheduling near face k, Lemma 6, PDF p. 10, gives the expected-workload bound

$$\mathbb E[\langle c^{(k)},Q^{(\epsilon)}\rangle] \ge \frac{\zeta^{(\epsilon,k)}}{2\epsilon}-B_1^{(k)}.$$

where the finite-\(\epsilon\) coefficient is \(\zeta^{(\epsilon,k)}=\langle(c^{(k)})^2,(\sigma^{(\epsilon)})^2\rangle+\epsilon^2\), and \(B_1^{(k)}=b^{(k)}/2\). Its limit is \(\zeta^{(k)}=\langle(c^{(k)})^2,\sigma^2\rangle\), yielding the universal first-order lower coefficient \(\zeta^{(k)}/2\). Keep the finite-\(\epsilon\) correction \(+\epsilon^2\) distinct from the common limiting coefficient.

## Moment state-space collapse, then tight upper bounds

The critical method is to establish moment bounds on the queue component orthogonal to the workload/collapse direction before using a stationary drift equation. Lemma 7, PDF p. 11, bounds the drift of V_perp(Q)=||Q_perp|| by the difference of the squared-norm drifts of Q and Q_parallel, divided by 2||Q_perp||, and gives a uniform one-step increment bound. Hajek's drift lemma then turns a uniform negative drift outside a fixed radius into exponential tails and uniform bounds on every fixed moment of Q_perp. Section 4 carries this out for JSQ and MW.

For JSQ, Proposition 3 and Eqs. (34)–(35), PDF pp. 16–18, use the zero stationary drift of the squared total queue norm, the previously proved moment SSC, and the unused-service identity to match the single-server heavy-traffic coefficient. For MW, Proposition 4 and Eqs. (41)–(42), PDF pp. 18–22, use the squared drift of the active-face workload. MW service lies on that face with probability 1-O(epsilon); SSC controls the residual term. The upper bound is

$$\mathbb E[\langle c^{(k)},\bar Q^{(\epsilon)}\rangle] \le \frac{\zeta^{(\epsilon,k)}}{2\epsilon}+\bar B_1^{(\epsilon,k)}.$$

with the finite-\(\epsilon\) coefficient \(\zeta^{(\epsilon,k)}\) and \(\epsilon\bar B_1^{(\epsilon,k)}\to0\). In later displays the source also uses \(\zeta^{(k)}\) for the limiting coefficient; do not silently substitute that limit into the finite-\(\epsilon\) formula. Thus MW is first-moment heavy-traffic optimal for this weighted workload. It does not identify the full stationary distribution.

## Higher moments and fading

Section 6 extends the drift expansion to nth powers. Proposition 5, PDF pp. 23–24, gives matching-order lower and upper bounds on the nth moment of the MW workload; the leading term is n! (zeta/2)^n after multiplying by epsilon^n, with finite-order error terms under the paper's boundedness assumptions. The proof expands the nth-power stationary drift, reuses the moment SSC bounds to control cross terms, and handles unused service separately. Section 6.2 extends the bounds and SSC argument to channel fading, with the effective service variance and face geometry retained.

## Transfer limit

These are steady-state mean and moment results under the paper's bounded-input, finite-schedule, independence, and stationarity assumptions. The argument shows how SSC sharpens drift bounds; it does not make drift equations identify a joint limit or solve B054's functional equation. Check the model-specific covariance, active face, and policy assumptions before reusing its coefficient.
