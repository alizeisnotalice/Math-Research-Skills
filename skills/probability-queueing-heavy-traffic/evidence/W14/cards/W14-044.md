## Main SRBM statements

The paper studies orthant SRBMs with data $(\theta,\Sigma,R)$ and the associated deterministic fluid paths, which depend on $(\theta,R)$ while stochastic recurrence also depends on $\Sigma$. Completely-S $R$ is the paper’s existence condition. Theorem 2.1 recalls the Dupuis–Williams implication: if every associated fluid path converges to the origin, then the SRBM is positive recurrent. The paper notes the converse in dimensions at most three and constructs counterexamples beginning in dimension four.

**Four dimensions (Theorems 2.2 and 2.3).** Let $\theta=(-1,-1,-1,-1)^T$, $R=J_1+J_2$, with $J_1$ the all-ones matrix and

$$
J_2=\begin{pmatrix}
0&\delta_2&\delta_2&-\delta_4\\
0&0&-\delta_3&-\delta_3\\
0&-\delta_3&0&-\delta_3\\
\delta_1&-\delta_3&-\delta_3&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3,\delta_4)=(.05,.05,.05,.6),
$$

and

$$
\Sigma_\beta=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
0&\beta+1&1-\beta&0\\
0&1-\beta&\beta+1&0\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \beta\ge 2.00046.
$$

Theorem 2.2 states that this SRBM is positive recurrent and has a divergent linear fluid path; Theorem 2.3 gives the positive-recurrence conclusion used in the proof. Proposition 2.1 exhibits the path directly: $z_0=0$, $u=e_1$, $v=\delta_1 e_4$, $y(t)=ut$, and $z(t)=vt$. Indeed $\theta+Ru=v$, $u,v\ge0$, and the only increasing regulator corresponds to a coordinate that stays zero.

**Five dimensions (Theorems 2.4 and 2.5).** With $\theta=(-1,-1,-1,-1,-1)^T$, the analogous five-dimensional $R=J_1+J_2$ and the same $\delta$ values, set

$$
\Sigma_\alpha=\operatorname{diag}(1,\alpha^2,\alpha^2,\alpha^2,1),\qquad \alpha=1.28020.
$$

Theorem 2.4 states positive recurrence with a divergent linear fluid path. The exhibited path has $u=e_1$, $v=\delta_1e_5$. Theorem 2.5 diagonally scales the process by $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,1/\alpha,1/\alpha,1/\alpha,1)$ to identity covariance; under this transformation the drift also changes to $(-1,-1/\alpha,-1/\alpha,-1/\alpha,-1)^T$. Do not report the identity-covariance example as retaining the original drift/reflection data.

**Covariance dependence (Theorem 5.1).** Holding the four-dimensional $\theta$ and $R$ fixed, the correlated $\Sigma_\beta$ choice is positive recurrent for $\beta\ge2.00046$, while replacing it by $I_4$ gives a non-positive-recurrent SRBM. The proof starts at $(0,2,2,2)^T$ and lower-bounds the expected hitting time of the unit ball by the infinite-mean capture time in the basic three-predator Brownian pursuit model. Thus recurrence is not determined by the fluid paths/$(\theta,R)$ alone.

## Proof route and exact limits

For four dimensions, Sections 3–4 prove Brownian/regulator/coordinate estimates, use the two-predator capture tail in Proposition 3.1, and combine Propositions 4.2–4.6 with a stopping-time Foster criterion (Proposition 4.1) to establish Theorem 2.3. The proof is a case split with exponentially small exceptional events and moment bounds. Section 4.8 gives a **summary**, rather than a full parallel derivation, for the five-dimensional recurrence proof; it adapts the estimates and uses the three-predator capture result.

The predator–prey statements are auxiliary estimates, not queueing-network theorems. Theorem 6.1 proves a tail bound $P(T>t)\le C t^{-(1+\nu)}$ with $\nu=.000073$ for a coupled two-predator model when $\beta\ge2.00046$. Theorem 6.2 proves the same form for three independent predators with variance $\alpha^2$ and unit prey variance when $\alpha\ge1.28020$. Its proof reduces Brownian cone exit to a spherical Dirichlet eigenvalue estimate and uses a numerical approximation to locate a hypergeometric-function zero; the paper reports this computation in Mathematica. These numerical thresholds are the source’s stated values, not an independent rigorous interval certificate in this card.

## Reuse boundaries

The source establishes that the fluid-convergence sufficient criterion is not necessary in dimension four or above, and that dimension three is the largest dimension in which positive recurrence forces convergence of all associated fluid paths. It supplies a five-dimensional identity-covariance example. It leaves open whether such a counterexample exists in four dimensions with identity covariance. The four-dimensional example uses a correlated, nonidentity covariance matrix. These are SRBM examples: they do not establish positive Harris recurrence of a queueing network, a queueing-switch diffusion limit, BAR uniqueness, or the B054 probability-admissible transform claim. Do not transfer a result to queueing networks without separately proving the diffusion approximation and state-space-collapse interface.
