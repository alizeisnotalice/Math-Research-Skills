# W14-039 — Gamarnik, undecidability of constrained walks and queue stability

**Reading:** Complete author-hosted Mathematics of Operations Research article, physical PDF pp. 1–22 (printed pp. 272–293), SHA-256 e7d81511e3a1a379f19d7a5527dd1eee89da13c67fcafbc89efc94435b7b8666. Read all pages, including the reduction figures and conclusion. See [coverage](../reading_coverage.json#W14-039).

## Exact models and theorem scope

The paper treats two deterministic settings. First, a homogeneous walk on \(\mathbb Z_+^d\) has bounded transition increments whose rule depends only on which coordinates are positive; in the deterministic case the face determines a single increment. Stability is defined by repeated visits to a bounded set, and global stability requires this for every initial state.

Second, the queueing construction is a single-station network with deterministic integer service times, deterministic periodic external arrivals, and a **generalized priority** rule that maps the binary vector of nonempty buffers to a selected buffer or to idling. The rule is state-dependent and may intentionally idle even when work is present. Theorem 4 (§5.3, physical PDF pp. 16–17) proves that no algorithm decides whether such a specified system/policy/initial queue vector is stable. The paper also proves undecidability for deterministic homogeneous orthant walks and for global stability variants, and shows fluid-limit computation can be undecidable. The reduction encodes counter-machine states and counters in queue buffers and uses monitor jobs to enforce a sequence of service subcycles; an unbounded counter produces an unstable queue, while a halting machine is modified to remain in its halting configuration and yields a bounded construction.

## Proven versus conjectured

The proved queue result is for the paper's generalized state-dependent policy class and its deterministic one-station construction. The paper explicitly says that undecidability for ordinary FIFO and ordinary priority policies is a **conjecture**, not a theorem. This 2002 journal article is an expanded work distinct from Gamarnik's 2000 SODA conference paper W14-007; do not mark W14-007 as read or transfer this result to static nonpreemptive buffer priorities without a reduction preserving the exact policy and stability definition.

## Proof route

The undecidability proof reduces counter-machine halting to stability. It first constructs deterministic orthant walks whose face transitions simulate a machine; it then constructs buffers for machine-state and counter encodings, plus monitor buffers that enforce the transition order. Assuming a stability decision algorithm would decide halting, contradicting the counter-machine theorem. The assumptions and definition used here concern bounded deterministic trajectories, not stochastic positive Harris recurrence.
