# W14-012 — Stolyar, CRP MaxWeight heavy traffic

**Reading:** Complete paper, PDF pp. 1–53. Local PDF and extracted text: data/stolyar04_generalized_switch.pdf and .txt. See reading_coverage.json#W14-012 for section/subsection/appendix coverage.

## Model and heavy-traffic regime

The paper studies a generalized switch with finitely many input flows and feasible service schedules. The capacity region is the convex hull of mean service vectors. Its base model has ergodic countable-state Markov arrivals; the heavy-traffic theorem uses the more specific per-flow IID arrival assumptions stated in §§7–8. MaxWeight chooses a feasible schedule maximizing the weighted queue sum, with flow n's weight proportional to \(\gamma_n q_n^\beta\), \(\beta>0\). Under resource pooling (RP), the limiting capacity point has a one-dimensional workload normal \(\zeta\); the paper also defines a static service split (SSS) comparator.

## Main pathwise heavy-traffic result

Under the heavy-traffic assumptions (PDF §§7–8, pp. 14–17), the theorem does **not** require the full arrival-rate vector to approach the boundary point along the RP normal. The normal projection is the controlled quantity: \(r(\zeta\cdot\lambda^r-\zeta\cdot\lambda)\to a\). The theorem also assumes convergence of the per-flow second moments and the uniform second-tail condition (14); it is not a bounded-arrival theorem. The initial-state scaling condition (16) is part of the process-level conclusion. Theorem 1(i) gives diffusion-scale workload convergence to a one-dimensional reflected Brownian motion and process-level state-space collapse of the queue vector onto the invariant manifold. Theorem 1(ii) compares MaxWeight with **any** scheduling discipline \(G\) allowed by the theorem, not only with SSS.

The proof route is §§10–12: fluid-sample-path compactness and uniform attraction to the invariant manifold precede the diffusion limit. The Lyapunov function is \(\phi(q)=\sum_n\gamma_n q_n^{\beta+1}/(\beta+1)\); the MaxWeight differential inclusion makes it nonincreasing, while the RP workload coordinate is nondecreasing along fluid paths. A compactness contradiction gives uniform attraction. Under diffusion scaling, subsequential limits lie on the manifold; the workload equation becomes a one-dimensional Skorohod reflection problem, with a separate argument ruling out asymptotic idleness.

## Limitations and transfer

Section 9.1 states **Conjecture 1** for stationary distributions: the proposed limit is the stationary RBM workload multiplied by the invariant-manifold vector. It is not a claim that the stationary queue vector converges to a deterministic invariant point. The theorem is a pathwise diffusion-limit result under its initial-state condition, not a stationary-distribution interchange theorem. Do not infer a joint transform uniqueness result for B054 from SSC or workload minimization alone.
