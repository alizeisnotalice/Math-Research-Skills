# W14-009 — Dai–Meyn, fluid stability and queue-length moments

**Reading:** Full 16-page IEEE paper read, PDF pp. 1–16 (printed pp. 1889–1904). Local fulltext: `data/dai_meyn95_moments.pdf` and `.txt`. Identity/version checked against the PDF title page and DOI metadata.

## Model and fluid criterion

The network has (d) single-server stations and (K) customer classes, deterministic or Bernoulli routing matrix (P) with (I+P+P^2+cdots) finite, infinite buffers, nonidling disciplines, and a Markov state enlarged with residual external interarrival and service times. The paper permits general renewal external input and class-dependent services. Assumptions A1–A2 on PDF p. 6 are: mutually independent IID primitive sequences; for an integer (p\ge1), finite ((p+1))-st moments of nonnull external interarrivals and all service times. For positive Harris conclusions, A3 on p. 6 requires the zero state to be a singleton and, for each nonnull arrival stream, an unbounded interarrival tail plus a spread-out convolution component; alternatively A3′ requires every compact state set to be small.

The undelayed fluid equations (3.8)–(3.13), PDF pp. 5–6, replace renewal counts and service counts by their rates and preserve routing, nonidling, and discipline-specific complementarity. Fluid stability (Definition 3.3, PDF p. 6) means a single finite drainage time (t_0) works for **every** fluid solution with ‖\(\bar Q(0)\)‖=1, after which ‖\(\bar Q(t)\)‖=0. Lemma 3.1 transfers this to the delayed fluid model when residual arrival/service clocks are included.

## Main theorem and proof chain

**Theorem 4.1, PDF p. 6 (printed p. 1894):** stable fluid model + A1–A2 gives a finite time-averaged (p)-th queue-length moment for every initial state. With A3 or A3′, the network is positive Harris recurrent; transient queue moments of orders (1,ldots,p) converge to their stationary values, the first moment converges at the stated (t^{p-1}) rate, and the time averages obey an SLLN. This is a sufficient implication; the paper does not prove the converse “positive Harris recurrent ⇒ fluid stable.”

The argument is deliberately staged. Proposition 5.1 (PDF p. 10) obtains a contraction at time (t_0\|x\|): (\mathbb E_x\|Q(t_0\|x\|)\|^{p+1}/\|x\|^{p+1}\to0). Its proof combines delayed-fluid stability and fluid-limit convergence with uniform integrability of renewal counts, supplied by the ((p+1))-moment assumption. Proposition 5.3 then bounds expected (p)-weighted occupation up to a return to a compact set by a constant times (\|x\|^{p+1}+1). Proposition 5.4 converts this return/occupation bound to a long-run average. These steps yield **Theorem 5.5**, PDF p. 12 (printed p. 1900):

\[
\frac1t\int_0^t \mathbb E_x[\|Q(s)\|^p]ds\le \kappa_p\left(\frac{\|x\|^{p+1}}t+1\right),\qquad t>0.
\]

For convergence, Proposition 6.1 builds a resolvent Lyapunov function with extended-generator drift (\mathcal GV_{p+1}\le-f_p+b\mathbf1_C). Under the small-set/recurrence hypothesis, **Theorem 6.2**, PDF p. 13, gives pointwise (f_p)-total-variation convergence and convergence of the (p)-th queue moment. **Theorem 6.3**, PDF pp. 13–14, sharpens the first-moment rate to (t^{p-1}\|P^t(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{f_1}\to0). Theorem 6.4, PDF pp. 14–15, supplies the SLLN for integrable state functions.

The mechanism is not “fluid stability directly gives every stochastic conclusion.” Fluid stability first provides a scaled-state contraction; moment assumptions control uniform integrability; return-time estimates give drift; A3/A3′ then supplies Harris recurrence and the ergodic limits.

## Polling application and scope boundary

The paper also treats an \(\ell_k\)-limited token ring: class \(k\) arrivals have rate \(\alpha_k\), service rate \(\mu_k\), nominal load \(\beta_k=\alpha_k/\mu_k\), and total load \(\rho_0=\sum_k\beta_k\). The switch from buffer \(k\) to \(k+1\) has iid duration \(\eta_k^0(n)\), switch-over rate \(\mu_k^0=\mathbb E[\eta_k^0(1)]^{-1}\), and mean full-cycle switch-over time \(u^0=\sum_k(\mu_k^0)^{-1}\) (PDF pp. 7–8, Eq. (4.3)). Proposition 4.2 and its proof (PDF pp. 7–8) establish the fluid conservation identities. The piecewise-linear workload argument yields condition (4.8), PDF p. 8 (printed p. 1896), as a **sufficient** stability condition:

\[
\beta_k < \frac{(1-\rho_0)\ell_k}{\mu_k u^0},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
\]

Theorem 4.3 (PDF p. 8, printed p. 1896) then transfers the \(p\)-moment bound by Theorem 4.1; under A3′ it also transfers the recurrence and convergence conclusions. The authors cite a separate converse for this ring in the polling literature; do not upgrade (4.8) alone to an iff condition.

## Use in the current skill

Use this paper to separate fluid drainage, Harris recurrence, moment bounds, and convergence of moments. For A008 it is an auxiliary general theorem only: its general fluid-to-Harris implication does not close the three-queue adaptive limited-service gap when (l_2>1); the exact primitive assumptions and associated-fluid-model conditions still need to match.
