# W14-017 — Wang–Maguluri–Javidi, reconfiguration delay

**Reading status:** Full local arXiv v1 paper read, PDF pp. 1–14 (the journal version is Queueing Systems 98, 2021). Formula-heavy PDF pp. 5–9 and 12–14 were rendered with scripts/render_pdf_page.swift and visually checked. Local PDF: data/wang_maguluri_javidi17_reconfiguration.pdf.

## Model and assumptions

The model is an \(n\times n\) input-queued switch with integer reconfiguration delay \(\Delta_r>0\). Arrivals are independent over queues and iid over slots, have finite support \(a_{ij}(t)\le a_{\max}\), and have mean \(\lambda^{(\epsilon)}=(1-\epsilon)\nu\), where \(\nu\in F\) has every row and column saturated and \(\nu_{\min}=\min_{ij}\nu_{ij}>0\). The variance norm is uniformly bounded in \(\epsilon\). The state includes queue vector \(q\), current schedule \(s\), and remaining reconfiguration delay \(r\); each member of the family is assumed positive recurrent.

Adaptive MaxWeight compares the current schedule weight with a maximum-weight schedule and switches when the gap exceeds an increasing, sublinear hysteresis function \(g\). Fact 1 gives throughput optimality for increasing sublinear \(g\); Theorem 1 additionally assumes \(g\) is concave. The results below require \(0<\epsilon\le\nu_{\min}/(4\|\nu\|)\), fixed \(n,\Delta_r\), and bounded arrivals/variance.

## Weak steady-state SSC

The cone \(K=\{x:x_{ij}=w_i+\widetilde w_j,\ w_i,\widetilde w_j\ge0\}\), and the orthogonal projections \(q_\parallel=\operatorname{Proj}_K(q)\), \(q_\perp=q-q_\parallel\), are inherited from the zero-delay switch. Lemma 1 (PDF pp. 4–5) is a \(T\)-step drift bound: if the \(T\)-step drift of a nonnegative Lyapunov function is negative outside a threshold and its increments are bounded by \(D\), then the stationary mean is at most \(\kappa+2D^2/\eta\).

Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 5, 12–14) uses \(Z(X)=\max\{\|q_\perp\|-\theta\|q_\parallel\|,0\}\), with fixed \(0<\theta<1/2\). Bounded increments follow from nonexpansiveness of both cone projections and the bounded arrival jumps. For the negative \(T\)-step drift, the proof combines: (i) the squared-norm drift of \(q\), (ii) the squared-norm drift of \(q_\parallel\), (iii) the MaxWeight deficit estimate \(\langle q,\nu-s^*(q)\rangle\le-\nu_{\min}\|q_\perp\|\), and (iv) a deterministic bound on how many slots in an interval of length \(T\) can be spent in reconfiguration when the maximum schedule weight is large. The chosen \(T\) is \(8\sqrt n\,\Delta_r/\nu_{\min}\). Applying Lemma 1 yields a constant \(M_\theta\), independent of \(\epsilon\), such that
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\|q_\perp^{(\epsilon)}\|-\theta\|q_\parallel^{(\epsilon)}\|\right]\le M_\theta.
\]
The authors call this weak SSC. With the diverging total queue scale, they derive
\[
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{\mathbb E\|q_\perp^{(\epsilon)}\|}{\mathbb E\|q^{(\epsilon)}\|}=0.
\]
This is weaker than the all-moments bounded orthogonal residual proved for the instantaneous-service MaxWeight switch by Maguluri–Srikant (W14-013).

## Drift and schedule-duration chain

The drift section uses the quadratic Lyapunov function
\[
V_4(q)=\sum_i\Big(\sum_jq_{ij}\Big)^2+
\sum_j\Big(\sum_iq_{ij}\Big)^2-\frac1n\Big(\sum_{ij}q_{ij}\Big)^2.
\]
In stationarity, its zero drift is rearranged as \(T_1=T_2+T_3+T_4\). Unlike the zero-delay case, \(T_1\) contains the reconfiguration indicator \(1_{\{r(t)>0\}}\). The arrival-variance contribution is \(T_2\); unused service determines \(T_3\); \(T_4\) uses \(u_{ij}(t)q_{ij}(t+1)=0\) and the cone projection identity. Equations (3)–(8), PDF pp. 6–8, give a mean-queue bound involving both \(\Pr\{r>0\}\) and \(\mathbb E\|q_\perp\|\). The latter is lower order by weak SSC.

To determine the reconfiguration probability, the paper samples the Markov chain at schedule-change times \(t_k^S\) and uses the schedule weight \(W(X)=\langle q,s\rangle\). Zero drift of \(W\) over a schedule period gives Eq. (10), PDF p. 8: the conditional expected duration between schedule changes is
\[
\mathbb E[t_{k+1}^S-t_k^S\mid X(t_k^S)=\widehat X]
=\frac{\mathbb E[g(\widehat W^*)+\delta_W\mid\widehat X]}
{(n-\alpha)(1-\epsilon)},
\]
where \(0<\delta_W<na_{\max}\) and \(\alpha\) denotes the paper's mean pairing of the critical rate matrix with the schedule chosen at a change epoch. The stationary duration must be at least \(\Delta_r/\epsilon\), which yields a lower bound on expected maximum weight through Jensen and \(g^{-1}\), and the reconfiguration fraction is related to the mean schedule duration by Eq. (13), PDF p. 9. The subsequent inequalities identify the leading order of \(\mathbb E g(\widehat W^*)\) as \((n-\alpha)\Delta_r/\epsilon\) (equivalently, \(\epsilon\,\mathbb E g(\widehat W^*)\to(n-\alpha)\Delta_r\) in the fixed-parameter interpretation). This gives a lower bound of order \(g^{-1}(1/\epsilon)\) on mean queue size. The paper's proposed \(O(n g^{-1}(1/\epsilon))\) queue approximation is explicitly presented as an approximation, not a general theorem from Jensen alone.

For the uniform-traffic simulations, \(\nu_{ij}=1/n\) and \(\alpha=n-1\). The paper reports simulated exponents for \(g(x)=(1-\gamma)x^{1-\delta}\), but those regressions are not proof of a sharper queue-length limit.

## Scope for B054

This is an adjacent switch model with schedule reconfiguration, not the instantaneous-service MaxWeight model in B054. It demonstrates that policy memory and setup delay change both the SSC strength and the unused-service drift. It supplies no solution of B054's functional equation and no uniqueness proof for the B054 probability-admissible transform/boundary-measure pair.
