# W14-002 — Chernova–Foss–Kim, adaptive polling stability

**Reading:** Full paper, PDF pp. 1–21. Local fulltext: `data/ordos_primary_106.pdf` and `.txt`. Identity checked against arXiv:1105.2069v3.

## Model and conclusions

The paper studies a single server and three queues with standard cycles (1\to2\to3\to1) and reduced cycles (1\to3\to1). A cycle is reduced when the server finds queue 2 empty during the current cycle; after a reduced cycle, the next is standard. Arrivals, service times, and the four walk-time sequences are mutually independent IID sequences with finite means; the standard mean cycle walk time \(\zeta\) exceeds the reduced one \(\zeta^*\) (assumption (2.1)). The state is augmented to make the process strong Markov. Stability means positive Harris recurrence; the stated instability notion is transience, with queue norm diverging a.s. The input interarrival law also has unbounded-support and spread-out assumptions.

**Theorems 1–2, PDF pp. 5–6:** for limited service the paper proves sufficient stability and instability inequalities, not a general iff criterion. For gated and exhaustive service, the criterion is \(\rho_0<1\) for stability and \(\rho_0\ge1\) for instability (transience for \(\rho_0>1\)). **Corollary 1, p. 6:** when \(l_2=1\), the limited-service sufficient conditions meet and give an iff criterion.

For \(l_2>1\), the exact stability region remains open. The two-sided fluid bound (3.14) is explicitly “not tight if \(l_2>1\)” (discussion §5, PDF p. 16). The authors conjecture that the relevant fractions can depend on the entire primitive distributions and may not be determined by finitely many moments. Their examples illustrate the issue; they do not prove full-distribution dependence for every parameter choice.

## Proof chain and boundary

The proof constructs fluid equations from functional SLLNs and counting identities (§3), uses stable-fluid-implies-positive-Harris recurrence for sufficiency, and combines fluid instability with Markov-regenerative visit rates for necessity bounds. A three-step drainage argument uses \(W(t)=\sum_k \bar Q_k(t)/\mu_k\). The source's PDF text extraction garbles the two-column displays (2.2)–(2.7); this card therefore does not reconstruct their full algebra from OCR. Do not promote the two-queue 2012 example (W14-004) to a theorem for this three-queue model.
