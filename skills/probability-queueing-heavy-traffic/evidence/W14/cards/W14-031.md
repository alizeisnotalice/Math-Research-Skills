## Model and theorem

This sequel considers sequences of multiple-channel queueing facilities, possibly embedded in a network, and includes batch arrivals and service. For each system, the \(r+s\) channel processes are independent across channels, but the input sequences within a channel need not be iid or independent. The core assumptions are weak convergence of the appropriately normalized arrival and potential-service processes and a limit for the centered net drift. The modified system again keeps potential-service clocks running and assigns customers to the server with earliest completion; the standard FCFS version has the same heavy-traffic queue limit by the paper's coupling argument.

Theorem 1 (physical PDF pp. 6–7; printed pp. 360–361) assumes weak convergence of the channel input processes in \(D[0,1]\) and gives three drift regimes. Writing \(X\) for the limiting net input and \(M(t)=ct\): (a) if \((\lambda^n-\mu^n)\sqrt n\to c\in\mathbb R\), then \(Q_n^{\prime}\Rightarrow f(X+M)\); (b) if this scaled drift tends to \(+\infty\), then \(Q_n^{\prime\prime}\Rightarrow X\); and (c) if it tends to \(-\infty\) and \(\Pr\{X\in C[0,1]\}=1\), then \(Q_n^{\prime}\Rightarrow0\). Here \(C[0,1]\) is the continuous-path subspace: in the proof of (c) (physical p. 7; printed p. 361) the authors use this assumption to obtain C-tightness of \(X_n\); it is not a nonnegative-path-support condition. Theorem 2 gives analogous departure-process limits. These are functional weak limits, not invariant-law or total-variation results.

Section 3 verifies the process hypotheses for triangular arrays, deterministic/periodic channels, exchangeable order-statistic constructions, batches, and split streams. Section 4 gives joint limits for several facilities in an open acyclic network; it does not establish a general cyclic-network result.

## Proof route and scope boundary

The proof first obtains weak limits for the aggregate net input from the per-channel process limits, then applies the reflection mapping and drift-regime comparison. The standard/modified comparison is transferred using shared primitive data. For a network, joint limits are built from the facility-level limits and the network's dependency structure.

Use this source to distinguish weak process limits and model robustness from stronger stationary claims. It does not prove convergence of stationary G/G/s distributions, total-variation convergence, or a finite coupling time under stationary ergodic paired input; those remain separate interfaces in A011.
