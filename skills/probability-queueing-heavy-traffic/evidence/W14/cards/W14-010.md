# W14-010 — Dai–Weiss, piecewise-linear fluid Lyapunov functions

**Reading:** Full 20-page article read, PDF pp. 1–20 (printed pp. 115–134). Identity checked against the D01 handoff and rendered title page; local PDF is `../../evidence/fulltexts/W14-010.pdf` with SHA-256 `c6996d4db0bd840c13a0ee8b9d778d234291d311ffdf7b7f1bcabb84e2214706`.

## Model and stability implication

The paper studies a multiclass reentrant line with a single deterministic route, infinite buffers, nonidling servers, and class-specific mean service times (m_k). For customers indexed by (n), the vector of the external interarrival and stage service times is IID across (n); service times across stages for one customer may be dependent. Means are finite, arrival intertimes are unbounded and spread out, and every station load satisfies ρ_i<1 (assumptions (1.1)–(1.7), PDF pp. 1–2). The queueing state includes residual arrival/service times and discipline state; “stable” for the stochastic network means positive Harris recurrence (Definition 1.1, PDF p. 3).

The work-conserving fluid equations are (1.8)–(1.13), PDF pp. 4–5. Fluid stability (Definition 1.3, PDF p. 5) requires a common finite drainage time for **every** fluid solution with normalized initial queue mass. Theorem 1.1, p. 5, states only the forward implication: under the stated primitive assumptions, a stable fluid model implies the queueing discipline is positive Harris recurrent. The paper explicitly leaves the converse open; an unstable fluid trajectory is not by itself a stochastic-network instability theorem.

## Reusable max-of-linear-functions argument

Proposition 2.1 gives absolute continuity of fluid paths. Lemma 2.2, PDF p. 6, is the scalar drainage step: if a nonnegative absolutely continuous (g) has derivative zero whenever (g=0), and ẋg≤-ε whenever (g>0) (a.e.), then it reaches zero by (g(0)/\epsilon) and stays zero. Lemma 3.2, PDF pp. 7–8, lifts this to (G=\max_i G_i) for nonnegative linear forms: (a) whenever station (i)'s workload (W_i>0), ẋ(G_i\le-\epsilon_i); (b) if (W_i=0), then (G_i<\min_{j\ne i}G_j). At a positive active maximum, condition (b) prevents the associated station from being empty; at least one active maximizer therefore has negative derivative, so the maximum drains at rate at least (\min_i\epsilon_i).

For the three-buffer route (1\to2\to3\) with buffers 1 and 3 at station 1 and buffer 2 at station 2, Theorem 3.1, PDF pp. 6–8, proves all work-conserving fluid models stable when (m_1+m_3<1), (m_2<1). It takes (\theta=m_1/(m_1+m_3)), (G_1=\theta Q_1^+ +(1-\theta)Q_3^+), (G_2=Q_2^+), and applies Lemmas 3.2 and 2.2. For general reentrant lines, Theorems 4.3 and 4.4, PDF pp. 10–11, prove fluid stability under FBFS and LBFS respectively, with different discipline-specific arguments: sequential buffer drainage for FBFS and a uniform negative derivative of total queue mass for LBFS.

## Lu–Kumar four-buffer boundary

For the two-station route (1\to2\to3\to4\), with buffers 1,4 at station 1 and 2,3 at station 2, assume (m_1+m_4<1) and (m_2+m_3<1). **Theorem 5.1 together with the equality case in its proof, PDF pp. 11–14 (printed pp. 125–128),** gives the exact fluid-model boundary: there exists an unstable work-conserving policy iff (m_2+m_4\ge1). The class-2/4 higher-priority policy realizes the unstable case. When (m_2+m_4>1), the constructed fluid solution has cycle minima

\[
Q(s_n)=\left(\left(\frac{m_4}{1-m_2}\right)^n,0,0,0\right),
\]

which diverge; at equality, it is periodic and so fails the paper's finite-drainage fluid-stability definition. When (m_2+m_4<1), all work-conserving fluid policies are stable. The proof chooses (0<\theta_2\le\theta_1<1) so (G_1=\theta_1Q_1^+ +(1-\theta_1)Q_4^+), (G_2=\theta_2Q_2^+ +(1-\theta_2)Q_3^+) satisfy Lemma 3.2. Remark 4 gives an LP (5.5) that maximizes a uniform drift margin and yields a sharp drainage-time bound. Do not confuse “there exists an unstable policy when (\ge1)” with “every policy is unstable.”

Theorem 6.1 extends all-work-conserving fluid stability to two-station Kelly-type lines with no immediate feedback; its no-feedback condition cannot be dropped. Theorem 6.2 proves all-work-conserving stability for the unidirectional ring under station loads (\beta_iA_i<1). Both use max-of-linear workload functions. These are fluid conclusions, and stochastic positive-Harris conclusions require Theorem 1.1's assumptions.

## Transfer boundary

The paper supplies a general proof pattern for fluid drainage and a sharp counterexample boundary for its own Lu–Kumar route. It does not establish an iff criterion for every reentrant line, nor solve A008: the adaptive three-queue limited-service model has different route/state and fluid dynamics. W14-009 supplies the separate stochastic moment/ergodic consequences once fluid stability and its own assumptions are verified.
