## Model and positive-recurrence route

The source studies open multiclass queueing networks with infinite buffers and work-conserving disciplines. Its Markov state retains queue order/class information and residual interarrival and service times, with discipline-specific additions (Section 2). Stability means positive Harris recurrence of this state process. The standing assumptions (1.2)–(1.5) include mutually independent IID primitive sequences, finite interarrival and service means, and unbounded spread-out interarrival distributions; the paper notes some network-specific relaxations of the independence condition.

Theorem 3.1 (physical PDF p. 12) is a Lyapunov-to-Harris criterion: if there exists $\delta>0$ such that

$$\lim_{\lVert x\rVert\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lVert x\rVert}\,\mathbb E\big[\lVert X^x(\lVert x\rVert\delta)\rVert\big]=0,$$

then a bounded sublevel set has the required return-time bound and the process is positive Harris recurrent. The proof chooses a state-dependent sampling time to turn expected-norm contraction into a Foster drift inequality, then invokes the petite-set lemmas (Lemmas 3.1–3.2); the spread-out arrival assumption is used for petite sets.

## Fluid-limit theorem and proof chain

Theorem 4.1 (physical PDF pp. 18–19) extracts, from every diverging sequence of initial states and almost every primitive sample path, a subsequence whose scaled queue and cumulative-service processes converge uniformly on compact time intervals to a fluid limit satisfying the network flow equations, nonnegativity, work conservation and discipline-specific constraints. Definition 4.1 (p. 20) distinguishes actual fluid limits from all solutions of the larger fluid model; those solutions need not be unique. Theorem 4.2 (p. 20) states that under (1.2)–(1.5), stability of the fluid **limit model** implies positive Harris recurrence of the Markov state process.

The implication is not just “fluid stability implies stability”: the proof uses fluid compactness, a uniform-integrability estimate for scaled renewal counts (Lemma 4.5), the fluid drainage bound, and then Theorem 3.1. Sections 5–7 give applications: generalized Jackson networks and feedforward networks under nominal loads below one, and FBFS preemptive-resume stability in reentrant lines (Theorem 7.1, physical PDF p. 25) under the same traffic condition. The theorem is sufficient; the paper does not claim the converse for arbitrary multiclass networks or disciplines.

Before applying it, verify the exact Markov state, primitive dependence/moments, spread-out condition, work conservation, and whether the proposed stable paths belong to the actual fluid-limit model or only to a larger associated fluid network.
