# W14-013 — Maguluri–Srikant, MaxWeight switch heavy-traffic means

**Reading status:** Full local arXiv v3 paper read, PDF pp. 1–30. Formula-heavy pp. 7–8, 12–14, 17, 19, 22–26, and 30 were rendered with scripts/render_pdf_page.swift and visually checked. Local PDF: data/maguluri_srikant15_switch.pdf.

## Model and assumptions

The model is a discrete-time \(n\times n\) input-queued switch. Arrivals are independent across input-output pairs and iid over slots, bounded by \(a_{\max}\), and independent of scheduling; the paper also assumes a positive probability of zero arrivals to each queue to obtain irreducibility on its chosen state space. A feasible schedule is a partial matching; the proof may take the selected MaxWeight schedule to be maximal, hence a perfect matching. The queue process is positive recurrent for each interior arrival rate.

For the heavy-traffic family, \(\lambda^{(\epsilon)}=(1-\epsilon)\nu\), where \(\nu\) lies in the Birkhoff face \(F\) (every row and column sum is one) and \(\nu_{\min}=\min_{ij}\nu_{ij}>0\). The variance vector must remain uniformly bounded as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\); the limit theorem assumes \((\sigma^{(\epsilon)})^2\to\sigma^2\). All stated asymptotics fix \(n\).

## Geometry and SSC proof chain

The collapse cone is
\[
K=\{x:x_{ij}=w_i+\widetilde w_j,\;w_i,\widetilde w_j\ge0\}.
\]
Write \(q_\parallel=\operatorname{Proj}_K(q)\) and \(q_\perp=q-q_\parallel\). Projection onto the closed convex cone gives \(q_\parallel\in K\), \(q_\perp\in K^\circ\), and \(\langle q_\parallel,q_\perp\rangle=0\). Lemma 1, PDF p. 7, gives the coordinate identity
\[
x_{ij}=\frac1n\sum_{j'}x_{ij'}+\frac1n\sum_{i'}x_{i'j}
-\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i',j'}x_{i'j'}\qquad(x\in K).
\]
This identity later aligns the unused-service drift with \(K\).

Lemmas 2–3 (PDF p. 8) convert a Lyapunov drift that is uniformly negative outside a compact set, together with a bounded one-step increment, into geometric tails and explicit moment bounds. Proposition 2 (PDF pp. 12–15) applies this to \(W_\perp(q)=\|q_\perp\|\). The drift uses \(V(q)=\|q\|^2\), \(V_\parallel(q)=\|q_\parallel\|^2\), and the square-root comparison
\[
\Delta W_\perp\le \frac{\Delta V-\Delta V_\parallel}{2\|q_\perp\|}.
\]
The key geometric claim is \(\nu+(\nu_{\min}/\|q_\perp\|)q_\perp\in C\). MaxWeight then gives a negative drift of order \(-\nu_{\min}\) once \(\|q_\perp\|\) exceeds \(4(\|\lambda\|^2+\|\sigma\|^2+n)/\nu_{\min}\), provided \(\epsilon\le\nu_{\min}/(2\|\nu\|)\). The bounded increment is \(D=na_{\max}\). Lemma 3 yields, for every integer \(r\ge1\),
\[
\mathbb E\|q_\perp^{(\epsilon)}\|^r\le (M_r^{(\epsilon)})^r,
\]
with the explicit \(M_r^{(\epsilon)}\) displayed in Proposition 2. Under a uniform variance bound, these constants are bounded in \(\epsilon\). This is a steady-state moment SSC bound, not merely a fluid-limit collapse statement.

## Main heavy-traffic theorem and proof route

Proposition 1 (PDF pp. 9–11) is a policy-independent lower bound. Aggregate arrivals at each input into a single-server queue. A pathwise coupling shows its stationary workload is no larger than that input's total switch backlog. The stationary second-moment drift and the unused-service identity \(\mathbb E u=\epsilon\) give
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{ij}q_{ij}^{(\epsilon)}
\ge \frac{\|\sigma^{(\epsilon)}\|^2}{2\epsilon}
-\frac{n(1-\epsilon)}2.
\]
Hence \(\liminf_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\epsilon\,\mathbb E\sum q_{ij}^{(\epsilon)}\ge\|\sigma\|^2/2\).

Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 17–23) proves the MaxWeight mean limit. It combines the steady-state zero drift of
\[
V_4(q)=\sum_i\Big(\sum_jq_{ij}\Big)^2+
\sum_j\Big(\sum_iq_{ij}\Big)^2-\frac1n\Big(\sum_{ij}q_{ij}\Big)^2
\]
with a decomposition into \(T_1,T_2,T_3,T_4\). The row/column saturation gives \(T_1=2\epsilon\,\mathbb E\sum q_{ij}\); independent arrivals give \(T_2=n\epsilon^2+(2-1/n)\|\sigma^{(\epsilon)}\|^2\); unused service gives \(-2n\epsilon\le T_3\le n\epsilon\). For \(T_4\), the coordinate identity for \(K\), polar-cone signs, Hölder, and Proposition 2 yield, for each integer \(r\ge2\),
\[
-6n^{2-1/r}\epsilon^{1-1/r}M_r^{(\epsilon)}
\le T_4\le
4n^{2-1/r}\epsilon^{1-1/r}M_r^{(\epsilon)}.
\]
Thus, with the paper's \(B_1,B_2=o(1/\epsilon)\),
\[
\left(1-\frac1{2n}\right)\frac{\|\sigma^{(\epsilon)}\|^2}{\epsilon}-B_1
\le\mathbb E\sum_{ij}q_{ij}^{(\epsilon)}
\le
\left(1-\frac1{2n}\right)\frac{\|\sigma^{(\epsilon)}\|^2}{\epsilon}+B_2,
\]
and
\[
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\epsilon\,\mathbb E\sum_{ij}q_{ij}^{(\epsilon)}
=\left(1-\frac1{2n}\right)\|\sigma\|^2.
\]
The source gives
\[
B_1(\epsilon,n)=-\frac{n\epsilon}{2}+n+3n^{2-1/r}\epsilon^{-1/r}M_r^{(\epsilon)},\qquad
B_2(\epsilon,n)=\frac{n(1+\epsilon)}2+2n^{2-1/r}\epsilon^{-1/r}M_r^{(\epsilon)},
\]
for integer \(r\ge2\) (Theorem 1, PDF p. 17). In particular the dimension factor is \(n^{2-1/r}\), not \(n(2-1/r)\). The argument for zero drift relies on finite stationary expectations established in Lemma 5 and Appendix B (PDF pp. 17–18, 30).

For uniform Bernoulli traffic, \(\lambda_{ij}^{(\epsilon)}=(1-\epsilon)/n\), so each limiting variance is \((n-1)/n^2\), and \(\|\sigma\|^2=n-1\). The limit is therefore \(n-\tfrac32+\tfrac1{2n}\) (Theorem 2, PDF pp. 24–25). The universal lower-bound coefficient is \((n-1)/2\); the paper concludes the MaxWeight coefficient is within a factor below two. Corollary 1 (PDF pp. 25–26) treats a simultaneous \(n\to\infty,\epsilon(n)=\gamma_n n^{-\beta}\) regime and obtains optimal \(\Theta(n^{1+\beta})\) scaling for \(\beta>4\) under its stated restriction \(2\gamma_n\le n^{\beta-1}\).

## Scope for B054

This paper proves a stationary **mean total queue length** asymptotic for a specific MaxWeight switch model. It neither identifies the joint weak limit nor proves uniqueness of the pair \((L,M)\) in B054 Eq. (7), whether in the source's analytic class or in the probability-admissible class. Its SSC moment bounds and mean formula are useful checks on a proposed B054 limit, but cannot replace transform uniqueness or boundary-measure arguments.
