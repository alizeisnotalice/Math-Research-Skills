# W14-011 — Gamarnik–Hasenbein, instability in stochastic and fluid networks

**Reading:** Complete paper, PDF pp. 1–41. Local PDF and extracted text: `data/gamarnik_stolyar02_instability.pdf` and `.txt`. Formula and proof claims below were read in the full paper; see `reading_coverage.json#W14-011` for section-by-section coverage.

## Definitions and deterministic-fluid result

The model is an open multiclass network with single-server stations, fixed routes, HOL non-idling scheduling, and a Markov state augmented by residual interarrival/service times. The paper separates (i) global stability of every fluid solution, (ii) global weak stability, which rules out a fluid trajectory leaving zero after reaching it, (iii) stochastic rate stability, and (iv) positive Harris recurrence. Assumption A requires each primitive interarrival/service sequence to be IID with finite mean; B requires uniform large-deviation bounds for both primitive partial sums and their renewal counts, conditional on residual age; C requires that from every state, under every scheduling policy, each finite queue-norm level is hit in finite time almost surely. FDP is an additional fluid-decomposition property, not assumed for every network with more than two stations.

**Theorem 6, PDF pp. 14–15:** if the associated fluid model is not weakly stable, then from every initial fluid state $q\ge0$ there is a non-idling fluid solution with linear divergence, $\|\bar Q(t)\|\ge\gamma t$ for all $t\ge0$, and the stated lower bound away from zero. The construction takes a nonzero excursion from zero, rescales it to a unit interval, then concatenates dyadic time-and-space copies. This is a result about existence of a fluid trajectory, not yet a stochastic instability conclusion.

## Stochastic converse and its scope

**Theorem 7, PDF pp. 15–16:** under the paper's stochastic assumptions A, B, C, failure of global weak stability, and FDP, there is a non-idling policy for which the queue process has a.s. positive linear growth rate. For two-station networks FDP holds (Proposition 1, §3 / proof in §4.2), giving Corollary 1: if the associated two-station fluid model is not globally weakly stable, the stochastic network is unstable in the paper's rate-divergence sense under a suitable non-idling policy. Combined with the cited two-station fluid characterization, Corollary 2 gives global rate stability iff the maximum virtual traffic intensity \(\rho^*\le1\).

The proof of the stochastic result uses the linearly divergent fluid trajectory to define a policy U. The proof in §4.3 constructs high-probability blocks in which arrivals and service remain near their fluid rates, controls departures from below and above, and concatenates the blocks; §4.4 combines those estimates to obtain the explicit positive rate in (22). This conclusion is not positive Harris transience for every fixed policy: it is instability under an explicitly constructed non-idling policy. For more than two stations the needed FDP remains an open interface in the paper.

## Transfer boundary

Use this as a precise fluid-to-stochastic instability converse in its stated two-station/FDP regime. Do not transfer the conclusion to B157's deterministic buffer-priority undecidability model, or infer a fixed-policy positive-Harris conclusion. The fluid criterion concerns the associated fluid model and must not be silently replaced by only those fluid paths known to arise as stochastic limits.
