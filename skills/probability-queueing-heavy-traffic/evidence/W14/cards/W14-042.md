## Theorem 5.1: steady-state heavy traffic under SBP

The paper studies a sequence of multiclass queueing networks under a static-buffer-priority (SBP) service discipline, with complete Markov state including queue counts and residual interarrival/service times. The central conclusion is the stationary heavy-traffic limit

\[
r Z^{(r)}\Rightarrow (Z_L^*,0_H),\qquad r\downarrow0,
\]

where the low-priority component has the stationary distribution of an SRBM and the high-priority component collapses to zero.

Theorem 5.1 requires Assumptions 5.1–5.5 and positive definiteness of the limiting covariance \(\Sigma\). These include: finite \(2+\delta_0\) primitive moments (with the stated weaker 5.1A alternative); a linear heavy-traffic parameterization with each station critically loaded in the limit and a positive workload drift vector \(c>0\); a unique stationary law for each prelimit network; uniform integrability of each high-priority steady-state queue count (the moment-SSC assumption); and the Schur complement

\[
R=A_L-A_{LH}A_H^{-1}A_{HL}
\]

being completely-S, with \((R,b)\) satisfying the paper's tight-system condition. Under these hypotheses the limiting SRBM BAR has a unique probability solution. Corollary 5.1 specializes the theorem to reentrant lines under its additional unbounded/spread-out arrival conditions: LBFS, and a specified two-station/five-class SBP example when Condition 2.11 holds.

## Proof route

The proof starts from stationary BAR identities for the prelimit Markov process and applies Palm measures to obtain an asymptotic BAR. It expands the arrival/service exponential transforms to second order, extracts subsequential transform limits, and uses the high-priority uniform-integrability assumption to establish transform SSC. A particular choice

\[
\theta_H=-(A_H^{-1})^T A_{LH}^T\theta_L
\]

eliminates the high-priority boundary terms in the limit. The resulting low-priority BAR has parameters \((R,\Sigma,b)\); the tight-system lemma gives a unique probability measure, identifying every subsequential limit and hence the stationary limit.

## Scope boundary for B054

This is a rigorous steady-state convergence result for the specified SBP multiclass networks under its stability, moment-SSC and matrix hypotheses. It is a useful benchmark for what a BAR-based stationary proof needs. It does not prove the B054 switch equation's uniqueness in its probability-admissible class: the switch model, unused-service terms, test domain and SSC interface must be derived separately.
