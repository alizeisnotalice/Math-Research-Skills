# W14-005 — Jhunjhunwala–Maguluri, switch heavy-traffic transform

**Reading:** Full preprint, PDF pp. 1–46. Local fulltext: `data/ordos_primary_123.pdf` (the duplicate `queue_joint.pdf` has the same hash). Identity checked against arXiv:2206.06504v2. A 2024 SIGMETRICS short version still states the general result conditionally.

## Exact functional equation

For an \(n\times n\) input-queued switch with \(n^2\) queues, let \(B\in\{0,1\}^{n^2\times 2n}\) be the row/column incidence matrix, \(S=\operatorname{col}(B)\), and \(\operatorname{rank}(B)=2n-1\). Under the stated heavy-traffic family and a scheduling rule satisfying SSC (Definition 2), Theorem 3, PDF p. 15, defines

\[
\Theta=\{\theta\in\mathbb C^{n^2}:\theta\in S,\ \operatorname{Re}(B^T\theta)\le 0_{2n}\},
\]

\[
L(\theta)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}E_{\pi^\epsilon}[e^{\epsilon\langle\theta,q\rangle}],\qquad
M_k(\theta)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\epsilon^{-1}E_{\pi^\epsilon}[u_k e^{\epsilon\langle\theta,q\rangle}],
\]

and obtains

\[
\bigl(2\langle\theta,\mathbf1_{n^2}\rangle-n\langle\theta,\sigma^2\theta\rangle\bigr)L(\theta)-2n\langle\theta,M(\theta)\rangle=0,\quad\theta\in\Theta.\tag{7}
\]

Here \(u_k\) is unused/wasted service at queue \(k\); it records boundary effects. The derivation uses stationarity of the complex-exponential Lyapunov drift, a second-order expansion in \(\epsilon\), and SSC to restrict the test vector to the collapsed subspace.

## Uniqueness scope and conditional distribution

The paper's precise analytic class, Eq. (8), p. 15, is

\[
\mathcal L(\Theta)=\{f:\Theta\to\mathbb C: f\ne0,\ f\text{ holomorphic, continuous, and bounded on }\Theta\}.
\]

**Conjecture 1, p. 15** says that \((L,M)\) is the unique solution of (7) when all these functions lie in \(\mathcal L(\Theta)\). The probability-derived pair belongs to this analytic class by Lemma 3, but the paper does not define \(\mathcal L(\Theta)\) as a probability-Laplace class.

**Normalization audit (PDF pp. 15–16; equations and statement checked on the rendered pages).** Equation (7) is homogeneous of degree one in the pair: if \((L,M)\) solves it, then \((cL,cM)\) also solves it for every nonzero constant \(c\). Equation (8)'s \(\mathcal L(\Theta)\) excludes the zero function but does not require \(L(0)=1\). Thus the **literal displayed analytic-class formulation** of Conjecture 1 cannot have a unique pair once it has one solution. This is a source-formulation ambiguity/normalization omission, not a counterexample in the probability-transform class: Theorem 3 itself defines its queue-derived \(L\) as a steady-state limit, which implies \(L(0)=1\), and describes it as the limiting probability transform. The conjecture may be intended to retain that stochastic normalization implicitly, but its stated function-class condition does not say so. State the literal scaling obstruction separately; do not present it as solving the intended normalized or probability-admissible uniqueness problem.

There are three distinct targets: (a) the literal unnormalized analytic class in (8), where scalar homogeneity rules out uniqueness; (b) a **normalized analytic class**, adding at least \(L(0)=1\), which removes this elementary scaling obstruction but still has no uniqueness proof here; and (c) **probability-admissible pairs**, which additionally encode queue-transform positivity/support and a valid origin for all boundary terms. For (c), \(L\) must be the Laplace transform of a probability law supported on the nonnegative SSC cone (including \(L(0)=1\), positivity/completely-monotone restrictions on the real domain, and the needed moment/extension bounds); each \(M_k\) must arise as a limit of nonnegative boundary-service measures, with tightness/uniform-integrability and face support justified. Do not call an arbitrary analytic solution a probability transform. A proof in class (c) is the relevant queueing uniqueness result; it remains open. Uniqueness in the normalized analytic class would imply uniqueness among probability-admissible solutions **if every probability-admissible solution is proved to belong to that analytic class**. That inclusion/membership has not been verified here, so the implication remains conditional.

**Theorem 4, p. 16** gives, only assuming Conjecture 1 and symmetric arrival variance \(\sigma^2 I_{n^2}\),

\[
\epsilon q_{i+n(j-1)}\Rightarrow \Upsilon_i+\Upsilon_{n+j}-2\min_{1\le r\le 2n}\Upsilon_r,
\]

where the \(\Upsilon_r\) are IID exponentials of mean \(\sigma^2/2\). A coordinate marginal is exponential with probability \(1/n\) and Erlang-2 with probability \(1-1/n\) (Remark 4, pp. 16–17). This formula is conditional for general \(n\). The 3-queue switch and N-system have separate two-variable uniqueness proofs (Lemmas 2 and 5); those do not settle the general switch.

**Numerical check, not proof:** `../experiments/b054_candidate_marginal.m` simulated 500,000 candidate samples for \(n=3,\sigma^2=2\). The empirical mean was 1.66934435 versus 1.66666667; maximum absolute CDF error on the five-point grid \(\{0.1,0.5,1,2,4\}\) was 0.00118560. This checks the stated marginal mixture only; it says nothing about Eq. (7) uniqueness or the switch limit theorem.
