## Theorem and assumptions

The state space \(G\) is a convex polyhedral cone with specified inward normals and constraint directions. The Skorokhod map must be well defined for all càdlàg inputs and globally Lipschitz (Condition 2.1). The diffusion coefficients \(b,\sigma\) are globally Lipschitz and \(\sigma\) is bounded (Condition 2.2). Outside a bounded set, the drift lies in \(C(\delta)\), a uniform interior portion of the cone \(C=\operatorname{cone}\{-d_i\}\) generated by the negative constraint directions (Condition 2.3). The diffusion covariance is uniformly nondegenerate (Condition 2.4). Under these four conditions, Theorem 2.2 (physical PDF p. 9) says the strong Markov constrained diffusion is positive recurrent and has a unique invariant probability measure.

## Proof chain

Section 3 converts the constrained ODE path \(z(t)=\Gamma(x+\int_0^t v(s)\,ds)\) into an ODE through a discrete projection. If \(v(t)\in C(\delta)\), Theorem 3.2 (physical pp. 10–12) proves the decay estimate

\[
|z(t)|\le \frac{K^2|x|^2}{K|x|+\delta t},
\]

where \(K\) is the Lipschitz constant of the Skorokhod map. Section 4 uses this deterministic decay to construct a Lyapunov function for the diffusion, controls the martingale fluctuations with exponential bounds, proves uniformly finite expected hitting times to a sufficiently large compact set (Theorem 4.1, physical pp. 20–22), and establishes tight transition laws (Lemma 4.4). The Feller/strong Markov property, uniform ellipticity and the recurrence estimates yield existence and uniqueness of the invariant probability.

This is a constrained-diffusion theorem. It does not by itself imply positive Harris recurrence for a queueing network or an input-queued switch; a queueing application must derive the correct Markov state, Skorokhod map, drift condition and nondegeneracy assumptions.
