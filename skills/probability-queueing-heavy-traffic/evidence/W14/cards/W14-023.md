## Model and stability criterion

The paper treats SRBM in the three-dimensional nonnegative orthant with nonsingular covariance and completely-$\mathcal S$ reflection matrix. Stability means finite expected hitting time of every open neighborhood of the origin from every starting state. The necessary condition (1.6) is that $R$ is nonsingular and $R^{-1}\theta<0$; Appendix C proves its necessity. The paper shows that in dimension three this condition together with one of two tests is necessary and sufficient:

1. If $(\theta,R)\in C=C_1\cup C_2$, where $C_1,C_2$ are the clockwise/counterclockwise boundary-spiral regimes of §3, stability holds exactly when the one-cycle gain $\beta(\theta,R)<1$. For $C_1$, the gain is the product of the three successive axis-intercept ratios in Eq. (3.6); for $C_2$, Eq. (3.7) gives the reversed-cycle counterpart.
2. If $(\theta,R)\notin C$, stability holds exactly when the LCP (2.5)–(2.7) has no divergent solution. Under (1.6), the proper solution is $(u^*,0)=(-R^{-1}\theta,0)$, and any other LCP solution is divergent (Lemma 1).

## Proof chain and scope boundary

Dupuis–Williams' fluid criterion supplies sufficiency when every associated fluid path is attracted to zero. Theorem 3 proves non-positive-recurrence when the boundary spiral has gain at least one: the proof constructs a scalar projection $\xi=u'Z$, a continuous martingale with bounded quadratic-variation rate, and a nonnegative compensator so that $\xi-\xi(0)\ge M+A$; optional stopping forces an infinite expected hitting time. For the non-spiral case, Theorem 4 reduces instability to divergent LCP solutions. Sections 6–7 classify those solutions: Lemmas 7–8 eliminate/reduce Categories IV and III, and the remaining Categories I, II and V yield transience by explicit face-by-face Brownian estimates and stopping arguments. Conversely, when no divergent solution remains, the previously known 3D fluid result gives positive recurrence.

The equivalence between stability and attraction of every fluid path is special to dimension three in this paper. The authors explicitly leave recurrence classification in dimension four and above open. Do not apply the LCP classification, spiral gain criterion, or full converse as a general $d$-dimensional SRBM theorem.
