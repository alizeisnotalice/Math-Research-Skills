# W14-006 — Gamarnik–Katz, undecidability of queue stability

**Reading:** Full paper, PDF pp. 1–31. Local fulltext: `data/queue_undecidable.pdf` and `.txt`. Identity and journal DOI checked from the title page / arXiv:0708.1034v2.

Theorem 1, physical PDF p. 7 (printed pagination differs), proves algorithmic undecidability of deciding whether a specified deterministic multiclass queueing network, nonpreemptive static buffer-priority policy, and initial queue vector form a stable triplet. In their Definition 1, a triplet is stable when \(\sup_{s\ge0}\sum_i Q_i(s)<\infty\); the network-policy pair is stable if this holds for every initial state. Interarrival and service times are deterministic. The construction permits finite or infinite buffers and possibly zero service times; finite-buffer arrivals can be dropped.

The proof reduces the halting/stability behavior of a counter machine to a queueing-network instance. Its stability definition and deterministic model are not positive Harris recurrence of a stochastic queueing chain.

The authors explicitly conjecture that undecidability persists when **all** buffers are infinite and **all** service times are nonzero. That extension is not proved in this paper. A bounded web search on 2026-11-13 found the original conjecture and related general undecidability work but no direct proof/counterexample; this is not an exhaustive literature-status certificate.
