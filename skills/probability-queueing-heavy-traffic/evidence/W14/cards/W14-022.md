# W14-022 — Dai–Williams, SRBM existence and uniqueness in convex polyhedra

**Reading:** Complete paper, physical PDF pp. 1–40 (printed pp. 1–40). Local fulltext: `data/dai_williams95_srbm.pdf` with extracted text in `.txt`; the scanned pages were read page by page. Core statements and formula displays were visually checked on rendered PDF pp. 4, 22, and 37. See `reading_coverage.json#W14-022` for section and appendix coverage.

## Model and theorem scope

The state space is a convex polyhedron $S=\{x:n_i\cdot x\ge b_i, i\in J\}$ with a minimal face description. The SRBM has constant drift and positive-definite Brownian covariance, and face $F_i$ has reflection direction $v_i$. Assumption 1.1 imposes, for every maximal set $K$ of intersecting faces, both conditions: there are $a_i>0$ with $w^K=\sum_{i\in K}a_i v_i$ satisfying $n_j\cdot w^K>0$ for each $j\in K$, and there are $c_i>0$ with $\eta^K=\sum_{i\in K}c_i n_i$ satisfying $\eta^K\cdot v_j>0$ for each $j\in K$. These are the source's $S.a$ and $S.b$ conditions, respectively, equivalent to the corresponding S-matrix conditions on the principal submatrices of $NR$.

Theorem 1.3 (physical PDF p. 4) states that Assumption 1.1 gives existence for every starting point and uniqueness in law of the pair $(Z,Y)$; the resulting canonical process is Feller continuous and strong Markov. Proposition 1.2 establishes necessity of the condition in the simple-polyhedron case. For a nonsimple polyhedron, necessity of $S.b$ is explicitly left open; the theorem supplies sufficiency only. Do not turn the geometric condition into a general BAR or stationary-transform uniqueness theorem.

## Proof route and usable interface

Section 2 constructs a solution to a patchwork martingale problem and establishes compact containment and subsequential martingale limits. Section 3 constructs a global test function whose directional derivatives control boundary push, then time-changes to the constrained martingale problem and recovers an SRBM by the martingale characterization and Itô's formula. The test-function construction is in Appendix B. For uniqueness, Section 4 proves an oscillation estimate controlling increments of $(Z,Y)$ by increments of the driving Brownian path and proves that local-time measures do not charge intersections of multiple faces (Theorem 4.4, physical PDF p. 22). Section 5 localizes to cones and uses induction on dimension; in the pointed-cone case it combines radial scaling, annular compactness and an ergodic/Doeblin argument, then patches the local result for a general polyhedron.

Use the theorem to check well-posedness and Markov/Feller properties of a proposed polyhedral SRBM limit, after checking its geometric hypotheses. It does not prove existence of a stationary distribution, BAR uniqueness in a broad signed or analytic class, or a queueing heavy-traffic limit.


## 2005 correction note (merged supplement)

The two-page author-hosted correction note was read in full and merged into this source card; it is not counted as an independent research paper. It states that Lemma 4.1 as originally printed is false for arbitrary \(\epsilon\) (the note gives an interval example), but is valid for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon<\epsilon_0\). The corrected decomposition is

\[
S=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal C}F_K^\epsilon,
\]

where \(\mathcal C\) consists of maximal intersecting face sets together with the empty set. The proof uses positive separation between disjoint closed faces and chooses \(\epsilon\) small enough for the face-neighborhood estimate.

The note also replaces the global oscillation claim in Lemma 4.3 by a **local** version: there are \(\kappa,\delta>0\) depending only on \((S,R)\), such that for every \(T>0\), continuous input \(x\) with \(x(0)\in S\), every \((S,R)\)-regulation \((y,z)\), and interval \([t_1,t_2]\subset[0,T]\),

\[
\operatorname{Osc}(y,[t_1,t_2]),\quad \operatorname{Osc}(z,[t_1,t_2])
\le \kappa\operatorname{Osc}(x,[t_1,t_2])
\quad\text{if }\operatorname{Osc}(x,[t_1,t_2])\le\delta.
\]

Dai and Williams state that these corrected local forms suffice for the paper's main existence and uniqueness results because only local versions are used to establish tightness. Do not cite the uncorrected global Lemma 4.3 estimate.
