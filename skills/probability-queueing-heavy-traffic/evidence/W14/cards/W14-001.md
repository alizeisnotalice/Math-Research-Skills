# W14-001 — Boon–Boxma–Winands, polling open problems

**Reading:** Full paper, PDF pp. 1–9. Local fulltext: `data/ordos_primary_104.pdf` and `.txt`. Bibliographic identity checked against arXiv:1408.0142.

## A006: two-queue G/1-L transform

The model in §2 (PDF pp. 2–3) has independent Poisson arrivals, IID service and switch-over times, and a stable joint steady state. Queue 1 is gated; queue 2 is 1-limited. Equations (1)–(4) give the embedded queue-length PGF recursion. The branching-type substitution fails at the 1-limited queue: its update includes the boundary term (F_2(z_1,0)). In E/1-L the authors can close the recursion because the exhaustive-queue offspring PGF depends only on (z_2); that simplification is unavailable for G/1-L.

**Open Problem 1, §3.2, PDF p. 4:** determine the joint queue-length PGF at polling instants for G/1-L. The paper identifies two failed substitutions: (z_1=0) yields only a derivative, while (z_1=h_1(z_1,z_2)) creates nested shifts and does not close by iteration. Equation (4) plus analyticity suggests a Riemann–Hilbert boundary problem with shift; this is a proposed route, not a solution.

## A007: large deterministic switch-over

For branching-type service and Poisson arrivals, §4.1, Eq. (7), PDF p. 4 gives

\[
\frac{W_i}{S}\Rightarrow \frac{1-\rho_i}{1-\rho}U_i,
\qquad U_i\sim \mathrm{Unif}\!\left[\frac{1-\Phi_i}{\Phi_i},\frac1{\Phi_i}\right],
\]

as deterministic switch-over times grow with fixed ratios. Here \(\Phi_i=1-\partial h_i/\partial z_i|_{z=1}\) is the discipline's exhaustiveness. This is a Poisson-input result.

**Conjecture 4.2, §4.2, PDF p. 5; Open Problem 2, p. 7:** for general renewal arrivals, a cyclic polling system converges to a deterministic cyclic system as deterministic switch-over times tend to infinity. The paper offers a circular fluid/SLLN heuristic and simulations, and explicitly says the Poisson proof uses the Poisson structure. No proof, rate, or general renewal limit law is supplied.

**Transfer boundary:** keep the arrival law, branching-type requirement, deterministic scaling, embedded observation epoch, and weak/distributional mode explicit. A006 is a PGF problem; A007 is a large-switch-over limit problem. Neither is resolved by the other.
