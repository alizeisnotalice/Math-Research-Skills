# W14-019 — Boxma–Groenendijk, cyclic-service pseudo-conservation

**Reading status:** Full paper read, PDF pp. 1–16 (printed pp. 949–964). Formula-heavy PDF pp. 3–5, 7–9, 11–15 were rendered with scripts/render_pdf_page.swift and visually checked. Local PDF: data/boxma_groenendijk87_polling.pdf.

## Model and stability conditions

There are \(N\) infinite-capacity queues with independent Poisson arrival streams of rates \(\lambda_i\). Type-\(i\) service times are iid with first two moments \(\beta_i,\beta_i^{(2)}\); \( \rho_i=\lambda_i\beta_i\) and \(\rho=\sum_i\rho_i\). One server visits the queues in a fixed cyclic order. Switch-over times are independent, with mean \(s_i\) for the transition \(i\to i+1\); \(s=\sum_i s_i\) is mean total switch-over time per cycle and \(s^{(2)}\) is the second moment of the total cycle switch-over time. The paper allows mixed exhaustive (E), gated (G), nonexhaustive one-customer (NE), and semiexhaustive (SE) strategies. It assumes an ergodic stationary system.

The load condition \(\rho<1\) is necessary and sufficient for E/G queues. At each NE queue one also needs \(\lambda_i s/(1-\rho)<1\); at each SE queue one needs \(\lambda_i s(1-\rho_i)/(1-\rho)<1\). For mixed disciplines, apply the extra condition for each NE/SE queue. These are specific to the paper's strict cyclic-visit model, where the server switches even when the system is empty.

## Theorem 1: workload stochastic decomposition

Let \(V_c\) be total workload at an arbitrary epoch in the cyclic system, \(V\) the workload in the corresponding \(M/G/1\) queue with aggregate Poisson input and the mixture of service laws, and \(Y\) the cyclic-system workload observed at an arbitrary epoch in a switching interval. Theorem 1 (PDF pp. 7–10, printed pp. 955–958) states
\[
V_c\ \overset d=\ V+Y,\qquad V\text{ independent of }Y.
\]
Proof chain: stationarity gives \(\Pr\{\text{server serving}\}=\rho\) and \(\Pr\{\text{switching}\}=1-\rho\), so the LST of \(V_c\) splits into serving and switching contributions. Lemma 1 factors the serving-epoch transform into the corresponding \(M/G/1\) serving-epoch workload transform times the LST of \(Y\). Its proof couples the cyclic system to an \(M/G/1\) system with nonpreemptive LCFS service and vacations during the switch intervals; it tracks the ancestor/offspring family of the job found in service, using PASTA to identify its arrival workload with \(Y\), then uses the regenerative work-conserving evolution of the remaining workload. Substitution yields the product LST and hence the independent-sum distribution. The result depends on Poisson arrivals/PASTA and the stated ergodic vacation structure; it is a workload decomposition, not a joint queue-length PGF identity.

## Theorem 2: mixed-strategy pseudo-conservation law

Write \(E W_i\) for mean type-\(i\) waiting time before service. The paper first obtains
\[
\sum_i\rho_i E W_i
=\rho\,\frac{\sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i^{(2)}}{2(1-\rho)}+E Y
\tag{3.4}
\]
from the decomposition and the \(M/G/1\) workload formula. It then computes \(E Y\) by averaging switching-epoch workload over switch intervals (Eqs. (3.5)–(3.10), PDF pp. 11–12). The strategy-dependent residual \(E M_i^{(1)}\) is evaluated separately:

- E: \(E M_i^{(1)}=0\), since the queue is emptied at departure.
- G: \(E M_i^{(1)}=\rho_i^2s/(1-\rho)\).
- NE: relate the probability a visit serves one customer, PASTA, Little's law, and the leftover work \(T_i\), obtaining Eq. (3.15).
- SE: use the one-customer reduction rule, the \(M/G/1\) busy-period identity, and the arrival-epoch population to obtain Eqs. (3.16)–(3.21).

Theorem 2 (PDF pp. 14–15, printed pp. 962–963) combines those residual terms. Let \(e,g,ne,se\) be the queue-index sets with the corresponding strategies. The displayed pseudo-conservation identity is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{i\in e}\rho_i E W_i+\sum_{i\in g}\rho_i E W_i
+\sum_{i\in ne}\rho_i\left(1-\frac{\lambda_i s}{1-\rho}\right)E W_i\\
&\quad+\sum_{i\in se}\rho_i\left(1-\frac{\lambda_i s(1-\rho_i)}{1-\rho}\right)E W_i\\
&=\rho\frac{\sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i^{(2)}}{2(1-\rho)}
-\sum_{i\in se}\frac{\lambda_i^2\beta_i^{(2)}\rho_i s}{2(1-\rho)}
+\rho\frac{s^{(2)}}{2s}
+\frac{s}{2(1-\rho)}\left(\rho^2-\sum_i\rho_i^2\right)
+\frac{s}{1-\rho}\sum_{i\in g\cup ne}\rho_i^2 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.22}
\]
The coefficients on \(E W_i\) are not all \(\rho_i\); in particular the NE/SE stability multipliers must be retained. The right side separates the corresponding zero-switch-over workload term from switch-over variability, inter-queue interaction, and strategy-specific residual work.

Section 4 states that FCFS can be replaced by any work-conserving within-queue discipline consistent with the global visit strategy. The identity is useful as a check on approximations or asymptotics for mean waits. It does not solve a general polling PGF boundary problem or the large-deterministic-switch fluid limit.

## Relevance and transfer limit

This is a solved cyclic polling model with Poisson arrivals, work-conserving queue service, fixed cyclic order, and explicit switch-over moments. It provides workload identities and a pseudo-conservation check for A006/A007/A008. It does not establish the adaptive limited-service stability criterion in A008, nor does it prove the shifted two-variable PGF uniqueness or renewal-fluid limit requested in A006/A007.
